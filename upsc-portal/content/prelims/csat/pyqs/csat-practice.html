CSAT — Practice Pyramid

Mixed-difficulty question set in official-style proportions; answers at the end of each block.

Block A — Rapid numeracy (10)

  1. A sum doubles in 8 years at simple interest. SI rate % = ?
  2. Average of 7 numbers is 50; removing 68 and 85 yields the average = ?
  3. Two pipes fill in 4 h and 6 h together time = ?
  4. Selling price with 20% profit = cost ₹840 → ?
  5. 48 students sit in rows of 6 — % students of 250?
  6. LCM(15,20,24)=?
  7. 0.125 as fraction of 0.25 = ?
  8. If 10% loss at ₹450 → CP = ?
  9. Square root of 1764?
  10. 3:5 ratio, sum 64 → smaller share?

Ans: 1) 12.5% 2) 46 3) 2.4 h 4) ₹1,008 5) 19.2% 6) 120 7) 1/2 8) ₹500 9) 42 10) 24

Block B — Reasoning set (8)

  1. A is 4th from left; B is 9th from right in a 15-line queue — how many between? (2)
  2. Leap-year Mondays: Jan 1 2024 = Mon → Jan 1 2025 = ? (Wed — leap step)
  3. In a code: CAT=24, DOG=26 → figure rule? (A=1…Z=26 sums: 3+1+20=24; 4+15+7=26); write "FISH" (42)
  4. P & Q are sisters; R is P's brother; S is R's son — Q's relationship to S → (paternal aunt)
  5. Series: 7, 14, 28, 56 → next (112)
  6. No cats are dogs; some dogs are fishes → which follows? options: (a) no fish-cat overlap guaranteed → (only "some dogs not necessarily cats" stands; classical Venn answer: none of definite "all/Some are" follow directly — prefer "can't infer" breed)
  7. Direction: walk 3 km E, 4 km S → displacement from origin (5 km SE)
  8. If all engineers are swimmers and some swimmers are lawyers, conclusion "some engineers are lawyers" → (does not follow)

Block C — RC (2 minis)

Mini-1 "DPI is often lumped as a growth story, but its architecture matters more than its size. Where private rails extract tolls, public rails distribute capacity; both are infrastructures of trust. The risk is not expansion but ossification — fixed standards that outpace imagination. India's digital stack works because it is open-standards-first; it declines when compliance becomes congestion."

(i) The author's primary concern = (a) DPI expansion (b) balance between openness and ossification (c) cost of rails (d) none (ii) The passage implies tolls belong to which model? (a) public (b) private (c) both (d) neither

Ans: (i) b (ii) b

Mini-2 "Cities panic about flood, not because of rainfall event counts but because of their own memory-loss: wetlands paved, storm-drains choked, percolation punished by parking lots. Nature's response time is geological; policy's is electoral."

(i) The writer's tone is best described as: (a) neutral descriptive (b) sardonic commentator (c) optimistic reformer (d) elegiac (ii) The second sentence highlights: (a) rainfall-as-cause (b) infrastructure memory-loss (c) policy efforts (d) geological slowness vs policy tempo

Ans: (i) b (ii) d

Block D — Practice test mapping sheet (score-keeper)

  • A (10) + B (8) + C (4) = 22 markers here ≈ 27.5% of a full CSAT paper; extrapolate accuracy → target 25/22 zone is not needed; aim ≥18 correct with ≤3 wrong for safe zone.

Common fail patterns (final pass)

  • Negative questions ("which is NOT true") flipping under fatigue.
  • Fractions denominators forgotten post-LCM.
  • Code-question alphabet vs positional-sum confusion.
  • RC: answering from general knowledge instead of passage.
  • Bubble-offset errors: strictly bubble one row at a time.
