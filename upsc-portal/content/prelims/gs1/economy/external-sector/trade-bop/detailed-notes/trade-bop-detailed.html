External Trade and Balance of Payments — Detailed Notes

BoP structure, India's services-led profile, FTAs and export-promotion instruments.

1. BoP structure

  • Current account: goods (chronic BoT deficit), services (IT-BPM surplus), primary income (interest-dividends, negative), secondary income (remittances — world #1, ~$120bn band). Capital account: FDI (stable), FPI (volatile), ECBs, NRI deposits, trade credit. Balancing item = reserve changes.
  • CAD anchors: FY13 peak ~4.8% GDP (taper-tantrum) → FY21 sub-1% → FY24 ~0.7%; oil-gold sensitivity; rupee managed float; reserves ~$650-700bn band (top-4).

2. Trade profile

  • Merchandise exports: engineering, petroleum products, pharma, gems-jewellery, electronics (rising), agri (rice #1, marine, spices). Imports: crude (largest), gold, electronics, machinery, coal. Partners: US #1 destination, China #2 source (largest deficit), UAE-Gulf. FTP 2023: $2 trillion combined exports by 2030; districts-as-export-hubs.
  • Services: IT-BPM engine (~$200bn band); travel recovering; GATS modes 1-4 — India pushes Mode 1 (cross-border supply) + Mode 4 (movement of natural persons).

3. Policy instruments

  • FTP 2023 (continuous, no sunset); RoDTEP (2021; replaced MEIS; WTO-consistent duty remission); RoSCTL (apparel-made-ups); EPCG + Advance Authorisation; SEZ (2005 act) - EOU; DGTR (2018 merged trade-remedies authority); QCOs; LEADS logistics index; Gati Shakti.
  • FTAs: UAE CEPA (2022), Australia ECTA (2022; CECA talks), Japan-Singapore-ASEAN-Korea-Malaysia legacy, EFTA TEPA (2024: $100bn/15-yr investment commitment), India-UK CETA (2025), EU negotiations; RCEP exit (2019); IPEF partial join (supply-chain pillar).

MCQ hooks

  • CAD = goods + services + primary + secondary income (capital flows excluded).
  • Remittances = secondary income, world #1.
  • RoDTEP → replaced MEIS 2021 (WTO ruling context); DGTR = 2018.
  • FTP 2023 target: $2tn by 2030; no sunset clause.
  • EFTA TEPA 2024 ($100bn/15y); UK CETA 2025; RCEP exit 2019.
  • GATS: Mode 1 supply, Mode 3 presence, Mode 4 person-movement.

6. Balance of Payments: Anatomy of the Accounts

The Balance of Payments is a double-entry record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world over a period, usually a year. Because it is double-entry, the BoP always balances in accounting terms; what people mean by a "BoP crisis" is a deficit on the current account that cannot be financed, forcing a drawdown of reserves. Keep the vocabulary precise — the balance of trade is merely goods; the current account is goods plus services plus income plus transfers; the BoP is the current account plus the capital and financial accounts plus reserve movements and errors and omissions.

The current account has four components:

  • Merchandise trade — visible exports minus imports. India runs a persistent and large merchandise deficit, driven structurally by crude petroleum, gold and, increasingly, electronics and Chinese manufactured goods.
  • Services — the invisibles that rescue India's external position: software and IT-enabled services, business and professional services, travel, transport and, lately, Global Capability Centres. India runs a large services surplus.
  • Primary income — investment income, interest, dividends and compensation of employees. India is typically in deficit here because it pays out more on foreign investment than it earns abroad.
  • Secondary income / transfers — chiefly remittances from the Indian diaspora. India is the world's largest recipient of remittances, and these unilateral transfers are the second great stabiliser after services.

The arithmetic that matters: a huge goods deficit, partially offset by services and remittances, leaves a current account deficit that in normal years is modest, roughly 1–2% of GDP. The conventional prudential threshold is around 2.5% of GDP; beyond it, financing becomes fragile. Note the identity CAD = investment minus domestic saving — a current account deficit is by definition the import of foreign savings, which is benign if it finances productive investment and dangerous if it finances consumption.

The capital and financial account covers foreign direct investment (a lasting management interest, generally 10% or more of equity — stable, long-term, bringing technology and management), foreign portfolio investment in equity and debt (liquid, volatile, the classic "hot money" whose sudden reversal precipitates crises), external commercial borrowings, NRI deposits, trade credit and banking capital. India's capital account is partially convertible: the current account was made fully convertible in 1994 under Article VIII of the IMF, but capital account convertibility remains calibrated — a decision widely credited with insulating India in 1997 and 2008. The Tarapore Committees (1997, 2006) laid out preconditions of fiscal consolidation, low inflation and a sound financial system.

7. Exchange Rates, Reserves and External Vulnerability

India follows a managed float: the rupee's value is market-determined, but the RBI intervenes to smooth volatility rather than to defend a level. Depreciation is a market-driven fall in value under a floating regime; devaluation is a deliberate official reduction under a fixed regime — India devalued in 1949, 1966 and 1991. Depreciation makes exports cheaper and imports dearer, which should narrow the trade deficit, but only if demand is sufficiently elastic; the Marshall-Lerner condition requires the sum of export and import demand elasticities to exceed one, and the J-curve effect describes the initial worsening of the deficit before it improves.

REER and NEER. The nominal effective exchange rate is a trade-weighted index of the rupee against a basket of currencies; the real effective exchange rate adjusts for relative inflation. A REER above 100 indicates the rupee is overvalued and export competitiveness is eroding, even if the nominal rate looks stable — a subtlety frequently tested.

Foreign exchange reserves comprise foreign currency assets, gold, SDRs and the reserve tranche position with the IMF. Adequacy is judged by import cover (months of imports financeable) and by the ratio of short-term debt to reserves. India's reserves, well over 600 billion dollars, provide a comfortable cushion — the contrast with 1991, when reserves covered barely two weeks of imports and gold was physically pledged to the Bank of England, is the standard illustration. Reserves are not costless: they earn low returns and their accumulation must be sterilised to prevent monetary expansion, historically through the Market Stabilisation Scheme.

External debt indicators to know: the external debt to GDP ratio, the short-term debt share (the key vulnerability metric), the debt service ratio, and the currency composition, in which the US dollar dominates. The impossible trinity or trilemma states that a country cannot simultaneously have a fixed exchange rate, free capital movement and an independent monetary policy — India's chosen corner is a managed float with partial capital controls and monetary autonomy. The taper tantrum of 2013, when the rupee collapsed on the mere signal of US monetary tightening, is the canonical case study of a wide CAD meeting volatile portfolio flows.

8. India's Trade Composition, Policy and Institutions

Export basket: petroleum products, gems and jewellery, engineering goods, chemicals and pharmaceuticals, textiles and garments, agricultural products (rice, marine products, spices), and electronics — the last growing fast on the back of mobile-phone assembly. Import basket: crude petroleum (the single largest item, making India acutely exposed to oil prices), gold, electronics and machinery, coal, edible oils and fertilisers. The trade deficit with China is the standing political concern, reflecting India's dependence on intermediate goods, active pharmaceutical ingredients and electronic components.

The Foreign Trade Policy, administered by the DGFT, is now open-ended rather than five-yearly. Instruments include the RoDTEP scheme, which rebates embedded duties and taxes on exports in a WTO-compatible way after the export subsidy schemes were found non-compliant; Advance Authorisation and EPCG for duty-free inputs and capital goods; SEZs, now being reworked into the DESH framework; Districts as Export Hubs; the Interest Equalisation Scheme; and export credit insurance through ECGC. Trade facilitation has advanced through faceless assessment, the Authorised Economic Operator programme and the National Logistics Policy, since India's competitiveness is constrained as much by logistics costs as by tariffs.

Trade agreements. Distinguish a preferential trade agreement (tariff concessions on a limited list), a free trade agreement (tariffs eliminated on substantially all trade), a customs union (FTA plus a common external tariff), a common market (adding free factor movement) and an economic union (adding policy harmonisation). India has agreements with ASEAN, Japan, South Korea, the UAE (CEPA), Australia (ECTA) and EFTA, and is negotiating with the UK and the EU. India withdrew from RCEP in 2019 citing the trade deficit with China, inadequate protection for agriculture and dairy, the absence of credible rules of origin safeguards and an unsatisfactory base year for tariff cuts. Rules of origin exist precisely to prevent trade deflection, whereby goods from a non-member are routed through a member to capture preferential tariffs.

WTO issues of Indian interest: the peace clause shielding public stockholding for food security from subsidy challenge pending a permanent solution; the paralysis of the Appellate Body owing to blocked appointments, which has rendered dispute settlement largely toothless; fisheries subsidies; the moratorium on customs duties on electronic transmissions, which India and others wish to end; and the developed-country push on the "new issues" of investment facilitation, e-commerce and environment, which India resists as departures from the development mandate of the Doha Round.

Trap watch. The BoP always balances by construction; only its components can be in deficit. Balance of trade counts goods only. FDI is 10% or more equity with lasting interest; below that it is portfolio. Devaluation is deliberate and official, depreciation is market-driven. A REER above 100 means overvaluation. India's current account is fully convertible, the capital account only partially. India left RCEP but is in ASEAN, Japan, Korea, UAE and Australia agreements. CAD equals investment minus domestic saving. The J-curve means a deficit worsens before it improves after depreciation.
