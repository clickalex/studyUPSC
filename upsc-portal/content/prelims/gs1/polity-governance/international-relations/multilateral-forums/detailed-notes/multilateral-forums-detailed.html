Multilateral Forums and India — Detailed Notes

UN system + economic bodies + climate + newer clubs. Match forum → membership → India's status.

1. UN system

  • UNGA: 193 members, one-nation-one-vote, resolutions non-binding; UNSC: 15 (P5 veto + 10 non-perm 2-year); India non-perm 8 times (latest 2021-22), G4 (Brazil-Germany-Japan) expansion push, Coffee Club/Uniting-for-Consensus (Italy-Pakistan) opposition.
  • ECOSOC (54), UNHRC (47 seats, India elected), ICJ (15 judges; Dalveer Bhandari re-elected 2017), UNESCO, WHO, ILO, FAO, WIPO and ITU; UNFPA-UNICEF-UNDP funds; UN Peacekeeping: among largest troop contributors historically.
  • Financing: UNGA budget scale; India: small funding share but among the largest troop contributors.

2. Economic institutions

  • IMF: quota-votes
  • India ~2.75% quota (among the top 10)
  • SDRs
  • World Bank Group: IBRD-IDA-IFC-MIGA-ICSID
  • WTO (1995, successor GATT): MCs — Bali 2013 (food-stockholding peace clause), Nairobi 2015, MC12 2022 fisheries-subsidy deal, MC13 Abu Dhabi 2024 (fisheries extension)
  • India leads G33 + G20 developing grouping on agriculture
  • TRIPS waiver fight (vaccines).
  • ADB (1966), AIIB (2016), NDB (BRICS 2015, Shanghai HQ); IMF-WB annual meetings.

3. Climate & energy bodies

  • UNFCCC (1992 Rio)
  • Kyoto (1997 CBDR)
  • Paris 2015 (NDCs, 1.5-2C, ratchet)
  • COP21-28 milestones (COP26 Glasgow coal-phase-down; COP27 loss-damage fund agreed; COP28 Dubai operationalised + global-stocktake; COP29 Baku 2024 finance-goal 'NCQG'). India: Panchamrit 2022 (500GW non-fossil 2030, 45% intensity cut, net-zero 2070), LiFE mission
  • ISA (2015, HQ Gurugram), CDRI (2019, Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure), Global Biofuel Alliance 2023.

4. Newer clubs

  • G20: 19+EU+AU (2023)
  • India presidency 2023 (New Delhi Declaration, African Union admission, Global Biofuels Alliance, IMEC announced)
  • troika = past-current-next. BRICS: term coined 2001 (O'Neill's 'BRIC')
  • 2024 expansion (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, Saudi?, UAE; partner-country tier 2024). SCO (2001; India full member 2017), RIC, IBSA, I2U2 (India-Israel-UAE-US 2022), IMEC (India-Middle East-Europe corridor, G20 2023), Quad (revived 2017; leaders' level 2021; Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative synergy)
  • SAARC (1985, stalled), BIMSTEC (1997, Bay of Bengal, secretariat Dhaka).

⚡ MCQ hooks

  • UNSC: 5 permanent (veto) + 10 non-perm; India 8 terms; G4 members list.
  • WTO MC12 2022 = fisheries; MC13 = 2024 Abu Dhabi.
  • ISA HQ Gurugram; CDRI India-led 2019; net-zero 2070; 500GW/45% by 2030 Panchamrit.
  • G20: African Union joined at India's presidency (New Delhi 2023); troika concept.
  • BRICS 2024: new full members (Egypt-Ethiopia-Iran-UAE) + partner tier.
  • SCO full membership 2017; BIMSTEC secretariat Dhaka.
  • ICJ: Dalveer Bhandari beat UK candidate 2017.

5. The United Nations System and the Reform Debate

The United Nations, established by the Charter signed at San Francisco in 1945, rests on six principal organs. The General Assembly, in which all 193 members have one vote each, is deliberative and its resolutions are recommendatory rather than binding — except on budgetary and internal matters. The Security Council has fifteen members: five permanent with the veto (China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States) and ten elected for two-year terms on an equitable geographical basis; its resolutions under Chapter VII are binding and may authorise sanctions or the use of force, while Chapter VI concerns pacific settlement. The Economic and Social Council coordinates the specialised agencies; the International Court of Justice at The Hague settles disputes between states, its jurisdiction resting on consent — distinguish it firmly from the International Criminal Court, which is a separate treaty body under the Rome Statute prosecuting individuals for genocide, war crimes, crimes against humanity and aggression, and to which India, the US, China and Russia are not parties. The Secretariat is headed by the Secretary-General, appointed by the General Assembly on the Security Council's recommendation. The Trusteeship Council is suspended.

India's case for UNSC reform is a standing mains topic. The argument: the Council's composition reflects the power distribution of 1945 and excludes Africa and Latin America entirely from permanent membership; India's population, economy, sustained troop contributions to peacekeeping — among the largest cumulative contributors — democratic credentials and record of non-aggression justify a permanent seat. India works through the G4 with Brazil, Germany and Japan, each supporting the others' candidature. The obstacles: the Uniting for Consensus group led by Italy, Pakistan, Argentina, Mexico and South Korea, which opposes new permanent seats and favours more elected members; the African Union's Ezulwini Consensus, demanding two permanent seats with veto for Africa; disagreement among aspirants over whether new permanent members should have the veto; and, decisively, the amendment procedure, which requires a two-thirds vote of the General Assembly and ratification by all five permanent members — giving each an effective veto over reform itself. The Intergovernmental Negotiations process has run for over a decade without a text-based outcome.

Specialised agencies and programmes to place accurately with their headquarters and mandate: WHO (Geneva), ILO (Geneva, uniquely tripartite with governments, employers and workers), FAO (Rome), UNESCO (Paris), IMO (London), ICAO (Montreal), WIPO and WMO (Geneva), IAEA (Vienna, which reports to the General Assembly and Security Council and is not technically a specialised agency), UNICEF, UNDP, UNEP (Nairobi, the only major UN body headquartered in the developing world), UNHCR, UNCTAD and UN Women.

6. Plurilateral Groupings and India's Multi-Alignment

India's contemporary diplomacy is best characterised not as non-alignment but as multi-alignment or strategic autonomy — participating simultaneously in groupings with divergent and sometimes competing memberships, and refusing to be locked into a single bloc. The clearest illustration is India's simultaneous membership of the Quad with the United States, Japan and Australia, and of the SCO and BRICS with China and Russia.

7. Global Governance: Challenges and India's Position

  • The crisis of multilateralism is the organising theme of contemporary answers. Symptoms: the paralysis of the WTO's Appellate Body through blocked appointments, leaving disputes unresolvable on appeal
  • Security Council deadlock over Ukraine and West Asia owing to the veto
  • the retreat into plurilateral and minilateral arrangements as states bypass blocked universal forums
  • the weaponisation of interdependence through sanctions, export controls and supply-chain restrictions
  • and contested norms around data, technology and the use of force. India's diagnosis, articulated repeatedly, is that the institutions of global governance suffer a legitimacy deficit because they underrepresent the developing world, and that "reformed multilateralism" is the necessary response.

India's positions across the principal issues, useful as ready material:

  • Climate. Insistence on common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities, historical responsibility and equity
  • emphasis on climate finance and technology transfer
  • the LiFE — Lifestyle for Environment — initiative reframing demand-side behaviour
  • net zero by 2070 and the panchamrit commitments
  • opposition to unilateral measures such as the EU's carbon border adjustment mechanism as green protectionism.
  • Trade. Defence of the peace clause for public stockholding for food security and a demand for a permanent solution; restoration of the dispute-settlement system; special and differential treatment for developing countries; resistance to the premature introduction of the "new issues" of investment facilitation, e-commerce and labour and environment standards.
  • Terrorism. The long-standing push for a Comprehensive Convention on International Terrorism, stalled since 1996 over the definition of terrorism and the treatment of national liberation movements and state forces; the critique of politicisation in the UNSC's 1267 sanctions committee listings.
  • Global South leadership. The Voice of the Global South Summit, vaccine diplomacy through Vaccine Maitri, development partnership through lines of credit and capacity building under ITEC, and disaster and humanitarian assistance as a first responder in the region.
  • Technology and data. Advocacy of digital public infrastructure as a global public good, data sovereignty, and equitable AI governance.

A balanced answer should also register the constraints on India's multilateral ambition: limited financial contributions relative to China's, the difficulty of sustaining positions equidistant from Washington and Moscow, unresolved regional disputes that complicate SAARC and neighbourhood policy, and the tension between demanding a rules-based order and preserving policy space at home. The reconciling formulation India itself uses — Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam, "the world is one family," adopted as the theme of its G20 presidency — is the customary closing note.

Trap watch. General Assembly resolutions are recommendatory; Security Council decisions under Chapter VII are binding. The ICJ settles disputes between states; the ICC prosecutes individuals and India is not a party. UNSC reform requires ratification by all five permanent members, which is why it has stalled. India is in the Quad and simultaneously in the SCO and BRICS — this is multi-alignment, not non-alignment. BIMSTEC excludes Pakistan and the Maldives; SAARC includes both. UNEP is headquartered in Nairobi. The African Union joined the G20 in 2023. India has not joined the IPEF trade pillar.
