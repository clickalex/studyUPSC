Flagship Schemes and Policies — Detailed Notes

Scheme → ministry → launch year → headline number. High-frequency prelims zone; verify current figures each cycle.

1. Poverty & rural

  • MGNREGA 2005 (100 days/household; wage-material 60:40; unemployment allowance; social audit (Sec 17); demand-driven (DBT wages via NREGASoft)). PMAY-G/U (rural pucca homes; urban CLSS component), DAY-NRLM (women SHGs — Ajeevika lineage; Lakhpati Didi target 3cr), PM-KISAN 2019 (₹6,000/yr, landholding farmer families, exclusion caps), PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (NFSA top-up; made free 2023), Ayushman Bharat: PM-JAY (₹5 lakh/family secondary-tertiary) + HWC/AB-HWC now Ayushman Arogya Mandirs.

2. Financial inclusion & pension

  • PMJDY 2014 (zero-balance, RuPay, overdraft ₹10k), PMJJBY (life ₹2L) + PMSBY (accident ₹2L) + APY (pension) trio 2015, MUDRA (Shishu ≤50k, Kishore 50k-5L, Tarun 5-10L; now Tarun-Plus 20L), PM SVANidhi (street vendors); Stand-Up India (SC-ST-women entrepreneurs ₹10L-1cr).

3. Health-education-skill

  • PM Matru Vandana (₹5,000 in instalments; 2017), POSHAN Abhiyaan 2018 (now POSHAN 2.0 — Mission Saksham Anganwadi), PM POSHAN (mid-day meal rename 2021). Education: Samagra Shiksha (2018 school integration), NEP 2020 (5+3+3+4, FLN NIPUN), PM SHRI (14,500 exemplar schools), PM Vidyalaxmi (education loans). Skill: PMKVY 4.0, DGT apprenticeships (NAPS).

4. Water-energy-urban

  • Jal Jeevan Mission 2019 (har ghar jal tap target; under Jal Shakti), PM Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (per-drop-more-crop micro-irrigation), eNAM (mandi e-platform), FPO scheme (10,000 FPOs), Ujjwala (LPG to poor women; 8cr+ connections), PM Surya Ghar (rooftop solar 1cr homes 2024), Saubhagya (electrification 2017), AMRUT 2.0 + Smart Cities Mission (100 cities, 2023-24 sunset), SBM 2.0 (ODF-plus-plus), PM-eBus Sewa.
  • Newer 2023-24 banners: PM Vishwakarma (artisans, 18 trades), PM-JANMAN (PVTGs, 11 interventions), PM PVTG mission, Mission Karmayogi (civil-service capacity), Aspirational Districts (112, NITI), Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM health IDs).

6. How to Organise Schemes for the Exam

Rote-listing schemes fails; examiners test structural facts. For any scheme, fix four things: the nodal ministry, the funding pattern, whether beneficiaries are targeted or universal, and the legal basis (statutory right versus executive scheme).

The funding classification is itself a favourite question. Central Sector Schemes are 100% funded by the Centre and implemented by central machinery on Union List subjects (PM-KISAN, MPLADS, Namami Gange). Centrally Sponsored Schemes are cost-shared with states on State or Concurrent List subjects, typically 60:40 (90:10 for the eight north-eastern and three Himalayan states, 100% for UTs without a legislature). CSS are further grouped into Core of the Core (the six most protected, including MGNREGA and the umbrella programme for vulnerable groups), Core, and Optional. The constitutional basis for the Centre funding State-subject schemes is Article 282, a discretionary grant power — and the resulting encroachment on state autonomy is a standing federalism critique.

Distinguish also schemes that confer a justiciable right — the NREGA guarantee of 100 days, the RTE entitlement under Article 21A, the NFSA legal entitlement to grain — from schemes that are mere executive promises revocable at will. This distinction is the sharpest analytical point available in a rights-based-governance answer.

7. Livelihood, Employment and Rural Development

  • MGNREGA (2005) — the flagship rights-based programme. Guarantees 100 days of unskilled manual wage employment per rural household per financial year, on demand. Key design features: work must be provided within 15 days or an unemployment allowance becomes payable by the state; work should be within 5 km or a 10% travel allowance is due; at least one-third of beneficiaries must be women; wages must be paid within fifteen days; gram sabha recommends works and conducts the social audit; and contractors and labour-displacing machinery are prohibited. It functions as a self-targeting safety net and a wage floor. Criticisms: delayed wage payment, inadequate central allocation forcing rationing of work, weak asset quality, and Aadhaar-based payment exclusions.
  • DAY-NRLM (rural livelihoods through Self-Help Group federations and bank linkage) and DAY-NULM for urban livelihoods.
  • PMAY-Gramin and PMAY-Urban for housing; PMGSY for all-weather rural roads; Jal Jeevan Mission for functional household tap connections; Swachh Bharat Mission for sanitation and ODF status; Saubhagya and DDUGJY for household electrification.
  • Skill development — PMKVY under the Skill India Mission, the National Skill Development Corporation, ITIs, and the apprenticeship scheme. The persistent problem is low placement conversion and a mismatch with employer demand.

8. Food, Nutrition, Health and Education

National Food Security Act, 2013 — converts food access into a legal entitlement covering up to 75% of the rural and 50% of the urban population, comprising Priority Households (5 kg per person per month) and Antyodaya Anna Yojana households (35 kg per household). It also mandates maternity benefit of ₹6,000 and free meals for children and pregnant and lactating women, and designates the eldest woman of the household aged 18 or above as the head for the ration card — a deliberate gender-empowering provision. One Nation One Ration Card makes entitlements portable for migrants through the IM-PDS platform.

POSHAN Abhiyaan targets stunting, wasting, anaemia and low birth weight, delivered largely through ICDS anganwadi centres; PM POSHAN is the renamed mid-day meal scheme, which improves enrolment, attendance, retention and nutrition simultaneously. The relevant indicators come from the National Family Health Survey: stunting is low height-for-age (chronic), wasting is low weight-for-height (acute) and underweight is low weight-for-age — a distinction routinely tested.

Ayushman Bharat has two components that must not be conflated: Health and Wellness Centres (now Ayushman Arogya Mandirs) delivering comprehensive primary care, and PM-JAY, a publicly funded health assurance scheme providing ₹5 lakh per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation, targeted using SECC deprivation criteria, cashless and portable across empanelled hospitals. It covers hospitalisation, not outpatient care — which is where most catastrophic out-of-pocket expenditure actually arises, the standard critique. Add the National Health Mission (NRHM and NUHM) with ASHA workers, Janani Suraksha Yojana and Mission Indradhanush.

Education — the Right to Education Act, 2009 operationalises Article 21A for ages 6–14, mandating 25% reservation in private unaided schools for disadvantaged groups, prohibiting capitation fees, screening, corporal punishment and the detention of children up to Class VIII (since relaxed), and prescribing pupil-teacher and infrastructure norms. Samagra Shiksha is the umbrella scheme. The National Education Policy 2020 introduces the 5+3+3+4 curricular structure, foundational literacy and numeracy through NIPUN Bharat, mother-tongue instruction in early years, a four-year multidisciplinary undergraduate degree with multiple entry and exit, the Academic Bank of Credits, and a target of 6% of GDP for education.

9. Financial Inclusion, Women, Children and Vulnerable Groups

PM Jan Dhan Yojana — zero-balance basic savings accounts with RuPay card, accident and life cover, and overdraft facility; the foundation of the JAM architecture and of DBT. Complementary micro-insurance and pension schemes: PM Jeevan Jyoti Bima (life), PM Suraksha Bima (accident), Atal Pension Yojana, and PM Shram Yogi Maandhan for unorganised workers. MUDRA loans are categorised Shishu, Kishore, Tarun by size; Stand Up India targets SC/ST and women entrepreneurs.

Women and children: Beti Bachao Beti Padhao addressing the declining child sex ratio, Sukanya Samriddhi, PM Matru Vandana Yojana (conditional maternity benefit), Mission Shakti with its Sambal and Samarthya sub-schemes, One Stop Centres and the women's helpline, Mission Vatsalya for child protection, and the POCSO Act and Juvenile Justice Act as the statutory frame. Legal landmarks worth citing: the Maternity Benefit (Amendment) Act 2017 extending paid leave to 26 weeks, the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace Act 2013 flowing from the Vishaka guidelines, and the prohibition of triple talaq.

Social justice: post-matric and pre-matric scholarships, the PM-AJAY and PM-JANMAN (for Particularly Vulnerable Tribal Groups) programmes, Eklavya Model Residential Schools, the Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers Act 2013 with the NAMASTE scheme for sanitation workers, the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016 (which expanded recognised disabilities to 21 and raised reservation in government jobs to 4%) with the Accessible India Campaign, Atal Vayo Abhyuday Yojana for the elderly, and the Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 with the SMILE scheme.

Economy and industry: Make in India, Atmanirbhar Bharat, Production Linked Incentive schemes across fourteen sectors, Startup India, PM Gati Shakti (the National Master Plan for multimodal infrastructure integrating ministries on a GIS platform) and the National Logistics Policy, Smart Cities Mission and AMRUT for urban infrastructure, and PM Vishwakarma for traditional artisans.

Trap watch. MGNREGA guarantees 100 days per household, not per person, and the unemployment allowance is a state liability. PM-KISAN is a Central Sector Scheme; MGNREGA is a Centrally Sponsored Scheme. PM-JAY covers hospitalisation only, not outpatient care, and is an assurance rather than an insurance model in most states. Under the NFSA the eldest woman aged 18+ is deemed head of household. The RTE covers ages 6–14, so pre-primary and secondary lie outside it. Stunting is height-for-age; wasting is weight-for-height. Ayushman Bharat's two pillars are HWCs and PM-JAY — not two insurance products.
