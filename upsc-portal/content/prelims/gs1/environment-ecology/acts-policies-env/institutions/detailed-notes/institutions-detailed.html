Environmental Institutions — Detailed Notes

Ministry-regulator-tribunal-mission bodies and their statutory anchors. Body-role-year recall.

1. Apex & regulators

  • MoEFCC (policy; carries UNFCCC-CBD portfolios; PS division); CPCB (Water Act 1974 statutory; functions widened by Air 1981) + SPCBs/PCCs — standards-monitoring-consent mechanism; CAQM (2021, commission for NCR-airshed; supersedes EPCA; statutory under EP Act 1986 frame).
  • NGT (2010 act; scheduled: principal bench New Delhi + zonal benches; judges + experts; appointment and term per the act; jurisdiction: substantial environment questions; appeals to Supreme Court; not strictly bound by CPC — enquiry-bound by natural justice principles).

2. Statutory & scientific bodies

  • NTCA (2006, Wildlife Act chapter IV-C; tiger conservation plans, core-buffer grammar); NBA (2003, Chennai; ABS approvals); National Ganga Council / NMCG (2016 River Ganga Act-frame: NGC chaired by PM; NMCG authority); Central Zoo Authority (1992); Wildlife Institute of India (Dehradun), FSI (Dehradun; ISFR biennial — forest-cover bands), ICFRE, GBPIHED (Himalayan institute); Botanical-Zoological surveys (BSI-ZSI).
  • Advisory-participatory: National Board for Wildlife (NBWL, PM chairs), National Green Corps (eco-clubs), state boards; EAC-SEIAA appraisal committees (EIA chain).

3. Mission vehicles

  • NAPCC 2008: 8 missions (solar, efficiency, habitat, water, Himalayan, GIM, agri, strategic knowledge) + later health mission; Green India Mission (GIM 2014); Namami Gange (2014 flagship, NMCG exec); Mission LiFE (2022); SECURE Himalaya; CAMPA funds (compensatory afforestation; NPV-CAA 2016 use rules).
  • Finance-compliance: environment cess lineage (clean energy cess → GST-compensation), green bonds, ESG-disclosure (BRSR-core SEBI), NCAP-X apportionment studies.

MCQ hooks

  • CPCB = Water Act 1974 (statutory birth), functions widened by Air 1981.
  • CAQM 2021 (NCR airshed; replaced EPCA).
  • NGT 2010: principal ND + zonal benches; SC appeal route.
  • NTCA 2006 statutory (Project Tiger 1973 = scheme) — scheme-vs-body pair.
  • NBWL chaired by PM; NGC chaired by PM — chairperson asks.
  • NAPCC 8 missions; GIM 2014; Namami Gange 2014.
  • BSI = botanical survey (Kolkata-Howrah), ZSI = zoological survey (Kolkata).

6. The Domestic Environmental Institutional Map

Environmental governance in India is layered, and questions test which body sits where. The apex policy body is the Ministry of Environment, Forest and Climate Change (MoEFCC), the nodal agency for planning, promotion and coordination, and the implementing authority for the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. Under and alongside it sit regulatory, advisory and adjudicatory bodies.

7. Climate Governance Architecture

The Prime Minister's Council on Climate Change oversees the National Action Plan on Climate Change (2008) and its eight missions, which must be reproducible: National Solar Mission; National Mission for Enhanced Energy Efficiency; National Mission on Sustainable Habitat; National Water Mission; National Mission for Sustaining the Himalayan Ecosystem; National Mission for a Green India; National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture; and National Mission on Strategic Knowledge for Climate Change. Later additions include missions on health, coastal areas and human capital. Each state prepares a State Action Plan on Climate Change.

Implementing and financing bodies: the Bureau of Energy Efficiency, which runs the Perform, Achieve and Trade scheme, the star labelling programme and the Energy Conservation Building Code; the Ministry of New and Renewable Energy; the Indian Renewable Energy Development Agency; the National Adaptation Fund for Climate Change; and the National Clean Energy Fund. India's Nationally Determined Contributions under the Paris Agreement — emissions-intensity reduction, non-fossil installed capacity share, and the carbon-sink target, with net zero by 2070 — should be cited alongside the Panchamrit commitments and Mission LiFE. The Carbon Credit Trading Scheme under the amended Energy Conservation Act establishes a domestic compliance carbon market.

8. International Environmental Institutions

  • UNEP — headquartered at Nairobi, the only UN body headquartered in Africa; the principal environmental authority of the UN system, whose governing body is the UN Environment Assembly. Publishes the Emissions Gap Report, Adaptation Gap Report and Global Environment Outlook.
  • IPCC — established by UNEP and WMO in 1988. It does not conduct original research; it assesses published literature. Three working groups cover the physical science basis, impacts and adaptation, and mitigation. Its Assessment Reports and Special Reports (notably on 1.5 °C) are the scientific reference for negotiations.
  • IUCN — a hybrid membership union of states, agencies and NGOs, based at Gland, Switzerland; maintains the Red List and the Protected Area management categories. It is not a UN body and its Red List has no legal force.
  • Global Environment Facility (GEF) — the financial mechanism for several conventions (CBD, UNFCCC, UNCCD, Stockholm, Minamata), with the World Bank as trustee. The Green Climate Fund (Songdo, South Korea) is the UNFCCC's dedicated climate finance vehicle, alongside the Adaptation Fund and the new Loss and Damage Fund agreed at COP27 and operationalised at COP28.
  • WMO, FAO (which produces the Global Forest Resources Assessment and the State of the World's Forests), WWF (a non-governmental body producing the Living Planet Report and its Living Planet Index), TRAFFIC (a joint IUCN-WWF wildlife trade monitoring network), and the World Resources Institute.
  • Convention secretariats: UNFCCC at Bonn, CBD at Montreal, CITES and Ramsar at Gland, UNCCD at Bonn, and the Basel, Rotterdam and Stockholm conventions sharing a Geneva secretariat.

India-led institutions deserve emphasis: the International Solar Alliance, headquartered at Gurugram and co-founded with France, is the first international intergovernmental organisation headquartered in India; the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure is based in New Delhi; and the International Big Cat Alliance and Global Biofuel Alliance are recent additions.

9. Assessing Institutional Performance

A strong mains answer must go beyond enumeration to institutional critique. Recurring weaknesses of India's environmental bodies: capacity deficits — pollution control boards operate with a fraction of sanctioned technical staff and outdated laboratories; regulatory capture and the placement of both promotional and regulatory functions in the same ministry; fragmentation, where forests, water, land and pollution are governed by different ministries and tiers with weak coordination, when ecosystems do not respect these boundaries; weak deterrence, since penalties have historically been low and criminal prosecution slow, and the 2023 Jan Vishwas amendments decriminalised several environmental offences in favour of monetary penalties — a change defended as pragmatic and criticised as dilution; data gaps in monitoring coverage; and the tension between the NGT's activism and executive resistance, alongside concerns about tribunal vacancies.

The constructive prescriptions to offer: an independent, professionally staffed environmental regulator on the lines proposed by the Supreme Court in Lafarge and by the T.S.R. Subramanian Committee; separating environmental appraisal from project promotion; strengthening cumulative and strategic environmental assessment rather than project-by-project clearance; deploying remote sensing and continuous emission monitoring for objective compliance data; genuine decentralisation through BMCs and gram sabhas backed by the FRA and PESA; and treating environmental accounting and natural-capital valuation as inputs into fiscal decisions.

Trap watch. CPCB was created by the Water Act, 1974, though it also administers the Air Act. GEAC is under MoEFCC, not DBT. The NGT's schedule excludes the Wildlife Protection Act and the Forest Rights Act. The National Board for Wildlife and the National Ganga Council are both chaired by the Prime Minister. IPCC assesses existing literature and does not conduct research; it was set up by UNEP and WMO, not by the UNFCCC. IUCN is a hybrid union, not a UN agency. UNEP is at Nairobi, the Green Climate Fund at Songdo, and the ISA at Gurugram.
