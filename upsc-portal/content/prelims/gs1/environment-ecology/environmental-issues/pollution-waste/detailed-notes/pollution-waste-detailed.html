Pollution and Waste Management — Detailed Notes

Air-water-soil-noise pollution frames + India's waste-rule stack. Rule-year-EPR recall.

1. Air pollution

  • AQI (CPCB 2014-15): 6 bands good-severe on 8 pollutants; PM2.5-PM10-O3-NO2-SO2-CO-NH3-Pb; health: PM2.5 deep-lung penetration; NCAP (2019; 2022-26 target ~40% PM reduction in 131 non-attainment cities; PRANA portal, city action plans); CAQM (2021 statutory, NCR-adjoining airshed; Graded Response Action Plan stages I-IV); sources-stubs: Delhi winter inversion, farm stubble (bio-decomposer push).
  • Tools: SAFAR forecasts, continuous monitoring CAAQMS, satellite assimilation; indoor air + ASHRAE-type guidance skip; odd-even, anti-smog guns, cloud-seeding pilots (2025) = response arsenal.

2. Water, soil, noise

  • Water-quality trio: DO-BOD-COD + faecal coliform; CPCB priority polluted river stretches (351 band 2022 list); eutrophication-algal blooms-toxins (HABs); heavy metals (arsenic-fluoride groundwater belts).
  • Soil: salinisation, nutrient depletion, pesticide residue (ban list moving); noise: Noise Rules 2000 (day-night dB zones: industrial-commercial-residential-silence 100 m).
  • Light pollution emerging ask; thermal pollution (power plants); radiation (BARC-AERB oversight).

3. Waste rules stack (2016-22 generation)

  • SWM Rules 2016 (source segregation, doorstep collection, landfill last resort, bulk-generator norms); Plastic Waste Management (Amendment) Rules 2021-22: 19 single-use plastic items banned from 1 Jul 2022 + EPR for plastic packaging (4 categories); E-Waste (Management) Rules 2022 (EPR certificates; expanded list; recycling targets); BMW Rules 2016 (CBWTFs); Hazardous 2016; C&D 2016; Battery Waste Management Rules 2022 (EPR); plastic-supply: PCB-PET recycling frames.
  • Remediation: bioremediation (oil-eating bacteria, microbial consortia), phytoremediation (hyperaccumulators), WtE plants (Jajmau-Okhla debates), remediation of legacy dumps (Ghazipur-type), circular-economy missions (11 sectoral CE baskets 2024-25 news).

MCQ hooks

  • AQI = 6 bands, 8 pollutants (CPCB).
  • NCAP 2019; CAQM 2021 NCR; GRAP stages I-IV — plan-body pair.
  • DO-BOD inverse relation; coliform = faecal contamination marker.
  • SUP ban date = 1 Jul 2022 (19 items).
  • E-Waste Rules 2022 = EPR-based; BMW 2016 = CBWTF route.
  • Noise: silence zone 100 m around hospitals-courts; night dB lower.
  • Bioremediation vs phytoremediation — microbe vs plant pair.

6. Air Pollution — Pollutants, Standards and Sources

Distinguish primary pollutants, emitted directly (particulate matter, sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, volatile organic compounds, lead), from secondary pollutants, formed in the atmosphere by reaction — the key example being ground-level ozone and peroxyacetyl nitrate, produced when NOₓ and VOCs react in sunlight to create photochemical smog. This is chemically distinct from classical or London smog, which is sulphurous, reducing, and forms in cold humid conditions.

Particulate matter is the most examined pollutant. PM10 (coarse, ≤10 μm) lodges in the upper airways; PM2.5 (fine, ≤2.5 μm) penetrates the alveoli and enters the bloodstream, and is therefore the principal driver of mortality. Ultrafine PM1 is an emerging concern. India's National Ambient Air Quality Standards cover twelve pollutants, and the National Air Quality Index aggregates eight of them into six categories from Good to Severe, the AQI being determined by the worst-performing pollutant.

Specific effects to know: carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin with roughly 200 times the affinity of oxygen, forming carboxyhaemoglobin; SO₂ and NOₓ produce acid rain (sulphuric and nitric acid), damaging the Taj Mahal through "marble cancer" and acidifying lakes and soils; lead impairs neurological development in children; benzene is carcinogenic; and black carbon both warms the atmosphere and accelerates glacier melt by darkening snow.

Two meteorological concepts explain why pollution spikes. Temperature inversion occurs when a warm layer overlies cooler surface air, inverting the normal lapse rate and capping vertical mixing, so pollutants accumulate — the principal reason for Delhi's winter episodes. The mixing height and ventilation coefficient fall in winter, compounding the effect of stubble burning, vehicular load, construction dust and firecrackers.

Indoor air pollution from biomass and kerosene combustion is a major and under-appreciated cause of respiratory disease among women and children, which is the public-health rationale for the Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana. Policy responses to name: the National Clean Air Programme with its city-wise particulate reduction targets, BS-VI emission norms (leapfrogged from BS-IV), the Commission for Air Quality Management in the NCR as a statutory body replacing EPCA, the Graded Response Action Plan, FAME for electric mobility, and the ethanol blending programme.

7. Water and Soil Pollution

Indicators. BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) measures the oxygen consumed by microorganisms decomposing organic matter — high BOD means heavy organic pollution and falling dissolved oxygen. COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand) measures oxygen needed to oxidise all matter, organic and inorganic, and is therefore always higher than BOD. Dissolved oxygen falls as temperature and organic load rise; coliform count indicates faecal contamination.

Eutrophication follows nutrient enrichment from fertiliser runoff and phosphate detergents: algal bloom → light blocked → algae die → decomposers deplete oxygen → hypoxic dead zone and fish kill. Harmful algal blooms include red tides. Biomagnification is the increase in concentration of a persistent, fat-soluble, non-degradable substance at successive trophic levels — DDT and mercury are the standard examples, with top predators worst affected; bioaccumulation is the build-up within a single organism over time. Distinguish the two carefully.

Named pollution episodes and problems: Minamata disease (methylmercury, Japan), Itai-itai (cadmium), blue baby syndrome or methaemoglobinaemia (nitrate in drinking water), fluorosis (skeletal and dental, endemic in parts of Rajasthan, Andhra and Telangana), and arsenic contamination of groundwater in the Ganga-Brahmaputra plain, especially West Bengal and Bihar — a natural geogenic problem aggravated by over-extraction.

Thermal pollution from power-plant cooling water reduces dissolved oxygen and disrupts aquatic breeding. Oil spills are treated with booms, skimmers, dispersants and bioremediation, including the "superbug" Pseudomonas putida engineered by Ananda Chakrabarty. Microplastics — fragments under 5 mm, including primary microbeads and secondary fragmentation products — now pervade the food chain.

Soil pollution arises from excess fertiliser and pesticide use, industrial effluent, heavy metals and improper waste disposal, producing salinisation, alkalinisation and loss of soil organic carbon. Remediation techniques worth naming: phytoremediation (plants extracting contaminants), bioremediation, and capping.

8. Solid, Plastic, E-Waste and Hazardous Waste

The governing hierarchy is Reduce → Reuse → Recycle → Recover → Dispose, with prevention preferred to end-of-pipe treatment. The circular economy reframes waste as a resource, contrasting with the linear take-make-dispose model.

  • Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 — mandate source segregation into wet (biodegradable), dry (recyclable) and domestic hazardous waste; impose user fees and spot fines; make bulk generators responsible for on-site composting. Treatment routes: composting and vermicomposting for wet waste; anaerobic digestion (biomethanation) producing biogas; waste-to-energy incineration, which is contested in India because high moisture and low calorific value make it inefficient and it can emit dioxins and furans; refuse-derived fuel for cement kilns; and sanitary landfill with liners and leachate collection, as the last resort. Legacy dumpsites require bioremediation and biomining.
  • Plastic Waste Management Rules — Extended Producer Responsibility makes producers, importers and brand owners responsible for end-of-life collection, operationalised through EPR certificates. India banned identified single-use plastic items and raised the minimum carry-bag thickness. Note the distinction between biodegradable and merely oxo-degradable plastics, the latter fragmenting into microplastics and now prohibited.
  • E-Waste (Management) Rules — cover a schedule of electrical and electronic equipment, impose EPR with collection targets, and restrict hazardous substances (RoHS). The hazard lies in lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium and brominated flame retardants; the informal sector's practices of open burning and acid leaching to recover gold and copper cause severe exposure. India is among the largest e-waste generators, and urban mining is the constructive framing.
  • Bio-Medical Waste Rules — colour-coded segregation: yellow (anatomical, soiled, chemical — incineration), red (contaminated recyclable plastics — autoclave then recycle), white (sharps, in puncture-proof containers), blue (glassware and metallic implants). Common Bio-medical Waste Treatment Facilities handle disposal.
  • Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules, Construction and Demolition Waste Rules, and Battery Waste Management Rules complete the framework.

9. Institutions, Instruments and Critique

Enforcement rests with the Central Pollution Control Board (under the Water Act 1974, functioning also under the Air Act and as the technical arm of MoEFCC) and the State Pollution Control Boards, which issue consent to establish and consent to operate. Industries are classified Red, Orange, Green and White by pollution potential, White being essentially non-polluting and exempt from consent. The National Green Tribunal adjudicates, applying the polluter pays and precautionary principles and awarding environmental compensation.

Economic instruments to cite: Pigouvian taxes internalising the negative externality, cap-and-trade (India has piloted an emissions trading scheme for particulate matter in Surat, the first of its kind), tradable permits, deposit-refund schemes, and the Perform, Achieve and Trade mechanism for energy efficiency.

The standard critique for mains: chronically understaffed and underfunded pollution control boards; monitoring stations too few and unevenly distributed; penalties historically too low to deter; the informal recycling sector excluded from formal systems despite doing most of the actual recovery, so any reform must provide for just transition and the dignity and safety of safai karamcharis and waste-pickers; and the airshed problem, where pollution crosses municipal and state boundaries so that city-level action alone cannot succeed — the argument for airshed-level governance such as the CAQM.

Trap watch. Photochemical smog is oxidising and forms in warm sunlight; London smog is reducing and forms in cold damp conditions. Ground-level ozone is a secondary pollutant and harmful, while stratospheric ozone is protective. COD is always greater than BOD. Biomagnification acts across trophic levels; bioaccumulation within one organism. Oxo-degradable plastic is not biodegradable. Yellow bio-medical waste is incinerated, red is autoclaved and recycled. The AQI reflects the worst pollutant, not an average. White-category industries need no consent.
