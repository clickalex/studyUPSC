Scriptures, Languages & Literature — Prelims Fact Card

Prelims GS-I › History & Culture › Art & Culture. Text–author–language matching and the Eighth Schedule/classical language list.

Religious & philosophical texts

  • Vedic corpus (see Vedic card): Samhitas → Brahmanas → Aranyakas → Upanishads; Vedangas; Sutras (Shrauta, Grihya, Dharma — Gautama, Baudhayana, Apastamba, Vasishtha); Smritis — Manusmriti (c. 200 BCE–200 CE), Yajnavalkya (Mitakshara by Vijnaneshvara; Dayabhaga — Jimutavahana, Bengal), Narada, Parashara.
  • Epics: Mahabharata (Vyasa; 18 parvas; Jaya 8,800 → Bharata 24,000 → 1,00,000 shatasahasri samhita; Bhagavad Gita in Bhishma parva, 18 chapters, 700 shlokas), Ramayana (Valmiki; 7 kandas; 24,000 verses; adikavya).
  • Puranas (18 Maha; pancha-lakshana — sarga, pratisarga, vamsha, manvantara, vamshanucharita; Vishnu, Bhagavata, Matsya (oldest), Vayu, Markandeya (Devi Mahatmya), Agni, Garuda, Skanda (largest)).
  • Buddhist: Pali Tripitaka; Jatakas (547), Dhammapada, Milindapanho, Mahavamsa/Dipavamsa (Sri Lanka), Sanskrit — Lalitavistara, Buddhacharita, Saundarananda (Ashvaghosha), Mahavastu, Divyavadana, Nagarjuna Mulamadhyamakakarika, Vasubandhu Abhidharmakosha, Shantideva Bodhicharyavatara, Dignaga/Dharmakirti (logic).
  • Jain: 12 Angas, 12 Upangas, Prakirnas, Chhedasutras, Mulasutras (Ardha-Magadhi); Kalpasutra (Bhadrabahu), Tattvartha Sutra (Umaswati — both sects), Acharanga, Bhagavati Sutra; Kannada — Adipurana (Jinasena/Pampa), Vikramarjuna Vijaya.
  • Sikh: Guru Granth Sahib (Adi Granth 1604 Guru Arjan; Bhai Gurdas scribe; 1430 angs; 31 ragas; Bhagat bani — Kabir, Namdev, Ravidas, Farid; Guru Gobind Singh added Tegh Bahadur's hymns 1705 Damdama; Guru 1708), Dasam Granth, Janamsakhis.
  • Bhakti/Sufi: Alvars (12; Nalayira Divya Prabandham — Nathamuni; Andal Tiruppavai), Nayanars (63; Tevaram; Periya Puranam — Sekkizhar; Tirumurai 12; Manikkavachakar Tiruvachakam), Shankara (Advaita; Brahmasutra bhashya; 4 mathas), Ramanuja (Vishishtadvaita; Sri Bhashya), Madhva (Dvaita), Nimbarka (Dvaitadvaita), Vallabha (Shuddhadvaita; Pushtimarg), Basavanna (Vachanas; Lingayat/Virashaiva; Anubhava Mantapa; Akka Mahadevi), Jnaneshwar (Jnaneshwari 1290; Marathi), Namdev, Eknath, Tukaram (abhang), Ramdas (Dasbodh), Kabir (Bijak; doha), Ravidas, Nanak, Chaitanya (Gaudiya; sankirtana), Shankaradeva (Borgeet, Kirtan Ghosha; Ekasarana), Mirabai, Surdas (Sursagar), Tulsidas (Ramcharitmanas 1574, Awadhi; Hanuman Chalisa), Narsi Mehta (Vaishnava jana to), Dadu, Malik Muhammad Jayasi (Padmavat 1540, Awadhi), Amir Khusrau, Bulleh Shah, Sultan Bahu, Shah Latif (Risalo), Lal Ded (Kashmir vakhs), Nund Rishi (Sheikh Noor-ud-din).

Secular classics

  • Grammar/lexicon: Panini Ashtadhyayi (c. 4th BCE; 4,000 sutras), Katyayana Varttika, Patanjali Mahabhashya, Amarasimha Amarakosha, Tolkappiyam (Tamil grammar; earliest).
  • Polity/economy: **Kautilya *Arthashastra*** (15 adhikaranas; rediscovered Shamasastry 1905, published 1909), Kamandaka Nitisara, Shukra Nitisara, Somadeva Nitivakyamrita.
  • Science: Aryabhata (Aryabhatiya 499), Varahamihira (Brihat Samhita, Pancha-siddhantika, Brihat Jataka), Brahmagupta (Brahmasphutasiddhanta 628; zero rules), Bhaskara I, Bhaskara II (Siddhanta Shiromani 1150 — Lilavati, Bijaganita), Madhava of Sangamagrama (Kerala school; series), Nilakantha (Tantrasangraha), Charaka & Sushruta Samhitas, Vagbhata (Ashtanga Hridaya), Nagarjuna (Rasaratnakara), Kanada (atomism), Lagadha (Vedanga Jyotisha), Sulba sutras (Baudhayana — "Pythagoras").
  • Drama/kavya: Bhasa, Kalidasa, Shudraka, Vishakhadatta, Bhavabhuti, Harsha (Nagananda, Ratnavali, Priyadarshika), Bana (Harshacharita, Kadambari), Dandin (Dashakumaracharita, Kavyadarsha), Subandhu (Vasavadatta), Magha (Shishupalavadha), Bharavi (Kiratarjuniya), Sriharsha (Naishadhiyacharita), Bilhana (Vikramankadevacharita), Kalhana (Rajatarangini 1148–50 — Kashmir; first "history"), Somadeva (Kathasaritsagara), Jayadeva (Gita Govinda), Kshemendra, Vatsyayana (Kamasutra), Bhartrihari (Shatakatraya), Vishnu Sharma (Panchatantra), Narayana (Hitopadesha), Vetala Panchavimshati, Brihatkatha (Gunadhya).
  • Tamil Sangam: three sangams (Madurai); Ettutogai (8 anthologies), Pattuppattu (10 idylls), Padinenkilkanakku (18 minor; Tirukkural — Tiruvalluvar; 1,330 couplets; aram, porul, inbam), epics — Silappadikaram (Ilango; Kannagi), Manimekalai (Sattanar; Buddhist), Jivaka Chintamani (Tiruttakkadevar; Jain), Valayapathi, Kundalakesi; Kamba Ramayanam (12th c.); Nakkirar, Kapilar, Avvaiyar.
  • Other regional firsts: Kannada — Kavirajamarga (Amoghavarsha/Srivijaya, 9th c.), Pampa-Ponna-Ranna (ratnatraya), Telugu — Nannaya (Andhra Mahabharatam; adikavi), Tikkana, Errana (kavitraya); Malayalam — Ramacharitam, Ezhuthachan (Adhyatma Ramayanam; father of Malayalam), Cherusseri; Odia — Sarala Das (Mahabharata), Jagannatha Das (Bhagabata), Balarama Das; Bengali — Charyapada (oldest; Buddhist siddhas, 8th–12th), Krittibas (Ramayana), Kashiram Das, Chandidas, Mukundaram (Chandimangal), Bharatchandra; Assamese — Madhava Kandali (14th c. Ramayana), Shankaradeva; Marathi — Jnaneshwari, Mahanubhava panth (Lilacharitra), Leelacharitra; Hindi — Prithviraj Raso, Amir Khusrau (Khaliq Bari), Kabir, Jayasi, Tulsi, Sur, Bihari (Satsai), Keshavdas; Punjabi — Farid, Nanak, Waris Shah (Heer 1766), Bulleh Shah; Urdu — Amir Khusrau (proto), Quli Qutb Shah (first diwan), Wali Dakhani, Mir, Sauda, Ghalib, Zauq, Momin, Iqbal (Tarana-e-Hind 1904, Bang-e-Dara); Persian — Barani, Abul Fazl, Faizi, Badauni, Dara (Majma-ul-Bahrain), Khafi Khan (Muntakhab-ul-Lubab).
  • Modern: Bankim (Anandamath 1882 — Vande Mataram; Durgeshnandini first Bengali novel 1865), Michael Madhusudan (Meghnad Badh Kavya; blank verse), Tagore (Gitanjali 1910; Nobel 1913; Jana Gana Mana 1911 Calcutta Congress; Amar Sonar Bangla; Gora, Ghare Baire), Premchand (Godan, Nirmala; Soz-e-Watan banned 1909), Bharatendu Harishchandra (Andher Nagari; father of modern Hindi), Subramania Bharati, Bharatidasan, Kumaran Asan, Vallathol, Kuvempu, Bendre, Karanth, Mahadevi Verma, Nirala, Pant, Prasad (Chhayavad), Iqbal, Faiz, Manto, Sarat Chandra (Devdas, Pather Dabi banned), Fakir Mohan Senapati (Chha Mana Atha Guntha), Gopinath Mohanty, Lakshminath Bezbaroa, O. Sankara Kurup; 2023 Gulzar & Rambhadracharya; 2024 Vinod Kumar Shukla); Sahitya Akademi awards 24 languages.

Languages & scripts

  • Eighth Schedule: 22 languages (14 original + Sindhi 1967 (21st Amdt) + Konkani, Manipuri, Nepali 1992 (71st) + Bodo, Dogri, Maithili, Santali 2003 (92nd)); English is not in it; Hindi in Devanagari official (Art 343); Official Languages Act 1963; Classical 11 (see fairs card).
  • Families: Indo-Aryan (~78%), Dravidian (~20%; Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Tulu, Gondi, Brahui (Balochistan), Kurukh, Malto), Austro-Asiatic (Munda — Santali (Ol Chiki script, Raghunath Murmu 1925), Ho, Mundari, Kharia; Khasi; Nicobarese), Tibeto-Burman (Bodo, Manipuri/Meitei — Meitei Mayek, Mizo, Naga groups, Lepcha, Ladakhi), Tai-Kadai (Ahom, Khamti), Great Andamanese, Ongan; Census 2011 — 121 languages, 19,569 raw mother tongues, 22 scheduled speakers 96.7%.
  • Scripts: Brahmi (Ashokan; mother of all Indic; Prinsep 1837), Kharosthi (right-to-left; NW; Aramaic-derived; died 3rd c. CE), Gupta → Siddham/Nagari → Devanagari; Sharada (Kashmir), Grantha (Tamil Sanskrit), Vatteluttu (early Tamil), Tamil-Brahmi (Keezhadi finds 6th BCE claim), Kadamba-Chalukya → Kannada-Telugu, Modi (Marathi), Gurmukhi (Guru Angad standardised), Ol Chiki, Meitei Mayek, Tolong Siki (Kurukh), Wancho, Sorang Sompeng (Sora), Tigalari, Takri (Dogri), Landa (Sindhi/Punjabi mercantile), Nastaliq (Urdu), Sylheti Nagri; Indus script undeciphered.
  • Language policy hooks: Three-language formula (Kothari 1968; NEP 2020 flexible), Art 350A (mother tongue primary), 350B (Special Officer for linguistic minorities), Art 351 (Hindi development), Bhasha Sangam, Bharatavani portal, NTM (translation), Scheme for Protection of Endangered Languages (SPPEL — CIIL Mysuru; 117 languages <10,000 speakers), PM e-VIDYA, Bhashini (AI translation); UNESCO Atlas — 197 Indian languages endangered; Census 2011 non-scheduled 99 languages (Bhili largest, ~1.04 crore).

Traps

  • Tirukkural is part of Padinenkilkanakku (post-Sangam), not Ettutogai.
  • Kharosthi is right-to-left; Brahmi left-to-right; both on Ashokan records.
  • 22 scheduled languages ≠ 11 classical (Pali & Prakrit are classical but not scheduled; Sindhi, Nepali, Bodo etc. are scheduled but not classical).
  • Vande Mataram is from Anandamath (1882; song composed 1875); Jana Gana Mana first sung Dec 1911.
