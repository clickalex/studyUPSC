Indian Painting — Detailed Notes

Chain: wall murals → palm-leaf minis → Mughal atelier → Rajasthani-Pahari offshoots → folk living traditions. Pair school-patron-signature.

1. Wall murals

  • Ajanta (2nd BCE–6th CE): tempera (fresco-secco) on mud-plaster; icons: Padmapani (cave 1), Dying Princess (16), Bodhisattva & Jataka panels (17); Chalukya Badami, Pallava Panamalai panels; Kailasa (Ellora) Shaiva panels; Lepakshi (Vijayanagara, Veerabhadra — hanging pillar lore); Kerala: Mattancherry (Ramayana panels), Guruvayur; Odisha: Raghurajpur pata-chitra (palm + cloth).
  • Technique note: true fresco (wet lime) vs Indian tempera (dry plaster + binder) — one-line differentiator.

2. Miniature arc

  • Pala (Bengal-Bihar, 8th-12th): palm-leaf Buddhist manuscripts, monastic patronage, Vajrayana icons.
  • Jain (western Indian): Kalpasutra/Kalakacharya katha; Gujarat-Rajasthan; profuse gold, 'pata' style; profile eyes convention; paper from 13th-14th c.
  • Mughal: Humayun returns from Persia with Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad → the imperial atelier is born; Akbar: Tutinama, Hamzanama (1400 folios), Razmnama (Mahabharata Persian), Akbarnama — Indian-Persian synthesis, Daswant and Basawan masters; Jahangir: connoisseur peak — portraits, flora-fauna (Ustad Mansur — dodo/painted stork), European prints arrive (Jesuit missions); Shah Jahan: courtly polish, jewel tones, formal borders, harem albums, Padshahnama; decline post-aurangzeb → provincial offshoots (Awadh, Murshidabad).
  • Rajasthani (Rajput courts): Mewar (Chawand Ragamala 1605, Mewar Ramayana), Kishangarh (Bani-Thani — Savant Singh-Nihal Chand), Bundi-Kota (hunting, love scenes), Bikaner (Mughal-trained), Jodhpur (Marwar bold), Dhundhar-Amber-Jaipur; themes: Ragamala, Baramasa, Bhagavata, Gita-Govinda.
  • Pahari (Himalayan foothills): Basohli (c.1660-90 — bold primaries, Rasamanjari (Devidasa atelier); 'Chaurapanchasika' lineage), Kangra (post-1765 Guler-trained: Manaku-Nainsukh family; Gita-Govinda, Bihari Satsai; lyric wash style under Raja Sansar Chand), Guler (first naturalism), Garhwal, Jammu.
  • Deccan: Bijapur (Ibrahim Adil Shah II), Golconda (lush gold, dancing girls), Ahmadnagar ('Nujum al-Ulum' manuscript).

3. Folk-tribal living traditions

  • Madhubani/Mithila (Bihar): kohbar (wedding-chamber) symbols; fish motif; women-led; Bharni-Kachni styles; post-1934 earthquake revival credit (William Archer documentation).
  • Warli (Maharashtra): white rice-paste on mud walls, tarpa dance circles, chauk (marriage square), Jivya Soma Mashe revival.
  • Phad (Shahpura, Rajasthan): Pabuji-Devnarayan epic scrolls, bhopa-bhopi performance.
  • Patachitra: Odisha (Jagannath icons) + Bengal (Patua scroll-singers), Sanjhi (Mathura paper-stencil).
  • Kalamkari (Andhra-Telangana): Srikalahasti (pen) vs Machilipatnam (block) — draw-with-pen vs block-print; myrobalan + mordant chemistry.
  • Gond (MP), Pithora (Gujarat-Rajasthan, Bhilala), Cheriyal (Telangana scrolls), Thangka (Ladakh-Sikkim-Tawang — Vajrayana iconometry), Manjusha (Bihar), Kalighat (Bengal pat).

MCQ hooks

  • Bani-Thani = Kishangarh; 'India's Mona Lisa' tag.
  • Mansur = Jahangir's animal painter; Hamzanama = Akbar launch project.
  • Basohli vs Kangra: bold vs lyrical; Nainsukh = Guler-trained realist.
  • Kalamkari twin towns: Srikalahasti pen, Machilipatnam block.
  • Warli tarpa circle ≠ Madhubani kohbar.
  • Ajanta tempera ≠ European true fresco.
