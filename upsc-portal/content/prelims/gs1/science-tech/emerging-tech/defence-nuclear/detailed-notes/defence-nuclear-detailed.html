Defence and Nuclear Technology — Detailed Notes

Missile families, induction systems, nuclear-power chain, agencies. System-type-origin matching.

1. Missile families (DRDO-led)

  • Ballistic: Agni series (Agni-V ~5,000+ km ICBM-class, canisterised; MIRV test Mar 2024 — 'Mission Divyastra'); K-15/K-4 SLBMs (Arihant-class SSBNs); Pralay quasi-ballistic.
  • Cruise: BrahMos (Indo-Russian supersonic ~Mach 2.8; land-ship-air-submarine variants; first export Philippines 2022); Nirbhay/ITCM subsonic line.
  • Air-defence-strike: Akash (+NG), MR-SAM/Barak-8 (Indo-Israel), QRSAM, VSHORADS; Astra BVR air-to-air; Nag ATGM; Pinaka rocket artillery; ATAGS howitzer; S-400 (Russian, 5 squadrons); BMD interceptors (PDV-Ashwin).
  • Induction frame: INS Vikrant (2022; first indigenous carrier), P-75 Scorpene (Mazagon-Naval Group; +3 approved 2025), Arihant-class SSBNs, LCA Tejas Mk1A (83+97 ordered), Rafale-M (2025 deal), iDEX startup route, positive-indigenisation lists, defence exports ~₹21,000 cr FY24 band.

2. Nuclear power (DAE-NPCIL-BARC-AERB)

  • Fleet: PHWR backbone (Kaiga-Rajasthan-Madras-Kakrapar-Narora; Kakrapar-4 connected 2024), Kudankulam VVER-1000 (Russian; 6-unit plan), Tarapur BWRs (oldest); PFBR Kalpakkam (500 MWe fast breeder, Bhavini) achieved first criticality 2024; new approvals (RAPS-7 etc.); SMR push (2025 budget: Bharat Small Reactors 220-MW PHWR-based); 100-GW-by-2047 vision.
  • Three-stage programme (Homi Bhabha): stage-1 PHWR (natural uranium) → stage-2 FBR (Pu-bred U-233 from thorium) → stage-3 thorium breeders (AHWR concept); thorium via monazite sands (Kerala-TN).
  • Oversight: AERB regulator; CLNDA 2010 liability law; deterrence chain: NCA-SFC; nuclear-command terms in news (Agni-Prime rail-based, canisterised).

6. Nuclear Physics for the Exam

Fission splits a heavy nucleus (uranium-235, plutonium-239) into lighter fragments, releasing energy and 2–3 neutrons that sustain a chain reaction. Fusion combines light nuclei (deuterium and tritium) into helium, releasing far more energy per unit mass — the process powering the Sun and the hydrogen bomb, and the goal of ITER and tokamak research. Fusion is cleaner, produces no long-lived high-level waste and cannot run away, but requires temperatures of millions of degrees and plasma confinement, so no reactor yet produces sustained net power.

Reactor components and their functions are directly examinable:

  • Fuel — natural uranium (0.7% U-235) or enriched uranium. Enrichment raises the U-235 fraction: about 3–5% for power reactors, above 90% for weapons.
  • Moderator — slows fast neutrons to thermal energies so they can be captured by U-235. Heavy water, graphite or light water. India's PHWRs use heavy water, which allows the use of natural, unenriched uranium — a deliberate choice given India's lack of enrichment capacity in the early years.
  • Control rods — cadmium, boron or hafnium; absorb neutrons to regulate or halt the reaction. Do not confuse moderating with absorbing.
  • Coolant — removes heat; water, heavy water, liquid sodium in fast reactors.
  • Fertile versus fissile — U-235 and Pu-239 are fissile; U-238 and Th-232 are fertile, converting on neutron capture into Pu-239 and U-233 respectively. This distinction underpins India's entire nuclear strategy.

7. India's Three-Stage Nuclear Programme

Conceived by Homi Bhabha, the programme is a direct response to resource endowment: India has modest uranium reserves but among the world's largest thorium reserves, in the monazite sands of Kerala, Tamil Nadu and Odisha.

  1. Stage I — Pressurised Heavy Water Reactors. Natural uranium fuel, heavy water moderator and coolant. Burns U-235 and produces plutonium-239 as a by-product. This is the operational mainstay today.
  2. Stage II — Fast Breeder Reactors. Use the Pu-239 from Stage I in a mixed-oxide fuel with a U-238 blanket, breeding more fissile material than consumed. Once sufficient plutonium is accumulated, a thorium blanket is introduced to breed U-233. The Prototype Fast Breeder Reactor at Kalpakkam, sodium-cooled, is the demonstration unit.
  3. Stage III — Thorium-based reactors. U-233 and thorium in advanced systems, giving India centuries of energy security. The Advanced Heavy Water Reactor is the design vehicle; KAMINI at Kalpakkam is the world's only operating U-233-fuelled reactor.

Note that thorium is fertile, not fissile — it cannot sustain a chain reaction by itself and must first be converted to U-233. This single fact explains why the three stages must be sequential and why Stage III remains distant.

Institutions: the Department of Atomic Energy reporting to the Prime Minister; BARC (research); NPCIL (operation of power reactors); BHAVINI (fast breeders); UCIL (mining); and the Atomic Energy Regulatory Board — whose lack of statutory independence from the DAE has been criticised by the CAG. The Civil Liability for Nuclear Damage Act, 2010, with its supplier-liability provision under Section 17(b), is the reason foreign vendors have hesitated despite the Indo-US deal.

8. Nuclear Doctrine, Delivery Systems and Arms Control

India's declared nuclear doctrine (2003) rests on: credible minimum deterrence; No First Use, with the qualification that a nuclear response may follow a major attack with biological or chemical weapons; massive retaliation designed to inflict unacceptable damage; civilian political control through the Nuclear Command Authority, whose Political Council alone can authorise use; and a commitment to non-use against non-nuclear-weapon states. Tests: Pokhran-I (1974), styled a "peaceful nuclear explosion", and Pokhran-II (1998), Operation Shakti, after which India declared itself a nuclear-weapon state.

The nuclear triad — delivery from land, air and sea — was completed with INS Arihant, the indigenous SSBN, whose sea leg provides assured second-strike capability, the logical requirement of a No First Use posture. Missiles to know by class: Prithvi (short-range ballistic), the Agni series (I to V, with Agni-V intercontinental-class and Agni-P the newer canisterised variant; MIRV capability demonstrated under Mission Divyastra), K-15 Sagarika and K-4 (submarine-launched), BrahMos (supersonic cruise, an India-Russia joint venture, now exported), Nirbhay (subsonic cruise), Akash, QRSAM and MRSAM (surface-to-air), Astra (air-to-air), Nag and Helina (anti-tank), and Pinaka (multi-barrel rocket). Mission Shakti (2019) demonstrated a direct-ascent anti-satellite capability. Distinguish ballistic missiles (powered ascent then a free-falling parabolic trajectory, re-entering at hypersonic speed) from cruise missiles (continuously powered, aerodynamically guided, low-flying and therefore harder to detect).

Arms control regimes. India is not a party to the NPT — which it regards as discriminatory for dividing the world into nuclear haves and have-nots — nor to the CTBT. It is a member of the IAEA and has signed the Chemical and Biological Weapons Conventions. Among export-control regimes India belongs to the MTCR, the Wassenaar Arrangement and the Australia Group, but not the Nuclear Suppliers Group, where China blocks consensus; the 2008 NSG waiver nonetheless permits civil nuclear commerce. India separated its civil and military facilities under an IAEA safeguards agreement, placing only the civil ones under inspection.

9. Defence Production, Reform and Emerging Technologies

Structural reforms to cite: the creation of the Chief of Defence Staff and the Department of Military Affairs in 2019, implementing a long-standing Kargil Review Committee and Naresh Chandra Committee recommendation; the corporatisation of the Ordnance Factory Board into seven defence public sector undertakings; the Agnipath scheme of short-term enlistment; and the planned move to integrated theatre commands, of which the Andaman and Nicobar Command and the Strategic Forces Command are the existing tri-service formations.

Indigenisation instruments: the Defence Acquisition Procedure 2020 with its priority to Buy Indian – IDDM (Indigenously Designed, Developed and Manufactured); positive indigenisation lists banning import of specified items; raising FDI in defence to 74% via the automatic route and 100% by government route; defence industrial corridors in Uttar Pradesh and Tamil Nadu; iDEX for startups; and the SRIJAN portal. Flagship platforms: the Tejas Light Combat Aircraft, Arjun tank, INS Vikrant as the first indigenous aircraft carrier, and the Dhanush and ATAGS artillery systems. India remains among the largest arms importers, which is the standing critique, though defence exports have risen sharply.

Emerging domains that increasingly appear in the paper: hypersonic weapons (above Mach 5, manoeuvrable and hence hard to intercept — the HSTDV demonstrator); directed energy weapons; drones and counter-drone systems, including swarm tactics; cyber warfare and the Defence Cyber Agency; space as a warfighting domain with the Defence Space Agency; artificial intelligence in ISR and autonomous systems, raising the debate on lethal autonomous weapons; and ballistic missile defence, India's two-tier PAD/AAD system. Also know the concept of grey-zone and hybrid warfare — coercion below the threshold of open conflict through disinformation, economic pressure and proxies.

Trap watch. Moderators slow neutrons; control rods absorb them. Thorium and U-238 are fertile, not fissile. India's PHWRs use natural uranium precisely because heavy water moderation permits it. India is in the MTCR, Wassenaar and Australia Group but not the NSG, NPT or CTBT. No First Use carries a chemical and biological weapons caveat. The sea leg of the triad provides second-strike capability. Ballistic missiles are unpowered for most of their flight; cruise missiles are powered throughout. KAMINI is the only U-233-fuelled reactor in the world.
