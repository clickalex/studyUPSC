World Physical Geography — Detailed Notes

Continents, mountains, plateaus, rivers, deserts, straits — atlas-speed recall.

1. Continental anchors

  • Asia (largest ~44.6 Mkm2; Everest; Dead Sea lowest land), Africa (plateau continent; Sahara; Nile-Amazon river-length debate; Great Rift Valley; Kilimanjaro), North America (Rockies; Great Lakes; Mississippi-Missouri), South America (Andes longest; Amazon largest discharge; Atacama driest), Antarctica (~98% ice; Vinson Massif; no natives), Europe (peninsulas; Alps; Rhine), Australia/Oceania (flattest; Great Dividing Range; Great Barrier Reef).
  • Oceans: Pacific largest-deepest (Mariana), Atlantic S-shape (mid-ridge), Indian (monsoon-driven north), Southern (Antarctic circumpolar), Arctic smallest-shallowest.

2. Mountains, plateaus, rivers

  • Ranges: Himalaya-Karakoram (highest), Andes (longest), Rockies, Alps (fold-young), Urals (Europe-Asia divide), Atlas, Drakensberg, Great Dividing; volcanoes: Fuji, Vesuvius-Etna, Cotopaxi, Kilimanjaro dormant-shield debate.
  • Plateaus: Tibetan (highest, 'roof of the world'), Deccan, Brazilian, Patagonian, Colorado (canyon), East African; plains: West Siberian (largest), Amazon, Mississippi, Indo-Gangetic, North European.
  • Rivers: Amazon (largest volume), Nile-Amazon length race, Yangtze longest Asia, Mississippi-Missouri N America, Congo (deepest discharge 2nd), Danube-Rhine Europe, Murray-Darling Australia, Volga longest Europe.

3. Deserts, extremes, straits

  • Deserts: Sahara (largest hot), Antarctica-Arctic (largest overall-cold), Arabian, Gobi (cold winter), Kalahari, Atacama (driest), Thar, Great Victoria; Death Valley = lowest N America; Danakil = hottest average.
  • Extremes: highest = Everest, lowest land = Dead Sea shore, largest lake = Caspian (saline), deepest lake = Baikal, largest freshwater = Superior, highest waterfall = Angel, largest island = Greenland; key straits: Malacca (busiest), Hormuz (oil), Bab-el-Mandeb, Bosphorus-Dardanelles, Gibraltar, Bering, Palk; canals: Suez (sea-level), Panama (locks), Kiel, Erie.

⚡ MCQ hooks

  • Longest range = Andes; highest = Himalaya; largest plain = West Siberian; highest plateau = Tibet.
  • Amazon = volume king; Nile-Amazon length race (satellite measures vary).
  • Caspian = largest lake (saline); Baikal = deepest; Superior = largest freshwater.
  • Straits: Hormuz-oil, Malacca-traffic, Bab-el-Mandeb-Suez link, Bosphorus-Europe/Asia.
  • Atacama driest; Danakil hottest; Sahara largest hot desert.
  • Angel Falls highest; Greenland largest island.

4. The Earth's Interior, Plate Tectonics and Landform Genesis

Knowledge of the interior comes chiefly from seismic waves. P-waves are longitudinal, faster, and travel through solids, liquids and gases; S-waves are transverse, slower, and cannot pass through liquids. The resulting shadow zones — the S-wave shadow beyond 103° and the P-wave shadow between 103° and 142° — are the direct evidence for a liquid outer core. Discontinuities to name: Mohorovičić between crust and mantle, Gutenberg between mantle and core, and Lehmann between the liquid outer and solid inner core. The crust divides into SIAL (continental, granitic, lighter) and SIMA (oceanic, basaltic, denser); the lithosphere comprises crust plus uppermost mantle and rides on the plastic asthenosphere.

Wegener's continental drift proposed the break-up of Pangaea surrounded by Panthalassa into Laurasia and Gondwana, supported by the jig-saw fit of the Atlantic coasts, the distribution of Glossopteris flora and Mesosaurus fossils across continents, matching Gondwana rock sequences, and glacial striations on now-tropical landmasses. Its fatal weakness was the mechanism — the proposed pole-fleeing and tidal forces were far too weak. The gap was filled by Harry Hess's sea-floor spreading, confirmed by the youthful age of ocean-floor rocks, their increasing age away from the ridges, and the symmetrical palaeomagnetic striping either side of mid-ocean ridges.

Plate tectonics synthesises these. Convection in the mantle, aided by ridge push and slab pull, moves rigid plates. Three boundary types:

  • Divergent (constructive) — plates move apart, magma rises, new crust forms. Mid-Atlantic Ridge; on land, the East African Rift Valley. Associated with shallow-focus earthquakes and basaltic, effusive volcanism.
  • Convergent (destructive) — one plate subducts. Oceanic-continental gives a deep trench, a Benioff zone of deepening earthquake foci, and an explosive andesitic volcanic arc — the Andes. Oceanic-oceanic gives an island arc — Japan, the Philippines, the Andamans. Continental-continental gives fold mountains with no subduction and hence no volcanoes — the Himalaya, which is why the Himalaya is seismically very active but volcanically inert, a favourite distinction.
  • Transform (conservative) — plates slide past one another; crust is neither created nor destroyed; shallow but destructive earthquakes. The San Andreas Fault.

Hotspots are stationary mantle plumes over which plates move, producing age-progressive island chains such as Hawaii and Réunion, and continental flood basalts such as the Deccan Traps. The Pacific Ring of Fire concentrates about 70% of earthquakes and most active volcanoes along the Pacific's subduction margins.

5. Continents, Mountains and Major Landforms

Mountain types: fold mountains, formed by compression at convergent margins — the young Alpine system (Himalaya, Alps, Andes, Rockies, Atlas) and the older, worn-down Caledonian and Hercynian systems (Appalachians, Urals, Aravallis); block mountains or horsts, with intervening graben or rift valleys formed by tensional faulting — the Vosges and Black Forest flanking the Rhine graben, and India's Satpura-Vindhya with the Narmada and Tapi rift valleys; volcanic mountains such as Kilimanjaro, Fuji and Cotopaxi; and residual mountains produced by differential erosion.

Plateaus: intermontane and the highest, such as Tibet, the "roof of the world," and the Bolivian and Colorado plateaus; piedmont, at the foot of mountains; continental or tableland, such as the Deccan; and volcanic, such as the Columbia. Plateaus are disproportionately important as sources of minerals, because ancient shield rocks are exposed.

Plains by origin: structural, depositional — alluvial, glacial, loess (the Chinese loess plateau being wind-deposited silt), lacustrine — and erosional or peneplains. The great alluvial plains of the Indo-Gangetic, Hwang Ho, Mississippi and Amazon basins carry the densest populations on Earth.

Ocean floor relief: the continental shelf (shallow, gently sloping, richest fishing grounds because sunlight reaches the bottom and rivers deliver nutrients; also the locus of petroleum), the steep continental slope, the continental rise, the vast abyssal plain, mid-ocean ridges, trenches — the Mariana Trench with the Challenger Deep being the deepest point — seamounts, guyots (flat-topped seamounts), atolls and submarine canyons.

Ocean currents follow a predictable pattern that repeats in every basin: warm currents flow poleward along western margins of oceans (that is, the eastern coasts of continents) and cold currents flow equatorward along eastern margins of oceans. Gyres are clockwise in the northern hemisphere and anticlockwise in the southern. Know the Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift (moderating north-west Europe, keeping Norwegian ports ice-free), the Kuroshio and Oyashio, the California, Canary, Benguela, Peru/Humboldt and West Australian cold currents — each associated with a coastal desert and, through upwelling, with rich fisheries. Where warm and cold currents meet, as at the Grand Banks (Gulf Stream and Labrador) and off Hokkaido, dense fog and outstanding fishing grounds occur. El Niño is the periodic warming of the eastern Pacific with weakened trade winds, suppressing Peruvian upwelling and fisheries and typically weakening the Indian monsoon; La Niña is the opposite phase; the Southern Oscillation is the associated pressure see-saw, and the Indian Ocean Dipole is the analogous Indian Ocean mode, a positive phase of which aids the monsoon.

6. Exogenic Processes and Regional Physical Geography

Weathering — the in-situ breakdown of rock — is physical (frost shattering, exfoliation, thermal expansion, salt crystallisation), chemical (carbonation, hydrolysis, oxidation, solution) or biological. It differs from erosion, which involves transport. Physical weathering dominates in cold and arid climates, chemical weathering in hot humid climates — the reason for deep tropical regoliths and laterite.

Fluvial landforms: in the youthful stage, V-shaped valleys, gorges and canyons, waterfalls, rapids and potholes; in maturity, meanders and river cliffs; in old age, floodplains, ox-bow lakes, natural levees, braided channels and deltas — arcuate (Nile, Ganga), bird's-foot (Mississippi), estuarine (Narmada, Tapi where strong tides prevent deposition) and cuspate. Karst landforms in limestone: sinkholes and dolines, uvalas and poljes, caverns with stalactites hanging and stalagmites rising, lapies and disappearing streams.

Glacial: erosional — cirque, arête, pyramidal peak, U-shaped valley, hanging valley, fjord, roche moutonnée; depositional — moraines, drumlins, eskers, kames and outwash plains. Arid/aeolian: erosional — mushroom rocks, yardangs, zeugen, ventifacts, inselbergs, deflation hollows; depositional — barchan (crescent, horns pointing downwind), seif or longitudinal dunes, loess. Coastal: erosional — cliffs, wave-cut platforms, caves, arches and stacks; depositional — beaches, spits, bars, tombolos and lagoons.

Regional anchors worth memorising with their locations: the Rockies, Appalachians, Great Lakes, Mississippi and Grand Canyon in North America; the Andes, Amazon, Atacama, Pampas and Angel Falls in South America; the Sahara, Congo, Nile, Great Rift Valley, Kilimanjaro and Victoria Falls in Africa; the Alps, Danube, Rhine, Volga, Urals and Scandinavian fjords in Europe; the Himalaya, Tibetan Plateau, Gobi, Yangtze, Mekong, Dead Sea (lowest land point) and Caspian (largest lake) in Asia; and the Great Dividing Range, Great Barrier Reef and Murray-Darling in Australia. For prelims, pair each with its country, drainage and any strait or canal association — Malacca, Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, Gibraltar, Bosphorus, Bering, Magellan, Panama and Suez.

Trap watch. S-waves cannot travel through liquids, which proves the outer core is molten. Continental-continental convergence builds fold mountains but produces no volcanoes — the Himalaya has earthquakes, not eruptions. Warm currents run along the eastern coasts of continents. Barchan horns point downwind. Stalactites hang from the ceiling. Wegener's drift failed on mechanism, not evidence. Hotspots are stationary while plates move. Guyots are flat-topped, seamounts are not. The Deccan Traps are hotspot flood basalts, not subduction volcanism.
