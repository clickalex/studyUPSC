CSAT Mock — Paper II (30 Questions · 45 Minutes)

30 questions

Qualifying paper: 33% (66/200) needed. This 30-question set mirrors the real mix — reading comprehension 10, quantitative aptitude 10, logical reasoning & data interpretation 10. Marking +2.5 / −0.83. Worked solutions follow the questions.

Part A — Reading Comprehension (Q1–Q10)

Passage 1. Urbanisation in India is often described as 'messy and hidden'. Messy, because a large share of urban growth takes the form of unplanned peripheral sprawl and informal settlements; hidden, because official definitions understate the extent of urbanisation. Census towns—settlements that meet the demographic criteria of a town but continue to be governed as villages—illustrate this. They receive neither the infrastructure investment of statutory towns nor the rural programmes tailored to villages, and fall through the cracks of public policy.

Q1

Which one of the following best reflects the central idea of the passage?

  1. (a)India should abolish census towns
  2. (b)Official statistics overstate India's urbanisation
  3. (c)Definitional and governance gaps leave many urbanising settlements poorly served
  4. (d)Peripheral sprawl is inevitable in developing countries
Show answer
Correct: (c)Both 'messy' and 'hidden' point to gaps in definition and governance.
Q2

According to the passage, census towns are:

  1. (a)Statutory urban local bodies
  2. (b)Villages that have acquired urban demographic characteristics but rural governance
  3. (c)Settlements with no infrastructure at all
  4. (d)Towns created by State notification
Show answer
Correct: (b)Direct from the passage.
Q3

The word 'hidden' in the passage most nearly implies:

  1. (a)Illegal
  2. (b)Understated in official measurement
  3. (c)Located in remote areas
  4. (d)Governed secretly
Show answer
Correct: (b)Official definitions 'understate'.

Passage 2. The precautionary principle holds that where an activity raises threats of serious or irreversible harm, lack of full scientific certainty should not be used as a reason for postponing cost-effective measures to prevent it. Critics argue that applied rigidly it would paralyse innovation, since no technology can be proven harmless in advance. Supporters respond that the principle does not demand proof of safety, only that the burden of justification shift towards those proposing the risky activity.

Q4

Which of the following is the most logical inference from the passage?

  1. (a)The precautionary principle bans all new technologies
  2. (b)Supporters and critics differ on whether the principle requires proof of absolute safety
  3. (c)Scientific certainty is always achievable before deployment
  4. (d)Innovation is more important than environmental protection
Show answer
Correct: (b)The last two sentences set out precisely that disagreement.
Q5

The author's treatment of the topic is best described as:

  1. (a)Polemical
  2. (b)Balanced exposition
  3. (c)Satirical
  4. (d)Nostalgic
Show answer
Correct: (b)Both sides are presented without endorsement.
Q6

'Shift the burden of justification' in the passage means:

  1. (a)Governments must pay for damages
  2. (b)Proponents of an activity must show why it should proceed despite possible harm
  3. (c)Critics must prove harm before objecting
  4. (d)Courts decide all such disputes
Show answer
Correct: (b)Proponents must justify.

Passage 3. Many economists argue that direct cash transfers are more efficient than in-kind subsidies because they respect the preferences of beneficiaries and eliminate leakages in distribution. Others counter that in-kind transfers such as food grains protect households against price volatility and are less likely to be diverted to non-essential consumption. Evidence from pilot studies is mixed: outcomes depend heavily on market access, banking penetration and the design of the transfer.

Q7

Which one of the following statements is best supported by the passage?

  1. (a)Cash transfers are always superior
  2. (b)In-kind transfers have no advantages
  3. (c)The relative efficacy of cash and in-kind transfers is context-dependent
  4. (d)Pilot studies conclusively favour in-kind transfers
Show answer
Correct: (c)'Outcomes depend heavily on…'.
Q8

The passage suggests that cash transfers may perform poorly where:

  1. (a)Banking penetration is high
  2. (b)Markets are well integrated
  3. (c)Households lack access to markets or banks
  4. (d)Food prices are stable
Show answer
Correct: (c)Market access and banking penetration are named preconditions.

Passage 4. A language dies not when its last speaker dies, but when its last child learner disappears. Intergenerational transmission is the true measure of vitality. Documentation projects that record vocabulary and grammar preserve a language as an artefact; only communities that use it in homes, schools and markets preserve it as a living system.

Q9

The author would most likely agree that:

  1. (a)Recording dictionaries is sufficient to save a language
  2. (b)A language with many elderly speakers but no child learners is endangered
  3. (c)Schools should teach only global languages
  4. (d)Language death is impossible to prevent
Show answer
Correct: (b)Loss of child learners = death of the language.
Q10

The critical assumption underlying the passage is that:

  1. (a)Children learn languages more easily than adults
  2. (b)Vitality depends on everyday use across generations rather than on records
  3. (c)Governments oppose minority languages
  4. (d)Markets are more important than homes for language use
Show answer
Correct: (b)Intergenerational transmission is 'the true measure'.

Part B — Quantitative Aptitude (Q11–Q20)

Q11

The average of five consecutive odd numbers is 31. What is the product of the smallest and the largest?

  1. (a)891
  2. (b)945
  3. (c)961
  4. (d)1,023
Show answer
Correct: (a)Numbers 27, 29, 31, 33, 35; 27 × 33 = 891.
Q12

A shopkeeper marks an article 40% above cost and offers a discount of 20%. His profit percentage is:

  1. (a)8%
  2. (b)10%
  3. (c)12%
  4. (d)20%
Show answer
Correct: (c)SP = 1.4 × 0.8 = 1.12 of CP → 12%.
Q13

A can complete a work in 12 days and B in 18 days. They work together for 4 days, after which A leaves. In how many more days will B finish the remaining work?

  1. (a)6
  2. (b)8
  3. (c)10
  4. (d)12
Show answer
Correct: (b)4 days' work = 4(1/12 + 1/18) = 10/18; remaining 8/18 at 1/18 per day → 8 days.
Q14

A train 240 m long crosses a platform 360 m long in 30 seconds. Its speed is:

  1. (a)54 km/h
  2. (b)60 km/h
  3. (c)72 km/h
  4. (d)80 km/h
Show answer
Correct: (c)600 m / 30 s = 20 m/s = 72 km/h.
Q15

The ratio of the ages of a father and son is 7:2 now and will be 8:3 after 10 years. What is the father's present age?

  1. (a)56
  2. (b)63
  3. (c)70
  4. (d)77
Show answer
Correct: (c)3(7x + 10) = 8(2x + 10) → 5x = 50 → x = 10; father = 70.
Q16

How many three-digit numbers are divisible by 7?

  1. (a)127
  2. (b)128
  3. (c)129
  4. (d)130
Show answer
Correct: (b)From 105 to 994: (994 − 105)/7 + 1 = 128.
Q17

A sum doubles itself in 8 years at simple interest. In how many years will it become four times?

  1. (a)16
  2. (b)24
  3. (c)32
  4. (d)20
Show answer
Correct: (b)Doubling in 8 yrs → SI = P in 8 yrs → 3P needs 24 yrs.
Q18

The LCM of two numbers is 120 and their HCF is 4. If one number is 24, the other is:

  1. (a)16
  2. (b)20
  3. (c)40
  4. (d)60
Show answer
Correct: (b)120 × 4 / 24 = 20.
Q19

In how many ways can the letters of the word 'DELHI' be arranged so that the vowels are together?

  1. (a)24
  2. (b)48
  3. (c)60
  4. (d)120
Show answer
Correct: (b)Treat EI as one unit: 4! × 2! = 48.
Q20

A mixture of 40 litres contains milk and water in the ratio 3:1. How much water must be added to make the ratio 3:2?

  1. (a)5 litres
  2. (b)8 litres
  3. (c)10 litres
  4. (d)12 litres
Show answer
Correct: (c)Milk 30, water 10; need water 20 → add 10 litres.

Part C — Logical Reasoning & Data Interpretation (Q21–Q30)

Q21

If in a code, 'MONSOON' is written as 'NPOTPPO', how is 'CYCLONE' written?

  1. (a)DZDMPOF
  2. (b)DZDMPNF
  3. (c)DZCMPOF
  4. (d)DYDMPOF
Show answer
Correct: (a)Each letter +1.
Q22

Five friends P, Q, R, S and T sit in a row facing north. R is to the immediate right of P. S is at the left end. T is between Q and S. Who sits in the middle?

  1. (a)P
  2. (b)Q
  3. (c)R
  4. (d)T
Show answer
Correct: (b)Only arrangement: S T Q P R; Q is in the middle.
Q23

Statement: All rivers are streams. Some streams are lakes. Conclusions: I. Some rivers are lakes. II. Some lakes are streams.

  1. (a)Only I follows
  2. (b)Only II follows
  3. (c)Both follow
  4. (d)Neither follows
Show answer
Correct: (b)Conversion of 'some streams are lakes'.
Q24

Pointing to a photograph, Ravi said, 'She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son.' How is the woman related to Ravi?

  1. (a)Mother
  2. (b)Sister
  3. (c)Aunt
  4. (d)Cousin
Show answer
Correct: (b)Grandfather's only son is Ravi's father; his daughter is Ravi's sister.
Q25

Find the next term: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

  1. (a)40
  2. (b)42
  3. (c)44
  4. (d)48
Show answer
Correct: (b)Differences 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 → 42.
Q26

A clock shows 3:30. The angle between the hour and minute hands is:

  1. (a)60°
  2. (b)75°
  3. (c)90°
  4. (d)105°
Show answer
Correct: (b)
Q27

Which day of the week was 26 January 1950?

  1. (a)Wednesday
  2. (b)Thursday
  3. (c)Friday
  4. (d)Saturday
Show answer
Correct: (b)26 January 1950 was a Thursday.

Q28

In which year was the qualifying percentage the lowest?

  1. (a)2021
  2. (b)2022
  3. (c)2023
  4. (d)2024
Show answer
Correct: (c)2.5%, 2.5%, 2.4%, 2.5% — lowest in 2023.
Q29

The percentage increase in the number of candidates appearing from 2021 to 2024 is approximately:

  1. (a)25%
  2. (b)30%
  3. (c)33%
  4. (d)40%
Show answer
Correct: (c)(640 − 480)/480 = 33.3%.
Q30

The average number of qualified candidates per year over the period was:

  1. (a)13,600
  2. (b)13,850
  3. (c)14,000
  4. (d)14,200
Show answer
Correct: (b)(12 + 13 + 14.4 + 16)/4 = 13.85 thousand.
