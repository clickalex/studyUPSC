PSIR — PYQ Patterns & Approaches

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Model approaches included.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. "Rawls, Sen and Nozick offer three incommensurable grammars of justice." Compare. (15 marks)

Approach: Rawls — basic-structure fairness (difference principle); Sen — capability + comparative justice (nitya vs transcendental institutional); Nozick — entitlement, minimal state → Indian resonance (reservations as Rawlsian, DBT as capability, tax-debate as Nozickian) → conclude: policy mixes all three.

Q2. Examine Ambedkar's and Gandhi's differing routes to social transformation. (15 marks)

Approach: constitutional-modernity + state-led annihilation of caste vs moral-spiritual self-reform + village reconstruction → convergences (dignity, anti-untouchability) → post-47 synthesis in constitutional design → contemporary relevance.

Q3. Assess the three faces of power with an Indian example each. (10 marks)

Approach: Lukes — decision-making, agenda-setting, preference-shaping → examples: ordinance timing, Parliamentary committee referrals (agenda), advertising-led aspiration shifts (preference) → evaluate measurability of the third face.

Q4. "Coalition era federalism bargained; GST-era federalism negotiates." Critically examine. (15 marks)

Approach: periodise federalism (centralisation → coalition bargaining → one-dominant + GST/CESS frictions, CVS disputes) → institutions (ISC, FC devolution) → court-arbitrated lines → way-ahead: treaty-style fiscal federal compact.

Q5. Write a note on deliberative democracy and its panchayat experiment. (10 marks)

Approach: define deliberative turn (Habermas lineage: communicative reason) → gram sabha/social audit as deliberation infrastructure → limits (capture, quorums) → design fixes (facilitation, rotation, technology).

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. "Structural realism explains the balance; constructivism explains its language." Discuss with reference to Indo-Pacific alignments. (15 marks)

Approach: Waltz — polarity-driven balancing vs Wendt — identities constituting interests → QUAD/I2U2 as material balance + normative community ("free, open, rules-based" vocabulary) → India's multi-alignment → assessment: theories complementary.

Q7. Analyse India–China border infrastructure asymmetry as a security dilemma. (10 marks)

Approach: define security dilemma (defensive moves read as offensive) → evidence: LAC roads/rails asymmetry → escalatory spiral risk (Galwan-type) → mitigation: CBMs, hotlines, de-escalation protocols, deterrence-by-denial.

Q8. Evaluate BIMSTEC as SAARC's successor logic. (10 marks)

Approach: SAARC paralysis (bilateralism clause, India-Pakistan freeze) → BIMSTEC's Bay-of-Bengal geography + sectoral mechanism → limits (secretariat capacity, trade share) → verdict: complementary, not replacement — plus Act-East coupling.

Q9. Make a case for India's permanent UNSC seat with arguments critics would marshal against. (15 marks)

Approach: for: demography, economy, peacekeeping record, G4/UfC logic → against: regional rivalries, NAM-era ambiguity, treaty-amendment arithmetic (Art. 108) → India's counters (EsRey?) — actually use: Ezulwini-consensus nuance, Coffee-club opposition → conclude: pragmatic staged route.

How to practise

  • Paper-map 8 years of PYQs: theory hot nodes (justice, power, ideologies), IR hot nodes (neighbours, institutions, UN reform).
  • Maintain a current dossier: one page per neighbour/quarter + one per global summit.
  • Timed loop: 3 answers/35 min alternating papers; scholar-density audit on every answer.
