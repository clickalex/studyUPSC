Public Administration — Syllabus Map & Strategy

Paper 1: administrative theory — thinkers, structures, processes. Paper 2: Indian administration — union to panchayat, welfare machinery and reforms. Heavy GS-2/GS-4 resonance, a thinker-canon to memorise, and a governance-currents coupling that rewards daily newspaper reading with an administrator's eye.

Paper 1 — Unit Map (Theory)

BlockUnitsAnchors
Introduction1.x–2.xMeaning/scope/significance lineages (politics-administration dichotomy — Wilson, Goodnow); New Public Administration (Minnowbrook I/II/III), New Public Management, New Public Service, post-NPM talk
Thinkers3.xWilson (dichotomy), Taylor (scientific management), Fayol (14 principles), Weber (bureaucracy/legal-rational), Simon (bounded rationality, proverbs critique), Barnard (cooperative systems, acceptance zone), Waldo (administration as moral science), Merton (goal displacement), McGregor (X-Y), Maslow-Herzberg (motivation), Argyris (maturity), Riggs (prismatic sala), Presthus, Ostrom (public-choice)
Structures & processes4.x–8.xOrganisation forms (line-staff, matrix), leadership; accountability & control (legislative/executive/judicial, citizen charters); personnel administration (recruitment-commission legacy, career systems, pay commissions); financial administration (budget types — ZBB/PPBS, CAG-PAC loop); administrative law (delegated legislation, tribunals, natural justice); comparative & development administration (Riggs's prisms, ecology); OTs & policy (intent — implementation gap, Sabatier/Pressman- Wildavsky lineage)

Paper 2 — Unit Map (Indian Administration)

  • Foundations: Mauryan-Mughal legacies → colonial administration → constitutional framework (Art. 53, 73rd/74th, All-India Services' Art. 312).
  • Union & state machinery: Cabinet secretariat, NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission, ministries-DOPT ecosystem; CM secretariat, chief secretary's role; field administration — DM's evolving role debate.
  • District & local: district planning committees, PESA, municipal acts, devolution index debates, e-governance stack (DBT, e-Office, CMIS?), actually: e-Office, GeM, PFMS.
  • Personnel & reforms: civil-services values (GS-4 handshake), training architecture (LBSNAA, mid-career), performance appraisal reform, police-reform line (Prakash Singh), administrative-reforms lineage (1st & 2nd ARC volumes).
  • Welfare & issue administration: poverty/food (NFSA-PDS), health/education missions, women-child (Mission Shakti/Poshan), tribal & area development, disaster management architecture (DM Act 2005), law-and-order interface, corruption-controls (Lokpal/CVC), citizen-centricity (CPGRAMS, charters, social audit).

Booklist (lean and sufficient)

  • Thinkers: Prasad & Prasad (thinker chapters) + one theory text (Nicholas Henry's paradigms overview).
  • Structures: standard coaching compilations + Fadia or Sharma-Sadana selective.
  • India: Rajni Goyal & Arora (Indian Public Administration) + 2nd ARC summaries + annual Economic Survey governance chapters.
  • Current: PIB-governance watch, ARC reports, punching bag debates (simultaneous elections, police reform) as case stock.

Answer Strategy

  • Theory formula: thinker → context → core claim → Indian evidence/critique → contemporary echo ("Simon's critique returns in NPM dashboards").
  • Paper 2 formula: constitutional hook → institutional map → performance record → reform proposal (ARC-referenced) → case datum.
  • Currents coupling: every Paper-2 answer gains one recent programme/court line; maintain a "reform ledger" (police, district, appraisal, delivery).
  • Draw the machinery: secretariat-flow diagrams, budget cycle ring, accountability-web sketch — one per answer.

Scoring Profile and Fit

  • Best for: GS-2 lovers, working professionals (experiential dividend), interview-compound seekers.
  • Watch-outs: large competitive pool with polarised scoring — answers must be thinker-dense (P1) and case-dense (P2); the subject's "common sense" trap lures shallow writing.

Twelve-Week First Pass

  • Wk 1-2 intro + paradigms. Wk 3-5 thinker canon (3-4 per week with application cards). Wk 6 structures + accountability/finance/law. Wk 7 comparative-development + OTs. Wk 8-10 Indian machinery + reforms ledger. Wk 11 welfare-administration cases. Wk 12 PYQ mapping + sectional.

7. How Paper 1 Thinkers Are Won: The Canon as an Argument, Not a List

The Paper 1 thinker canon (Wilson → Ostrom, twenty-odd names) is won by learning it as five generations arguing with each other, because the examination's thinker questions are overwhelmingly relational (\"compare,\" \"critique,\" \"in the light of\"). Generation one — founding dichotomies: Wilson (1887 — politics–administration dichotomy, administration as businesslike science) and Goodnow (1900 — politics as will, administration as execution) create the field's founding separation; Taylor (1911 — scientific management: time-study, differential piece-rate, functional foremanship) and Fayol (1916 — 14 principles, five elements, the manager's doctrine) industrialise it; Weber (bureaucracy — legal-rational authority, hierarchy, files, career officials — the ideal type as analytical yardstick; the Protestant Ethic–disenchantment civilisational frame) gives it sociology. The first critique-wave is internal: Simon demolishes the \"proverbs\" (1946 — for every principle its contradiction — span-of-control versus hierarchy; unity-of-command versus specialisation), and Waldo historicises the dichotomy itself (1948 — the field's politics-denial as ideology; administration as moral science). Generation two — the decision and the human: Simon (bounded rationality — satisficing, not maximising; decision-premises — fact/value, the organisation as decision-system), Barnard (cooperative systems — acceptance zone, contribution-satisfaction equilibrium, the executive's moral function), McGregor (Theory X–Y — assumptions about workers as self-fulfilling), Maslow–Herzberg (need hierarchy; hygiene-versus-motivators), Argyris (maturity theory — formal organisation infantilises; double-loop learning), Likert (System 4 — participative management), Herzberg completed by McClelland (achievement motivation). The exam's favourite pairing is Simon-versus-Weber (decision-realism against ideal-type formalism) and Barnard-versus-Taylor (cooperation against control).

Generation three — systems, power, pathology: Merton (bureaucratic dysfunction — goal displacement, trained incapacity, the ritualist), Selznick (cooptation — formal versus informal; TVA's grass-roots mythology), Gouldner (mock versus representative bureaucracy — the gypsum-plant patterns), Blau–Scott (who benefits? — the cui bono typology), Crozier (the vicious circle — rigidity breeding the circumvention that deepens rigidity), Simon–March–Cyert (the behavioural theory of the firm — coalitions, slack, standard operating procedures). Generation four — development and comparison: Riggs (prismatic society — the sala model — fused–prismatic–refracted; development administration as ecology-sensitive), Weidner (development administration as goal-oriented change), Heady (comparative administration's configuration), Esman (institution-building). Generation five — markets, state, and return: public choice (Buchanan–Tullock — bureaucrats as utility-maximisers; Niskanen's budget-maximising bureau; Ostrom's polycentric governance — the commons without Leviathan, 2009 Nobel), NPM (Hood 1991 — disaggregation, competition, incentivisation; Osborne–Gaebler's \"reinventing\"), the Minnowbrook arc (I 1968 — relevance, values, equity; II 1988 — leadership in the hollow state; III 2008 — global–comparative–collaborative), and New Public Service (Denhardt — \"serve, not steer\" — citizens, not customers; democratic values over market values). The answer formula (thinker → context → core claim → Indian evidence/critique → contemporary echo) works because each generation supplies the previous one's critique: always stage the argument, never recite the list.

Application cards (one per thinker: claim in 30 words, Indian illustration, one-line critique, contemporary echo — \"Simon's critique returns in NPM dashboards\") convert the canon into deployable ammunition: the thinker-dense Paper 1 answer quotes three thinkers per question minimum, staged as thesis–antithesis–Indian evidence.

8. How Paper 1 Structures Are Won: Budgets, Personnel, Law, and Control

The structures–processes block (units 4–8) is won through institutional logics with Indian illustrations — each topic has a theory-core and a Paper-2 echo, and answers that bridge both score in both papers' currencies. Accountability and control runs the three-branch web: legislative (questions, debates, committees — PAC, Estimates, Public Undertakings — the CAG–PAC loop as the accountability spine; the decline-of-parliamentary-control debate — guillotine, ordinance frequency, the Estimates Committee's \"continuous economy committee\" aspiration), executive (hierarchical supervision, O&M, audit–vigilance, the minister–secretary compact — the T.S.R. Subramanian tenure architecture from GS-4), judicial (judicial review, writs, administrative adjudication — tribunals' post-L. Chandra Kumar position, Rojer Mathew's tribunal-reform directions), and the citizen-side controls (charters, social audit — MGNREGA's Jan Sunwai grammar, CPGRAMS, RTI as the master control). Personnel administration runs recruitment–training–promotion–morale as a lifecycle: the Commission legacy (UPSC's constitutional insulation — Articles 315–323 — versus lateral-entry's 2018 opening salvo and the neutrality debate), career systems (rank-versus-position classification — India's rank bias and its reform proposals), training (foundation–professional–mid-career — LBSNAA's role, the Kiran Aggarwal committee lineage, the Aarambh common-foundation experiment, Mission Karmayogi's 2020 competency architecture — iGOT, FRAC, the capacity-building commission), performance appraisal (the ACR-to-APAR transition and its honesty deficits; 360-degree and outcome-linkage proposals), and pay (Pay Commission lineage — 7th CPC's fitment–allowance architecture — versus performance-pay's NPM promise). Financial administration runs the budget cycle (preparation–enactment–execution–audit) through budget theories: line-item (control-first), performance budgeting (India's 1968 experiment), PPBS (planning-programming-budgeting — Rand's rationalism, India's non-starter), zero-based budgeting (Carter's ZBB — India's 1980s flirtation), gender budgeting (2005–06 statement onwards), outcome budgeting (output–outcome frameworks since 2007–08) — with the Finance Ministry–CAG–PAC triangle and the fiscal-federalism interface (Finance Commissions, the cess-division distortion) as the institutional map.

Administrative law (delegated legislation — necessity, safeguards, the laying procedures, the Committee on Subordinate Legislation; tribunals — rationale, pendency, independence deficits; natural justice — audi alteram partem, bias, speaking orders — the Maneka-inflected Article-14 expansion; administrative discretion and its judicial control — proportionality, legitimate expectation) bridges GS-2 directly. Comparative and development administration (Riggs's prisms and the sala; Ferrel Heady's configurations; development administration as change-administration — Gant, Weidner, Esman; the Indian critique — Pai Panandiker–Kshirsagar on plan-implementation gaps, the \"missing middle\" of district capacity) and organisation theory's applied tail (line–staff–auxiliary, span, delegation–decentralisation, the district collector's span-crisis, e-organisation — e-Office, PFMS, GeM as structural reforms disguised as software) complete the block. Policy sciences (the policy cycle — agenda–formulation–implementation–evaluation; implementation-gap theory — Pressman–Wildavsky's Oakland, Sabatier–Mazmanian's conditions, Lipsky's street-level bureaucracy — the teacher–constable–nurse as the real policymakers; Bardach's implementation game; evaluation — concurrent, terminal, impact — NITI's DMEO as the Indian evaluator) supplies the reform vocabulary Paper 2 consumes: every \"why do schemes fail\" answer is an implementation-gap answer wearing Indian clothes.

One diagram per answer is the block's scoring discipline: the budget-cycle ring, the accountability web, the personnel lifecycle, the policy cycle, the secretariat–directorate–field flow — machinery drawn is machinery understood, and the examiner prices the sketch as comprehension evidence.

9. How Paper 2 Indian Administration Is Won: Machinery, District, and the Reform Ledger

Paper 2's formula (constitutional hook → institutional map → performance record → ARC-referenced reform → case datum) works because Indian administration is constitutionally anchored machinery with a documented reform conversation — the candidate who enters that conversation (naming the ARC report, the committee, the Court direction) writes inside the discipline rather than beside it. Union machinery: the cabinet secretariat (coordination, crisis — the Cabinet Secretary's primus-inter-pares role), the PMO (institutionalised since Shastri–Indira; the power-centre debate — governance efficiency versus cabinet-diffusion), NITI Aayog versus Planning Commission (the 2015 transition — cooperative-competitive federalism, the SDG-index–aspirational-districts toolkit, the \"think-tank not allocator\" settlement and its critics), ministries–departments (allocation of business, DOPT's personnel empire, the secretariat–directorate separation debate — tenure versus specialisation), and regulatory architecture (independent regulators' design tensions — TRAI, SEBI, CCI — capture, capacity, appellate coherence). State machinery: the CM secretariat and chief secretary (coordination under political proximity), the secretariat–field compact, state finance commissions (the Article 243-I promise versus the constitute-and-ignore pattern), and the Governor–CM–bureaucracy triangle in opposition-ruled States (the Sarkaria–Punchhi–Venkatachaliah lineage on gubernatorial discretion).

District and local is Paper 2's gravitational centre. The DM's evolving-role debate (collector–magistrate–development-officer trinity under stress — the \"subordinate judiciary of development\" versus the coordinator-convener model; the 2nd ARC's district-government proposals; the Aspirational Districts Programme's delta-ranking as the new DM scoreboard); the 73rd–74th settlement (three Fs — functions, functionaries, funds — the devolution index's uneven report card; activity mapping's promise; parastatal bypass — DRDA, missions, societies that route around panchayats; the own-revenue crisis — property tax, user charges; district planning committees' Article-243ZD stillbirth; PESA 1996 — the tribal-autonomy charter honoured in breach — Malkangiri-to-Niyamgiri as the case arc; municipal capacity — the JNNURM–AMRUT–Smart Cities lineage, the mayor-in-council weakness, the 74th amendment's unfulfilled corporation); and the e-governance stack as structural reform (DBT–JAM as leakage engineering, PFMS as expenditure visibility, GeM as procurement integrity, e-Office as file-velocity, DigiLocker–API-Setu as paperlessness, faceless assessment as discretion-removal). Personnel-and-reforms (Mission Karmayogi's competency promise; police reform's Prakash Singh non-implementation ledger — tenures, boards, complaints authorities — the most-cited reform failure in the paper; the ARC lineage — 1st ARC's 20 reports, 2nd ARC's 15 — quoted by volume: ethics, e-governance, local governance, personnel, crisis management) and welfare-administration cases (NFSA–PDS leakage curves, Poshan–Samagra Shiksha delivery, Mission Shakti's convergence bet, DM Act 2005's NDMA–SDMA–DDMA pyramid tested by Odisha's cyclone record) supply the case-density Paper 2 demands. The reform ledger (police, district, appraisal, delivery — each with one Court direction, one ARC volume, one live datum) is the maintenance system: four rows, updated fortnightly, deployed everywhere.

10. Scoring Discipline: Thinker-Density, the Commonsense Trap, and Currents Coupling

Public Administration's polarised scoring has a documented cause: the subject's vocabulary overlaps ordinary language (accountability, leadership, reform), so under-prepared answers sound fluent while saying nothing — the commonsense trap. The escape is measured in densities. Thinker-density (Paper 1): every answer names at least three thinkers staged as argument (claim → counter-claim → synthesis or Indian verdict) — the paragraph that says \"accountability matters\" without the CAG–PAC loop, Finer–Friedrich (external versus internal control — the great debate: Friedrich's professionalism against Finer's democratic mastery), and Mosher's \"who guards\" lineage is commonsense in academic dress. Case-density (Paper 2): every answer carries one constitutional hook, one institutional map (drawn), one performance datum, one reform reference — the paragraph that says \"panchayats need funds\" without the three Fs, the State Finance Commission record, and the own-revenue data is a newspaper editorial, not an optional answer. The self-test is brutal and effective: strike every sentence a well-read non-student could have written — what remains is your score.

Currents coupling is the paper's compounding machine: daily newspaper reading with an administrator's eye (every governance story filed under one unit — the police encounter under accountability, the municipal bond under finance, the mission-mode scheme under implementation theory) means Paper 2 preparation happens inside current-affairs time rather than beside it. The coupling artefacts are the reform ledger (above), the case bank (thirty Indian administration cases — Odisha cyclones, Kerala decentralisation, Andhra e-governance, Gujarat water, Tamil Nadu PDS, MP's Ladli-to-DBT, Aspirational Districts turnarounds, the Kuno–Niyamgiri–Malkangiri arc — each one paragraph: context, mechanism, result, lesson), and the quotation discipline (five thinker-lines verbatim — Wilson's \"business,\" Simon's \"proverbs,\" Waldo's morality, Denhardt's \"serve, not steer,\" Ostrom's polycentrism — deployed where wording is the point, paraphrased elsewhere). Working professionals carry the experiential dividend (the filed noting, the chaired meeting, the inspected site as lived evidence) — spend it as illustration inside theoretical answers, never as substitute for theory: experience narrated is anecdote; experience theorised (\"my block office illustrated Lipsky's street-level discretion when…\") is marks.

The twelve-week first pass (Section 6) compounds through the application-card system: every thinker, every structure, every reform gets one card (claim–illustration–critique–echo), and revision is card-drilling plus PYQ-mapping (ten years' papers tagged by unit — the frequently-examined pairings, Simon–Weber, NPM–NPS, DM-role, police-reform, emerge as the must-master set). Second-pass strategy reverses the emphasis: Paper 1 answers re-drill thinker-pairings under time, Paper 2 answers re-drill the formula (hook–map–record–reform–datum) until it runs without thought — because the examination rewards the candidate for whom structure is automatic and judgment is the only variable left.

Trap watch. Wilson (1887) founds with the dichotomy and Waldo (1948) historicises it — administration as moral science. Simon demolishes the principles-as-proverbs (1946) and founds bounded rationality (satisficing) — pair against Weber's ideal type. Barnard's acceptance zone answers Taylor's control; Merton's dysfunctions (goal displacement) qualify Weber. Riggs's prismatic sala (fused–prismatic–refracted) founds comparative-development administration — ecology over universals. Minnowbrook I (1968) is relevance-values-equity, III (2008) global-collaborative; Hood (1991) codifies NPM and Denhardt answers with New Public Service (\"serve, not steer\"). Ostrom's polycentrism (2009 Nobel) answers Leviathan on the commons. Finer–Friedrich is the accountability master-debate (external mastery versus internal professionalism). Pressman–Wildavsky (Oakland) and Lipsky (street-level) found the implementation-gap canon. Prakash Singh (2006) and T.S.R. Subramanian (2013) are the reform ledger's court anchors; the 2nd ARC's volumes (ethics, local governance, citizen-centric, public order, personnel) are its report anchors. The three Fs (functions–functionaries–funds) frame every devolution answer; PESA (1996) is the honoured-in-breach charter. Mission Karmayogi (2020) is the live personnel architecture. Strike every sentence a well-read non-student could write — the remainder is the score.
