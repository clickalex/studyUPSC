# Choosing an Optional Subject — Decision Guide

> The optional (2 papers × 250 = 500 marks) is the single largest scoring block in Mains after GS. This note is the *selection* playbook; per-subject deep notes are a separate expansion track.

## The selection stakes
- 500/1750 Mains-written marks ≈ 29% of merit weight.
- Optional-score spread is wider than GS-spread in most years — a strong optional choice frequently swings final rank lists more than incremental GS effort.
- Time budget reality: optionals absorb 8–10 hours/week of a serious attempt for 6–9 months.

## The 7-factor selection matrix
1. **Graduation overlap** — reduces marginal cost of basics; strongest predictor of sustained comfort.
2. **Interest stamina** — you will re-read the syllabus 3–4 times; fatigue risk is real.
3. **Material ecosystem** — standard books, coaching handouts, evaluated test-series availability, peer community size.
4. **GS overlap** — cheaper preparation where it exists (see comparison table in `bullet-points/`).
5. **Scoring history** — treat "scoring optional" lore cautiously: success rates move with cohort size and marking cycles; verify against recent UPSC annual-report tables rather than rumour.
6. **Syllabus size** — some optionals are famously compact (Anthropology, Philosophy, PSIR core), others' breadth is heavy (History, Geography, Law).
7. **Answer-writing texture** — humanities-essay-style vs diagram/map accuracy vs derivation/case-law precision; pick the texture your hand can sustain for 3 hours × 2 consecutive sessions.

## The practical shortlist method
- Step 1: From the full list, drop every subject you have never opened a book of (unless a strong mentor argues otherwise). Usually leaves 4–7.
- Step 2: Score remaining subjects 1–5 on the seven factors above; total is indicative, not decisive — weight #2 (stamina) and #3 (material) highest.
- Step 2.5: Read one standard book chapter + one PYQ set of your top two candidates over a weekend each; pick the one you *enjoyed re-reading*.
- Step 3: Lock it before prelims results; switching post-Mains qualification window is the classic trap year.

## Signals that you picked right (check after 2 months)
- You can read the optional for 3 hours without clock-checking.
- You connect optional answers to current affairs unprompted (Anthropology→policy stories; Sociology→survey data; PSIR→op-eds; Geography→maps in news).
- Your test-series scores show rising *placement* (percentile), even if marks are modest.

## Signals you may need to switch (check honestly, once)
- Dreading every session after genuine 6-week effort.
- Chronic inability to finish mock in time — texture mismatch.
- Material chaos: three competing book lists and no single standard spine.

## Overlap quick-map
- **Geography** ↔ GS-1 geography, Prelims maps/env **+** essays (climate/urban).
- **Sociology** ↔ GS-1 society, essays; digestible research vocabulary.
- **Anthropology** ↔ compact syllabus; tribal-affairs linkages (GS-2 welfare).
- **PSIR** ↔ GS-2 polity/IR halves sync well; answer-style near-GS.
- **History** ↔ GS-1 modern + world + art-culture; heavy but synergistic.
- **Public Administration** ↔ GS-2 governance core; falling cohort means sparser ecosystem though.
- **Economics** ↔ GS-3 economy; quantitative stamping security.
- **Literature optionals** (Hindi/s regional languages, English) ↔ near-zero GS overlap; dominated by familiarity + writing craft, rewarding for native-depth candidates.
- **Philosophy** ↔ compact; essay-paper resonance; abstract-answer discipline.
- **Law** ↔ jurisprudence-prone candidates with prior legal coursework.
- **Sciences/Engineering** (Physics/Chemistry/Maths/Botany/Zoology/Med Sci/all Engineering branches) ↔ high objectivity + predictable marking for those already trained; minimal GS synergy; ecosystem thin but loyal.

## Timeline archetype
- Pre-prelims: optional 1st pass (syllabus map + one standard spine book + notes frame).
- Post-prelims: 2nd pass + PYQ-resolution + test series (6–8 full tests across the 2 papers).
- Between Mains papers: PYQ-pattern flash revision only; no new source material.
