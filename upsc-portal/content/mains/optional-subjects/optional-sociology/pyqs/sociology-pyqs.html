Sociology — PYQ Patterns & Approaches

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Each with a model approach.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. "Science is a social institution." Examine with reference to the sociology-of-science debate. (10 marks)

Approach: define scientific ethos (Merton's CUDOS norms) → counter-readings (Kuhn's paradigms, social construction) → example: funding & priority disputes → conclude: science is socially embedded, not socially determined.

Q2. Compare Durkheim and Weber on the nature of social facts and social action. (15 marks)

Approach: Durkheim — external, coercive, sui generis (suicide rates as evidence) vs Weber — meaningful action, verstehen, ideal types → methodological implications → bridge via contemporary mixed methods.

Q3. Explain Merton's modes of adaptation with Indian illustrations. (10 marks)

Approach: anomie + goals/means table (conformity → rebellion) → illustrations: exam-cheating innovation, ritualism in offices, rebellion in movements → assessment of model's universality.

Q4. Critically evaluate Weber's bureaucracy in the light of new public management. (15 marks)

Approach: ideal-type features (hierarchy, rules, impersonality, careers) → strengths (predictability, equality of treatment) → NPM critiques (flexibility, targets, customer-orientation) → Indian hybrid reality (Mission Karmayogi line) → conclusion: iron cage softened, not broken.

Q5. Distinguish sex from gender; show how gender is socially constructed. (10 marks)

Approach: biological vs social categories → construction mechanisms (socialisation, institutions, language) → evidence: occupational segregation, care-work asymmetry (NFHS/time-use data) → change levers.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. Analyse Sanskritization as a process of social change; assess its limits. (10 marks)

Approach: Srinivas' definition + Rampura evidence → mechanisms (emulation, mobility paths) → critiques (reinforces hierarchy, ignores power/economy, female-ethics blind spot — Srinivas' own note) → contemporary near-equivalents (dominant-caste style status quests).

Q7. "The village was never the idyll tradition describes." Discuss with reference to village studies. (15 marks)

Approach: canon summaries (Srinivas — dominant caste; Beteille — caste/class/tripartite stratification; Dube — faction networks) → colonial & market penetrations of "traditional" village → agrarian change evidence (landless shares, migration) → conclude: continuity of hierarchy, change of idiom.

Q8. Evaluate the women's movement's trajectories — from reform-era campaigns to digital feminism. (15 marks)

Approach: phases (19th-c reform → post-1975 autonomous groups → law-led phase (Dowry/PCMA/Vishaka → 2013 Act) → #MeToo digital turn) → social bases widening → assessment: legislative wins vs implementation & intersectional gaps.

Q9. Examine the relationship between migration and urban informal work. (10 marks)

Approach: circular migration patterns → informality as entry mode (PLFS share) → lacks: portability, housing (e-Shram/OSAY framing) → sociological reading: chain migration, caste networks in urban labour markets.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers to units; Paper 1 hot nodes: thinkers, stratification, methods; Paper 2 hot nodes: caste change, movements, family, urbanisation.
  • Weekly: 4 answers (2 per paper) with mandatory Indian-study + datum checks; peer-grade for vocabulary use.
