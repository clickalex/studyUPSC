History — PYQ Patterns & Approaches

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Model approaches included.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. "The Harappan decline is a palimpsest of environmental, hydrological and social readings." Discuss. (15 marks)

Approach: decline-theory ledger — Ghaggar-Hakra desiccation, monsoon weakening (palaeoclimate proxies), river shifts, trade-network disruption, elite-crisis readings → caution: no single-cause verdict → de-urbanisation into late-Harappan cultures → conclude: layered transformation, not annihilation.

Q2. Evaluate the Mauryan state through Kautilyan and Ashokan lenses. (15 marks)

Approach: Kautilya — centralised treasury-army-spy architecture (saptanga frame) vs Ashokan inscriptions — dhamma as welfare-morality state, provincial viceroys → tensions: coercive-administrative vs ethical-integrative → legacy readings (Thapar's synthesis) → map: pillar/rock-edict spread.

Q3. "The feudalism debate is about the state as much as about land." Examine. (10 marks)

Approach: Sharma's feudalism-from-above/below (land grants, decentralisation, peasantry subordination) → Mukhia's critique (Indian exception: no serfdom, mobility evidence) → state form: samanta hierarchy → verdict: debate refined into early-medieval state-formation frames (integrative polity model).

Q4. Assess the Chola local-self-government evidence and its historiography. (10 marks)

Approach: Uttaramerur inscription — sabha committees (kudavolai system) → qualifications/disqualifications → historiographic caution: elite-brahmadeya skew; "self-government" romanticism vs state-integration readings → credit with caveats.

Q5. Explain the mansab-jagir system's crisis in the 17th century. (10 marks)

Approach: design (zat-sawar ranks, escheat logic) → pressures: be-jagir crisis, price inflation (Abul Fazl vs 17th-c indices), succession wars → consequences: agrarian strain, Deccan campaigns → debate link: Mughal decline theses (satellite-states vs crisis readings).

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. "The Permanent Settlement froze agriculture and unlocked property." Analyse. (10 marks)

Approach: design (Cornwallis, zamindar-as-collector) → effects: absconding auctions, subinfeudation, rent-receiver economy → agrarian stagnation vs secure-property claim tested (productivity data) → comparison: ryotwari's state-facing ryot → long shadow: land-reform era targets.

Q7. Evaluate the drain theory's analytical power and political work. (10 marks)

Approach: Naoroji's mechanism (home charges, council-bill remittances, pensions/interest) → analytical assessment (partial: ignores capital inflows; strong: effective-demand loss) → political work: Congress economic critique foundation, moderate-mass bridge → legacy in nationalist fiscal thought.

Q8. "Civil Disobedience was a designed escalation with designed suspension." Discuss with historiographical notes. (15 marks)

Approach: sequence: Purna Swaraj pledge → Dandi choreography → spread (forest laws, north-western front) → Gandhi-Irwin suspension logic → readings: Cambridge (pressure-bargain), nationalist (stage-mastery), subaltern (local militancy exceeding script) → verdict: dialectic of control and spontaneity.

Q9. Compare decolonisation's routes in Africa and Asia with one case each. (10 marks)

Approach: frames: settler-vs-non-settler colonies, Cold War timing → India: negotiated transfer with mass-movement leverage → Ghana: Nkrumah's convention politics + pan-African context → Algeria/Angola contrast (settler wars) → conclude: metropolitan cost calculus + local mobilisation decide.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: P1 hot nodes — Harappan/Mauryan/Gupta, feudalism, Mughal institutions; P2 — settlements, Gandhi phases, world revolutions.
  • Monthly: one debate-card revision cycle + 2 map practices; weekly: 3 answers with mandatory historiography line.
