Economics — PYQ Patterns & Approaches

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Model approaches included.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. Decompose the price effect into income and substitution effects (Hicksian or Slutskian — state your choice). (10 marks)

Approach: compensated-variation construction → Slutsky equation sign logic (normal vs inferior vs Giffen) → labelled indifference-map with auxiliary budget line → one-line relevance: Giffen evidence debates.

Q2. Compare Cournot and Stackelberg duopoly outcomes. (10 marks)

Approach: simultaneous-quantity vs first-mover games → reaction-function diagram → outputs/prices ranking (Stackelberg leader gains, industry output higher than Cournot, lower than competition) → welfare note.

Q3. "An economy cannot simultaneously fix the exchange rate, allow free capital flows and run independent monetary policy." Elaborate. (15 marks)

Approach: Mundell-Fleming trilemma → corner vs middle-ground solutions → India's managed-float + partial-capital-controls position → reserve-accumulation logic → diagram: triangle vertices.

Q4. Contrast Solowian and endogenous growth models on the role of policy. (15 marks)

Approach: Solow — exogenous technology, convergence, policy affects levels not steady-state growth → Romer — ideas' non-rivalry, R&D externalities, policy shifts long-run growth → evidence sketch: persistent growth differentials → India hook: PLI/R&D-mission framing.

Q5. Explain the Ramsey rule of efficient taxation and its equity tension. (10 marks)

Approach: inverse-elasticity logic (tax inelastic bases more) → efficiency defence → equity critique (basics are inelastic) → resolution paths: differentiated GST architecture, Pigouvian layer.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. "1991 was a balance-of-payments crisis that became a policy philosophy." Trace the reform sequence. (15 marks)

Approach: crisis anatomy (CAD, reserves cover, gold airlift episode) → stabilisation (fiscal correction, devaluation) vs structural reform (delicensing, trade, financial — Narasimham, FEMA path) → sequencing debates → unfinished-agenda ledger (factor markets, SOEs).

Q7. Evaluate inflation targeting as India's monetary framework. (10 marks)

Approach: MPC design (4% ±2 band, CPI headline) → record: anchoring gains, supply-shock tensions (food-weighted CPI) → debates: band adequacy, core-vs-headline, growth-trade-off → way-ahead: review levers (house-price inclusion? keep general: index refinements, communication).

Q8. Discuss MSP-APMC reforms through an economics lens — efficiency versus procurement-security trade-offs. (15 marks)

Approach: MSP's open-ended procurement geography concentration (paddy-rice belts) → water-table externality + crop-mix distortion → APMC frictions and market-fee wedge → reform options: price-deficiency payments, warehouse-based (PM-AASHA line), private-market competition with farmer-safeguards → federal politics acknowledged.

Q9. Analyse the IBC's incentive effects on credit culture. (10 marks)

Approach: design shift: debtor-in-possession → creditor-in-control, time-boxed resolution → record: recovery-architecture gains; haircuts & tribunal-capacity constraints → behavioural effect: pre-IBC discipline (market pricing of default risk) → completion items: cross-border insolvency, DFI? — keep: resolution-professional ecosystem.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: P1 hot nodes — Slutsky, market structures, trilemma, Solow-Romer, Ramsey; P2 — 1991 lineage, MPC, GST/IBC, agri-reforms, federal finance.
  • Weekly: 2 graph-complete answers + 1 derivation; refresh Survey/RBI figures each cycle.
