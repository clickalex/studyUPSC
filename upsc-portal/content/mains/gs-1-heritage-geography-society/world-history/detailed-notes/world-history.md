# World History — Detailed Notes

> **Syllabus slot:** GS 1 (Mains) · World History — Industrial Revolution, World Wars, colonisation & decolonisation, political philosophies (communism, capitalism, socialism) and their effects
> **Note:** World History is **Mains-only** (not in Prelims syllabus explicitly) — orient for quality, not trivia.

## 1. Industrial Revolution — the hinge of modernity

### 1.1 Why Britain first (c. 1760–1840)
- **Agricultural revolution** (enclosure, new crops) freed labour.
- **Empire & mercantilist income**: slave-trade & colonial revenue feeding capital accumulation.
- **Institutions**: property rights, patent system, joint-stock banking.
- **Coal + iron geography**; island insulation from Continental wars.
- *Tech cluster*: **Spinning Jenny (Hargreaves 1764)**, **water frame (Arkwright)**, **mule (Crompton 1779)**, **steam engine (Newcomen → Watt 1769/76)**; railways: Stephenson's *Rocket* (1829), Manchester-Liverpool (1830).

### 1.2 Consequences
- Urbanisation & factory discipline (Friedrich Engels' *Manchester* observations).
- New classes: industrial bourgeoisie and proletariat; Luddites (1811–16) as machine-breaking protest.
- Imperial redirection: colonies turned into captive markets & raw-material appendages (deindustrialisation of India — Dhaka muslin case study).
- Second Industrial Revolution (steel, chemicals, electricity, internal combustion, 1870–1914) → mass production (Taylorism, Fordism).

## 2. Political philosophies — the ideological triad

| Ideology | Core claim | Key thinkers | Effects on society |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Liberalism** | individual rights, limited government, markets | Locke; JS Mill; Smith | revolutions of 1688/1776/1789; constitutionalism |
| **Capitalism** | private ownership + market allocation | Smith, Ricardo | industrial growth; inequality & crisis cycles |
| **Socialism** | collective/social provision vs exploitation | Owen; Saint-Simon; Fourier | welfare states, labour laws, cooperatives |
| **Marxism-Leninism** | class struggle; party-led revolution | Marx & Engels (1848 Manifesto); Lenin | 1917 Bolshevik state; planned economies |
| **Fascism/Nazism** | nation/race supremacy; anti-liberal, anti-communist | Mussolini; Hitler | totalitarianism, WWII, Holocaust |
| **Keynesian social democracy** | managed capitalism + welfare | Keynes; Beveridge | post-1945 settlements, NHS, welfare states |

## 3. Colonisation & decolonisation

### 3.1 Mechanisms of colonisation
- **Mercantilist phase** (companies; 17–18c.) → **free-trade imperialism** (19c.) → **New Imperialism** & scramble (Berlin 1884–85).
- Tools: unequal treaties (China), gunboat diplomacy, settler colonies, indirect rule.
- Extraction logic in India: drain-of-wealth analyses (Dadabhai Naoroji).

### 3.2 Decolonisation waves
- Post-WWII Asia: **India–Pakistan 1947**, Indonesia 1945, Vietnam 1954 (Dien Bien Phu), Philippines 1946.
- *Bandung 1955*: Afro-Asian solidarity; NAM (Belgrade 1961) with Nehru-Tito-Nasser-Sukarno-Nkrumah.
- Africa: **Ghana 1957 (first sub-Saharan)**, the "Year of Africa" 1960, Algeria 1962 after brutal war, Portuguese colonies 1974–75 (Angola, Mozambique, Guinea-Bissau), **Zimbabwe 1980**, **Namibia 1990**, **South Africa 1994** (end of apartheid).
- Peaceful vs armed paths — Gandhi's civil resistance model vs Algeria/Vietnam liberation wars; contextual causation.

## 4. World War I & II — causes, course contours, consequences

### WWI (1914–18)
- Causes via **MANIA** mnemonic: Militarism, Alliances (Triple Alliance/Entente), Nationalism, Imperialism, Assassination (Sarajevo spark, Franz Ferdinand 28 June 1914).
- Schlieffen plan → trench stalemate (Verdun, Somme 1916); new weapons (tank, gas, submarine).
- US entry 1917 (unrestricted submarine warfare, Zimmermann telegram); Russian exit (Brest-Litovsk 1918 after Bolshevik revolution).
- **Treaty of Versailles 1919**: war-guilt Article 231, reparations, League of Nations; redrawing of Mittel-Europa → grievance inflation.

### Interwar capitalism crisis
- 1929 Great Depression → Keynesian reflation ideas; rise of aggressive regimes; Spanish Civil War (1936–39) as lab bench.

### WWII (1939–45)
- Causes: Versailles dissatisfaction, appeasement (Munich 1938), Lebensraum ideology, system failures of the League.
- Turning points: Stalingrad (1942–43), Midway (1942), El Alamein (1942); D-Day Normandy (6 June 1944, Operation Overlord).
- Pacific: Pearl Harbor (7 Dec 1941) → atomic strikes Hiroshima (6 Aug 1945) & Nagasaki (9 Aug 1945) → surrender.
- Consequences: 50–85 mn deaths; Holocaust & Nuremberg; **UN 1945**; Cold War bipolar geometry; European integration seed (Schuman 1950).

## 5. Cold War & its end

- Containment (Truman Doctrine 1947, Marshall Plan), NATO (1949) vs Warsaw Pact (1955); proxy wars (Korea 1950–53, Vietnam 1955–75, Afghanistan 1979–89); Cuban Missile Crisis (1962), détente (SALT I/II).
- **Endgame**: Gorbachev's glasnost & perestroika (1985+), Eastern European revolutions of 1989 (Berlin Wall: 9 Nov 1989), USSR dissolution (25 Dec 1991); unipolar moment & post-9/11 reordering.

## 6. 21st-century continuations
- US "unipolarity" 1991–2000s → multipolar contestation: China's rise (BRI, 2013), Russian revisionism (2014 Crimea; **2022 Ukraine war**; 2025 diplomatic thaw still unsettled), Global South non-alignment 2.0, de-dollarisation narratives, Gaza conflicts (2023–25) reshaping the UN/ICJ terrain.
- Effects on India: strategic autonomy, QUAD, BRICS expansion (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran/…), G20 2023 summit legacy of African Union's full membership.
