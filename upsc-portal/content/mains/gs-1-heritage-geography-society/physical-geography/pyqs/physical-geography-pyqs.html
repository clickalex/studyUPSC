UPSC Prelims PYQs

Q12015

In the structure of planet Earth, below the mantle, the core is mainly made up of which one of the following?

  1. (a)Aluminium
  2. (b)Chromium
  3. (c)Iron
  4. (d)Silicon
Show answer
Correct: (c)the core is iron-nickel; the crustal rocks are dominated by silica, aluminium (SIAL) and magnesium (SIMA).
Q22016

With reference to the ocean currents, consider the following:

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. The Gulf Stream is a warm current.
  2. The Oyashio (Kurile) is a cold current.
  3. The Benguela is a warm current.
  1. (a)1 and 2 only
  2. (b)2 and 3 only
  3. (c)1 and 3 only
  4. (d)1, 2 and 3
Show answer
Correct: (a)the Benguela is a cold current off SW Africa (the companion fact: it underlies the Namib's aridity).
Q32013

Which one of the following pairs correctly contains the phenomenon and the region of maximum impact?

  1. (a)El Niño — Indian Monsoon
  2. (b)La Niña — Horn of Africa
  3. (c)Southern Oscillation — Australian Alps
Show answer
Correct: (a)the classic teleconnection: El Niño years tend to weaken the Indian monsoon (positive IOD can offset — as in some recent seasons).
Q42020

  1. The Himalayas formed at a convergent plate boundary.
  2. The East African Rift is a divergent boundary.
  3. The San Andreas Fault is a transform boundary.
  1. (a)1 only
  2. (b)1 and 2 only
  3. (c)2 and 3 only
  4. (d)1, 2 and 3
Show answer
Correct: (d)the three boundary types with their exemplary landforms.
Q52021

Which one of the following is a reason for the Red Sea being a highly saline water body?

  1. (a)High rate of evaporation and low inflow of fresh water
  2. (b)Submarine volcanism releasing dissolved minerals in excess
  3. (c)The presence of salt flats along its coast
  4. (d)Deep-ocean circulation pumping saline water northward
Show answer
Correct: (a)the Red Sea combines intense evaporation with negligible river inflow and limited exchange with the Indian Ocean.

Q72022

With reference to tropical cyclones in the Bay of Bengal, consider the following statements:

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?

  1. They derive energy from the warm waters of the Bay (sea-surface temperatures above ~26.5 °C).
  2. They typically have a warm core and an eye.
  3. They are steered mainly by the polar-front jet.
  1. (a)1 and 2 only
  2. (b)2 and 3 only
  3. (c)1 and 3 only
  4. (d)1, 2 and 3

UPSC Mains PYQs

Q2013

"

  1. (a)What do you understand by the phenomenon of 'temperature inversion' in meteorology?
  2. (b)How does it affect weather and habitants of the place?" (10 marks)
Model answer outline

Model answer skeleton

Introduction — The normal tropospheric rule is a lapse rate (~6.5 °C/km up); temperature inversion is a reversal — temperature rises with altitude over a local layer.

Body

  1. Types — ground inversion (clear, calm winter nights over plains — NW India's winter fog chain), subsidence inversion (subtropical highs), frontal inversion (warm/cold air boundaries), advection inversion.
  2. Effects on weather — traps moisture & pollutants (haze/fog episodes), stabilises the layer (stratified, discourages convection — suppresses ordinary afternoon showers), enables frost pockets in valleys (crop damage), and where lifted, can channel smog plumes.
  3. Effects on habitants — Delhi/Indo-Gangetic winter smog (vehicle + stubble + inversion) — public-health spikes (PM 2.5 at hazardous levels), transport disruption (fog), crop stress, and the pattern it creates for winter-crop moisture distribution.

Conclusion — An apparently 'meteorological technicality' turns out to order pollution, safety and agricultural risk in Indian winters.

Q2015

"The Himalayas are highly prone to landslides." Discuss the causes and suggest suitable measures of mitigation. (12.5 marks)

Model answer outline

Model answer skeleton

Introduction — The Himalayas are a young fold-mountain range on an active convergent boundary — structurally fragile, tectonically alive.

Body

  1. Natural causes — fragile rock strata (young sedimentary folds), seismicity (the entire arc in seismic zone IV–V), steep slopes, intense monsoon rain (the western disturbance + monsoon coupling), glacial retreat creating unstable moraine and glacial lakes (GLOF risk — e.g., 2023 Sikkim event).
  2. Anthropogenic causes — road widening and slope cutting without benching, deforestation, unregulated construction in the pilgrimage corridors, hydel projects' tunnelling/blasting, quarrying.
  3. Climate amplifier — more intense rain bands and cloudburst-type episodes (Kedarnath 2013 lessons).
  4. Mitigation — slope-stabilisation (retaining walls, berms, bioengineering with vetiver & local shrubs), land-use regulation (building bye-laws localised to slope), early-warning rain-gauge networks + ISRO/Bhuvan landslide susceptibility maps, disaster-resilient road engineering (cut-and-cover tunnels vs open cuts), catchment reforestation.

Conclusion — Landslides in the Himalaya are a system problem — geology + climate + unplanned development; mitigation must be engineering (slope works) + governance (zoning) + research (susceptibility mapping).
