Physical Geography: Geomorphology, Climatology, Oceanography & Biogeography — Detailed Notes

Syllabus slot: GS 1 (Mains) · Physical Geography: Geomorphology, Climatology, Oceanography Also relevant for: Prelims GS Paper I — Geography · Environment & Ecology

PART A — GEOMORPHOLOGY: Earth's Interior & Landforms

  • Waves as the probe: P (primary, fastest, travel through all states) vs S (secondary, solids only — so S-wave shadow zone proves a liquid outer core) vs L/R (surface Love & Rayleigh — most destructive in earthquakes).
  • Seismic focus/epicentre, isoseists, Richter vs Moment-Magnitude (Mw): Richter is an amplitude scale; modern practice uses Mw (energy-based).

2. Continental Drift → Plate Tectonics

  • Alfred Wegener (1912) "Origin of Continents and Oceans" — drift hypothesis; evidence: jigsaw fit of Africa–South America coasts, fossil correspondence (Mesosaurus Brazil–West Africa), coal in Antarctica, glacial striations (India, Australia, Africa — the Gondwana argument), rocks of the same age across the Atlantic.
  • Why drift failed in his time: no mechanism.
  • Mechanism found 1960s: sea-floor spreading (Harry Hess, 1962 — mid-ocean ridges as creation zones), paleomagnetism (Vine–Matthews' stripes), subduction zones; McKenzie & Parker (1967) formalised plate tectonics.

3. Plate boundaries — the classification every map answer needs

TypeProcessLandformExample
Divergent (constructive)Plates separate; magma upwellsMid-ocean ridges, rift valleysMid-Atlantic Ridge; East African Rift (Red Sea is a young ocean basin)
Convergent (destructive)Collision/subductionFold mountains (continent–continent), island arcs + trenches (ocean–ocean), Andes-type (ocean–continent)Himalayas (Indo-Australian vs Eurasian); Andes (Nazca subducting South America); Japan arc
Transform (conservative)Lateral slideFault zones, no new crustSan Andreas Fault (Pacific vs North American)
  • Seven major plates — Eurasian, North American, South American, African, Indo-Australian (Indian + Australian), Pacific, Antarctic (+ minors: Nazca, Cocos, Philippine, Arabian, Juan de Fuca).
  • Hotspots — mantle plumes punching through plates: Hawaiian–Emperor chain, Deccan Traps (formed ~66 Ma when India passed over the Réunion hotspot — associated with the K–Pg extinction context; the Siberian Traps for the Permian).

4. Landforms & rock cycle

  • Rocks: Igneous (primary — granite intrusive, basalt extrusive; Deccan = basalt), Sedimentary (layered, fossils; sandstone, shale, limestone; India's coal and petroleum occur here), Metamorphic (heat/pressure: gneiss from granite, marble from limestone, slate from shale).
  • Exogenous processes: weathering (mechanical: frost action, exfoliation; chemical: oxidation, carbonation — limestone karst), mass wasting, erosion-transport-deposition by rivers/glaciers/wind/waves.
  • Selected landforms: river meander-oxbow-delta-levee system
  • U &
  • V-shaped valleys
  • hanging valley
  • moraines (glacial)
  • barchans/loess (aeolian)
  • karst stalactites/stalagmites
  • estuary vs delta (Narmada/Tapti estuaries; Ganga delta).

5. Earthquakes & tsunamis (India-relevant)

  • India seismic zones II–V (BIS 1893; Zone V = highest risk: entire NE, Kashmir, Kutch).
  • Great events for answers: 1905 Kangra, 1934 Bihar–Nepal, 1950 Assam, 2001 Bhuj (Zone V), 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami (Sumatra subduction — thrust India's east coast/Andamans; ODA/WC established INCOIS ocean-chemistry/ tsunami warning), 2015 Nepal (Gorkha).
  • Tsunami generation: submarine megathrust earthquakes
  • shallow-water amplification
  • INCOIS (Hyderabad) is India's warning centre
  • UNESCO's Tsunami Ready covers several Indian coastal communities.

PART B — CLIMATOLOGY: Atmosphere, Monsoon & Cyclones

1. Atmosphere — structure & composition

  • Composition: N₂ 78%, O₂ 21%, argon 0.93%, CO₂ ~0.04% (crossed 420 ppm in the 2020s), H₂O vapour (variable — the weather gas).
  • Layers (bottom-up): Troposphere (weather here; lapse rate 6.5°C/km; tropopause ~18 km equator, 8 km poles)
  • Stratosphere (ozone layer ~20–40 km absorbs UV — warms with altitude, hence temperature inversion)
  • Mesosphere (coldest)
  • Thermosphere/Ionosphere (auroras; radio reflection)
  • Exosphere.

2. Heat & pressure engine

  • Insolation & heat budget: ~51% of solar radiation absorbed by the surface; greenhouse gases trap long-wave back-radiation.
  • Pressure belts: Equatorial Low (ITCZ), Subtropical Highs (~30°N/S — horse latitudes; trade winds originate), Subpolar Lows (~60°N/S), Polar Highs; winds: trade winds, westerlies, polar easterlies — deflected by Coriolis (Ferrel's law: right in N-hemisphere, left in S-hemisphere).
  • Jet streams: upper-tropospheric westerly jets (STJ & PFJ) steer mid-latitude weather; the Tropical Easterly Jet over India helps drive the monsoon's rainfall bursts.

3. The Indian Monsoon — mechanism (Mains-worthy depth)

  1. ITCZ migration — the ITCZ shifts north in summer over the Indo-Gangetic plains; the low-pressure cell over NW India (Rajasthan/Pakistan) draws moisture-laden SW winds.
  2. Differential heating — land (fast) vs Indian Ocean (slow); pressure gradient drives the cross-equatorial SE trades that curve to SW over India (Coriolis).
  3. Jet stream choreography — the sub-tropical westerly jet retreats north of the Himalayas while the Tropical Easterly Jet (TEJ) sets in overhead — the upper-air pattern that 'unlocks' monsoon.
  4. Tibetan Plateau as an elevated heat engine reinforces the pressure pattern.
  5. El Niño & Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD): El Niño = (generally) weakened monsoon; positive IOD can compensate (as in recent El-Niño years when the monsoon still delivered).
  6. Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO) — 30–60-day intra-seasonal pulse that makes the "active/break" alternation within the season.
  • Pattern facts: monsoon onset over Kerala ~1 June (± a week)
  • ~75% of annual rain June–September
  • highest rainfall on Earth — Mawsynram (Meghalaya) (Cherrapunji the historic second)
  • Western Ghats' orographic wall makes the west coast wet (windward) and the Deccan rain-shadowed
  • the NE monsoon (retreating monsoon) rains Tamil Nadu's coast (Oct–Dec — Chennai's main season)
  • northwesterly "western disturbances" bring winter rain/snow to NW India &
  • are vital for rabi (wheat).

5. Climate classification (Köppen quick code)

  • A tropical (Af equatorial, Am monsoon, Aw savanna), B arid (BWh hot desert — Thar; BSk steppe), C temperate mesothermal (Cwa monsoon-with-dry-winter; much of India = Amw/Cwg mix), D continental, E polar.
  • India's Tropic of Cancer split — tropical South, sub-tropical North.
  • Micro-climates/variability: western disturbances; ENSO-IOD interaction; winter inversion fog over Ganga plains.

6. Climate-change signals in the physical system (gate way to GS-3 environment)

  • Jet-stream meandering
  • extreme events
  • Arabian Sea warming
  • more intense cyclones (research consensus trend)
  • Himalayan cryosphere loss (glacier-lake outburst floods — e.g., the 2023 South Lhonak GLOF, Sikkim)
  • IMD's Mission Mausam (approved 2024) to upgrade forecasting &
  • research weather modification.

PART C — OCEANOGRAPHY

1. Ocean-floor relief (depth order, from the coast)

  • Continental shelf (shallow, resource-rich — Mumbai High ONGC field), continental slope, continental rise, abyssal plains (deep), mid-ocean ridges, trenches (deepest = Mariana/Challenger Deep ~10,994 m), seamounts/guyots, and submarine canyons.

2. Temperature & salinity

  • Temperature decreases with depth; thermocline is the steep gradient zone (latitude-dependent).
  • Salinity (34–37 ‰ on average; 35 ‰ the reference): Red Sea among the highest (~40 ‰ — evaporation > precipitation + no rivers); Baltic Sea among the lowest (river supply + ice melt); Bay of Bengal lower than the Arabian Sea because of river inflow (Ganga–Brahmaputra).

  • Why cold currents on west coasts of continents = coastal deserts: cold water stabilises air → no convection → little rain (Atacama, Namib, Kalahari-edge, Baja, Western Australia).
  • Warm + cold current meeting zones = world fisheries (Grand Banks — Newfoundland; North Sea; Japan coast — Kuroshio+Oyashio).
  • El Niño: Peruvian upwelling weakened → warm surface water eastward → global teleconnection incl. weakened Indian monsoon odds; La Niña = opposite. ENSO + IOD jointly script India's rainfall.

4. Tides & India's coast

  • Spring tides — new/full moon (sun-moon aligned); neap — quarters.
  • Regions of high tidal range: Gulf of Khambhat (Cambay) &
  • Gulf of Kutch (funnels)
  • embayment shape amplification
  • Hooghly bore.
  • India's ports chain and the Mumbai High oilfield on the shelf; deep-ocean mission (Deep Ocean Mission 2021) on polymetallic nodules of the Central Indian Ocean Basin; blue-economy policy field.

PART D — BIOGEOGRAPHY (soils & natural vegetation)

Soils

  • Soil formation factors: parent rock, climate, organisms, relief, time (the five CLORPT items).
  • India's soil palette: alluvial (Indo-Gangetic — agriculture base; khadar vs bhangar), black/regur (Deccan basalt — cotton soil; self-mulching), red & laterite (old crystalline + leached; laterite in Kerala/Karnataka), desert, mountain/forest, saline-alkaline (usar), peaty/marshy.
  • Soil degradation: water erosion (gullies → Chambal badlands), wind erosion (Thar), salinisation from canal irrigation's waterlogging (the old Punjab case).

Natural vegetation & biomes

  • Tropical rainforests (Western Ghats, Andaman & Nicobar)
  • moist deciduous (teak/sal belts)
  • dry deciduous
  • thorn forests (NW dry zone)
  • montane Himalayan belts (temperate → alpine → tundra-tundra-transition)
  • mangroves — Sundarbans (world's largest mangrove delta, home of the Royal Bengal Tiger; salinity-tolerant pneumatophores).
  • India's four biodiversity hotspots (Western Ghats, Himalayas, Indo-Burma (NE), Sundaland (incl. Nicobar)); biogeographic zones (10) per Rodgers & Panwar classification for protected areas.
  • Fire-adaptive and invasive issues: _lantana_, _prosopis juliflora_ invasives; shifting monsoon + heat pushing treelines/biomes.

PART E — Drainage as applied geomorphology: the Himalayan–Peninsular contrast

The trunk covers landforms but leaves drainage implicit; the examiner treats river systems as geomorphology you can draw, so this section makes the Himalayan–Peninsular contrast answer-ready. Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra systems) are antecedent — older than the mountains they cut through, which is why the Indus, Sutlej, and Brahmaputra (Tsangpo) slice gorges clean across the rising range; perennial (snow + rain fed); long courses with youthful upper gorges, mature middle meanders, and old-age deltas; massive sediment loads building the Indo-Gangetic plain. Peninsular rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri east-flowing; Narmada, Tapti west-flowing) are consequent/superimposed on an old shield — seasonal, shorter, graded profiles, shallow valleys; the east-flowers build deltas (Godavari–Krishna–Kaveri deltas) while the Narmada–Tapti flow through rift valleys and form estuaries, not deltas — the Prelims trap the trunk already flags.

Three drainage patterns complete the diagram kit: dendritic (tree-like, on uniform rock — most of the Gangetic plain), trellis (folded terrain — Siwalik foothills), radial/centripetal (domes/basins — Amarkantak radial, Loktak centripetal), plus antecedent vs superimposed as the process pair (Subansiri antecedent in the Himalaya; Damodar–Subarnarekha superimposed on the Chotanagpur shield). The Mains move is to fuse drainage with the trunk's tectonics: the Himalaya exist because the Indo-Australian plate subducts under Eurasia (§3 convergent), so Himalayan rivers are young, restless, and flood-prone; the Peninsula is a stable Gondwana fragment, so its rivers are old, seasonal, and dam-friendly. One line settles "compare" questions: Himalayan rivers make land (plains, deltas); Peninsular rivers reveal land (they expose the shield they drain).

PART F — Monsoon anomalies and extreme weather: how to reason, not recite

The trunk's six-step monsoon mechanism (ITCZ migration, differential heating, jet choreography with STJ retreat + TEJ onset, Tibetan heat engine, ENSO–IOD, MJO active/break pulse) plus the pattern facts (Kerala onset ~1 June, 75% June–September, Mawsynram, Western Ghats orographic wall vs Deccan rain shadow, retreating-monsoon Tamil Nadu rains Oct–Dec, western disturbances for rabi) is the complete theory; what fails in Mains is application when the monsoon misbehaves. Learn four anomaly grammars. (i) Break monsoon — the trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills: plains bake, foothills flood (classic July-break pattern); MJO phase + suppressed convection explains it. (ii) El Niño vs positive IOD tug-of-war — El Niño weakens the Walker circulation's Indian arm (descending air over India), but a positive IOD (warm western Indian Ocean) pumps moisture into the Arabian Sea branch; recent El Niño years with near-normal rain are IOD rescues — always cite both, never one. (iii) Cloudbursts and mini-cloudburst clusters — orographic locking + warm-cloud moisture convergence over steep Himalayan catchments (Uttarakhand 2013, Himachal 2023 events as type-cases); warming raises the moisture ceiling (Clausius–Clapeyron ~7%/°C), so extremes intensify even where totals don't. (iv) Cyclone geography shift — Arabian Sea warming raises intense-cyclone odds (research consensus the trunk notes); Bay remains frequency-dominant (Odisha 1999, Phailin 2013, Amphan 2020, Dana/Remal 2024) but the Arabian Sea's rising share is the climate-change exhibit.

  • The heat engine behind the anomalies is one paragraph: insolation
  • pressure belts (ITCZ, subtropical highs at ~30°, subpolar lows)
  • trades/westerlies/polar easterlies deflected by Coriolis (Ferrel's law)
  • jets steering weather. Every extreme-weather answer should open by locating itself in this engine, then name the anomaly grammar, then cite IMD evidence (Mission Mausam 2024 upgrades, MJO monitoring). Closing synthesis: the monsoon is not failing, it is redistributing — fewer rainy days, heavier downpours, longer breaks; the policy question is storage and drainage, not just averages.

PART G — Ocean–climate coupling and the diagram method

The trunk's oceanography (floor relief, thermocline, salinity contrasts — Red Sea ~40‰, Baltic low, Bay lower than Arabian Sea; the 12-current table; tides with Khambhat/Kutch funnels and the Hooghly bore) and its teleconnection line (ENSO + IOD jointly scripting Indian rainfall) converge on one Mains skill: explaining land weather from ocean physics. Master three couplings. ENSO: normal years see Peruvian upwelling + warm pool near Indonesia driving the Walker cell's rising arm over the maritime continent; El Niño flattens the Pacific gradient, the rising arm migrates east, and anomalous subsidence sits over India — hence weakened-monsoon odds; La Niña amplifies the normal pattern. IOD: positive phase (warm west, cool east) strengthens the Somali-jet cross-equatorial flow and the Arabian Sea moisture feed — India's independent booster. MJO: the 30–60-day eastward pulse whose Indian-Ocean phase triggers active spells and whose Pacific phase deepens breaks. The decision table for answers: El Niño + negative IOD = drought watch; La Niña + positive IOD = flood watch; El Niño + positive IOD = the compensated year (cite the recent rescues).

End with the diagram method that converts this lesson into marks: (1) every geomorphology answer gets a cross-section (plate boundary, river long-profile, or coast-to-trench floor relief — shelf/Mumbai High, slope, rise, abyssal plain, ridge, trench/Mariana ~10,994 m); (2) every climatology answer gets a circulation sketch (pressure belts + named winds + the season's jet positions); (3) every ocean answer gets a current map with warm/cold colour logic and the two meeting-zone fisheries (Grand Banks, Kuroshio–Oyashio) plus the upwelling trio (Peru–Atacama, Benguela–Namib, California). Pair diagrams with the trunk's PYQ anchors (S-wave shadow proof, three boundary types, west-coast deserts, NE monsoon, Mawsynram, Sundarbans, Zone V, Sumatra 2004) and the Deep Ocean Mission (2021, Central Indian Ocean Basin nodules) as the blue-economy closer.
