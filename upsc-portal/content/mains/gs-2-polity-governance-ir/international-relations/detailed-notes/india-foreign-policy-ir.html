International Relations: India's Foreign Policy, Bilateral Ties & Global Groupings — Detailed Notes

Syllabus slot: GS 2 (Mains) · International Relations: India & her neighbourhood; Bilateral, regional & global groupings; Indian diaspora Also relevant for: Prelims GS Paper I — Polity & Governance (IR questions); Essay (India's place in the world) Currency check: Updated to September 2026.

Institutional set-up

  • MEA — the professional core; NSA/NSCS — strategic coordination (KPS Menon-era → present Prime-Ministerial centrality); Parliamentary Standing Committee on External Affairs — consultative footprint; Indian Foreign Service (IFS 'A').
  • Economic arm: Development Partnership Administration (DPA, 2012) — Lines of Credit (LoCs) to Africa/South Asia; ISEC/ITEC — the training-diplomacy workhorse since 1964.
  • Cultural arm: ICCR; International Day of Yoga (adopted by UN 2014 — first observed 21 June 2015).

2. The great-power theatre

2.1 India–United States

  • Trajectory: estrangement (1971, 1998 sanctions) → Indo-US Civil Nuclear Deal (2005–08) — NSG waiver September 2008 — the paradigm shift; → defense frameworks: LEMOA (2016), COMCASA (2018), BECA (2020); foundational 2+2 ministerial dialogue.
  • iCET (2023) — Initiative on Critical and Emerging Technologies: semiconductors, AI, quantum, space (NASA-ISRO cooperation).
  • Trade & friction: largest trade partner by goods; 2025 saw a tariff clash (US duties on Indian goods raised, citing Russian-oil procurement) with a bilateral trade agreement under negotiation into 2026; H-1B visa politics recurrent.
  • Security: QUAD centrality; Malabar naval exercise; defence co-production (jet-engine tech transfer deals — GE-HAL framework).
  • Diaspora: ~5+ million Indian-Americans — the lobbying & talent bridge.

2.2 India–Russia

  • Legacy predicate: 1971 Treaty of Peace, Friendship & Cooperation; 2000 Strategic Partnership (annual summits alternately Moscow/Delhi).
  • Defence legacy: S-400 (deliveries continuing), Su-30, T-90, BrahMos (JV); share slowly diversifying to France/Israel/US.
  • Energy: post-2022 India became a top buyer of discounted Russian crude — largest single-country supplier multiple months — the friction point with the West.
  • Multilateral convergence: both in BRICS/SCO; Russia supports India's UNSC seat; Chabahar's INSTC connection.

2.3 India–China — the principal strategic challenge

  • Line of Actual Control (LAC) = 3,488 km contested boundary in 3 sectors (western Aksai Chin, middle Uttarakhand/Himachal, eastern Arunachal — China calls it "South Tibet").
  • Patterns: 1962 war; 1967 Nathu La/Cho La; the 1988 Rajiv visit reset; five border-management agreements (1993, 1996, 2005, 2012, 2013); Wuhan (2018) and Mamallapuram (2019) informal summits.
  • 2020 rupture — Galwan clash (15 June 2020) — fatal, scaled-down patrolling, trust collapse.
  • 2024 thaw — October 2024 patrolling arrangements announced (Depsang & Demchok friction points); leaders' meeting at Kazan BRICS (October 2024); 2025 SCO Tianjin summit saw a leaders' meeting on the sidelines and continued disengagement/de-escalation dialogue — the relationship is stabilising but rivalrous.
  • Economics: China remains a top-3 goods trade partner; trade deficit ~$85–100 bn range — the dependency critique persists (pharma APIs, electronics); FDI scrutiny (Press Note 3, 2020) continues for land-bordering states.
  • Structural contest: BRI/CPEC (PoK route), String-of-Pearls ports (Hambantota, Gwadar), Doklam (2017)/'17-model flashpoints, Tibet and the Dalai Lama.

2.4 India–Pakistan

  • The dyad of four wars (1947, 1965, 1971, Kargil 1999); the Indus Waters Treaty (1960) — India placed it in abeyance on 23 April 2025 after the Pahalgam terror attack (22 April 2025, 26 dead).
  • May 2025 — Operation Sindoor (7–10 May 2025): India struck terror & military infrastructure across the border after Pahalgam; four-day conventional exchange ended 10 May 2025 with a DGMO-level ceasefire understanding along the LoC — underscoring the new "normal" of calibrated escalation under the nuclear shadow.
  • Status items: Article 370 (Aug 2019); Kartarpur corridor suspended; SAFTA inoperative; dialogue channels ("comprehensive bilateral dialogue") dormant.

2.5 Europe, Quad partners, the middle

  • India–EU: strategic partnership since 2004; FTA negotiation — long-running; concluded in principle in January 2026 after years of laborious bargaining (the "mother of all deals" headline) — implementation/signature and ratification now the file.
  • France: strategic partnership (1998) — Rafale, Scorpene, space (CNES-ISRO), India-Middle East–Europe Corridor partner; 2047 Horizon roadmap.
  • UK: FTA signed 2025; 2030 Roadmap; the diaspora & education corridor.
  • Japan: special strategic & global partnership; bullet-train (E5 Shinkansen), act-east co-financing in NE India, QUAD, semiconductor fabs.
  • Australia: ECTA 2022; QUAD; critical minerals.

4. Regional & mini-lateral groupings

  • SAARC (1985) — paralysed by India–Pakistan (2016 Islamabad summit eternally postponed); sub-regional pivot: BBIN (Bangladesh-Bhutan-India-Nepal) motor vehicles agreement (2015).
  • BIMSTEC (1997) — Bay of Bengal initiative has become India's preferred South-Asian growth platform; summits reactivated (2018 Kathmandu, 2022 virtual Sri Lanka, 2025 Bangkok); Bangkok (2025) added maritime and connectivity agenda.
  • Indian Ocean Rim Association (IORA) — India pillar of the blue-economy architecture; Colombo Security Conclave (India-Sri Lanka-Maldives-Mauritius, expanded 2022) — the maritime-mini-lateral.
  • QUAD (India, US, Japan, Australia) — revived 2017; leader-level from 2021; working groups on vaccines/climate/critical tech/space/IPMDA — no military charter, but Indo-Pacific's consolidating coalition.
  • I2U2 (India, Israel, UAE, US) — first summit July 2022 — food security & clean energy.
  • India–Middle East–Europe Corridor (IMEC) — announced at G20 New Delhi (September 2023) — India-Gulf-Europe connectivity mechanism.
  • Act East / ASEAN: Act East Policy (2014); Indo-Pacific Oceans Initiative (2019); ASEAN Summit chair rotates; India co-patrols Malacca approaches informally through exercises (MILAN sea-phase).
  • SCO — membership 2017; hosted virtually (2023); Tianjin 2025 — the thaw venue for India–China PM-level contact; counter-terror structure RATS.
  • BRICS — founding member; expanded 2024 (Egypt, Ethiopia, Iran, UAE inducted; Saudi invited); India chairs BRICS in 2026.

5. Global groupings & multilateral agenda

United Nations

  • Founding member (1945, San Francisco — "India" signed before independence 1947, the anomaly rectified).
  • Eight non-permanent UNSC terms (latest 2021–22; presidency August 2021).
  • UNSC reform: G4 (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan) joint bid + L.69 group of developing countries; call for text-based negotiations in the IGN — the sticking points are the veto question and African representation (India supports the Ezulwini Consensus position of Africa in principle).
  • Largest cumulative UNSC peacekeeping contributor among democracies: since 1950 Korea through missions in Congo, Sudan, Lebanon; female peacekeepers' deployment pioneering.

G20

  • India's presidency (December 2022 – November 2023) — New Delhi Declaration by consensus; African Union admitted as a permanent member (2023); troika continuity with Brazil 2024 and South Africa (2025, first African host — Johannesburg), United States holding the 2026 presidency.
  • Deliverables tabled: Global Biofuel Alliance (GBA), digital public infrastructure stack model (UPI/MOSIP), IMEC.

Others

  • WTO — India's voice on public stockholding / MSP peace-clause politics, TRIPS-waiver leadership (COVID vaccines, 2020-22, with South Africa), fisheries subsidies (2022 deal signature with caveats).
  • Climate — Paris Agreement (2015); UNFCCC negotiating groups (G77+China, BASIC, LMDC positions); India's updated NDC: 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030, 50% cumulative electric installed capacity from non-fossil by 2030, net-zero 2070 (announced Glasgow 2021); International Solar Alliance (2015, HQ Gurugram); Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (2019); Mission LiFE (2021); GBA (2023).
  • Financial — IMF/World Bank quota concerns; New Development Bank (2015, HQ Shanghai); Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank (2016, HQ Beijing — India's second-largest shareholder); ADB.

6. Economic diplomacy & diaspora

  • Lines of Credit — ~$30+ bn extended, mainly Africa/South Asia; grants companion; ITEC training diaspora-spread.
  • Diaspora — ~35 million (NRIs + PIOs), largest in the world; remittances ~$120+ bn/year (world's largest); Pravasi Bharatiya Divas (9 January); OCI since 2005; sizeable political influence in the US, UK, Canada, Gulf.
  • Gulf — energy + 9-million-strong workforce + remittances; IMEC repositioning; India–UAE CEPA (2022) a template FTA.
  • Culture as power — Yoga Day (2015), Ayurveda WHO-GCTM at Jamnagar (2023), the "soft power stack".

7. Current issues (Mains-ready, 2024–26)

  1. Indus Waters Treaty in abeyance (Apr 2025) — legal review vs strategic leverage debate.
  2. Operation Sindoor (May 2025) — the calibrated deterrence precedent; international endorsement patterns.
  3. Census-Delimitation-Women's Reservation axis — neutral IR relevance but often an essay cross-theme.
  4. BRICS expansion + India's chairmanship (2026) — positioning between G7 outreach and Global South leadership; 'Voice of Global South' summits (2023–24 slots).
  5. US tariff turbulence & trade talks — economics and strategic balancing as one subject.
  6. Chabahar 10-year contract (May 2024) — INSTC-aligned connectivity with Iran & Afghanistan.
  7. Bangladesh transition (since Aug 2024) — energy, transit, and minority-security dilemmas.

8. Framework for IR mains answers

  • Anchor in principles (autonomy, Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam) → diagnose the dyad (history, instruments, current flashpoint) → weigh instruments (economic, defence, diaspora, multilateral) → conclude with India's systemic goal (UNSC reform, multipolarity with rules).

10. The rupture-and-repair cycle 2020–26 — Galwan to Kazan, Pahalgam to Sindoor

The trunk's great-power theatre (§2) documents two ruptures and two repairs that together define contemporary Indian statecraft; the Mains task is to theorise the cycle, not merely narrate it. Rupture one — Galwan (15 June 2020): the fatal clash collapsed the five-agreement border-management regime (1993/1996/2005/2012/2013) and the Wuhan–Mamallapuram (2018–19) informal-summit scaffolding; patrolling froze, trust evaporated, and economics decoupled at the margin (Press Note 3 FDI scrutiny for land-border states, app actions, supply-chain wariness — while goods trade paradoxically persisted with an $85–100 bn deficit). Repair one — Kazan (October 2024): patrolling arrangements at Depsang–Demchok, the BRICS-sidelines leaders' meeting, then the Tianjin SCO (2025) contact continuing disengagement/de-escalation dialogue. The doctrine to extract: stabilise-but-rivalrous — restore mechanisms (patrolling, hotlines, WMCC) without conceding positions (LAC's 3,488 km in three sectors, South-Tibet/Aksai Chin claims, CPEC-through-PoK and String-of-Pearls/Hambantota–Gwadar contestation intact).

Rupture two — Pahalgam (22 April 2025, 26 dead) → Indus Waters Treaty placed in abeyance (23 April 2025), the first suspension of the 1960 regime. Repair-that-isn't — Operation Sindoor (7–10 May 2025): strikes on terror and military infrastructure across the border, a four-day conventional exchange, then a DGMO-level LoC ceasefire understanding (10 May). The doctrine to extract: calibrated escalation under the nuclear shadow — terror imposes conventional costs met with cross-border conventional response, contained below the nuclear threshold by design, with the IWT lever held in abeyance as continuing pressure. Pair with the dormant file (Article 370 settled fact since August 2019, Kartarpur suspended, SAFTA inoperative, comprehensive dialogue dead) to argue the settled Indian position: terror and talks cannot coexist, but terror will now be answered in the domain of India's choosing — water, conventional strike, or both. Endorsement patterns (who condemned Pahalgam, who counselled restraint during Sindoor) are the multilateral weather-vane for essay conclusions.

11. The neighbourhood as the hard test — where doctrine meets churn

Neighbourhood First (2014) plus the Gujral Doctrine's non-reciprocal generosity (1996–97) is the stated grammar; the trunk's country table shows every neighbour testing it simultaneously — which is precisely the Mains question ("evaluate") waiting to be asked. Nepal: 1950 Treaty open border, Pancheshwar/power-trade upside, Kalapani–Lipulekh–Limpiyadhura boundary dispute — against 2025 political turbulence and an interim-led transition; argue that open borders convert neighbour instability into domestic politics (demonetisation-currency, Agniveer-recruitment, and boundary-nationalism spillovers). Bangladesh: the LBA-2015/akhaura-Agartala connectivity high-water mark and Hasina-era security cooperation vs the August-2024 transition's recalibration, 2026 elections ahead, Teesta still pending — the test case for whether Indian partnership survives regime change. Sri Lanka: the $4 bn 2022 crisis-support as Gujral Doctrine vindicated, now ECTA-track economics against Hambantota/Colombo port strategy and the 13th-Amendment devolution ask. Maldives: the 2018–23–25 alignment swings with the Thilamale bridge and Greater-Malé connectivity as Indian ballast — proof that deliverables outlast rhetoric. Myanmar: trilateral highway + Kaladan vs post-2021 junta reality, refugee/insurgency spillover, and the 2024 Free Movement Regime review. Bhutan: the stable anchor (1949/2007 treaty, Punatsangchhu hydro, Gelephu Mindfulness City) — and Doklam 2017's reminder that even anchors sit on contested geology. Afghanistan: wheat/vaccines via Chabahar (10-year contract May 2024) with Taliban engagement through the Doha channel.

The evaluation frame: score Neighbourhood First on three ledgers — crisis response (Sri Lanka 2022 ✓, Maldives water/airlift precedents), connectivity delivery (BBIN motor vehicles 2015, BIMSTEC's 2025 Bangkok maritime agenda, IMT highway delays ✗), and regime-change resilience (Bangladesh 2024–26 as the live exam). The structural honesties to concede: China's BRI/CPEC chequebook competes on every frontier; SAARC's 2016-eternal-postponement proves Pakistan vetoes regionalism (hence the BIMSTEC/IORA/Colombo-Security-Conclave pivot); and small-neighbour nationalism periodically monetises anti-India postures regardless of Indian generosity. Verdict line: the neighbourhood rewards the patient creditor — India wins by being the first responder in crises and the finisher of projects, not by winning news cycles.

12. The chairmanship year — multipolarity with rules

India chairs BRICS in 2026 — the trunk's multilateral chapters (§§4–5) supply the stage; this section supplies the script. The inheritance: G20 presidency (Dec 2022–Nov 2023) delivered the New Delhi consensus Declaration, African Union permanent membership (2023), Global Biofuel Alliance, the DPI-stack export (UPI/MOSIP model), and IMEC (announced September 2023, France a partner, 2047 Horizon roadmap with Paris); BRICS expanded 2024 (Egypt/Ethiopia/Iran/UAE in, Saudi invited); Voice of Global South summits (2023–24) built the constituency; SCO membership (2017, hosted 2023, Tianjin 2025) and QUAD (revived 2017, leader-level 2021, IPMDA/vaccines/tech/space working groups, no military charter) demonstrate multi-alignment as practice; I2U2 (July 2022) and the EU FTA concluded-in-principle (January 2026, "mother of all deals," signature/ratification pending) plus UK FTA (2025), ECTA with Australia (2022), and CEPA with UAE (2022) show the trade pillar rising alongside.

The 2026 chairmanship agenda to argue: (i) BRICS de-dollarisation realism — local-currency settlement pilots without rupturing the IMF/World Bank/ADB architecture where India also banks (NDB 2015 Shanghai, AIIB 2016 Beijing second-largest shareholder); (ii) UNSC reform endgame pressure — G4 + L.69 + text-based IGN negotiations, Ezulwini Consensus solidarity with Africa, eight non-permanent terms (latest 2021–22, August-2021 presidency) and the peacekeeping record (Korea 1950 → Congo/Sudan/Lebanon, women peacekeepers pioneering) as credentials; (iii) climate-trade linkage defence — Paris 2015, updated NDC (500 GW non-fossil 2030, 50% non-fossil installed capacity, net-zero 2070 Glasgow), ISA (2015 Gurugram), CDRI (2019), Mission LiFE (2021) against CBAM-style unilateralism, with WTO public-stockholding/MSP peace-clause and fisheries-subsidy positions as the Geneva front. Apply the trunk's answer framework (principles → dyad diagnosis → instrument weighing → systemic goal) and close: from Panchsheel (29 April 1954) through SAGAR (2015 Mauritius) to MAHASAGAR (2025 Mauritius), India's vocabulary keeps scaling — the 2026 test is whether chairmanship converts vocabulary into veto-proof outcomes.
