Social Justice: Welfare Schemes, Health, Education & HRD — Detailed Notes

Syllabus slot: GS 2 (Mains) · Social Justice: Welfare Schemes, Health, Education, Human Resource Development Also relevant for: Prelims GS Paper I — Polity & Governance; Essay (development & equity themes) Currency check: Updated to September 2026 — includes the VB-G RAM G Act, 2025 replacing MGNREGA from 1 July 2026.

1. Constitutional foundation of social justice

ProvisionContent
PreambleJustice — social, economic, political; equality of status and opportunity
FRsArts 14–16 (equality & reservation), 17 (social justice against untouchability), 21 (life incl. livelihood, health, education — judicially read), 21A (elementary education), 23–24 (exploitation)
DPSPArts 38, 39, 41 (work), 42 (just & humane work), 43 (living wage), 43A, 45 (early childhood care), 46 (promotion of SC/ST/OBC educational & economic interests), 47 (public health, nutrition)
73rd/74th AmendmentsLocal delivery institutions — panchayats & municipalities as the welfare state's first responders
Statutes that create rightsRTI (2005), RTE (2009), NFSA (2013), Forest Rights Act (2006), RPwD (2016), and — between 2005–2026 — the rural employment guarantee framework (MGNREGA 2005 → VB-G RAM G 2025/2026)

The rights-based turn: since 2004 India moved from schemes-as-charity to rights-as-entitlements (employment, food, education, information, forests). The VB-G RAM G transition again shifts the grammar — from open-ended entitlement to mission-mode, allocation-based guarantee.

2. The welfare delivery architecture

  1. Beneficiary identification — SECC 2011 (Socio-Economic Caste Census); State social registries; Aadhaar verification.
  2. JAM trinity — Jan Dhan (2014 → ~56 crore accounts by mid-decade) + Aadhaar + Mobile enabling Direct Benefit Transfer (hundreds of schemes piped through DBT).
  3. Institutional layering — Ministry of Rural Development, Women &
  4. Child Development, Health, Education (MoE after 2020 rename), Social Justice &
  5. Empowerment, Tribal Affairs, Minority Affairs, Labour
  6. plus State counterparts.
  7. Last-mile institutions — 2.5 lakh+ gram panchayats, 14 lakh+ anganwadis, SHG networks (DAY-NRLM, ~10 crore women in SHGs), public distribution (5.4 lakh+ Fair Price Shops), bank correspondents.

4. Food, nutrition & the social safety net

National Food Security Act (2013)

  • Legal entitlement to subsidised grains: up to 75% of rural and 50% of urban population covered (≈ 81 crore people — among the world's largest food schemes).
  • 5 kg of foodgrains/person/month at ₹3/2/1 per kg for rice/wheat/coarse grains (Priority Households); Antyodaya households: 35 kg/family/month.
  • Entitlements per child: midday meal; maternity benefit ₹6,000 (link to PMMVY); women as head of household for ration cards.
  • Grievance machinery: State Food Commissions, social audits, toll-free lines.

PM Garib Kalyan Anna Yojana (PMGKAY)

  • 2020 COVID response, later continuing as the free-grains layer over NFSA: from January 2023 NFSA grains made free (merging PMGKAY with NFSA) — announced for five years from January 2024.

Nutrition

  • POSHAN Abhiyaan (2018) → POSHAN 2.0 (2021) — merged umbrella of Supplementary Nutrition Programme (ICDS), anemia and POSHAN tracking; Saksham Anganwadis; targets on stunting, undernutrition, anemia (aligned to WHO targets and SDG 2).
  • PM POSHAN (2021) — successor of Mid-Day Meal, extending to Bal Vatikas (pre-primary).

5. Health

Architecture

  • National Health Mission (NHM, 2013) — merged NRHM (2005) + NUHM; funds ASHA (~10 lakh), ANMs, PHC/CHC upgrades.
  • Ayushman Bharat (2018) — two arms:
    1. Health & Wellness Centres → Ayushman Arogya Mandirs (150,000+) — comprehensive primary care.
    2. PM-JAY — ₹5 lakh/family/year cashless secondary & tertiary hospitalisation for ~12 crore poor/vulnerable families; expanded in October 2024 to all Indians aged 70+ regardless of income (~6 crore seniors).
  • Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM, 2021) — ABHA IDs, interoperable health records.
  • Structural reform: National Medical Commission (2020) replacing the MCI; NMC's regulation of MBBS seats & exits.
  • Key targets &
  • frameworks: SDG-3
  • NPCDCS (NCD screening)
  • Mission Indradhanush (immunisation — intensified rounds)
  • Anaemia Mukt Bharat
  • eSanjeevani telemedicine (>25 crore consultations by mid-2020s).
  • Mains angles: out-of-pocket spending still high (though declining per National Health Accounts); doctor/nurse density short of WHO norms; health is a State subject managing with tied Centrally-Sponsored transfers — cooperative-federalism test.

6. Education & HRD

Right to education

  • Art 21A (86th Amendment 2002)
  • RTE Act (2009, in force 2010): free &
  • compulsory education for 6–14 years
  • 25% private-school quota for disadvantaged (curtailed by some post-2019 developments)
  • norms for PTR, infrastructure
  • no-detention till Class 8 modified in 2019 to allow States' examinations at Classes 5 &
  • 8.

National Education Policy 2020 — the current superstructure

  • 5+3+3+4 school curricular structure (replacing 10+2)
  • ECCE integrated
  • FLN mission NIPUN Bharat (2021)
  • NCF-SE (2023) and new NCERT textbooks rolling out.
  • Higher education: GER 50% by 2035 ambition
  • single regulator idea (HECI) evolved into staged reforms
  • Academic Bank of Credits, multiple entry-exit, four-year UG
  • PM SHRI schools (14,500 model schools)
  • PM-Vidyalaxmi (2024) — collateral-free loans for quality-HE aspirants
  • AICTE/UGC alignment for Indian languages as medium of technical education.
  • Skilling: Skill India (2015), PMKVY, National Apprenticeship Promotion Scheme, ITIs' upgrade; AI/semiconductor curriculum pushes in 2024–26.

The HRD frame (for GS-2 answers)

  • Investment logic: education + health + skilling as human capital formation (Ha-Joon Chang-style argument acceptable in answers, with India data: ASER's learning outcomes and PLFS skilling share).
  • Capacity numbers: 1.5 lakh+ Higher Education institutions' target, 1,000+ universities; Women's entry in STEM agenda.

8. Gender & the political-economy frame (Mains analytics)

  1. Rights vs missions — India has oscillated: legal entitlements (RTI→RTE→NFSA→MGNREGA era, 2004–14) → programme saturation (toilets, LPG, water, bank accounts, housing — 2014–24) → and now mission-mode guarantees (VB-G RAM G).
  2. Delivery digitisation — DBT's fiscal savings (claimed leakage reduction) vs exclusion errors from biometric failures; Supreme Court's Aadhaar line (Puttaswamy (2018): Aadhaar valid for subsidies, not foundational exclusion).
  3. Fiscal federalism — CSS cost-sharing tensions; 60:40 VB-G RAM G funding shift as the latest instance.
  4. Outcome measurement — NITI Aayog SDG Index, NFHS-5/6 data as answer-ready evidence.
  5. Gender budgeting (from 2005–06, now a constitutional-era instrument) and women-led development as the rhetorical frame since 2023.

9. Recent developments to cite (2024–26)

  • December 2025 — VB-G RAM G Act receives Presidential assent (21 December 2025)
  • in force 1 July 2026
  • MGNREGA repealed same day
  • eight draft rules issued (steering committee, grievances, normative allocations, wage payment, transitional provisions).
  • October 2024 — PM-JAY extended to all 70+ seniors (₹5 lakh top-up).
  • 2024 — PMAY 2.0 (Rural: +2 crore houses through 2028-29; Urban Phase-II: 1 crore households).
  • 2025 — Jal Jeevan Mission extended to 2028 ("Har Ghar Jal"), with a reservoir-and-source-sustainability focus.
  • 2025–26 — Census 2027 announced (houselisting 2026 → population enumeration 2027) — the dataset that will drive delimitation, women's reservation (Art 334A) and welfare targeting.
  • 2024 — Supreme Court on sub-classification within SC reservations (State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh, August 2024 — 6:1 upholding sub-classification subject to empirical data) and the creamier-layer debate remains open for SCs (CJI's separate observation).
  • 2024 — Electoral Bonds struck down (ADR v. Union of India, February 2024) — relevant to welfare-funding transparency arguments.

12. The employment guarantee as constitutional drama — rights, missions, and the VB-G RAM G wager

No welfare instrument teaches social-justice reasoning better than the employment-guarantee arc the trunk documents: MGNREGA 2005 → VB-G RAM G Act (assent 21 December 2025), in force 1 July 2026, MGNREGA repealed the same day. Read it as three constitutional grammars in sequence. Grammar one — entitlement (2005–2026): demand-driven, any rural household asking gets 100 days, Centre pays, Gram Sabha's shelf of projects plans bottom-up. Grammar two — mission guarantee (2026–): allocation-driven, normative State allocations fixed by the Centre (using the 16th Finance Commission horizontal devolution formula), 125 days, 60:40 funding (90:10 NE/Himalayan), four strategic work priorities (water security, core rural infrastructure, livelihood infrastructure, extreme-weather mitigation), Viksit Gram Panchayat Plans aligned with PM Gati Shakti, a 60-day agri-season pause, and States bearing demand overshoot. The transition mechanics — e-KYC job cards valid until Gramin Rozgar Guarantee Cards issue, existing works migrating, eight draft rules on steering, grievances, allocations, wages — are the Prelims layer; the Mains layer is the wager.

State both frames with the trunk's honesty. Supporter frame: higher days (125 > 100), climate-linked durable assets instead of scattered works, convergence with Gati Shakti planning, saturation logic (A/B/C panchayat classification), Viksit Bharat 2047 alignment. Critic frame: repeal of a rights statute (the first rollback of the 2004–14 rights-based turn — RTI, RTE, NFSA, FRA), top-down normative allocations replacing demand, funding burden shifted to States precisely when the 60-day pause and digitisation barriers bite the poorest, exclusion risk from the planning centralisation. The examination synthesis: evaluate VB-G RAM G not against MGNREGA's promise but against MGNREGA's delivery failures (delayed wages, asset quality, demand suppression by fund starvation) — missions fix what entitlements promised badly, but only if allocations are genuinely normative and grievance rules genuinely justiciable. Cite the supporting ecosystem (DAY-NRLM's ~10 crore SHG women, DAY-NULM, PM SVANidhi 2020, PM Vishwakarma 2023, MUDRA 2015) as the livelihoods surround that employment policy alone cannot substitute.

13. Health, education, and the human-capital ledger

The trunk's health and education sections are fact-dense; the Mains value-add is the ledger frame — every scheme as an entry in India's human-capital balance sheet. Health side: NHM (2013, NRHM 2005 + NUHM) funds the ASHA (~10 lakh)–ANM–PHC/CHC floor; Ayushman Bharat's two arms (150,000+ Arogya Mandirs for comprehensive primary care; PM-JAY ₹5 lakh/family/year for ~12 crore families, extended October 2024 to all 70+ citizens regardless of income); ABDM (2021) ABHA IDs; NMC (2020) replacing MCI; Mission Indradhanush, Anaemia Mukt Bharat, NPCDCS, eSanjeevani (>25 crore consultations). Against this, three debits: out-of-pocket spending still high (though falling per National Health Accounts), doctor/nurse density below WHO norms, and the State-subject structure running on tied Central transfers — the cooperative-federalism test. Education side: Art 21A (86th Amendment 2002) → RTE (2009, in force 2010, 6–14 years, 25% quota, no-detention modified 2019); NEP 2020's 5+3+3+4, ECCE, NIPUN Bharat FLN (2021), NCF-SE (2023) with new NCERT texts; higher-education GER-50%-by-2035 ambition via ABC, multiple entry-exit, four-year UG, PM SHRI (14,500 schools), PM-Vidyalaxmi (2024) loans; Skill India (2015), PMKVY, NAPS, ITI upgrades, 2024–26 AI/semiconductor curriculum pushes.

Three ledger entries win marks. (i) The measurement spine: NFHS-5/6, ASER learning outcomes, PLFS skilling shares, NITI SDG Index — never assert outcomes without one of these. (ii) The federalism debit: health and education are State/Centre-State subjects executed through Centrally Sponsored Schemes — CSS cost-sharing fights (the VB-G RAM G 60:40 shift as latest instance) recur in every sector. (iii) The demography clock: Census 2027 (houselisting 2026 → enumeration 2027) will reset delimitation, Art 334A women's reservation, and welfare targeting simultaneously — argue that every human-capital claim after 2027 must be rebased. Closing ledger line: India's welfare state is scheme-rich and outcome-uneven; the rebasing decade (Census 2027 + SDG 2030) is when inputs must finally reconcile with outcomes.

14. Three doctrines that settle arguments — sub-classification, Aadhaar, and gender budgeting

Three recent doctrines give social-justice answers their 2024–26 edge. Sub-classification: State of Punjab v. Davinder Singh (August 2024, 6:1) upheld sub-classification within SC reservations subject to empirical data — with the CJI's separate observation reopening the creamy-layer-for-SCs debate. The Mains use: reservation answers can no longer treat SC/ST/OBC as monoliths; argue differentiation-with-data (quantifiable-data requirement from Nagaraj through Jarnail Singh to Davinder Singh) as the constitutionally mandated direction. Pair with the OBC architecture (NCBC constitutionalised, 102nd Amendment 2018, Art 338B; State-list power restored, 105th Amendment 2021, after the May 2021 Maratha reading-down) and Census 2027's caste enumeration — the data question and the doctrine question arrive together.

Aadhaar-after-Puttaswamy (2018): valid for subsidies, not foundational exclusion — the sentence that settles every DBT argument. JAM (Jan Dhan ~56 crore + Aadhaar + Mobile) piped hundreds of schemes through DBT with claimed leakage savings; the counter-ledger is biometric-failure exclusion, amplified by the VB-G RAM G digitisation layer (e-KYC job cards, Gati Shakti-aligned planning). Argue the balanced line: digitisation has reduced ghost leakage and created living exclusion — the remedy is exception-handling (offline overrides, social audits, State Food Commissions) designed as rights, not favours. Gender budgeting (since 2005–06) into women-led development (post-2023 frame): track the instruments — Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015), Mission Shakti's Sambal (OSC, 181, Nirbhaya) + Samarthya (PMMVY, Shakhi Sadan, creches), PM Ujjwala (2016, 2.0/3.0), and the 106th Amendment's 33% reservation pending post-2026-census delimitation — then apply the trunk's sensory checklist to any scheme question: constitutional basis → design & target → delivery mechanics → outcome data → critique (funding, exclusion, federal cost-sharing) → reforms (registries, portability, local capacity) → DPSP conclusion.
