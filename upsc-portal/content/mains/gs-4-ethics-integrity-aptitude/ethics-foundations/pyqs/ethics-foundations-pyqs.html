Ethics Foundations — PYQs

8 questions

GS-4 pattern: Part A ~6 mains theory Qs; Part B case studies. These originals mirror UPSC style with model approaches.

Prelims-style checks (concept positioning)

Q1

"Ethics of care" as a critique of dominant moral-development theory is linked to:

  1. (a)Carol Gilligan
  2. (b)Lawrence Kohlberg
  3. (c)John Rawls
  4. (d)Hannah Arendt
Show answer
Correct: (a)Gilligan (1982, In a Different Voice) critiqued Kohlberg's justice-bias stages and foregrounded relational care.
Q2

The "veil of ignorance" is an instrument for arriving at principles of:

  1. (a)Virtue, in Aristotle
  2. (b)Justice, in Rawls
  3. (c)Rights, in Nozick
  4. (d)Duty, in Kant
Show answer
Correct: (b)Rawls' original-position construct yields the two principles of justice.
Q3

"Banality of evil" emerged from Arendt's reporting on:

  1. (a)Stalin's show trials
  2. (b)Adolf Eichmann's Jerusalem trial
  3. (c)the Nuremberg prosecutor general
  4. (d)Milgram's obedience lab
Show answer
Correct: (b)Eichmann in Jerusalem (1963) — Arendt's analysis of routinised bureaucratic evil.

Mains theory questions with model-skeletons

Q4

"Emotional intelligence in administration is the thin line between empathy and capture." Discuss. (150 words)

Skeleton:

  • Define EI (Goleman: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skill).
  • Empathy value: grievance-design, disaster response, community protest management.
  • Capture risk: favouritism in service delivery; succumbing to local elite cues; sympathy fatigue → misallocation.
  • Guards: SOPs, rotation, periodic audits, ethical check-ins (2nd ARC style), mentoring.
  • Conclusion: "equilibrated empathy is intelligence; asymmetric empathy is capture."
Q5

"Rawls' difference principle would find Indian welfare more just if it actually served the least-advantaged first." Evaluate with NFSA/DBT evidence.

Skeleton:

  • Difference principle recap (inequality only if benefit-to-least-advantaged max).
  • NFSA entitlement cover + DBT leakage cuts; PM-KISAN as broad-base transfer.
  • Counter-evidence: widening gap post-2017 consumption scores debate; PLFS earnings concentration.
  • Assessment: partial alignment; structural shifts (agri income share, informal-labour precarity).
  • Way forward: social protection floors + wealth-instrument rationalisation (inheritance-tax debate neutrality).
Q6

"Conscience is the only genuine compass when orders and duties collide." Examine in the context of hierarchical administrative systems. (250 words)

Skeleton:

  • Define conscience (inner ethical voice shaped by socialisation).
  • Order-duty collisions: illegal orders (encounter killings), politically-motivated transfers, selective enforcement.
  • Thinker stack: Socrates (conscience over consensus), Thoreau (civil disobedience), Arendt warning of routinised complicity; also Kohlberg post-conventional stage.
  • Institutional backstops: dissent channels (2nd ARC), RTI, Lokpal, judicial review.
  • Practical rule: written-order request + documented dissent + remedial reporting — reduces whistleblower asymmetry.
  • Conclusion: conscience needs institutional amplification, not just moral courage.
Q7

"Attitude change, not merely rule change, explains sanitation success." Connect behavioural-attitude models with Swachh Bharat evidence.

Skeleton:

  • ABC model + ELM central/peripheral persuasion routes.
  • Swachh Bharat twin drivers: infrastructure (toilet construction) + behavioural messaging (celebrity endorsement + women's dignity frame — peripheral cues + identity leverage).
  • Evidence: ODF-village cadence 2014–19; SBM-U ODF++ 2020s refinement.
  • Limits: forest-hessian ("mirror" messaging backlash), O&M failures, caste-linked manual-scavenging persistence (Prohibition of Employment as Manual Scavengers Act 2013 updates).
  • Conclusion: rules enable; identity-aligned persuasion sustains.
Q8

"Nolan principles are aspirational universalism in a differentiated world." Do you agree? Illustrate with Indian civil-service codes.

Skeleton:

  • Nolan 7 recap.
  • Aspiration strength: selflessness/integrity/accountability as global baselines (UN Convention anti-corruption echoes).
  • Differentiation reality: India stack blends conduct rules (AIS 1968, CCS 1964) + penal spectrum (PC Act 1988 + BNS 2023) + social accountability (RTI + social audits).
  • Remaining gap: gift/hospitality norms in state services vary; political-neutrality enforcement ambiguity (transfers).
  • Conclusion: universal principles remain defaults; operational adaptation is legitimate, code drift must be audited.
