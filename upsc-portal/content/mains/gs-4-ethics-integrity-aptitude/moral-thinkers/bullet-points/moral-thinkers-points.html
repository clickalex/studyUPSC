Moral Thinkers — Bullet Points

Flash-card list: one line each — idea, quote, application.

ASCII mindmap

MORAL THINKERS
├── WEST: Socrates(conscience) Plato(justice) Aristotle(virtue/mean)
│         Kant(duty) Bentham(calculus) Mill(liberty)
│         Rawls(fairness) Arendt(banality of evil)
└── INDIA: Kautilya(statecraft+audit) Gandhi(talisman/means)
          Vivekananda(service) Ambedkar(constitutional morality)
          Aurobindo(integral) Radhakrishnan(ethics as religion) Ashoka(dhamma)

Thinker-citation pairings for triggers

  • Question says "least advantaged" → Rawls' difference principle + Gandhi's talisman.
  • "Just following orders" → Arendt's banality of evil + Kant's autonomy.
  • "Means and ends" → Gandhi: means are ends in the making.
  • "Corruption controls" → Kautilya's audit machinery + Bentham's calculus of detection.
  • "Empathy in service" → Vivekananda's service-as-worship + Aristotle's phronesis.

Ten-second quotes

  • "Unexamined life is not worth living" — Socrates.
  • "Act only on maxims you can will universal" — Kant.
  • "In the happiness of subjects lies the king's happiness" — Kautilya.
  • "Recall the face of the poorest man…" — Gandhi's talisman.
  • "Inequalities must benefit the least advantaged" — Rawls.

Application habits

  • One thinker per paragraph; always convert to an administrative behaviour.
  • End with your synthesis line; don't stack quotes as filler.

Pitfall flags

  • Don't attribute utilitarianism to Mill alone (qualitative twist is his) or liberty to Bentham (it's Mill's harm principle).
  • Avoid misquoting Gandhi's talisman — it is a decision test, not a slogan.

Golden phrase

  • "Phronesis is prudence with a conscience — the virtue every file needs."
