EIA and Clearance Jurisprudence: Procedure as Substance

The host's architecture (§1) and litigation (§6) frame environmental law; this section takes its most-examined instrument — Environmental Impact Assessment — because EIA questions test procedure, and procedure is where environment answers win or lose. The EIA Notification 2006 (under EPA 1986) structures clearance as a sequenced gate: screening (Category A — national appraisal — versus B — state appraisal — with B1/B2 subdivisions), scoping (terms of reference), public consultation (the hearing within the affected area — the democratic heart of the instrument), appraisal (Expert Appraisal Committees — sectoral expertise), and decision (approve, approve-with-conditions, reject — with the compliance-monitoring tail). The draft EIA 2020 controversy (ex-post-facto clearance legitimised, public consultation diluted for expansion projects and \"strategic\" categories, the compliance-reporting weakened) is the instrument's stress-test: its critics (the \"clearance raj to exemption raj\" charge — procedure hollowed precisely where stakes are highest) and its defenders (ease-of-doing-business, the standardisation claim) stage the development–environment binary the examination loves — resolved, in strong answers, by procedure-as-substance (the hearing that actually hears, the appraisal that actually appraises — the Lafarge–Niyamgiri lineage: Orissa Mining Corporation v. MoEF, 2013 — gram-sabha consent under FRA as the clearance precondition, the Dongria Kondh's twelve village assemblies vetoing a bauxite mine — procedure vindicated as sovereignty).

Clearance jurisprudence supplies the case-law spine. Lafarge Umiam (2011 — the \"environmental rule of law\" coinage, the NGT-era compliance doctrine); Hanuman Laxman Aroskar v. UoI, 2019 (Goa airport — EIA as more than formality: the Court's \"precautionary principle plus sustainable development\" restatement, flawed-EIA quashing); Bengaluru–Chennai Expressway / highway-EIA line (linear projects' cumulative-impact blind spot); the Western Ghats arc (Gadgil 2011 — ecologically-sensitive-zones maximalism — versus Kasturirangan 2013 — 37 per cent ESA dilution — versus the 2024 draft notification's sixth iteration — the fifteen-year saga of science negotiated into procedure); the Great Nicobar litigation (§7's live case — transhipment-plus-township versus Shompen–Nicobarese rights, coral–turtle–forest equities, the NGT-bench review — argued as the EIA stress-test of the decade: strategic necessity, tribal consent, cumulative impact, island fragility — all four horns named, none dismissed). The FCA 2023 amendment (§1's hook — \"forest\" redefined to recorded forests — the deemed-forest exclusion controversy, the Godavarman-definition narrowed by statute — the live legislative frontier) and the Biodiversity (Amendment) Act 2023 (benefit-sharing eased for AYUSH–practitioner–company users — the access-versus-equity debate, the NBA–SBB federalism) show Parliament redrawing procedure's boundaries while courts police them — the answer-formula: notification → case → controversy → principle, always in that order.

Forests, Wildlife, and Coasts: The Conservation Triad in Operation

Sections 2 and 5 map the protected-area system and biodiversity governance; this section operates the conservation triad — forests, wildlife, coasts — through the conflicts that examine it. Forests: the FCA-1980 diversion gate (Stage-I/II clearances, compensatory afforestation — the CAMPA fund's ₹50,000-crore-plus accumulation and its utilisation pathology — money collected for forests, spent on everything-but), the FRA 2006 (individual + community forest rights — patta plus CFR — the conservation–rights synthesis: 40+ lakh titles claimed, the rejection-rate controversy, the Wildlife First eviction-order saga 2019–24 — the Court ordering evictions of \"rejected\" claimants, then staying — rights versus protection at its rawest), the Godavarman continuing mandamus (1996– : the \"forest\" dictionary-meaning era — now statutorily narrowed by FCA 2023 — the mandamus that governed Indian forestry for a quarter-century), and the Green Credit Programme (§7 — 2023 rules — afforestation credits tradeable — the commodification critique: forests as offset-ledger versus forests as ecology). The forest answer-formula: diversion-gate → rights-synthesis → fund-pathology → credit-frontier.

Wildlife: Project Tiger's fifty-year arc (1973 — nine reserves — to 57+ reserves and 3,682 tigers, 2022 census — the conservation success that manufactured the human–wildlife-conflict crisis: corridor fragmentation — the Veerangana Durgavati TR question, §2 — linear infrastructure through corridors, the NTCA's corridor guidelines versus the highway–railway lobby); Project Elephant (1992 — Schedule-I status, ~27–30,000 estimated — the conflict's other face: crop-raiding, retaliatory poisoning, the translocation debate); the cheetah reintroduction (Kuno 2022+ — Namibia–South Africa batches — the meta-debate: flagship ambition versus the Asiatic-lion–Gir alternative, mortalities audited openly, the open-arc verdict pending); and the snow-leopard count (§7 — 718, first national estimate 2024 — the high-altitude conservation frontier: SECURE-Himalaya, community-based homestay-plus-insurance models). Coasts–wetlands: CRZ Notifications (1991 → 2011 → 2019 — the No-Development-Zone dilution arc — 200m-to-50m for tidal creeks, the fisherfolk-consent debate); Ramsar's 80+ sites (§2 — the 2-February annual additions — quoted fresh at exam time); the Wetlands Rules 2017 (decentralised to States — the protection-dilution critique); mangroves–corals (the Mumbai–Nicobar development squeeze — sewage, reclamation, transhipment). The triad's synthesis: protection's success (tigers up, Ramsar lists growing) creates protection's second-generation problems (conflict, corridors, consent) — the examination's conservation questions are increasingly second-generation.

Pollution and Climate Policy: From NCAP to Net-Zero Architecture

Sections 3–4 map pollution regimes and climate architecture; this section traces the policy logic connecting instruments to outcomes — because environment answers score on mechanism-evaluation, not instrument-lists. Air: NCAP 2019 (20–30 per cent PM reduction by 2024, now the 40-per-cent-by-2026 trajectory — the target-slippage honestly admitted) runs through city action plans (non-attainment cities' micro-action — road dust, construction, biomass — the implementation audit: funds released versus outcomes measured), the CAQM–GRAP stack (Commission for Air Quality Management 2021 — the statutory body with closure powers; Graded Response Action Plan — stage-gated restrictions — the Delhi-winter ritual: construction halts, truck diversions, school closures — evaluated as emergency theatre atop structural failure), and the stubble question (Pusa decomposer, ex-situ paddy-straw economics, the SMG-ban ambiguity — the crop-residue problem as MSP–procurement design failure: rice-wheat locked in by procurement, straw burned by calendar — solved by diversification, not machines alone). Water–waste: Namami Gange (2015 — NMCG authority, the sewerage–ghat–biodiversity tripod — evaluated by BOD-load trends at monitoring stations, not mission statements); PWM–EPR (2016 rules → 2022 EPR notification → July-2022 single-use ban on 19 items — the enforcement gradient: manufacture-bans work, litter-behaviour lags); E-waste 2022 (EPR categories expanded — the formalisation bet: 90-per-cent-informal recycling formalised through producer obligation); the Global Plastics Treaty (§3 — INC-5 partial 2024–25 — India's production-cuts-versus-waste-management-first position — the development-sovereignty line carried into chemicals diplomacy).

Climate policy runs the pledge-to-plumbing chain: Paris 2015 (NDC-ratchet-global-stocktake) → India's 2070 net-zero with 2030 sub-targets (500 GW non-fossil, 50 per cent RE capacity share, 45 per cent intensity cut, 1 billion tonnes — §4) → instruments (PAT-to-CCTS — Perform-Achieve-Trade's efficiency certificates maturing into the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme 2023 — the compliance-carbon-market build; ISA–CDRI–LiFE — the diplomacy tripod: solar alliance, disaster-resilient infrastructure, lifestyle doctrine — India's climate foreign policy as institution-building; green hydrogen mission, PM-Surya Ghar, e-mobility FAME-to-PM-E-DRIVE — the sectoral wedges) → adaptation (NAPCC's 8 missions + LiFE-as-9th, SAPCCs, 23+ State HAPs, Mission Mausam's ₹2,000-crore observation upgrade — adaptation-as-development: heat, water, crop-calendar, Himalayan fragility). The COP-beats (§4.2 — Glasgow phase-down, Sharm L&D fund, Dubai GST's \"transition away,\" Baku NCQG's $300-billion-versus-$1.3-trillion dissent, Belém's TFFF agenda — outcomes quoted only from final decision texts) supply the diplomacy narrative; the domestic chain supplies the evaluation: pledge → instrument → outcome-data → gap-admission — the climate answer's four beats, with the gap stated honestly (coal's continued baseload role, DISCOM finances, the adaptation-finance shortfall).

The Environment Answer Method: Prelims Facts, Mains Frames, Map Discipline

The host's dual orientation (Mains frames + Prelims' 12–18 yearly questions — the heaviest non-core segment) demands a two-track method — and this section builds it. Prelims track: the environment MCQ universe organises into six high-yield banks — (i) conventions-protocols with years (Ramsar 1971, CITES 1973, CBD 1992, UNCCD 1994, Vienna 1985–Montreal 1987–Kigali 2016, Basel–Rotterdam–Stockholm, Minamata 2013, BBNJ/High-Seas Treaty 2023); (ii) species-status instruments (IUCN Red List categories — critically-endangered to least-concern — plus WPA Schedules I–VI and CITES Appendices I–III — the schedule-appendix-category triple the paper loves to cross); (iii) protected-area geography (tiger reserves, elephant reserves, Ramsar sites, biosphere reserves, WHS — mapped, not listed — the corridor question needs the map); (iv) pollution numbers (NCAP targets, NAAQS pollutants — PM, SO₂, NO₂, CO, O₃, Pb, NH₃, benzene-family — the eight-parameter set; BOD–COD–DO water grammar; decibel zones); (v) climate mechanisms (GHG basket — CO₂, CH₄, N₂O, fluorinated — GWP hierarchy with methane's ~80×-over-20-years; carbon-cycle versus nitrogen-cycle basics; ocean-acidification chemistry); (vi) institutions-acronyms (UNFCCC–CBD–UNCCD Rio trio, UNEP, GEF, GCF, Adaptation Fund, ISA, CDRI, IBCA, NGT, CPCB–SPCBs, CAMPA, NBA–SBBs — each with HQ, year, function). One atlas hour weekly plus one acronym drill fortnightly covers the track: environment Prelims is won by organised banks, never by reading harder.

Mains track: every environment answer runs the frame-chain — constitutional hook (21/48A/51A(g) — the M C Mehta-to-Ranjitsinh arc) → legal instrument (Act + year + mechanism) → institutional map (MoEFCC–CPCB–NGT–NBA–CAMPA — who does what) → performance record (one honest datum — tigers up, air flat, CAMPA unspent, NCAP slipping) → conflict staged (development–conservation, rights–protection, Centre–State, India–world) → reform sequenced (first procedure, then capacity, then finance — ordering as judgment). Diagrams earn disproportionately: the EIA gate-flow, the FCA-diversion chain, the PAT-to-CCTS carbon-market sketch, the CRZ zonation strip, the corridor map — one per answer, hand-drawn, labelled. CA-tethering (§7's discipline): environment facts expire fastest in the syllabus (Ramsar counts, census numbers, COP outcomes, NGT orders) — so every static anchor travels with its CA-twin (Project Tiger 1973 and 3,682-in-2022; Ramsar 1971 and 80+-in-2025; Paris 2015 and Baku-2024-dissent) — the twin cited together, the static proving depth, the current proving currency. The closing synthesis: Prelims banks the facts, Mains frames the conflicts, maps ground both — and the candidate who keeps the six banks current, the frame-chain automatic, and the atlas open writes environment as the paper's highest-yielding subject, which, mark-per-hour, it is.
