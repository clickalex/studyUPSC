6. The United Nations System

The UN was founded in 1945 by the Charter signed at San Francisco, with India an original signatory (having signed the Declaration by United Nations in 1942 while still under British rule). Its six principal organs:

  • General Assembly — all members, one vote each; decisions on "important questions" need a two-thirds majority. Its resolutions are recommendatory. The Uniting for Peace resolution (1950) allows the UNGA to act when the Security Council is deadlocked by veto.
  • Security Council — 15 members: five permanent (China, France, Russia, UK, USA) with the veto, and ten non-permanent elected for two-year non-renewable terms by the UNGA on equitable geographical distribution. Procedural matters need 9 votes; substantive matters need 9 including the concurring votes of all permanent members — though in practice abstention is not treated as a veto. Only UNSC resolutions under Chapter VII are binding.
  • ECOSOC — 54 members, coordinating economic and social work and the functional commissions.
  • International Court of Justice — at The Hague, 15 judges for nine-year terms elected concurrently by the UNGA and UNSC. It settles disputes between states only, with jurisdiction based on consent. Distinguish it from the International Criminal Court, which is not a UN organ, was created by the Rome Statute (1998, effective 2002), prosecutes individuals for genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes and aggression, and to which India is not a party.
  • Secretariat — headed by the Secretary-General, appointed by the UNGA on the UNSC's recommendation.
  • Trusteeship Council — suspended operation in 1994 after Palau's independence.

UNSC reform is a standing India topic. The G4 (India, Brazil, Germany, Japan) seek permanent seats and support each other; the Uniting for Consensus group or "Coffee Club" (Italy, Pakistan, South Korea, Argentina, Mexico) opposes new permanent members and favours more elected seats; the Ezulwini Consensus is the African Union's demand for two permanent seats with veto. India's case rests on population, economic size, the largest cumulative contribution of peacekeeping troops, and the Council's unrepresentative 1945 structure. The obstacle is that Charter amendment requires ratification by two-thirds of members including all five permanent members.

Specialised agencies and their headquarters, a recurring pairing question: WHO, ILO, WTO, WMO, ITU, WIPO, UNHCR and UNCTAD are at Geneva; UNESCO at Paris; FAO, IFAD and WFP at Rome; IMF and World Bank at Washington DC; IAEA, UNIDO and UNODC at Vienna; ICAO at Montreal; IMO at London; UNEP and UN-Habitat at Nairobi; UNICEF and UNDP at New York.

7. Economic and Financial Groupings

The Bretton Woods institutions (1944). The IMF addresses balance-of-payments crises and macroeconomic stability; its lending is conditionality-based; voting is by quota, giving the US an effective veto over decisions requiring an 85% majority; its reserve asset is the Special Drawing Right, valued on a basket of the dollar, euro, renminbi, yen and pound. Its flagship publications are the World Economic Outlook, Global Financial Stability Report and Fiscal Monitor. The World Bank Group finances development and comprises the IBRD, IDA (concessional lending to the poorest), IFC (private sector), MIGA (guarantees) and ICSID (investment dispute settlement); its reports include the World Development Report and Global Economic Prospects.

The WTO succeeded GATT in 1995, operating by consensus with a one-country-one-vote fallback. Core principles: Most Favoured Nation (no discrimination between trading partners) and National Treatment (no discrimination between imported and domestic goods once inside the market). Key agreements: TRIPS (intellectual property), TRIMS (investment measures), GATS (services), SPS and TBT (standards), and the Agreement on Agriculture with its Amber, Blue and Green Boxes — India's concerns centre on the public stockholding for food security issue and the peace clause. The Appellate Body is currently paralysed because the United States has blocked appointments, and the Doha Development Round remains unconcluded.

Other economic groupings: the G7 (advanced economies, informal, no charter); the G20 (major economies plus the EU and now the African Union, accounting for the bulk of world GDP — India held the presidency in 2023 and secured the AU's admission); the OECD, a policy think tank of largely developed economies, which India is not a member of but engages with; BRICS with its New Development Bank (Shanghai) and Contingent Reserve Arrangement, expanded to include new members; the AIIB (Beijing), where India is the second-largest shareholder and largest borrower; and OPEC and OPEC+.

9. Export Control Regimes, Environment and Miscellaneous Bodies

The four export control regimes are a guaranteed question. India is a member of three: the Missile Technology Control Regime (joined 2016), the Wassenaar Arrangement on conventional arms and dual-use goods (2017) and the Australia Group on chemical and biological weapons (2018). India is not a member of the Nuclear Suppliers Group, where China has blocked consensus on the ground that India has not signed the NPT — though India received a country-specific NSG waiver in 2008 following the Indo-US nuclear deal. India is also not a party to the NPT or the CTBT, but is a member of the IAEA and has signed the Chemical Weapons Convention and Biological Weapons Convention.

Financial and standards bodies: the Financial Action Task Force (Paris, anti-money-laundering and terror financing, maintaining grey and black lists), the Financial Stability Board, the Bank for International Settlements at Basel with its Basel III capital norms, and the International Energy Agency (India is an association member, not a full member) versus the International Renewable Energy Agency, of which India is a full member.

India-initiated bodies worth naming as evidence of "leadership of the global South": the International Solar Alliance (headquartered at Gurugram, co-founded with France), the Coalition for Disaster Resilient Infrastructure (New Delhi), the Global Biofuel Alliance, the International Big Cat Alliance, and the Voice of Global South Summit.

Trap watch. The ICJ settles disputes between states; the ICC prosecutes individuals and is not a UN organ — India is party to neither the Rome Statute nor the NPT. Non-permanent UNSC members serve two years and cannot be immediately re-elected. Only Chapter VII resolutions bind. India is in the MTCR, Wassenaar and Australia Group but not the NSG. India withdrew from RCEP. SAARC operates by unanimity and excludes bilateral issues. The Quad is not a military alliance. NAM and the G7 have no permanent secretariat. The New Development Bank is at Shanghai, the AIIB at Beijing.
