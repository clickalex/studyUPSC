PART E — Drainage as applied geomorphology: the Himalayan–Peninsular contrast

The trunk covers landforms but leaves drainage implicit; the examiner treats river systems as geomorphology you can draw, so this section makes the Himalayan–Peninsular contrast answer-ready. Himalayan rivers (Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra systems) are antecedent — older than the mountains they cut through, which is why the Indus, Sutlej, and Brahmaputra (Tsangpo) slice gorges clean across the rising range; perennial (snow + rain fed); long courses with youthful upper gorges, mature middle meanders, and old-age deltas; massive sediment loads building the Indo-Gangetic plain. Peninsular rivers (Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri east-flowing; Narmada, Tapti west-flowing) are consequent/superimposed on an old shield — seasonal, shorter, graded profiles, shallow valleys; the east-flowers build deltas (Godavari–Krishna–Kaveri deltas) while the Narmada–Tapti flow through rift valleys and form estuaries, not deltas — the Prelims trap the trunk already flags.

Three drainage patterns complete the diagram kit: dendritic (tree-like, on uniform rock — most of the Gangetic plain), trellis (folded terrain — Siwalik foothills), radial/centripetal (domes/basins — Amarkantak radial, Loktak centripetal), plus antecedent vs superimposed as the process pair (Subansiri antecedent in the Himalaya; Damodar–Subarnarekha superimposed on the Chotanagpur shield). The Mains move is to fuse drainage with the trunk's tectonics: the Himalaya exist because the Indo-Australian plate subducts under Eurasia (§3 convergent), so Himalayan rivers are young, restless, and flood-prone; the Peninsula is a stable Gondwana fragment, so its rivers are old, seasonal, and dam-friendly. One line settles "compare" questions: Himalayan rivers make land (plains, deltas); Peninsular rivers reveal land (they expose the shield they drain).

PART F — Monsoon anomalies and extreme weather: how to reason, not recite

The trunk's six-step monsoon mechanism (ITCZ migration, differential heating, jet choreography with STJ retreat + TEJ onset, Tibetan heat engine, ENSO–IOD, MJO active/break pulse) plus the pattern facts (Kerala onset ~1 June, 75% June–September, Mawsynram, Western Ghats orographic wall vs Deccan rain shadow, retreating-monsoon Tamil Nadu rains Oct–Dec, western disturbances for rabi) is the complete theory; what fails in Mains is application when the monsoon misbehaves. Learn four anomaly grammars. (i) Break monsoon — the trough shifts to the Himalayan foothills: plains bake, foothills flood (classic July-break pattern); MJO phase + suppressed convection explains it. (ii) El Niño vs positive IOD tug-of-war — El Niño weakens the Walker circulation's Indian arm (descending air over India), but a positive IOD (warm western Indian Ocean) pumps moisture into the Arabian Sea branch; recent El Niño years with near-normal rain are IOD rescues — always cite both, never one. (iii) Cloudbursts and mini-cloudburst clusters — orographic locking + warm-cloud moisture convergence over steep Himalayan catchments (Uttarakhand 2013, Himachal 2023 events as type-cases); warming raises the moisture ceiling (Clausius–Clapeyron ~7%/°C), so extremes intensify even where totals don't. (iv) Cyclone geography shift — Arabian Sea warming raises intense-cyclone odds (research consensus the trunk notes); Bay remains frequency-dominant (Odisha 1999, Phailin 2013, Amphan 2020, Dana/Remal 2024) but the Arabian Sea's rising share is the climate-change exhibit.

The heat engine behind the anomalies is one paragraph: insolation → pressure belts (ITCZ, subtropical highs at ~30°, subpolar lows) → trades/westerlies/polar easterlies deflected by Coriolis (Ferrel's law) → jets steering weather. Every extreme-weather answer should open by locating itself in this engine, then name the anomaly grammar, then cite IMD evidence (Mission Mausam 2024 upgrades, MJO monitoring). Closing synthesis: the monsoon is not failing, it is redistributing — fewer rainy days, heavier downpours, longer breaks; the policy question is storage and drainage, not just averages.

PART G — Ocean–climate coupling and the diagram method

The trunk's oceanography (floor relief, thermocline, salinity contrasts — Red Sea ~40‰, Baltic low, Bay lower than Arabian Sea; the 12-current table; tides with Khambhat/Kutch funnels and the Hooghly bore) and its teleconnection line (ENSO + IOD jointly scripting Indian rainfall) converge on one Mains skill: explaining land weather from ocean physics. Master three couplings. ENSO: normal years see Peruvian upwelling + warm pool near Indonesia driving the Walker cell's rising arm over the maritime continent; El Niño flattens the Pacific gradient, the rising arm migrates east, and anomalous subsidence sits over India — hence weakened-monsoon odds; La Niña amplifies the normal pattern. IOD: positive phase (warm west, cool east) strengthens the Somali-jet cross-equatorial flow and the Arabian Sea moisture feed — India's independent booster. MJO: the 30–60-day eastward pulse whose Indian-Ocean phase triggers active spells and whose Pacific phase deepens breaks. The decision table for answers: El Niño + negative IOD = drought watch; La Niña + positive IOD = flood watch; El Niño + positive IOD = the compensated year (cite the recent rescues).

End with the diagram method that converts this lesson into marks: (1) every geomorphology answer gets a cross-section (plate boundary, river long-profile, or coast-to-trench floor relief — shelf/Mumbai High, slope, rise, abyssal plain, ridge, trench/Mariana ~10,994 m); (2) every climatology answer gets a circulation sketch (pressure belts + named winds + the season's jet positions); (3) every ocean answer gets a current map with warm/cold colour logic and the two meeting-zone fisheries (Grand Banks, Kuroshio–Oyashio) plus the upwelling trio (Peru–Atacama, Benguela–Namib, California). Pair diagrams with the trunk's PYQ anchors (S-wave shadow proof, three boundary types, west-coast deserts, NE monsoon, Mawsynram, Sundarbans, Zone V, Sumatra 2004) and the Deep Ocean Mission (2021, Central Indian Ocean Basin nodules) as the blue-economy closer.
