7. Worked Case 3 — The Transfer Order: Loyalty, Legality, and the Recorded Dissent

You are the District Collector. The State headquarters issues oral instructions to transfer your Sub-Divisional Magistrate — an officer with an unblemished record who recently sealed three illegal sand-mining sites linked to politically connected contractors. The written order cites \"administrative exigency.\" The SDM asks you what to do; the local press is asking questions.

Facts: oral pressure + written pretext; honest subordinate punished for lawful action; mining interests with political cover; press attention (partial sunlight). Verify: the SDM's sealing orders and their legal basis (Mines and Minerals Act, NGT orders — Deepak Kumar, 2012 — environmental clearance for minor minerals); the transfer file's noting chain (who proposed, what reasons recorded); the mining sites' current status (resumed operations?). Dilemmas: loyalty to superiors and career prudence versus legality (the transfer as victimisation for lawful acts), integrity (complicity in shielding illegality), and leadership (the message to every honest subordinate watching — the transfer teaches the district's officers what honesty costs). Stakeholders: the SDM (career, morale), the mining-affected public (revenue loss, environmental damage, road destruction), honest officers district-wide (the precedent), headquarters (political convenience), the press and courts (the sunlight channels), yourself (conscience, career, credibility).

Options: (i) Execute silently — preserves headquarters' favour; betrays the SDM, legitimises the mining nexus, teaches the district that honesty is punished (precedent-cost catastrophic). (ii) Refuse openly and face defiance proceedings — heroic, satisfies conscience, but abandons the district to a compliant successor and converts a winnable institutional fight into a personal martyrdom (the heroics pitfall, §5). (iii) The institutional-resistance sequence: confirm the SDM's sealings are legally sound (record the verification); note on file requesting written, reasoned transfer grounds (\"administrative exigency\" tested — the transfer policy's tenure protections invoked); brief headquarters in writing on the reputational-legal risk (NGT's continuing mandamus, press scrutiny, the victimisation optics); protect the SDM through the Civil Services Board architecture (T.S.R. Subramanian, 2013 — premature-transfer safeguards); brief the press truthfully within conduct-rule bounds (facts of the sealing drive, no speculation on motives); if overruled in writing, comply-and-record the dissent, keep lawful copies, and activate the whistle route (CVC–PIDPI) only if illegality (not merely unfairness) is demonstrable. Evaluation: (iii) passes legality (every step rule-backed), proportionality (resistance scaled to the wrong — procedural, not theatrical), precedent (the district learns honesty is defended through channels), sustainability (the Collector survives to defend the next SDM). Decision & implementation: written noting within 24 hours; SDM briefed (stay the course, document everything, no press statements from him); headquarters briefed in writing within 48 hours; mining enforcement intensified visibly (the drive continues — the transfer, if it comes, cannot be read as surrender). Safeguards: the transfer file preserved; the Board's tenure norms cited for the next case; a district mining-enforcement SOP (joint teams, videographed seizures, e-challan trails) so enforcement no longer depends on one SDM's courage — personal virtue converted into system design (§6's rule).

8. Worked Case 4 — The Slum Demolition: Development, Displacement, and the Humane Sequence

You are the Municipal Commissioner. A court orders clearance of an \"encroached\" lakeside settlement (800 families, 20+ years' residence, voter IDs and school enrolments on record) for lake rejuvenation within 60 days. No rehabilitation plan exists; the monsoon arrives in 45 days; a builder lobby pushes for swift demolition; residents threaten self-immolation.

Facts: binding court order (contempt risk if ignored); long-settled community with documentary roots (adverse-possession equities, Olga Tellis, 1985 — pavement dwellers' right to livelihood under Article 21 — procedure must be just, fair, reasonable); no R&R plan; monsoon deadline making demolition a humanitarian catastrophe; vested interests favouring haste. Verify: the exact order (clearance vs clearance-with-rehabilitation — most such orders contemplate the latter); land records (lake boundary — the \"encroachment\" often includes survey ambiguities); residents' documentation (ration cards, enrolments — the equities file); alternative-site availability (housing-board stock, rental-voucher feasibility). Dilemmas: rule of law (court compliance) versus humane administration (800 families into the monsoon); development (the lake's ecological and flood-mitigation value — real) versus displacement (livelihoods, schooling, community — real); haste (builder–contempt pressure) versus due process (rehabilitation sequencing). Stakeholders: the 800 families (shelter, livelihood, children's schooling), the lake-dependent city (floods, water, ecology), the court (order's dignity), the builder lobby (profit in haste), the municipal staff (execution trauma — demolitions wound the demolishers too), future encroachers (the precedent).

Options: (i) Swift demolition per the order's letter — complies technically; manufactures a humanitarian crisis, invites judicial censure on review (courts punish inhumane compliance), destroys municipal legitimacy for a decade. (ii) Defy-or-delay (plead helplessness, slow-walk) — contempt risk, rule-of-law erosion, the lake's degradation continues, and delay without a plan is merely cowardice with a calendar. (iii) The humane-compliance sequence: seek the court's leave for a phased rehabilitation-first schedule (interim application — compliance through rehabilitation, citing Olga Tellis procedure and the monsoon impossibility — courts routinely grant structured timelines where good faith is shown); simultaneously stand up transit housing (school buildings post-session, rental vouchers, housing-board allotments — the R&R floor: shelter before demolition, always); enumerate transparently (biometric list published, grievance window — the entitlement made legible, ghost-claims excluded); demolish in phases post-relocation with videography and compensation disbursed first; prosecute the builder-nexus attempts at influence (the haste-lobby investigated, not indulged). Evaluation: (iii) passes legality (court's leave sought — compliance, not defiance), public interest (lake saved and families sheltered — the false binary refused), proportionality (the 20-year equities weighed against the ecological imperative), precedent (the city learns encroachment ends in rehabilitation, not impunity — deterrence preserved through process). Implementation: court application within 7 days; enumeration within 21; transit ready before day 40; phase-1 relocation days 40–60; demolition only of vacated structures; school-continuity (transfer certificates, transport) and livelihood-bridge (vendor-space in relocation site) as non-negotiables. Safeguards: the lake boundary surveyed and fenced (future encroachment physically prevented); a standing R&R SOP for all future clearances (rehabilitation-first as municipal doctrine); social audit of the relocation (resident-committee sign-off) — the next Commissioner inherits a humane protocol, not a trauma.

9. Worked Case 5 — The Tender Trap: Procurement Integrity Under Time Pressure

You head a Public Works division. A flood-damaged bridge must be rebuilt before the next monsoon (7 months). The Superintending Engineer pressures you to split the work into sub-threshold packages (avoiding e-tendering) and award them to \"reliable\" contractors. Two of the three firms suggested have directors related to local politicians; the third did shoddy work last year. Your Executive Engineer warns that proper tendering takes 3 months, leaving only 4 for construction — \"too tight,\" he says.

Facts: genuine urgency (monsoon deadline, public safety); proposed splitting as tender-avoidance (GFR 2017's anti-splitting norms; CVC guidelines — splitting to evade thresholds is a listed irregularity); connected contractors (conflict-of-interest equities); time-pressure as the corruption vector (urgency manufacturing opacity). Verify: the threshold rules (works-manual financial powers — what e-tendering triggers); the contractors' records (past performance, debarment lists, directorship patterns); the realistic tender timeline (emergency-procurement provisions — most codes provide accelerated tendering for calamity works — the lawful fast track); the bridge's technical state (temporary restoration feasibility — Bailey bridging as the monsoon stopgap). Dilemmas: speed versus integrity (lives depend on the bridge; the exchequer and the law depend on the process); loyalty to the SE (hierarchy, ACR-authorship) versus probity (the splitting proposal as an unlawful order — the \"manifestly illegal\" doctrine); pragmatism (\"reliable\" contractors, known devils) versus competition (unknown angels through open process). Stakeholders: bridge-users (safety — the collapsed-bridge test rules out shoddy construction absolutely), the exchequer (split-awards inflate costs 15–30 per cent by CVC's own estimates), honest contractors (excluded by the fix), the SE–EE chain (career equities), yourself (criminal liability under the PCA 2018 — bribe-giving/conspiracy exposure; §17A protects honest decisions, not rigged ones).

Options: (i) Split and award as pressed — meets the deadline on paper; commits a documented irregularity (the split is visible in every audit), enriches connected firms, risks a shoddy bridge (the third firm's record) — and the monsoon washes away both the bridge and the defence. (ii) Full-length tendering regardless of timeline — pristine process, collapsed schedule (the bridge unfinished when floods return — integrity that kills commuters is process-fetishism, not ethics). (iii) The lawful-fast sequence: invoke the emergency-procurement provisions (accelerated tendering — shortened bid periods for calamity works, e-tendering retained — speed through the lawful fast track, not around it); parallel-track a temporary restoration (Bailey/temporary bridging within weeks — the monsoon stopgap decoupling safety from construction); document the SE's oral pressure (seek written directions — the \"put it in writing\" shield; unlawful written orders are rarely issued, and the request itself usually kills the proposal); run e-tendering with integrity-pact clauses, past-performance weighting (the shoddy firm's record scored, not whispered), and third-party quality audit (the construction watched); brief the SE in writing on the audit-criminal exposure (CVC, PCA, CAG performance-audit — the fear-of-God memo, respectful in tone, devastating in content). Evaluation: (iii) passes legality (emergency provisions, not evasions), public interest (temporary safety + permanent integrity), proportionality (resistance through procedure — the written-directions request — not confrontation), precedent (the division learns urgency routes through emergency clauses, never around them). Implementation: temporary bridge in 30 days; emergency tender floated by day 10, awarded by day 45; construction months 2–7 with milestone-linked payments and third-party tests; every oral instruction converted to file. Safeguards: a division-level emergency-works SOP (calamity triggers, accelerated timelines, retained e-tendering); contractor performance-rating published (the shoddy record institutionalised as data); the case-study briefed to incoming engineers — urgency-with-integrity as divisional doctrine.

10. The Case-Writer's Craft: Time-Boxes, Test-Tables, and the Safeguard Habit

Sections 1, 5 and 6 state the rewards, pitfalls and pointers; this section builds the exam-hall craft — because case studies are 125 marks of structured writing under a clock, and craft converts knowledge into marks. Time-boxing: the GS-4 paper's Part B typically fields six cases in roughly 90 minutes — 15 minutes per case, brutally enforced. The box splits: 2 minutes to decode (underline the decision-maker's role, the dilemma's horns, the constraints — role + horns + constraints as the decode triple), 11 minutes to write (the 7 headings as sub-headings, verbatim — §6's rule — with word-budgets: facts 30, dilemmas 40, stakeholders 50, options 120, evaluation 60, decision 80, safeguards 40 — roughly 420 words of dense structure), 2 minutes to audit (the checklist: three options? law cited? silent stakeholders? implementation sequenced? safeguard institutional?). The case that overruns steals from its siblings — the 20-minute masterpiece plus the 10-minute stub scores below two 15-minute completes: completeness beats brilliance, uniformly.

Test-tables (§6's \"one ethical-test table per case\") are the evaluation step's compression engine: options as rows, tests as columns (legality, public interest, proportionality, precedent, sustainability, conscience — §2's six), ticks-and-crosses with one-line justifications — the table that replaces a paragraph of prose and proves systematic weighing visibly. Two more tables earn their keep: the stakeholder table (party–stake–voice: who gains, who pays, who decides — with the silent row always present: future generations, the unorganised, the affected-who-cannot-speak) and the implementation ladder (24 hours / 7 days / 30 days / standing SOP — the decision sequenced into executability, each rung with an owner and a record). Diagrams are sparing but potent: the dilemma's horns sketched (value A versus value B with the creative-third-option bridge), the accountability web for governance cases. Language discipline: first-person-decided (\"I will…\" — the officer deciding, not the essayist musing), law-named (the Act, the Article, the rule — §5's anti-vagueness rule), reason-recorded (every discretionary choice with its \"because\" — the speaking-order habit), and heroics-free (no resignations, no press conferences, no vigilante threats as first resorts — the institutional ladder climbed rung by rung: record, escalate, whistle-route, transfer-request, resign-last).

The safeguard habit (§6's closing rule — convert personal virtue into system design) is the craft's signature and the examiner's delight: every case ends with the institution that outlives the officer (the transfer-protection file and mining SOP in Case 3; the R&R doctrine and lake-fencing in Case 4; the emergency-works SOP and contractor ratings in Case 5; the triage protocol in §4's flood case; the corrected-MIS and whistle-route in §3's whistle case). The safeguard proves the candidate thinks like an administrator (systems, not episodes) rather than a moralist (sermons, not structures) — and it answers the paper's deepest question, the one beneath every case: will this candidate leave the office better-designed than she found it? Practise the habit until it is reflex: no case answer is complete without its final line beginning \"Henceforth, the office will…\" — the system-design sentence that converts 125 marks of dilemmas into one sustained demonstration of governability.

Trap watch. Six cases in ~90 minutes — 15 minutes each (2 decode + 11 write + 2 audit); completeness beats brilliance, overruns steal from siblings. The 7 headings (facts → dilemmas → stakeholders → options → evaluation → decision → safeguards) appear verbatim as sub-headings — examiners scan structure. Decode triple: role + horns + constraints underlined in 2 minutes. Minimum three options including \"do nothing\" explicitly rejected; heroics (resignation, press) are last resorts, never openers. Evaluation runs six tests (legality, public interest, proportionality, precedent, sustainability, conscience) — one test-table per case beats prose. Stakeholder tables always carry the silent row (future generations, unorganised, voiceless). Implementation ladders (24h / 7d / 30d / SOP) with owners and records; first-person-decided language with law named. Transfer-for-honesty is resisted through written-grounds requests + Board-tenure norms + recorded dissent (T.S.R. Subramanian, 2013). Demolition complies through rehabilitation-first court leave (Olga Tellis, 1985 — just-fair-reasonable procedure). Tender pressure routes through emergency-procurement provisions + written-directions shield + temporary restoration — never splitting (GFR/CVC irregularity), never full-length delay. Every case closes \"Henceforth, the office will…\" — virtue converted into system design.
