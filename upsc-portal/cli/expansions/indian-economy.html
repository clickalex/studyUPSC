12. The growth story told honestly — 1991–2026

The trunk of the Indian economy narrative runs from crisis to confidence: the 1991 BoP shock and Rao–Manmohan reforms, the 2003–08 boom (~9% average), the taper-tantrum stress of 2013, the formalisation wave after 2017, and the post-COVID run that made India the world's fastest-growing large economy by the mid-2020s. The Mains mistake is to narrate this as one smooth ascent; the honest version has three fractures the examiner rewards you for naming. First, growth without commensurate formal employment — GDP quadrupled in real terms since 1991 while organised-sector job growth lagged for most of those years; the 2020s formalisation (EPFO, e-Shram 29+ crore, GST-registered base) is the correction still mid-flight. Second, a consumption-led recovery with a hesitant private-capex cycle — government capex (~3% of GDP) has carried investment since FY21; private GFCF revival is the hinge on which 7%+ sustained growth turns. Third, the external comfort that masks concentration risk — headline exports past US$820 bn (FY25, goods + services) and sub-1.5% CAD look strong, but merchandise competitiveness in labour-intensive lines (textiles, leather, toys) still trails Vietnam and Bangladesh.

13. Two debates that decide answers — fiscal-monetary and formal-informal

The fiscal–monetary debate is where economy answers are won. The fiscal story: Centre fiscal deficit gliding from FY25 ~4.8% toward ~4.4% (FY26 budgeted) under the new debt-GDP anchor, with expenditure quality transformed by the capex push — but with GST 2.0's rate rationalisation (effective 22 September 2025) creating a revenue-math question that the 16th Finance Commission (2026–31, Panagariya) devolution arithmetic must absorb. The monetary story: RBI's 4% ± 2% CPI framework (2016, retained 2026–31), the corridor anatomy (SDF floor from 2022, repo, MSF roof), and a banking system at multi-year GNPA lows via IBC-NCLT plus NARCL (2021) — but with transmission, NBFC fragility, and the rupee's managed float in the high-80s/USD as live issues. The synthesis line: India now has rules-based fiscal plumbing and inflation-anchored monetary plumbing; the remaining argument is about expenditure composition (capex vs revenue) and credit depth (credit-GDP ratio), not about regime design.

The formal–informal debate is the second decider. The trunk gives you the stack — Jan Dhan (56+ crore), Aadhaar-DBT, UPI as the world's largest real-time rail (1,500+ crore transactions/month scale), e-Rupee pilots, Account Aggregator, Mudra (~48 crore loans), Udyam, TReDS — so the question is never "is formalisation happening" but "is it deep enough to change productivity?" Three tests work in answers: (i) firm formalisation lags worker formalisation — MSMEs (~6.4 crore) register on Udyam but stay sub-scale; (ii) credit formalisation via AA/OCEN is the frontier that converts transaction trails into working capital; (iii) social-security formalisation (e-Shram → Code on Social Security's gig/aggregator provisions) is where dignity meets data. The closing line that examiners remember: formalisation measured in accounts is the first decade's achievement; formalisation measured in productivity and protection is this decade's assignment.

14. The external sector and the Viksit Bharat test

Anchor the external answer in the BoP identity: a merchandise deficit financed by a services surplus plus remittances (~$120 bn+) plus capital flows — which is why a CAD in the ≤1.5% band coexists with import dependence. Then layer the 2025–26 specifics the trunk records: external debt ~$700 bn (~19% of GDP, long-term dominated, reserves covering 10+ months of imports); gross FDI $60–80 bn/year from Singapore–Mauritius–US into services, software, trading, autos; and the tariff shock — US tariffs up to 50% on Indian goods (August 2025) amid the Russia-oil dispute, answered by FTA catch-up (UAE CEPA ✓, UK FTA 2025 ✓, EU concluded in principle January 2026, US negotiating) plus rupee-settlement pilots. The Mains frame: friendshoring is the opportunity, weaponised tariffs the risk, and FTA diversification the insurance.

Close with the Viksit Bharat @2047 test, applying the trunk's structural list as pass/fail criteria: (1) manufacturing GVA stuck ~14–17% — PLI + four Labour Codes + National Logistics Policy (13%→8% logistics-cost target) must lift it toward 25%, or the jobs arithmetic fails; (2) GFCF ~30% must be held with private capex replacing government capex at the margin; (3) NEP 2020 + apprenticeship scale must convert demographic dividend into skilled dividend (ASER and PLFS as evidence); (4) 500-GW-non-fossil-2030 and the Green Hydrogen Mission (2023) must be financed while coal pays the transition; (5) EU CBAM and FTA rules-of-origin will audit Indian exports regardless of intent. The one-line conclusion: India has crossed every size milestone on schedule; the 2047 question is entirely about composition — of jobs, of capital, of exports.
