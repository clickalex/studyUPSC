Ecosystems, Food Chains & Nutrient Cycles

Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Levels, energy-nutrient logic, succession, productivity, aquatic ecosystems. Concept-pair recall.

1. Structural levels

  • Organism → population → community → ecosystem (biotic + abiotic interacting) → biome (large climatic region) → biosphere. Ecotone = transition zone between ecosystems (mangrove-estuary, grassland-forest edge) with edge effect; ecological niche = functional role (habitat = address, niche = profession).
  • Components: producers (autotrophs), consumers (herbivore-carnivore-omnivore; trophic levels), decomposers (fungi-bacteria — mineralisers), abiotic (inorganic-organic-climatic).

2. Energy flow & nutrient cycling

  • Energy: one-way, ~10% transfer between trophic levels (Lindeman); food chain (grazing vs detritus) + food web stability; energy pyramids always upright (biomass-number pyramids can invert — parasitic-tree chains).
  • Nutrient cycles: gaseous (C, N, O — global pools in atmosphere) vs sedimentary (P, S — crustal pools); phosphorus has no significant gaseous phase; nitrogen fixation (biological: rhizobia-cyanobacteria; industrial: Haber).
  • Ecological succession: bare area → pioneer (lichens on rock) → seral stages → climax community; primary (new substrate) vs secondary (disturbed, soil intact); hydrarch vs xerarch directions.

3. Productivity & aquatic systems

  • GPP − respiration = NPP; NPP rank: coral reefs-tropical rainforest-estuary-mangrove top, open ocean-desert bottom (ocean low per-unit but vast); standing crop vs turnover.
  • Wetlands: Ramsar definition (1971; used by humans) — India ~90 Ramsar sites (2025 band, highest in Asia); functions: flood buffer, recharge, carbon sink; threats: eutrophication-invasion-reclamation.
  • Mangroves: halophytes, viviparous germination, pneumatophores; sundari (Sundarbans); cyclone-shield value. Corals: polyp + zooxanthellae symbiosis; bleaching = symbiont expulsion under heat stress; India: Gulf of Kutch, Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep, Andaman-Nicobar. Estuaries: brackish, high-nutrient nursery (hooghly-mahanadi).

MCQ hooks

  • 10% law = Lindeman; energy one-way, nutrients cycle — direction pair.
  • Pyramid of energy always upright; number/biomass can invert.
  • Gaseous cycles (C-N-O) vs sedimentary (P-S) — cycle-type pair.
  • Ecotone = transition; edge effect high diversity claim often asked.
  • Coral bleaching = zooxanthellae loss (not polyp death directly).
  • Mangrove adaptations: vivipary + pneumatophores — term pair.
  • NPP: estuary-reef high, open ocean low (per-unit-area trap).

6. Structure of an Ecosystem

An ecosystem is a functional unit in which a biotic community interacts with its abiotic environment through energy flow and nutrient cycling. Its structure has two components. Abiotic — climatic factors (light, temperature, rainfall, humidity, wind) and edaphic factors (soil, pH, minerals, topography). Biotic — organised by nutritional role:

  • Producers (autotrophs) — green plants, algae and chemosynthetic bacteria that fix solar or chemical energy into organic matter.
  • Consumers (heterotrophs) — primary consumers are herbivores; secondary are carnivores feeding on herbivores; tertiary and quaternary follow. Omnivores occupy more than one level and detritivores (earthworms, termites) fragment dead matter.
  • Decomposers (saprotrophs) — bacteria and fungi that mineralise organic matter, returning nutrients to the abiotic pool. Without them nutrient cycling halts, which is why they are the true engine of the ecosystem.

Two terms carry precise meanings. A habitat is the physical address where an organism lives; a niche is its functional profession — its role, resource use and interactions. Gause's competitive exclusion principle holds that two species cannot occupy an identical niche indefinitely; one displaces the other, or they undergo resource partitioning. Distinguish the fundamental niche (theoretically usable) from the realised niche (actually occupied after competition).

7. Energy Flow — the Laws That Govern It

Energy flow is unidirectional: it enters as sunlight, passes through trophic levels and is lost as heat. It is never cycled, unlike nutrients. This follows from thermodynamics — the first law (energy is neither created nor destroyed, only transformed) and the second (every transformation dissipates some energy as unusable heat).

Productivity vocabulary must be exact:

  • Gross Primary Productivity (GPP) — total organic matter fixed by producers per unit area per unit time.
  • Net Primary Productivity (NPP) = GPP − respiration by producers. This is the energy actually available to herbivores.
  • Secondary productivity — the rate of assimilation by consumers.
  • Standing crop — the biomass present at a given moment, which is a stock, not a rate.

Lindeman's Ten Percent Law states that only about 10% of energy transfers from one trophic level to the next, the rest being lost in respiration, movement, heat and unassimilated matter. Two consequences follow and are frequently examined: food chains rarely exceed four or five trophic levels, because energy becomes insufficient; and a given land area can support far more vegetarians than non-vegetarians, since each additional level discards 90%.

Ecological pyramids represent trophic structure. The pyramid of energy is always upright — a direct consequence of the second law. The pyramid of number can be inverted in a tree ecosystem, where one tree supports thousands of insects. The pyramid of biomass is inverted in the ocean, where a small standing crop of rapidly reproducing phytoplankton supports a larger mass of zooplankton and fish — the classic exception, explained by high turnover rate rather than high standing stock.

A food chain is linear; a food web is the interlinked reality and confers stability, since predators can switch prey. Two chain types: the grazing food chain begins with living producers, dominant in aquatic systems; the detritus food chain begins with dead organic matter and dominates terrestrial and forest systems, where most production is not grazed but falls as litter.

8. Biogeochemical Cycles

Nutrients, unlike energy, cycle. Distinguish gaseous cycles (carbon, nitrogen, oxygen — reservoir in the atmosphere or hydrosphere, relatively fast and self-regulating) from sedimentary cycles (phosphorus, sulphur, calcium — reservoir in rock and sediment, slow, and prone to becoming a limiting factor).

Nitrogen cycle steps in order, a reliable question: nitrogen fixation (atmospheric N₂ to ammonia, by Rhizobium in legume root nodules, free-living Azotobacter and Clostridium, cyanobacteria such as Anabaena and Nostoc, plus lightning and the industrial Haber-Bosch process) → ammonification (organic matter to ammonia) → nitrification (ammonia to nitrite by Nitrosomonas, nitrite to nitrate by Nitrobacter) → assimilation by plants → denitrification (nitrate back to N₂ by Pseudomonas, under anaerobic conditions). Azolla, a fern hosting Anabaena, is the classic rice-paddy biofertiliser.

Phosphorus has no significant gaseous phase; it weathers from rock, is taken up by plants and returns via decomposition. Because its cycle is slow, phosphorus is the commonest limiting nutrient in freshwater — which is why detergent and fertiliser runoff triggers eutrophication: nutrient enrichment causes algal blooms, blocking light; when the algae die, decomposers consume dissolved oxygen, producing hypoxic dead zones and fish kills. Biological oxygen demand (BOD) rises as organic pollution rises, and is the standard indicator.

Carbon cycle — photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion and oceanic exchange. Note ocean acidification: absorbed CO₂ forms carbonic acid, lowering pH and impairing the calcification of corals and shellfish. This is chemically distinct from global warming, though it shares the same cause — a distinction examiners test.

9. Succession, Interactions and Major Ecosystem Types

Ecological succession is the orderly change in community composition over time toward a stable climax. Primary succession begins on a bare, never-colonised substrate (fresh lava, bare rock, glacial till), where lichens are the pioneer species and the process is very slow because soil must first form. Secondary succession begins where a community was destroyed but soil remains (abandoned farmland, burnt forest) and is much faster. Hydrarch succession proceeds from water toward mesic conditions; xerarch from dry conditions toward mesic — both converge on the mesic climax. Sere is the whole sequence; each transitional stage is a seral community.

Species interactions, with the sign convention: mutualism (+/+; lichen as alga and fungus, mycorrhiza, pollination); commensalism (+/0; epiphytic orchid on a tree, cattle egret with grazing cattle); parasitism (+/−; Cuscuta on a host); predation (+/−); competition (−/−); amensalism (−/0; allelopathy, as in the inhibitory chemicals of Parthenium).

Major ecosystem types. Terrestrial biomes are determined chiefly by temperature and precipitation: tropical rainforest (highest productivity and biodiversity, nutrient-poor soils because nutrients are locked in biomass), savanna, desert, temperate grassland, temperate deciduous forest, taiga and tundra. Aquatic systems divide into freshwater (lentic standing water with littoral, limnetic, profundal and benthic zones; lotic flowing water) and marine. Marine zones by light: the photic or euphotic zone where photosynthesis occurs, the disphotic twilight zone, and the aphotic zone. Estuaries and coral reefs are among the most productive ecosystems on Earth. Corals require warm, shallow, clear, saline water and live in mutualism with zooxanthellae algae; when stressed by temperature they expel these algae and undergo bleaching. Mangroves tolerate saline, anaerobic mud through pneumatophores (breathing roots), stilt roots, salt-excreting leaves and vivipary (seeds germinating on the parent), and provide storm-surge protection and nurseries for fish.

Ecosystem services, from the Millennium Ecosystem Assessment, are a ready framework: provisioning (food, timber, water), regulating (climate, flood, disease, pollination), cultural (recreation, spiritual value) and supporting (soil formation, nutrient cycling, primary production).

Trap watch. Energy flows unidirectionally; nutrients cycle. The pyramid of energy is always upright; biomass pyramids invert in oceans and number pyramids in tree ecosystems. NPP, not GPP, is available to consumers. Phosphorus has no gaseous phase and is the usual limiting nutrient in freshwater. Lichens are pioneers of primary succession; secondary succession is faster because soil already exists. Coral bleaching is loss of zooxanthellae, not death of the coral itself. Ocean acidification and global warming are distinct consequences of rising CO₂.

Basic concepts

  • Ecology (Haeckel 1866); ecosystem (Tansley 1935) = biotic + abiotic components interacting; biome = large regional ecosystem defined by climate and dominant vegetation (tundra, taiga, temperate deciduous, grassland, desert, tropical rainforest, savanna, chaparral); biosphere; ecotone = transition zone (edge effect — higher diversity; e.g., mangroves, estuaries, grassland–forest edge); ecotype; ecological niche (Grinnell/Elton/Hutchinson — functional role; Gause's competitive exclusion principle); habitat = address, niche = profession.
  • Levels: individual → population → community → ecosystem → landscape → biome → biosphere. Population attributes: natality, mortality, sex ratio, age pyramids (expanding/stable/declining), density, dispersion (clumped commonest); growth: exponential (J) vs logistic (S; carrying capacity K); r- vs K-selected species; keystone species (disproportionate effect relative to abundance — sea otter, elephant, fig trees, starfish Pisaster; the tiger is an apex predator and umbrella species rather than a classic keystone); umbrella species (tiger, snow leopard); flagship species (charismatic — tiger, panda); indicator species (lichens — SO₂; amphibians; mayflies; corals); foundation species (corals, kelp); invasive/alien species (Lantana, Parthenium, water hyacinth (Eichhornia), Prosopis juliflora, African catfish, Nile tilapia, red-eared slider, apple snail, Senna spectabilis (Wayanad), Acacia, golden apple snail, Argentine ant; Charru mussel (Kerala)); endemic species; pioneer species.
  • Interactions: mutualism (+/+: lichen, mycorrhizae, fig–wasp, coral–zooxanthellae), commensalism (+/0: epiphytes/orchids, cattle egret–cattle, barnacles–whale, clownfish–anemone), amensalism (0/−: Penicillium), parasitism (+/−: cuckoo brood parasitism, Cuscuta), predation, competition (−/−), protocooperation. Allelopathy (chemical inhibition — eucalyptus, black walnut). Symbiosis = close association (any of above).
  • Adaptations: Allen's rule (extremities shorter in cold), Bergmann's rule (larger body in cold), Gloger's rule (darker in humid), xerophytes (CAM photosynthesis — cacti, pineapple, agave), halophytes (mangroves: pneumatophores, vivipary, salt glands), hydrophytes (aerenchyma), thermoregulation (ectotherm/endotherm; hibernation vs aestivation — snails/lungfish), CAM/C4/C3 plants (C4 — maize, sugarcane, sorghum; efficient in hot; C3 — rice, wheat); phenotypic plasticity.

Energy flow & productivity

  • Sun → producers (autotrophs — photosynthetic or chemosynthetic (deep-sea vents, Nitrosomonas)) → primary consumers (herbivores) → secondary → tertiary; decomposers (bacteria, fungi) vs detritivores (earthworms, millipedes, dung beetles, vultures — scavengers).
  • 10% law (Lindeman 1942): ~10% of energy transferred to next trophic level; hence food chains short (4–5 links); energy flow unidirectional; nutrients cycle. Grazing food chain (starts with green plants) vs detritus food chain (starts with dead organic matter — dominant in forests, mangroves, deep sea); food web = interlinked chains; trophic level = position (an organism can occupy several); biomagnification (concentration increases up chain — DDT, mercury (Minamata), PCBs, microplastics; peregrine falcon eggshell thinning) vs bioaccumulation (in one organism over time).
  • Ecological pyramids (Elton): number, biomass, energy. Energy pyramid always upright; biomass inverted in aquatic/pond ecosystems (phytoplankton < zooplankton at a moment; high turnover); number inverted in tree-based (one tree → many insects → parasites) and parasitic chains; grassland upright.
  • Productivity: GPP − respiration = NPP; highest NPP per unit area: tropical rainforests, estuaries, coral reefs, swamps/marshes; lowest: deserts, open ocean (but oceans contribute most total NPP because of area); standing crop = biomass at a time; secondary productivity (consumers). Global NPP ~170 bn t dry matter/yr; oceans ~55 bn t.
  • Homeostasis, resilience vs resistance, ecological efficiency, trophic cascade (Yellowstone wolves → elk → willows → beavers/rivers), ecological succession (Clements): primary (bare rock/lava — lichens (pioneers) → mosses → herbs → shrubs → trees; xerarch) vs secondary (after disturbance — faster; e.g., abandoned farm); hydrarch (pond → marsh → meadow → forest); sere/seral stages; climax community (monoclimax vs polyclimax); autogenic vs allogenic; facilitation, tolerance, inhibition models; Gaia hypothesis (Lovelock).

Major ecosystem types (quick facts)

  • Forest: tropical rainforest (max biodiversity; nutrient-poor soils — laterite; rapid decomposition), temperate deciduous, boreal/taiga (largest terrestrial biome; conifers; podzol soils), mangroves (Sundarbans largest; India 4,992 km² ISFR 2023; WB 42%, Gujarat 24%; Avicennia, Rhizophora, Sonneratia; Pichavaram, Bhitarkanika 2nd largest, Coringa, Baratang); grasslands — savanna, steppe, prairie, pampas, veld, downs; India — shola-grassland (Western Ghats), terai, banni (Kutch), grasslands wrongly classed as 'wastelands'; deserts (Thar — 'green desert'; cold desert Ladakh/Spiti); tundra (permafrost; arctic/alpine); aquatic — lentic (still: lakes, ponds; zones — littoral, limnetic, profundal, benthic; thermal stratification — epilimnion/thermocline/hypolimnion; oligotrophic vs eutrophic; cultural eutrophication; algal bloom/dead zones — Gulf of Mexico, Arabian Sea Noctiluca), lotic (flowing), wetlands (Ramsar; kidneys of landscape; 4.86% of India; India 91 sites — Tamil Nadu most (20), then UP; largest Sundarbans; smallest Renuka/Vembannur; Montreux Record — Keoladeo & Loktak; Chilika removed 2002); estuaries (brackish; high NPP; nurseries); coral reefs (fringing/barrier/atoll — Darwin; 25% marine species on <1% area; India — Gulf of Kutch, Gulf of Mannar, Lakshadweep (atolls), Andaman & Nicobar; bleaching at >1 °C above summer max for 4+ weeks (DHW); 4th global bleaching event 2023–25 — 84% of reefs affected; zooxanthellae Symbiodinium; Great Barrier Reef; coral restoration — biorock, coral gardening (Mithapur, Gulf of Kutch)); seagrass (Gulf of Mannar/Palk Bay — dugong; blue carbon); kelp forests; hydrothermal vents (chemosynthesis); polar (krill; sea ice minimum Arctic Sept; Antarctic record low 2023).

Traps

  • Energy pyramid can never be inverted; biomass pyramid inverted in ponds/oceans; number pyramid inverted for a single tree or parasites.
  • Phosphorus cycle has no atmospheric/gaseous phase (sedimentary); nitrogen fixation by lightning is abiotic; **denitrification returns N₂ to atmosphere (Pseudomonas, Thiobacillus), nitrification is NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻; N₂O is both a GHG and an ozone-depleter**.
  • Decomposers (saprotrophs) absorb after external digestion; detritivores ingest — earthworms are detritivores, not decomposers.
  • Ecotone has higher species diversity (edge effect) but can be less stable; ecological niche is unique to a species (no two species occupy the same niche indefinitely — Gause).
  • Highest NPP per unit area: tropical rainforest/estuary/swamps; open ocean lowest per area but highest total; tropical soils are nutrient-poor (nutrients locked in biomass).
  • Primary succession starts on lifeless substrate (lichens are pioneers on rock; phytoplankton in water); secondary is faster because soil exists; climax community = stable end stage.
  • Keystone ≠ most abundant; indicator species — lichens (air quality), amphibians (water/land health); umbrella = large range; flagship = charismatic.
  • Biomagnification needs a fat-soluble, persistent, non-degradable pollutant (DDT, methyl-mercury) — water-soluble pollutants do not biomagnify.
