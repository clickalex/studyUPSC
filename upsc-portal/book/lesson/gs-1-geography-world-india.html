Drainage — the two systems

  • Himalayan rivers (perennial — glacier + rain fed): Indus (origin: near Lake Mansarovar; tributaries Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej — the Panchnad; flows into the Arabian Sea through Pakistan); Ganga system (Bhagirathi from Gangotri + Alaknanda + Yamuna; the Padma-Meghna in Bangladesh creating the Sundarbans delta — the world's largest); Brahmaputra (origin: near Mansarovar (Tibet — Yarlung Tsangpo); U-turn at Namcha Barwa; the great braided channel; largest river by discharge; Majuli — among the largest river islands).
  • Peninsular rivers (seasonal, rain-fed): east-flowing (deltas) — Mahanadi (Chilika lagoon), Godavari ("Dakshin Ganga" — longest peninsular), Krishna, Kaveri (Cauvery — Karnataka–TN dispute, resolved via tribunal & CWMA 2018); west-flowing (estuaries — because of rift valleys) — Narmada (between Vindhya & Satpura; Sardar Sarovar dam), Tapti.
  • Water-divide mnemonic: the Western Ghats are the main peninsular watershed.

Indian climate & water resources

  • Seasons: Winter (Dec–Feb — western disturbances), Summer/pre-monsoon (Mar–May — Kalbaisakhi/Nor'westers in Bengal; mango showers in the South), SW monsoon (Jun–Sep), retreating/NE monsoon (Oct–Nov — TN coast).
  • Groundwater — India is the world's largest groundwater user; aquifer stress in Punjab/Haryana/Rajasthan/TN; Atal Bhujal Yojana collective management push; CGWB's dynamic assessment cycle.
  • Inter-linking of rivers (ILR) — Ken–Betwa the flagship first project (foundation laid December 2024; cleared through environment/wildlife channels); the debate: drought-proofing vs ecological flows.

Industrial location logic (use in frame answers)

  • Weberian principles — raw-material pull (weight-losing industries to the source: sugar mills at the cane fields; aluminium smelters at power+pits) vs market pull (footloose/glassware/cotton after power liberalised location); agglomeration economies and the "just-in-time" counter.
  • India cases: jute on the Hooghly (Ganga water for retting + coal belt + port), cotton textile historically on black-soil belt + Mumbai port & damp climate; IT services in Bengaluru/Hyderabad/Pune (talent cluster argument, not Weberian raw material), Chotanagpur mineral belt → steel cities (Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Rourkela, Bhilai, Durgapur, Burnpur).

World resource map (memorise the famous regions)

  • Iron ore — Brazil (Carajás), Australia (Pilbara), Russia (Kursk), Ukraine, India, China; biggest exporters: Australia & Brazil.
  • Coal — China (largest producer + consumer), US (Powder River), Russia (Kuznetsk), Australia, Indonesia, India.
  • Petroleum — Persian Gulf arc (Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, UAE, Iran — ~half the world's proved reserves); Venezuela's Orinoco (biggest heavy oil); Russia (West Siberia); US shale (Permian); North Sea.
  • Gold & diamonds — South Africa (Witwatersrand), Russia, Canada, Botswana, Australia (gold); Russia, Botswana, Canada (diamonds).
  • Copper — Chile (Escondida — largest mine), Peru, Congo-Kinshasa belt, Zambia; Lithium triangle — Chile, Argentina, Bolivia (Atacama brines); cobalt — DR Congo; rare earths — China dominates processing.
  • Fisheries — NW Pacific (Japan), NE Atlantic (North Sea), NW Atlantic (Grand Banks), Humboldt anchovy (Peru–Chile).

World industrial regions

  • * Manufacturing heartlands: US Midwest (Rust Belt → re-tooling), Ruhr (Germany), Great Lakes, Japan's Tokyo–Osaka belt, China's coastal corridor (Shanghai–Yangtze delta; Pearl River delta), North Italy, South Korea's south-east coast, India's Mumbai–Ahmedabad / NCR / Chennai–Bengaluru corridors.

4. Population & settlement

  • Census milestones: India crossed China to become the world's most populous country in April 2023 (UN estimate; Census 2021 was deferred — the next census exercise (2027) has been announced with a two-phase design, its results feeding the post-freeze delimitation and women's-reservation machinery).
  • 7,935 towns in Census 2011, 53 cities >1 million; the new decade estimates put metropolitan populations much higher (Mumbai U.A. ~20+ m, Delhi NCR ~30 m).
  • Urbanisation ~35% (2023 estimate) — projected ~40% by 2036.
  • Mains-ready themes: rural push vs urban pull; peri-urbanisation & census towns; slum share declines in municipal data but absolute numbers persist; smart-city experiments (100 cities mission 2015).

5. Transport & connectivity geography

  • Golden Quadrilateral connecting Delhi-Mumbai-Chennai-Kolkata; N-S/E-W corridors; Bharatmala (2017) phase programme; Dedicated Freight Corridors (Eastern/Western) mostly commissioned.
  • Railways — world's 4th-largest network by route-km; first train Bombay–Thane (16 April 1853); Vande Bharat semi-high-speed fleet expansion; bullet-train (Mumbai–Ahmedabad) under construction.
  • Ports — 12 major ports (incl. Deendayal (Kandla), Mumbai, Chennai, Kamarajar (Ennore), VOC (Tuticorin), JNPT, Kochi, Vizag, Paradip); Sagarmala programme (2015); non-major state ports handle a majority of cargo.
  • Gati Shakti National Master Plan (2021) — geospatial infra integration platform (linked to VB-G RAM G planning in the social-justice note).
  • Chokepoints (world trade geography) — Malacca, Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb, Suez, Panama, Bosporus; post-2023 Red Sea re-routing via the Cape of Good Hope demonstrated chokepoint risk (freight-rate and transit-time effects on India-Europe trade).

9. The Himalaya–Monsoon Engine: Orogeny, Seasons, and the Water Tower

Section 1 maps the physiographic canvas (three Himalayan ranges, plains, plateau, coasts, islands) plus drainage and seasons; this section engines it — because geography answers score on process (why the relief rains, why the rivers run perennial), not inventory. Orogeny (§7's plate-anchor deepened): India on the Indo-Australian plate rifting from Gondwana (~120 Ma), racing north (~18–20 cm/year — the fastest plate-motion recorded), colliding with Eurasia (~50–40 Ma) — the Tethys sediments crumpled upward into the Himadri (granite core, 6,000m-plus, Everest–Kanchenjunga–Nanda-Devi — permanent snow, the water tower's tank), the Himachal (compressed sediments — Pir-Panjal–Dhauladhar — Shimla–Mussoorie–Darjeeling perched on thrusts), the Shiwaliks (foothill fanglomerates — youngest, still-rising — the duns — Dehradun, Kathmandu — flat valleys between Shiwalik ridges) — the collision continuing (~5 cm/year — the Himalaya still growing, the earthquakes (Kashmir-2005, Nepal-2015) priced in: Mains' Himalayan-fragility answers start here). The monsoon machine (§1's seasons as mechanism): summer's Tibetan-plateau heating (the elevated heat-source — the plateau at 4,000m+ heating faster than the ocean — the thermal low that drags the ITCZ north) → the SW-monsoon branches (Arabian-Sea branch — Western-Ghats-orographic (Mawsynram–Cherrapunji's 11,000mm-plus on the Khasi scarp, Mumbai's 2,000mm on the Sahyadris) — Bay-of-Bengal branch — the Gangetic-trough march, the break-monsoon mechanics (trough-to-foothills — plains-dry, foothills-flooded) and the western-disturbances (Caspian–Mediterranean westerlies — winter rain for rabi, snow for the tower's recharge) → retreating-NE-monsoon (October–November — Tamil-Nadu's main rain — the Coromandel exception: the coast that drinks while India dries) → the ENSO–IOD modulation (El-Niño-drought-link, La-Niña-flood-link, positive-IOD rescue (2019's bounty) — the forecast-factors IMD's Mission-Mausam now models: ₹2,000-crore radars–satellites–AI).

The water-tower ledger (drainage-as-argument): Himalayan rivers perennial (glacier-plus-rain — the Indus–Ganga–Brahmaputra systems: Panchnad (Jhelum–Chenab–Ravi–Beas–Sutlej) joining the Indus-to-Arabian-Sea; Ganga (Bhagirathi–Gangotri + Alaknanda + Yamuna — the Padma–Meghna–Sundarbans-delta — world's largest); Brahmaputra (Mansarovar-near-origin — Yarlung-Tsangpo — Namcha-Barwa U-turn — the braided giant, largest-by-discharge, Majuli's river-island) — the tower's outflows: treaties (Indus-Waters — abeyance-post-Sindoor; Ganga–Teesta with Bangladesh; Brahmaputra-data with China — rivers-as-diplomacy); peninsular rivers seasonal (rain-fed — east-flowing deltas — Mahanadi (Chilika), Godavari (Dakshin-Ganga, longest-peninsular), Krishna, Kaveri (CWMA-2018 — the tribunal-to-authority arc) — west-flowing estuaries — Narmada (Vindhya–Satpura rift, Sardar-Sarovar) and Tapti (rift-valley estuaries — the §7 anchor: rift-not-delta-because-gradient-plus-tide)) — the ledger's bottom line (tower-fed north, rain-fed south — storage (dams–aquifers) bridging the mismatch — Ken–Betwa-Dec-2024 as the transfer-prototype, Atal-Bhujal as the aquifer-answer).

10. Resources and Industry as Location Logic: Weber in India, Corridors in the World

Section 2's mineral tables (India's state→resource, the world's famous regions) plus industrial-location logic need the Weberian engine — because \"distribution\" questions (the Mains angle §2 names) are answered in location-theory, not mine-lists. Weber's least-cost (1909 — transport-plus-labour-plus-agglomeration: weight-losing industries (ore→metal: steel needs ~3 tonnes input per tonne output; sugarcane 10:1; aluminium's power-hunger — locate at source-or-power) versus weight-gaining/footloose (cotton-textiles post-power, glassware, IT-services — locate at market-or-talent)): Indian proofs (jute-on-Hooghly — Ganga-retting-water + Raniganj-coal + Kolkata-port — the triple-coincidence; cotton-textiles — black-soil-belt-plus-Mumbai (humid-climate-for-thread + port-for-imports-then-exports); Chotanagpur steel — Jharia-coking-coal + Singhbhum-ore + Damodar-water — Jamshedpur–Bokaro–Burnpur–Rourkela–Bhilai–Durgapur as the Weber-diagram built: ore-and-coal adjacent, the weight-losing logic in steel-and-smoke); aluminium (Odisha–Chhattisgarh bauxite + pithead-power — NALCO–BALCO–HINDALCO at the power-pit nexus); IT-services (Bengaluru–Hyderabad–Pune — talent-cluster, not Weberian — the agglomeration-economy proof: engineers attracting engineers — the post-Weber location factor). The energy transition's re-location (Bhadla-solar-Rajasthan, Gujarat-wind-lead, Himalayan-hydro (Arunachal-40-per-cent-untapped — the Subansiri–Dibang frontier, the China-dam anxiety upstream) — renewables re-pricing location: power-at-source (desert-sun, coastal-wind) versus power-at-demand (the green-corridor transmission bet — electrons moved, not coal).

World industrial regions (§2's heartlands as theory): Ruhr (coal-plus-iron-plus-Rhine — the classic Weber-cluster, now transitioned); US-Midwest–Great-Lakes (Mesabi-ore + Appalachian-coal + lake-transport — Rust-Belt-to-re-tooling — the deindustrialisation-and-CHIPS-resurrection arc); Tokyo–Osaka (import-everything, export-manufactures — the market-plus-talent-plus-port proof that industry needs no mines); Yangtze–Pearl-Deltas (labour-plus-ports-plus-policy — the SEZ-location-instrument: Shenzhen's 1980 bet replicated); lithium-triangle–Congo–Pilbara–Carajás (§2's critical-minerals map — the energy-transition's new geography: Chile–Argentina–Bolivia brines, DR-Congo cobalt, Australia–Brazil ore — India's KABIL-plus-auction response: the critical-mineral diplomacy). The distribution answer-formula: resource-map → Weber-test (weight/market/talent/policy) → Indian-case → world-mirror → transition-implication — five beats turning §2's tables into Mains arguments (\"why steel sits on coal, why IT sits on talent, why batteries will sit on brine\").

11. Agriculture, Population, Connectivity: The Human-Geography Triad

Sections 3–5 map crops, census-towns and corridors; this section theorises the human layer — because human-geography questions ask why people farm-move-settle where they do. Agriculture-geography (§3's table as soil–water–season logic): rice (delta-plus-rain — West-Bengal–UP–Punjab — kharif-60-per-cent — the water-footprint paradox: Punjab's tubewell-rice (free-power-plus-MSP — the agriculture-note's nexus) versus Bengal's rain-rice — same crop, opposite ecologies); wheat (rabi-irrigated — UP–Punjab–Haryana Green-Revolution core — the HYV-plus-tubewell-plus-procurement package as location-factor: policy creating geography); cotton (black-soil — Gujarat–Maharashtra–Telangana — the regur-plus-rain logic: moisture-retentive soil for a rainfed-cash-crop — Bt's 2002 yield-revolution as the technology-location-shock); sugarcane (UP–Maharashtra — weight-losing-to-mill (10:1 — Weber at the cane-field: mills within hours of harvest — the cooperative-mill belt as industrial-geography); jute (Hooghly — retting-water-plus-humidity — the §10 case re-appearing); tea–coffee–spices (Assam–Darjeeling, Kodagu, Kerala — the plantation-climate niche: altitude-plus-rain-plus-slope-drainage — colonial-capital's geography persisting). Land-and-water institutions (net-sown-~54-per-cent — highest among large countries — the intensity-frontier (irrigated-North double-cropping versus rainfed-Deccan single — PMKSY-per-drop-more-crop as the efficiency wedge); MSP–FCI–PM-KISAN–PMFBY–e-NAM (§3's institutions — the policy-geography: procurement creating the wheat-map, insurance buffering the dryland-map).

Population-and-settlement (§4's milestones as theory): the 2023-crossover (India-past-China — UN-estimate — the Census-2021-deferred-to-2027 gap: delimitation-frozen (1976–2026-freeze) plus women's-reservation-post-delimitation — demography-as-constitutional-timetable); 7,935-census-towns-2011 (the in-situ urbanisation — villages turning urban without migrating — the Rurban thesis: India urbanising in place, not just by migration — peri-urbanisation as the dominant form); urban-~35-per-cent-to-40-by-2036 (the agglomeration bet: Mumbai-UA-20m-plus, NCR-30m — megacity-governance as the 74th-Amendment's unmet test). Connectivity (§5's corridors as spatial-economics): Golden-Quadrilateral-plus-NS–EW (the road-grid that re-priced distance — the corridor-industrialisation: NCR–Mumbai–Chennai–Kolkata as the investment-quadrilateral); Bharatmala-2017 plus DFCs (Eastern–Western commissioned — freight-shifted-off-road: logistics-cost-to-GDP targeted down); rail (4th-largest-network, 1853-Bombay–Thane, Vande-Bharat-fleet, Mumbai–Ahmedabad-bullet — the speed-ladder: passenger-time re-valued); ports (12-major — Deendayal (Kandla)-plus-coast — Sagarmala-2015, non-majors-majority-cargo — the port-led-development bet: Mundra-plus-Krishnapatnam outgrowing the majors); Gati-Shakti-2021 (the geospatial-master-plan — 16-ministries-one-map — corridor-planning as data-integration). The triad's synthesis: crops follow soil–water–policy, people follow jobs-plus-in-situ-transformation, freight follows corridors — human geography as location-logic applied to life.

12. The Geography Answer Method: Sketch-Maps, Chokepoints, and the Atlas Habit

Section 6 (mapping as answer-tool) plus §7's PYQ angles demand the method — and geography's method is cartographic: the candidate who sketches outscores the candidate who lists. Sketch-map discipline: the 5-second India-outline (§6 — three mineral lines: Chotanagpur-coal-iron, Odisha-belt, Western-Ghats-thorium-coast; or the river-arrows: Panchnad-to-Arabian-Sea, Ganga-to-Bay, Narmada–Tapti-estuaries-west) plus the world-chokepoint-strip (Malacca–Hormuz–Bab-el-Mandeb–Suez–Panama–Bosporus — §5's six — with the Red-Sea-2023–24 Cape-rerouting as the live proof: freight-and-time effects on India–Europe trade — the chokepoint-risk priced). Every Mains distribution-answer carries one sketch (resources, rivers, crops, corridors — the map as the argument's skeleton: locate-then-explain, never explain-unlocated). Pass–strait–river drills (§6's Prelims triad, systematised): Himalayan passes west-to-east (Zoji-La — Srinagar–Leh; Banihal — Jammu–Srinagar-pir-Panjal; Rohtang — Atal-Tunnel-2020; Baralacha-La — Leh–Manali; Shipki-La — Sutlej-Himachal–Tibet; Lipu-Lekh — Uttarakhand–Tibet (Kalapani-dispute); Nathu-La — Sikkim–Tibet (1967-clash, 2006-trade)) — drilled as the Leh-to-Sikkim traverse; straits as connections (Palk — Mannar–Bay-of-Bengal; Malacca — Andaman-Sea–South-China-Sea; Hormuz — Gulf–Gulf-of-Oman; Bab-el-Mandeb — Red-Sea–Gulf-of-Aden; Bering — Arctic–Pacific; Gibraltar–Magellan — the connect-pairs); river-origins (Gangotri-Bhagirathi, Mansarovar-cluster (Indus–Brahmaputra–Sutlej near-neighbours), Namcha-Barwa-U-turn — the origin-triple).

The atlas habit (§8's sources as routine): Oxford/Orient-BlackSwan plus Bhuvan–IMD-current-layers — one atlas hour weekly (physical-India one week, resources-next, world-trade-third, revision-fourth — the monthly cycle); the distribution-answer template (locate (sketch) → physical-basis (relief–climate–soil–water) → human-layer (policy–market–history) → world-mirror (one comparative) → implication (transition/dispute/development) — five beats); the Prelims-trap sheet (§7's seven, expanded: Aravalli-oldest + Indo-Australian-plate; Barren-Island-active + Indira-Point-southernmost (2004-subsided); Narmada–Tapti-rift-estuaries; Mahanadi–Godavari–Krishna–Kaveri-deltas; Assam-tea/Kodagu-coffee/Hooghly-jute; Australia–Brazil-iron-exporters; 7,935-towns + Sundarbans-largest-delta — plus the near-miss guards: Mahendragiri-Eastern-Ghats-highest vs Anamudi-2,695-Western-Ghats; Bhadla-solar vs Gujarat-wind-lead; Digboi-first-refinery vs Mumbai-High-largest-producer). The method's close: geography is GS-1's map-or-nothing subject — the atlas open weekly, the sketch in every answer, the distribution explained by location-logic — and the candidate who draws first and writes second has already won half the marks before the first sentence.

Revision Digest

Condensed for the last mile before the exam.

One-liner

India's geography runs north-wall → alluvial plains → peninsular shield → twin coasts, with a monsoon-fed, west-flowing-exception river system (Narmada/Tapti = rift-valley estuaries) — whose resources (Chotanagpur minerals, Mumbai High oil, Brahmaputra water) and world chokepoints (Malacca–Hormuz–Suez) shape its economy.

India's physiography (snap memory: "H-P-P-C-I")

Himalayas (Himadri–Himachal–Shiwalik) → Plains (Bhabar–Terai; Khadar–Bhangar) → Peninsula (Western Ghats 1,600 km, Anamudi 2,695 m; Aravalli = oldest folds; Narmada's rift valley) → Coasts (east = deltas; west = estuaries) → Islands (A&N w/ Barren Island volcano; Lakshadweep atolls).

Rivers — the pattern

  • Himalayan (perennial): Indus (Panchnad — Jhelum/Chenab/Ravi/Beas/Sutlej), Ganga (Gangotri + Alaknanda + Yamuna), Brahmaputra (Mansarovar origin, Namcha Barwa U-turn, braided, Majuli).
  • Peninsular (rain-fed): east-flowing deltas — Mahanadi, Godavari ("Dakshin Ganga"), Krishna, Kaveri; west-flowing estuaries — Narmada, Tapti (rift valleys).
  • Largest delta on Earth — Sundarbans (Ganga-Brahmaputra).

Climate & water (India)

  • 4 seasons: winter (WD), pre-monsoon summer, SW monsoon (75% of rain Jun–Sep), retreating/NE monsoon (TN coast).
  • Groundwater: India = world's largest user; stress in NW + TN; Atal Bhujal Yojana.
  • Ken–Betwa — first river-link project launched (Dec 2024).

India's resources (match-region cheat table)

Coal — Jharia (Jh) + Raniganj (WB) · Iron ore — Odisha + Bailadila (CG) · Bauxite — Odisha · Mica — Koderma (Jh) · Oil — Mumbai High + Barmer · Gas — KG basin · Uranium — Jaduguda + Tummalapalle · Thorium — Kerala monazite · Solar — Bhadla (Raj) · Wind — TN/Gujarat · Steel cities — Jamshedpur/Bokaro/Bhilai/Rourkela/Durgapur (Chotanagpur belt).

World resource anchors

  • Oil — Persian Gulf arc ≈ half the proved reserves; Orinoco heavy oil (Venezuela); Permian shale (US).
  • Iron ore exporters — Australia (Pilbara) & Brazil (Carajás).
  • Copper — Chile (Escondida); lithium triangle — Chile/Argentina/Bolivia; cobalt — DR Congo.
  • Industrial belts — US Midwest, Ruhr, Tokyo–Osaka, Yangtze & Pearl River deltas, India's Mumbai–Ahmedabad & Chennai–Bengaluru.
  • Chokepoints — Malacca, Hormuz, Bab-el-Mandeb (+ 2023– era Red Sea re-routings via the Cape), Suez, Panama.

People & settlements

  • Most populous country (Apr 2023 — overtook China); Census-2027 (two-phase) announced; urbanisation ~35% (2023).
  • 7,935 census towns (2011); 53 cities >1 million; Delhi NCR & Mumbai ~20–30 m each.

Bullet facts (prelims gold)

India's physiography

  • Himadri (Greater Himalaya, Everest 8,848 m) · Himachal (Lesser — hill stations) · Shiwaliks (youngest — duns: Dehradun, Kathmandu).
  • Bhabar (stones, streams sink) → Terai (marshy, springs re-emerge) → alluvial plain (khadar floodplain vs bhangar upland).
  • Western Ghats (~1,600 km, continuous escarpment) vs Eastern Ghats (discontinuous); highest — Anamudi (2,695 m, Kerala); highest eastern — Mahendragiri (Odisha).
  • Aravalli — India's oldest fold mountains; Guru Shikhar (1,722 m).
  • Narmada — rift valley between Vindhya & Satpura; Sardar Sarovar on it.
  • Deccan slopes east → peninsular rivers pour into the Bay of Bengal.
  • Barren Island (A&N) — India's only active volcano; Indira Point (Great Nicobar) — India's southernmost (subsided after the 2004 tsunami).

Rivers

  • Indus: rises near Lake Mansarovar; Panchnad tributaries Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej; drains to the Arabian Sea via Pakistan.
  • Ganga system: Gangotri (Bhagirathi) + Alaknanda = Devprayag; Yamuna is the largest tributary; Sundarbans = world's largest delta (with Brahmaputra's Padma/Meghna arms).
  • Brahmaputra: U-turn at Namcha Barwa; braided; largest by discharge; Majuli among the largest river islands.
  • East-flowing deltas: Mahanadi, Godavari (longest peninsular, "Dakshin Ganga"), Krishna, Kaveri (tribunal → CWMA 2018).
  • West-flowing estuaries: Narmada & Tapti (rift valleys).

Resources (India match-ups)

  • Coal — Jharia (Jharkhand coking) + Raniganj (WB) + Talcher (Odisha) + Korba (CG).
  • Iron ore — Odisha (Keonjhar), Bailadila (CG), Singhbhum (Jh), Ballari (Kar).
  • Bauxite — Odisha (largest). Mica — Koderma (Jh).
  • Oil — Mumbai High offshore, Barmer-Mangala (Raj), Assam (Digboi — first refinery).
  • Gas — Krishna-Godavari (KG) basin, Tripura.
  • Uranium — Jaduguda (Jh), Tummalapalle (AP).
  • Thorium — monazite sands, Kerala coast.
  • Steel — Chotanagpur belt cities: Jamshedpur, Bokaro, Rourkela, Bhilai, Durgapur, Burnpur.

World resources

  • Oil reserves concentrated in the Persian Gulf arc (Saudi, Iraq, Kuwait, UAE, Iran); Venezuela's heavy oil largest single reserve.
  • Iron ore top exporters: Australia (Pilbara), Brazil (Carajás).
  • Copper — Chile (Escondida, largest mine); lithium triangle — Chile/Argentina/Bolivia; cobalt — DR Congo; rare-earth processing — China.
  • Fisheries — Grand Banks (NW Atlantic), North Sea, Japan coast (Kuroshio+Oyashio), Humboldt anchovy (Peru).

Population & settlements

  • India overtook China as the most populous country (April 2023, UN).
  • 2011 Census: 53 cities >1 million; 7,935 towns; urbanisation ~35% (2023 est.).
  • Mumbai–Ahmedabad bullet-train under construction; Vande Bharat expansion.

Transport & chokepoints

  • Golden Quadrilateral + N-S/E-W corridors; Bharatmala (2017); Sagarmala (2015); Gati Shakti (2021).
  • Ports: 12 majors (Deendayal-Kandla, JNPT, Mumbai, Chennai, Kamarajar-Ennore, VOC-Tuticorin, Kochi, Vizag, Paradip...) — non-major ports already carry the majority of cargo.
  • World chokepoints: Malacca · Hormuz · Bab-el-Mandeb · Suez · Panama · Bosporus; the Red Sea re-routings (from late 2023) showed chokepoint risk to India-Europe trade.
  • Atal Tunnel (2020) — Rohtang; Nathu La / Zoji La / Shipki La / Lipu Lekh = Himalayan passes on the map list.

Q2013

"

  1. (a)What do you understand by the theory of continental drift?
  2. (b)Discuss the prominent evidences in its support." (10 marks)
Model answer outline

Model answer skeleton

Introduction — Alfred Wegener's theory (1912), presented in Origin of Continents and Oceans, argued that today's continents were once united in a supercontinent (Pangaea surrounded by Panthalassa) and have drifted apart.

Body

  1. Evidences: (i) jigsaw fit of the Atlantic coasts (Brazil bulge into the Gulf of Guinea); (ii) identical fossil species on both sides (Mesosaurus — Brazil & West Africa; Glossopteris flora — Gondwana continents incl. India); (iii) glacial striations & tillites in India, South Africa, Australia, South America (a common ice-age); (iv) matching rock formations/coal seams across the oceans (including coal in Antarctica — formerly vegetated); (v) pole-wandering curves.
  2. Then why rejected initially — no plausible mechanism (tidal/gravitational forces computed too weak).
  3. Modern resolution — sea-floor spreading (Hess, 1962), paleomagnetic striping (Vine–Matthews, 1963), and plate tectonics (McKenzie–Parker, 1967) — subduction & ridge-pushes supply the mechanism; today GPS measures cm/year drift directly.

Conclusion — Continental drift was a hypothesis in search of a mechanism; plate tectonics delivered it and remains the organising theory of geomorphology.

Q2017

"Account for variations in oceanic salinity and discuss its multi-dimensional effects." (10 marks)

Model answer outline

Model answer skeleton

Introduction — Average ocean salinity ≈ 35 ‰. Regional deviations are substantial (~32–40 ‰) — a function of the water balance and ocean circulation.

Body

  1. Controls — evaporation > precipitation raises salinity (the subtropical high belts — Red Sea ~40 ‰); river inflow lowers it (Baltic; the northern Bay of Bengal has lower surface salinity than the Arabian Sea because of the Ganga-Brahmaputra outflow); ice melt lowers it (the Antarctic edge); isolation/constrictions (enclosed seas) amplify.
  2. Multi-dimensional effects:
    • Density & circulation: salinity + temperature set density, driving thermohaline circulation (the "global conveyor").
    • Freezing/boiling thresholds: salinity alters freezing point — sea-ice formation timing matters for polar climates.
    • Biology: osmoregulation limits species ranges — mangroves (e.g., Sundarbans) are salinity-tolerant specialists; estuaries are the richest nurseries (variable salinity zone).
    • Economy: salinity gradients constrain irrigation reuse, industrial cooling choices, and desalination siting; hypersaline pans for salt production (Little Rann of Kutch).

Conclusion — Salinity appears a small number but scripts climate circulation, biology and section of coastal economy; its freshening hotspots (Bay of Bengal) and hypersaline boxes (Red Sea; Persian Gulf) show that the global ocean's balance is dynamic & primed to shift in climate-change scenarios.
