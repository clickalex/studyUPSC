National Movement: Moderates, Extremists & Gandhian Era (1885–1919)

Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

From moderate petitions to mass agitation: INC genesis, Swadeshi explosion, Extremist surge, Home Rule bridge, Gandhian launch.

1. Pre-INC political associations

  • Zamindari Association 1838, British Indian Association 1851, East India Association (London, Naoroji 1866), Indian Association (Surendranath Banerjee, 1876), Madras Mahajana Sabha + Bombay Presidency Association 1884; A.O. Hume retires-Indian-organiser → INC 1885 Bombay (W.C. Bonnerjee first president; safety-valve thesis debate — Bipan Chandra refutes).

2. Moderates (1885-1905) & economic critique

  • Moderates: Naoroji ('Grand Old Man'; poverty & un-British rule; DRAIN THEORY — Poverty and Un-British Rule in India), R.C. Dutt (Economic History of India), Ranade-Gokhale (liberal constitutionalism), W.C. Bonnerjee, S.N. Banerjee; methods: prayers-petitions ('3P'), constitutional means; gains: Indian Councils Act 1892 seeds.
  • Drain debate anchors: Home Charges, Council Bills, 'bleeding' metaphors; Naoroji president thrice (1886, 1893, 1906).

3. Partition of Bengal & Swadeshi (1905-11) + Extremists

  • Curzon partitions Bengal 16 Oct 1905 (administrative plea; divide-and-manage); response: Swadeshi-boycott (Calcutta 7 Aug resolution), national education (Bengal National College — Aurobindo principal), Swadeshi Steam Navigation (V.O. Chidambaram Pillai), samitis (Swadesh Bandhab — Ashwini Dutta), art (Abanindranath Bharat Mata), press (Yugantar, Sandhya, Kesari), bande mataram spread.
  • Extremist trio: Lal-Bal-Pal (Lajpat Rai, Tilak — Kesari/Maratha; 'Swaraj is my birthright'; Ganapati 1893 + Shivaji 1895 festivals; imprisonment 1897, 1908 Mandalay, B.P. Pal — Bande Mataram paper); methods: swadeshi-boycott-national education-passive resistance; Surat split 1907 (Rash Behari Ghosh moderates) → reunited 1916 Lucknow session.
  • Revolutionary stream I: Anushilan + Jugantar (Barindra Ghosh, Khudiram-Prafulla Chaki Muzaffarpur 1908), Alipore case; Savarkar (Abhinav Bharat, 1857 book), Ghadar Party (San Francisco 1913 — Lala Har Dayal; Komagata Maru 1914), Berlin Committee; Defence of India Act crackdowns.

4. Morley-Minto, Muslim League, Home Rule, Lucknow Pact

  • Muslim League 1906 Dacca (Nawab Salimullah; Simla deputation 1906 — separate-electorate ask), Morley-Minto Reforms 1909: separate electorates institutionalised ('father of communal electorate' tag), Indian members into executive councils; partition annulled 1911 (Delhi durbar; capital shift announced — 1912 transfer).
  • Home Rule leagues 1916: Tilak (Poona — Maharashtra, Karnataka, Central Provinces) + Annie Besant (Madras, All-India; New India/Commonweal); June 1917 Besant internment → agitation; Montagu declaration 20 Aug 1917 ('responsible government' goal).
  • Lucknow Pact 1916: INC-League joint scheme (congress conceded separate electorates); Lucknow session = Extremist reunion + pact twin.
  • Gandhi lands 1915 (South Africa 1893-1914: satyagraha formative — Tolstoy Farm, Phoenix; first satyagraha in the Transvaal 1906-07); Gokhale guide; 1917 Champaran (indigo tinkathia), 1918 Kheda + Ahmedabad mill, Rowlatt Act 1919 → Rowlatt satyagraha (6 April hartal), Jallianwala Bagh 13 Apr 1919 (Dyer; Udham Singh 1940 revenge) → Khilafat + Non-Cooperation opens the next phase.

MCQ hooks

  • INC 1885 Bombay, Hume founder, Bonnerjee president, 72 delegates count.
  • Drain theory → Naoroji; Economic History → R.C. Dutt.
  • Swadeshi launch date 7 Aug 1905 (Calcutta Town Hall); partition 16 Oct 1905.
  • Surat 1907 split vs Lucknow 1916 reunion-pact twins.
  • Morley-Minto 1909 = separate electorates; League = Dacca 1906.
  • Home Rule 1916 duo = Tilak (Poona) + Besant (Madras).
  • Gandhi triple before Rowlatt: Champaran-Kheda-Ahmedabad (1917-18 order).

10. The Moderate Achievement Debate: Mendicancy or Foundation?

Section 2 lists achievements and limitations; this section argues the verdict — because \"were the Moderates mendicants or nation-builders?\" is the period's master-question, and Mains grades the argument, never the slogan. The prosecution (contemporary Extremists + later impatience): the methods (prayer–petition–protest — the annual December ritual of resolutions Britain filed-and-forgot); the sociology (urban, upper-caste, English-educated men — peasants unmobilised, women undelegated, Muslims under-represented — the \"microscopic minority\" charge); the psychology (faith in Britain's \"providential mission\" — Naoroji's own early loyalism, the apologetic tone — mendicancy as method: begging the master for the master's reasons); the record (Councils Act 1892's crumbs — indirect election, official majority retained; ICS age-cut from 21 to 19 (1878–Ly decree reversed only in 1892 — the door narrowed, not opened); the simultaneous-examination demand unmet till the 1920s). The defence (Bipan Chandra's classic rehabilitation — India's Struggle: the Moderates as the necessary first stage — nation-in-the-making): the economic critique (Naoroji's drain — Poverty and Un-British Rule, 1901 — the moral indictment quantified: Home Charges, salary-remittances, the \"un-British\" frame turning liberalism against empire; R.C. Dutt's Economic History — the deindustrialisation documented — the critique that converted loyalists into nationalists, including the young Gandhi and Nehru); the political education (annual sessions rotating across presidencies — the all-India vocabulary forged: liberty, representation, swaraj-seeded — Gokhale's \"training ground for self-government\" — the Servants of India Society 1905 as the cadre-proof); the constitutional wedge (1892 Act's foothold → 1909's promise — Morley–Minto conceded because the critique embarrassed — the Extremists inherited the platform the Moderates built).

The synthesis (the examination-grade verdict): judge the Moderates by stage-necessity (no mass movement without prior ideological consensus — the drain theory had to be believed before Swadeshi could be felt — the Moderates manufactured the belief) and by counterfactual (without 1885–1905's press–platform–petition infrastructure — the Hindu, Kesari-to-come, the session-network — Tilak's 1905–08 explosion would have had no wires to travel). Concede the prosecution its sociology (elite, slow, loyalist-tinged — the Surat-1907 verdict of history's impatience) and the defence its function (foundation, not failure — the Congress they built survived the split, reunited 1916, and became Gandhi's vehicle). The verdict-line: the Moderates petitioned the Empire and educated the nation — failing at the first (by design-impossibility) and succeeding at the second (by historical necessity) — mendicants toward London, schoolmasters toward India.

11. Swadeshi Dissected: Boycott, National Education, Culture, and Why It Ebbed

Section 3 narrates Swadeshi (boycott 7 August 1905, indigenous industry, National Council of Education 1906, annulment 1911); this section dissects it — because \"assess Swadeshi\" demands anatomy plus autopsy. The four arms: boycott (British cloth–salt–sugar — public bonfires — the import-curves visibly dented 1905–08: Manchester's lobby complaining to Whitehall — economic pressure proven); swadeshi-enterprise (Bengal Chemicals — P.C. Ray's proof that science could be nationalist; Bengal Potteries, Mohini Mills, Swadeshi steam-navigation (Chidambaram Pillai's Tuticorin–Colombo line — the Tamil extension) — enterprise as resistance: capital recruited to the flag); national education (National Council of Education, 1906 — Jadavpur's forerunner — vernacular-medium, technical-plus-national curriculum — the boycott of Calcutta-University examinations, the student-strike as method: education seized from the coloniser's syllabus); culture-as-politics (Vande Mataram — Bankim's 1882 hymn, sung at Congress-1896, adopted-1905-session — the anthem before the anthem; Tagore's rakhi-and-arandhan (tying bonds across the partition-line, the non-cooking day of mourning — 16 October 1905 — grief choreographed); Abanindranath's Bharat Mata (1905 — the nation imaged: saffron ascetic-mother — the Bengal-School's political birth); Tilak's Ganapati (1893)–Shivaji (1895) festivals as the Maharashtra template — religion's calendar converted to nationalism's timetable).

The ebb — autopsy in four causes (§8's decline-question answered): State repression (1907–08 press laws, the Tilak-Mandaley transportation 1908 — six years for \"terrorism\" journalism — the leadership decapitated; Aurobindo's Alipore-trial-to-Pondicherry-1910 exit; the Criminal Law Amendment's meeting-bans — the open movement criminalised); the Surat split (December 1907 — Rash Behari Ghose's chair, the Calcutta-four (swadeshi–boycott–education–swaraj) as the casus, shoes-and-chairs, expulsion — the movement beheaded and divided at its peak); the social ceiling (students–middle-class–zamindars mobilised, peasants-and-Muslims thinly reached — the boycott's cloth-cost falling on the poor, the Hindu-idiom (Shivaji, Ganapati, Kali) alienating Muslim artisans — the Morley–Minto 1909 separate-electorates wedge driven into exactly this crack); the annulment's deflation (1911 — George V's Delhi-Durbar announcement — partition revoked, capital shifted to Delhi — the grievance removed while the method was exhausted — victory that dissolved the movement: Swadeshi won its demand and lost its momentum). The dissection's verdict: Swadeshi invented the modern repertoire (boycott, national-education, cultural-politics — Gandhi's 1920 kit prefigured) and discovered its limits (repression-plus-division-plus-ceiling — the lessons Non-Cooperation would study) — the rehearsal whose script the main performance followed.

12. Surat, Lucknow, Home Rule: The Politics of Split and Reunion

Sections 3–5 stage the split (1907), the pact (1916) and the Leagues (1916); this section theorises the arc — because 1907–1919 is the Congress's education in unity, and the examination tests the lesson. Surat dissected (December 1907 — Gujarati-Sabha venue, Rash Behari Ghose presiding): the surface issue (confirm-or-dilute the Calcutta-1906 four — swadeshi, boycott, national education, swaraj — Naoroji's 1906 \"Swaraj\" declaration as the radical high-water mark the Moderates feared to reaffirm); the structural issue (who is Congress — the constitutional lobby (Mehta–Gokhale: resolutions as petitions-to-London) versus the national movement (Tilak–Aurobindo–Pal: resolutions as pledges-to-the-people) — two theories of the organisation in one hall); the trigger (Tilak's nomination blocked, the procedural melee — shoes, chairs, the Bipin-Chandra-Pal-led Moderate walkout — expulsion voted); the consequence (1908–14 — the wilderness years: Moderates alone (Loyalist cooperation — Morley–Minto 1909 welcomed — councils entered, momentum lost), Extremists jailed-or-exiled (Tilak-Mandalay, Aurobindo-Pondicherry, Lajpat-Rai-deported-then-America, Pal-England) — the movement's energy diverted underground: Anushilan–Jugantar–India-House–Ghadar — the revolutionary current as the split's bastard child).

Lucknow reunited (1916 — A.C. Majumdar presiding): the reunion mechanics (Tilak-released-1914 — Mandalay-mellowed, war-loyalty pledged — readmitted; Besant's mediation — the Home-Rule agitation as the common platform; the war-context — Britain needing Indian men-and-money — the loyalist bargain available); the Lucknow Pact (Tilak–Jinnah (then \"ambassador of Hindu–Muslim unity\" — Gokhale's phrase, Sarojini's biography-title) — Congress accepts separate electorates for Muslims plus weightages — the tactical compromise: unity-now (joint demands: elected-majority councils, half-executives, the reform-memorandum) at the price of principle-later (the League's separatism legitimised by Congress's own signature — Jinnah's 1940 Pakistan prefigured in 1916's concession — the PYQ's \"three-cornered compromise\" (Congress–League–British-reforms) unpacked: each corner gaining (Congress-unity, League-recognition, British-time) and the nation mortgaging (the communal principle constitutionalised by nationalist hands)). Home Rule bridged (1916 — Tilak's Poona League (April — Marathi-Deccan, \"Swaraj is my birthright\" as the war-cry) plus Besant's Madras League (September — all-India, English-press — New India, Commonweal — the Theosophist's organisation: 200+ branches — the first woman to preside over Congress, Calcutta-1917 — the League agitation as mass-meetings-plus-pamphlets: self-government-within-Empire demanded during the Empire's war — the loyalist-radical paradox that made it unbanishable and irresistible — Montagu's August-1917 declaration (\"increasing association… progressive realisation of responsible government\") as its direct yield). The arc's thesis: Surat taught the cost of division (wilderness-plus-underground), Lucknow the price of unity (separate-electorates mortgaged), Home Rule the method of pressure (constitutional-agitation at wartime) — the Congress graduating, term by term, toward the Gandhian university.

13. The Ghadar–Revolutionary Current and the Gandhian Takeover Technique

Section 5's international environment (Ghadar 1913, Komagata Maru 1914) plus §7's Gandhian dawn (Champaran–Ahmedabad–Kheda, Rowlatt, Jallianwala, 1919 Act) frame the period's two roads — this section walks both to their junction. The revolutionary current: Anushilan–Jugantar (Bengal — Barindra–Aurobindo's Bhavani Mandir manual, the Muzaffarpur-1908 bombing (Khudiram–Prafulla — the first gallows-martyrs), Alipore-trial-1908–09 (Chittaranjan Das's defence — Aurobindo acquitted, the organisation shattered)); India House (London — Savarkar's War of Independence-1909-banned, Madanlal-Dhingra's Curzon-Wyllie-1909 assassination — the Empire struck in its capital); Ghadar (1913 — San Francisco Hindustan Ghadar — Har Dayal, Sohan Singh Bhakna, Barkatullah — Punjabi-diaspora mutiny-plan for wartime: 1914–15 returnees intercepted (Komagata-Maru-September-1914 Budge-Budge firing as the prelude — Gurdit Singh's charter exposing dominion-racism), the Lahore-conspiracy trials 1915–16 (49 transported-or-hanged — the mutiny strangled by intelligence: the Central-Intelligence-Department's penetration); the Singapore-mutiny-1915 (Malay-States-Guides — suppressed, 47 shot) — the current's verdict: heroism without organisation (individual-martyrdom maximised, mass-linkage absent — the peasant unreached, the soldier unconverted — courage that inspired (Bhagat Singh's canon) but couldn't liberate).

The Gandhian takeover technique (§7's three experiments as method-building): Champaran-1917 (tinkathia-indigo — Rajendra-Prasad–Kripalani enlisted — the inquiry-plus-disobedience template: investigate openly, defy the externment-order, suffer-and-negotiate — first civil disobedience, the planter-raj dented); Ahmedabad-mill-1918 (plague-bonus — the workers' cause — Gandhi's first fast — 3 days, the moral-wager: starve-till-arbitration — the fast-as-weapon invented); Kheda-1918 (crop-failure-revenue — Vallabhbhai's debut — the no-tax peasant campaign — the Sardar found); then Rowlatt-1919 (the Anarchical-and-Revolutionary-Crimes-Act — detention-without-trial — the wartime-Defence-of-India-Act made permanent — Gandhi's first all-India satyagraha call: hartal-plus-disobedience — the technique scaled: peasants-and-workers (Champaran–Kheda-learnt) plus urban-masses (the Rowlatt-hartal crowds) — the bridge §7 names to Non-Cooperation-1920). Jallianwala (13-April-1919 — Baisakhi crowd, Dyer's 1,650 rounds, 379-official/1,000+-Indian dead — the Hunter-Committee-October-1919 whitewash, Dyer-censured-not-punished, the Morning-Post-fund glorifying him — the Empire's moral bankruptcy exhibited) plus the 1919 Act's dyarchy (transferred-versus-reserved, the placate-and-tighten: provincial-responsibility dangled, emergency-powers kept) complete the junction: the revolutionaries proved Indians would die for freedom, Gandhi proved they could organise for it — martyrdom's inspiration fused to satyagraha's method — the 1920s inheriting both the courage and the technique.

Pre-Congress associations

  • Landholders' Society 1838 · British Indian Association 1851 (Calcutta) · East India Association 1866 London (Naoroji) · Poona Sarvajanik Sabha 1870 (Ranade, Joshi) · Indian Association 1876 (S.N. Banerjea, Anandamohan Bose) · Madras Mahajan Sabha 1884 · Bombay Presidency Association 1885.
  • Ilbert Bill controversy 1883 (Ripon) → "white mutiny" → lesson in organisation.

Congress founding & sessions

  • 28 Dec 1885, Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit College, Bombay; 72 delegates; W.C. Bonnerjee president; A.O. Hume general secretary.
  • Firsts: Badruddin Tyabji (1887 Madras, first Muslim) · George Yule (1888, first Englishman) · Annie Besant (1917 Calcutta, first woman) · Sarojini Naidu (1925 Kanpur, first Indian woman) · Naoroji thrice (1886, 1893, 1906 — "Swaraj" word) · Nehru–Nehru father-son (1919 Amritsar Motilal; Jawaharlal 1929 Lahore).
  • Surat split 1907 (Rash Behari Ghosh); reunion Lucknow 1916 (A.C. Majumdar) with the Lucknow Pact (separate electorates accepted).

Moderate phase 1885–1905 — methods & economic critique

  • "Prayer, petition, protest"; Naoroji's Poverty and Un-British Rule (1901, drain theory), R.C. Dutt Economic History, G.V. Joshi, Wacha.
  • Achievements: Indian Councils Act 1892 (indirect election, budget discussion), Welby Commission 1895, public-service agitation (Aitchison Commission 1886).

Extremist phase 1905–1919

  • Trigger: Partition of Bengal announced July 1905, effective 16 Oct 1905 (Rakhi day, Raksha Bandhan); annulled 1911 (Delhi Durbar; capital shifted).
  • Swadeshi & Boycott: Calcutta session 1906; Bengal National College (Aurobindo principal); Bande Mataram, Sandhya, Yugantar.
  • Lal-Bal-Pal; Tilak: Ganapati 1893, Shivaji 1895 festivals; Kesari/Mahratta; sedition trial 1908 (six years Mandalay).
  • Revolutionary strand: Anushilan Samiti 1902 · Alipore conspiracy 1908 (Khudiram Bose, Prafulla Chaki — Muzaffarpur) · Curzon Wyllie shot by Madan Lal Dhingra 1909 (India House, Shyamji Krishna Varma) · Ghadar Party 1913 (San Francisco; Lala Har Dayal; Komagata Maru 1914) · Delhi conspiracy 1912 (Rash Behari Bose) · Kakori 1925 (HRA).
  • Morley–Minto (Indian Councils Act 1909) — separate electorates; Muslim League founded Dec 1906 Dhaka (Aga Khan, Nawab Salimullah).
  • Home Rule Leagues 1916: Tilak (April, Belgaum; Maharashtra-Karnataka-CP-Berar) and Besant (Sept, Madras; rest of India).
  • Champaran 1917 (indigo, tinkathia; Rajkumar Shukla) → Ahmedabad mill strike 1918 (first hunger strike) → Kheda 1918 (first non-cooperation; Patel joins).
  • Montagu Declaration 20 Aug 1917 ("responsible government") → Government of India Act 1919 (dyarchy).
  • Rowlatt Act March 1919 → Satyagraha 6 April → Jallianwala Bagh 13 April 1919 (Dyer; Hunter Committee; Tagore renounces knighthood; Shankaran Nair resigns from Viceroy's council).

Traps

  • Congress 1885 session was in Bombay, shifted from Poona due to plague.
  • Bengal partition annulled 1911 but Bihar-Orissa carved out and Assam made separate.
  • Home Rule League "first" was Tilak's (April 1916); Besant's in September.
