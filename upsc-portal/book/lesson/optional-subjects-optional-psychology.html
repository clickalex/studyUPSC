Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Paper 1: the science core (foundations, cognition, personality, social psychology). Paper 2: applications (well-being, disorders, organisational, educational, community). Compact syllabus, experiment-friendly examples, and a direct GS-4 bridge — but the marking rewards textbook precision plus Indian case application.

BlockUnitsAnchors

Paper 2 — Unit Map (Psychology in Action)

  • Well-being: stress (Selye, Lazarus appraisal), coping, health-psychology (lifestyle interventions), positive psychology (Seligman's PERMA).
  • Disorders & therapy: anxiety, mood, schizophrenia spectrum, personality disorders — descriptive features + causes (diathesis-stress); therapies: CBT, behavioural, humanistic, psychodynamic, mindfulness-based; rehabilitation & community mental health (NMHS).
  • Work & organisational: personnel selection, training evaluation, motivation at work (Herzberg applied), leadership, organisational culture, ergonomics, consumer behaviour.
  • Educational: learning-style debates, giftedness & remedial teaching, inclusive education, teacher effectiveness.
  • Community & environment: community psychology (deinstitutionalisation), environmental stressors (noise, crowding), pro-environment behaviour design.
  • Applied frontier: sports psychology, military selection, psychology of terrorism & deradicalisation, media influence.

Booklist (lean and sufficient)

  • Core: Morgan & King (Introduction to Psychology) or Ciccarelli; Baron for social psychology; standard coaching compilations for structure.
  • Paper 2: applied-psychology compilations + NCERT-Class-XII Psychology for the Indian applied voice; NMHS/DOSH? — rather: NMHS (National Mental Health Survey) findings for data.
  • Data/current: NCRB suicide data, NIMHANS survey figures, workplace-survey headlines — refreshed quarterly.

Answer Strategy

  • Definition → theory/model → one classic experiment (named: Pavlov, Skinner, Milgram, Schachter-Singer) → Indian application → brief critique.
  • Experiments are evidence: describe in two lines (design + finding) — examiners credit empirical texture.
  • Paper 2 always lands on an Indian programme (DISHA, Manodarpan, workplace EAPs, NMHS districts) — theory without programme looks imported.
  • Diagrams: model boxes (memory pipeline, stress-appraisal flow, therapy ladders) — one per answer.

Scoring Profile and Fit

  • Best for: aspirants with any science/medicine/HR affinity; GS-4 (EI, attitude) synergy; essay empathy-psychology prompts.
  • Watch-outs: strict marking on loose claims — hallucinated studies hurt; Paper 2's applied breadth needs programme literacy.

Twelve-Week First Pass

  • Wk 1-3 foundations + perception. Wk 4-5 learning-memory-thinking. Wk 6-7 motivation-emotion-personality-intelligence. Wk 8 social + attitudes. Wk 9-10 Paper 2 well-being + disorders/therapies. Wk 11 work-education-community. Wk 12 PYQ mapping + experiment bank final.

Revision Digest

Condensed for the last mile before the exam.

Why candidates pick it

  • GS-4 overlap in EI/attitude; tight syllabus; scoring history experiences steady median performance; useful for candidates with inclination toward human-behavior science.

Syllabus map

  • Paper-I (Foundations): scope-methods (experimental, correlational, field/biological bases — nervous-system/brain localisation + sensors-perception modules incl. signal-detection theory; learning theories (classical/operant/observational — Bandura social-learning; conditioning applications); memory models (multi-store → working-memory Baddeley; forgetting curves-Ebbinghaus); thinking-problem solving & intelligence (psychometrics — Spearman/Thurstone/Cattell-Horn-fluid-crystallised, Gardner multiple-intelligences, Sternberg triarchic, emotional-intelligence stream); motivation & emotion (Maslow, intrinsic-extrinsic debates, cognitive-appraisal emotion-theories — James-Lange/Cannon-Bard/Schachter-Singer comparison tables); personality theories (trait Big-Five — Costa-McCrae; psychodynamic Freud/Erikson stages; humanistic Rogers/Maslow); psychological measurement essentials (reliability-validity norms), research-methods basics + statistics intro (central-tendency/variability/correlation/hypothesis-inference norm-schematics).
  • Paper-II (Applications): psychological well-being & mental health (DSM-lite awareness, anxiety-mood disorder descriptions, community-psych wave); work-organisation psych (leadership styles, motivation-at-work models — Herzberg/Vroom/Porter-Lawler), consumer psychology + media-psych (persuasion-discipline bond with CSAT-adjacent knowledge); education (exceptional children guidance, learning-disability classification, career guidance); application to disadvantaged groups; social psychology in Paper-II wrap: attitudes-stereotypes-prejudice-intergroup dynamics (contact-hypothesis Tremblay), environment-behaviour interface, terrorism/violence-psych roots.

Framework skeleton

  • Pattern answer: theory card (name + mechanism) → study evidence line (seminal experiment anchors: Milgram, Zimbardo-critiques, Loftus memory-malleability, Harlow attachment-isolation, Bandura Bobo doll, Ainsworth strange-situation, Asch conformity, Stroop, Kohlberg-Heinz dilemma) → Indian application + measurement note.
  • Ethics of research block: informed consent + deception debriefing + orgaitho-Institute-Review-Boards (IRB) narrative.

Standard spine books

  • Ciccarelli & White (comprehensive foundations), Robert Baron (main-line text), NCERT XI-XII combo for scaffolding; Smarak Swain optional-targeted digests; Morgan-King older sets trusted for experiments summaries.

Overlap

  • GS-4 ethics (EI explicitly), essay material (human-science evidence), anthropological-lexical cohabitation.

PYQ pattern notes

  • Frequent: intelligence theory-comparison grids; motivation theory applications to governance-administration(!); prejudice-intervention drills; leadership models in organisations; research-ethics probes recur.

Caution

  • Don't over-statisticalise answers; examiners reward concept-to-application tightness over formula showers.
  • Maintain a 40-experiment rapid-recall deck — flexible deployment wins answer-texture comparisons.

  • Pop-psychology prose without experimental citation.
  • Personality answers stuck on Freud alone (trait models score better).
  • Paper 2 without Indian programmes/data.
  • Confusing stressors vs stress (Lazarus appraisal distinction).

Previous Year Questions

What the examiner has actually asked.

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Model approaches included.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. Distinguish top-down and bottom-up processing with perceptual examples. (10 marks)

Approach: define data-driven vs concept-driven → examples: reading degraded text (context fill — top-down) vs feature detection in novel stimuli (bottom-up) → interactionist evidence (word-superiority effect) → diagram the dual arrows.

Q2. Explain Baddeley's working-memory model and its evidence. (15 marks)

Approach: components — central executive, phonological loop, visuospatial sketchpad, episodic buffer → evidence: dual-task interference, phonological-similarity effect, word-length effect → upgrade from multistore account → applications: arithmetic, comprehension.

Q3. Compare James-Lange, Cannon-Bard and Schachter-Singer theories of emotion. (15 marks)

Approach: sequence diagrams (body→feeling; parallel; arousal+label) → supporting evidence each (spinal-cord cases; thalamic role; epinephrine experiment) → appraisal addition (Lazarus) → verdict: multi-route contemporary synthesis.

Q4. Evaluate the Big-Five against Freudian personality theory. (10 marks)

Approach: traits — dimensional, cross-cultural stability, psychometric base vs Freud — dynamic, developmental, defence mechanisms (clinical richness, low testability) → bridges: attachment research revives early-experience claims → use-case split: prediction vs depth-explanation.

Q5. How do Milgram's findings translate to bureaucratic wrongdoing? (10 marks)

Approach: design+finding (65% to 450V under authority) → agentic-state reading → moderators (proximity, dissent-peers, legitimate institution) → GS-4 translation: tone-at-top, note-making culture, whistle-blower protection as experimental remedies.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. Explain Lazarus's cognitive-appraisal model of stress with a workplace case. (10 marks)

Approach: primary (threat/challenge) + secondary (resource check) appraisal → reappraisal route → case: merger announcement — threat-framing vs opportunity-framing outcomes → intervention: cognitive restructuring + EAPs.

Q7. Outline CBT's logic in treating depression; evidence of efficacy? (15 marks)

Approach: cognitive-triad → automatic-thoughts → schema level → technique ladder (activity scheduling → thought records → behavioural experiments) → evidence: equal-to-pharmacotherapy in moderate cases, relapse-edge (metanalysis line) → Indian access angle: digital CBT, Manodarpan counselling lattice.

Q8. Discuss psychological contributions to deradicalisation. (10 marks)

Approach: radicalisation path (identity-quest, grievance, network pull) → counters: EXIT-style programmes (mentoring, family involvement), cognitive-opening, dignity-economic ladders → ethics: persuasion vs manipulation boundary → evaluation caution: small-N evidence.

Q9. How can nudges and norms design pro-environment behaviour? (10 marks)

Approach: social-norm feedback (opower-style home-energy reports), defaults (green tariff), commitment+public pledge → Indian cases: LED UJALA diffusion, Swachh Bharat social-proof messaging → limits: reactance, rebound effects → design principles.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: hot nodes — memory, emotion, personality, attitudes (P1); stress, therapy, IO, community (P2).
  • Weekly: 3 answers with mandatory named-experiment + one diagram; maintain the 20-experiment bank.
