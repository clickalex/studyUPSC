Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Paper 1: Indian + Western metaphysics/epistemology and logic. Paper 2: socio-political philosophy + philosophy of religion. The smallest syllabus among major optionals — but it pays only for clarity, structure and fair reconstruction of arguments.

1. How Paper 1 Indian Philosophy Is Won: Darshana Triads and the Pramana Spine

Paper 1's Indian block rewards the candidate who learns the nine darshanas as a single argument conducted across centuries rather than nine separate chapters. The argument's spine is pramana — the theory of valid knowledge — because every school's metaphysics follows from what it admits as evidence. The triad to carry runs from maximal scepticism to maximal acceptance: Carvaka admits only perception (pratyaksha), rejecting inference as uncertain and therefore rejecting soul, afterlife and Vedic authority in one stroke — materialism as epistemic hygiene, not mere hedonism. Bauddha and Vaisesika admit perception and inference, with the Buddhists adding the flux metaphysics (kshanikavada — momentariness; pratityasamutpada — dependent origination; anatman — no-self) that inference-from-impermanence yields, splitting into Madhyamaka (Nagarjuna's sunyata — emptiness of inherent existence, the middle way between eternalism and nihilism) and Yogacara (Vijnanavada — consciousness-only, the storehouse alayavijnana). Nyaya admits four pramanas (perception, inference, comparison/upamana, testimony/shabda) and builds on them the five-membered syllogism (pratijna, hetu, udaharana, upanaya, nigamana) — the inference anatomy that makes Nyaya the examination's logic engine, with the hetvabhasa fallacy list (savyabhichara, viruddha, satpratipaksha, asiddha, badhita) as its ready-made MCQ bank.

The orthodox six then arrange themselves as responses to the heterodox challenge. Samkhya (Kapila-tradition; Ishvarakrishna's Samkhyakarika) posits the dualism of purusha (consciousness, plural, passive) and prakrti (matter, active, tri-gunic), with satkaryavada (the effect pre-exists in the cause — the pot in the clay) against Nyaya's asatkaryavada (genuine novelty in causation) — the causation debate that recurs in every Paper 1. Yoga (Patanjali's Yogasutras) accepts Samkhya metaphysics and adds the theistic eighth-limb method (ashtanga — yama to samadhi) for citta-vrtti-nirodha, the cessation of mental modifications. Mimamsa (Jaimini's Purva-Mimamsa Sutras; Kumarila and Prabhakara sub-schools) defends Vedic ritual authority through svatah-pramanya (intrinsic validity of knowledge — truth needs no external certificate) and the karma-kanda's self-sufficient dharma. Vedanta closes the system with its internal triad: Advaita (Shankara — nirguna Brahman, maya as the inexplicable veiling power, jivanmukti, the mahavakya \"tat tvam asi\") against Visistadvaita (Ramanuja — qualified Brahman with attributes, saguna theism, prapatti surrender, the body-soul analogy) against Dvaita (Madhva — fivefold eternal difference/pancha-bheda, Vishnu's supremacy, graded liberation). Jaina thought (anekantavada — manifold predication; syadvada's seven-fold saptabhangi) supplies the pluralist meta-position that examiners love as a critical lens on every absolutism — including, reflexively, the Advaitin's.

The answer technology is the host's reconstruction format, sharpened: Sanskrit term + transliteration + plain-English rendering + one objection + the school's reply + assessment. A Carvaka answer that renders the position fairly (\"inference cannot guarantee universality from finite instances — the vyapti problem\") before deploying the Nyaya reply (\"Carvaka uses inference to deny inference — performative self-refutation\") demonstrates the fairness the mark scheme rewards; an answer that strawmans Carvaka as \"eat, drink and be merry\" announces it has not read the opponent. Build comparison tables in notes for the canonical pairings — Carvaka versus Nyaya on pramana, Samkhya versus Vedanta on causation and liberation, Advaita versus Dvaita on Brahman and moksha, Madhyamaka versus Yogacara on the external world's status — because Paper 1 questions are implicitly comparative even when phrased as expositions, and the candidate with the table writes the comparison while the candidate without it writes the paragraph.

2. How Paper 1 Western Philosophy and Logic Are Won

The Western block runs as a lineage of problems, and each problem must be carried with its full dialectic — thesis, antithesis, attempted synthesis — because the examination tests movement, not monuments. The ancient problem is being and knowing: Plato's Forms (universals as the truly real, the soul's recollection, the cave as epistemology) against Aristotle's immanent substance (the four causes — material, formal, efficient, final — form in matter, the categories, logic as the organon). The early-modern problem is certainty after doubt: Descartes' methodic doubt terminating in the cogito, then rebuilding God and world — but bequeathing the mind–body problem (interactionism's pineal embarrassment) that Spinoza dissolves into substance monism (Deus sive Natura, thought and extension as attributes of one substance) and Leibniz re-scores as pre-established harmony among windowless monads. The empiricist problem is where ideas come from: Locke's tabula rasa and primary–secondary quality distinction (size, shape, motion really in objects; colour, taste, sound in us) against Berkeley's esse est percipi (to be is to be perceived — the master argument that matter is an empty abstraction, with God sustaining continuity) against Hume's dissolution (impressions versus ideas, the self as a bundle, causation as constant conjunction plus mental habit — the fork that wakes Kant from \"dogmatic slumber\").

Kant is the block's summit and must be learned as a system, not a slogan. The Critique of Pure Reason asks how synthetic a priori judgments (mathematics, causality's necessity) are possible, and answers: the mind contributes space, time and the twelve categories to experience — hence phenomena (things-as-they-appear, knowable) versus noumena (things-in-themselves, thinkable but unknowable), with God, soul and world as Ideas of Reason that regulate inquiry without constituting knowledge. The moral philosophy then inverts the limit: the Categorical Imperative (act only on maxims universalisable; treat humanity always as end, never merely as means) grounds freedom, God and immortality as practical postulates — reason's needs where theoretical reason falls silent. Hegel closes the syllabus with the dialectic (being–nothing–becoming; the Absolute's self-unfolding through contradiction; master–slave as recognition's logic) — and the examination's favourite Western question is the rationalism–empiricism–Kant arc itself: carry it as one story with three acts and a Kantian coda, and half the block's questions answer themselves.

The logic block is Paper 1's most mechanical mark-earner and must be drilled, not read. Carry the Nyaya five-membered syllogism against the Aristotelian three-membered (Barbara–Celarent moods, figure rules), the square of opposition (contradictory–contrary–subcontrary–subaltern with immediate inferences), propositional basics (truth tables, the conditional's paradoxes), and the hetvabhasa list with one Sanskrit example each. Logic questions have right answers — the only block in the optional where precision is binary — so this is where mathematical comfort converts directly into marks, and where every candidate, comfortable or not, must reach flawless drills before the examination.

3. How Paper 2 Is Won: Politics, Religion, and the Contemporary Line

Paper 2 divides into socio-political philosophy and philosophy of religion, and both halves are won the same way: classical positions reconstructed fairly, then landed on a contemporary debate in one line. The socio-political block centres on five stations. Sovereignty: Austin's command theory (sovereign as illimitable commander, law as sanction-backed command) against the pluralist critique (Laski, MacIver — associations as real, sovereignty as divisible and federal) — with the Indian deployment ready (Parliamentary sovereignty bounded by basic structure). Rights: natural, legal and human-rights generations (claim-liberty-power-immunity in Hohfeld's analysis; universalism versus cultural relativism — the Asian-values debate, the Indian constitutional synthesis). Democracy: justifications (consent, participation, epistemic — Landemore) and limits (tyranny of the majority, Mill's harm principle as the boundary). Punishment: retributive (Kant, Hegel — desert as respect) versus deterrent (Bentham — utility calculus) versus reformative (probation, restorative justice — the Indian Supreme Court's Bachan Singh–Machhi Singh rarest-of-rare doctrine as the applied case). Justice frames: gender justice (liberal, radical, care-ethics feminisms — the Shah Bano-to-Shayara Bano arc as Indian material), environmental ethics (anthropocentric stewardship to biocentrism to deep ecology — Naess — with the Indian Court's Article 21 environmental jurisprudence as deployment), and corporate responsibility (Friedman shareholder primacy versus stakeholder theory — the Companies Act 2013 CSR mandate as the Indian verdict).

The philosophy of religion block is the most argument-dense in the syllabus and must be learned as proofs plus defeaters. The ontological argument (Anselm's \"that than which nothing greater can be conceived\" in the Proslogion; Descartes' perfection-entailing-existence; Gaunilo's perfect-island parody; Kant's \"existence is not a predicate\" demolition). The cosmological argument (Aquinas's first three ways — motion, causation, contingency; Leibniz's sufficient reason; Hume's and Russell's \"why must the whole need a cause?\" replies). The teleological argument (Paley's watchmaker; Hume's Dialogues critique — Cleanthes versus Philo on analogy, evil and alternative hypotheses; Darwin as the defeater-by-mechanism). The problem of evil (Epicurus's trilemma; the logical problem — Mackie's inconsistency charge — versus the evidential problem — Rowe's fawn; the free-will defence — Plantinga's transworld depravity — versus soul-making theodicy — Hick). Then religious experience (James's Varieties — noetic quality as evidence, with the conflicting-claims objection), religious language (verificationism's challenge — Ayer — versus Wittgensteinian language-games and Hick's eschatological verification), immortality (Plato's affinity argument, Hume's scepticism, Kant's postulate), and pluralism (Hick's Real an sich versus exclusivism and the feminist–postcolonial critiques). Each proof needs its strongest objector quoted fairly — the examiner marks whether you let Gaunilo, Philo and Mackie speak before you answer them.

The contemporary line — one sentence per answer — is what lifts Paper 2 from antiquarianism: punishment theory lands on the death-penalty and restorative-justice debates; environmental ethics on climate justice and the rights-of-nature litigation (Uttarakhand's Ganga order, Ecuador's constitution); religious pluralism on India's Articles 25–30 jurisprudence; AI on personhood and moral patiency (can a system without consciousness be wronged? — the Searle–Dennett frame). Philosophical neutrality is mandatory throughout, especially on religion: the examiner assesses argumentative fairness, not confessional loyalty, and the candidate who reconstructs the theist's case better than the theist — then the atheist's better than the atheist — is the candidate the mark scheme was written for.

4. The Small-Syllabus Strategy: Precision, Comparison Tables, and GS-4 Compounding

Philosophy's syllabus is the smallest among major optionals, and that smallness is both the opportunity and the trap. The opportunity is finishability: twelve weeks genuinely cover the ground (heterodox schools, orthodox systems, Western lineage, logic drills, socio-political, religion — the host's cadence), leaving months for the activity that actually scores — comparison-table marathons and reconstruction practice. The trap is that every candidate finishes, so completion confers no advantage — precision does. Marking in this optional punishes vague profundity more harshly than any other: the answer that gestures (\"Advaita teaches oneness…\") scores in the bottom band, while the answer that specifies (\"Shankara's nirguna Brahman, known through anvaya-vyatireka and the mahavakya, with maya as anirvacaniya — neither real nor unreal — veiling it\") scores at the top. The discipline is therefore terminological: every concept with its Sanskrit term, transliteration, plain-English rendering and one objection — a four-slot format enforced for every one of the roughly 150 load-bearing terms, from pratityasamutpada to the categorical imperative.

The compounding case runs through GS-4 and the Essay rather than GS-1/GS-2. Kant's deontology, Mill's utilitarianism and Aristotle's virtue ethics are the GS-4 ethics-theory spine; the punishment theories serve police–prison–justice questions; environmental and gender frames serve applied-ethics cases; and the whole syllabus is an essay-framework mine (consciousness, freedom, justice, religion, technology — the abstract topics that recur yearly). The PYQ map shows the heavily taxed zones (pramana debates, Vedanta contrasts, Kant, proofs of God, problem of evil, justice and punishment) and the rarely taxed ones (logical minutiae beyond the drills, minor Hellenistic schools) — preparation follows the map. The failure modes, in descending order: profundity without precision (the fatal one — ornament instead of argument); one-sided reconstruction (the opponent strawmanned, the reply therefore weightless); logic-block neglect (the only binary-mark block, skipped as \"too mathematical\" by exactly the candidates who need its guaranteed marks); religion-block partisanship (confession leaking into reconstruction); and contemporary-line absence (Paper 2 answers floating free of the present). Philosophy rewards the candidate who treats it as a gymnasium of fairness: the weights are your opponents' best arguments, and strength is measured by how faithfully you lift them before answering.

Trap watch. Carvaka admits perception only; Nyaya admits four pramanas (perception, inference, comparison, testimony) — the pramana count is the schools' fingerprint. Nyaya's syllogism is five-membered (pratijna to nigamana); Aristotle's is three-membered — do not merge the anatomies. Satkaryavada (Samkhya — effect pre-exists) versus asatkaryavada (Nyaya — genuine novelty) is the causation pair. Advaita (Shankara — nirguna Brahman, maya, jivanmukti) versus Visistadvaita (Ramanuja — qualified theism, prapatti) versus Dvaita (Madhva — pancha-bheda, graded moksha) — keep the Vedanta triangle's vertices distinct. Syadvada's saptabhangi is Jaina seven-fold predication, not scepticism. Berkeley's esse est percipi turns Locke's own premises against matter; Hume wakes Kant with causation-as-habit, which Kant rescues as a category — the lineage runs Locke to Berkeley to Hume to Kant, not as rivals but as a relay. Kant's phenomena–noumena divide limits theory and liberates practice (categorical imperative, freedom–God–immortality as postulates). Gaunilo parodies Anselm, Philo (Hume) cross-examines Cleanthes, Mackie states the logical problem of evil, Plantinga defends free will — objectors must be named before they are answered. Bachan Singh–Machhi Singh (rarest of rare) is the Indian punishment-theory deployment; the Companies Act 2013 CSR mandate is the stakeholder-theory verdict.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1

Explain anekantavada and syadvada; how do they answer the charge of self-contradiction? (10 marks)

  • Approach: define manifold-predication (reality's many-sidedness)
  • seven-fold modal scheme (syat-astina...)
  • objection: contradiction?
  • reply: conditional predication from standpoints (naya)
  • example: vase's material/persistent/temporal predicates
  • epistemic humility conclusion.
Q2

Compare Carvaka and Nyaya on the number and validity of pramanas. (10 marks)

  • Approach: Carvaka — perception alone; inference distrusted (observed-conjunction critique)
  • Nyaya — four pramanas with inference's anatomy (vyapti, paksata)
  • counter: Carvaka's own universal claims need inference
  • verdict: Nyaya's machinery survives the critique it answers.
Q3

Explain Hume's scepticism about causation; how does Kant respond? (15 marks)

  • Approach: Hume — no observed necessity, habit only
  • Kant — causation as a priori category constituting experience (Second Analogy)
  • implications: objective temporal order, limits on metaphysics
  • assessment: synthesis sets modern debate.
Q4

Reconstruct Anselm's ontological argument with Gaunilo's and Kant's objections. (10 marks)

  • Approach: definition-argument (greater-than-conceivable; existence as perfection)
  • Gaunilo's perfect-island parody
  • Kant: existence is not a predicate
  • evaluation: logical form vs theological intent
  • modern descendants (plantinga line) in one line.
Q5

Write a note on the five-membered Nyaya syllogism. (10 marks)

  • Approach: members (pratijna, hetu, udaharana, upanaya, nigamana) with hill-fire example
  • rhetorical redundancy?
  • reply: perspicuity for opponents + fallacy-checking seats
  • contrast with Aristotelian three-term structure.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6

"Punishment must be deserved, must deter, and must reform — three masters, one institution." Discuss. (15 marks)

Approach: retributive (proportionality, desert — Kant line) vs deterrent (Bentham's calculus, risks: scapegoating) vs reformative (rehabilitation evidence) → Indian jurisprudence hooks (rarest-of-rare doctrine balancing) → synthesis: desert as ceiling, reform as aim, deterrence as constraint.

Q7

Evaluate the free-will theodicy against the evidential problem of evil. (10 marks)

  • Approach: free-will defence (moral evil from genuine freedom)
  • evidential challenge (Mackie: why not freedom-without-holocaust?; Rowe's fawn)
  • natural-evil extension problems
  • assessment: defence vs theodicy distinction (Plantinga's logical aim)
  • humility close.
Q8

Discuss deep ecology as a critique of anthropocentric environmental ethics. (10 marks)

  • Approach: Naess's platform (intrinsic value, richness-diversity, vital needs, population-technology critique)
  • contrast with stewardship/extended-utilitarianism
  • policy translation: rights-of-river jurisprudence
  • objection: practical prioritisation
  • reply: considered judgements under ecological loading.
Q9

Can religious language be meaningful? Compare verificationist and Wittgensteinian answers. (10 marks)

  • Approach: logical-positivist challenge (cognitive-meaninglessness; Flew's gardener)
  • responses: Hare's bliks, Mitchell's partisan, Wittgenstein's language-games (grammar of worship ≠ empirical report)
  • assessment: distinct non-conflicting rationalities vs retreat-charge
  • Hick's eschatological verification as bridge.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: hot nodes — Carvaka/Bauddha/Jaina, Hume-Kant, God-proofs, punishment, environmental ethics.
  • Build 20 comparison tables; each answer must contain one objection-and-reply pair minimum.
