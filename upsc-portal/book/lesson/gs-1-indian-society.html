Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Syllabus slot: GS 1 (Mains) · Indian Society — salient features, diversity, women, population, urbanisation, communalism, regionalism, secularism, globalisation's effects

1. Structural features of Indian society

  • Unity in diversity: single civilisational frame (shared epics, pilgrimage circuits) + layered plurality (language, religion, caste).
  • Hierarchy (Dumont: pure/impure axis) braided with difference (Betéille: a society of unequal accommodations).
  • Ascriptive stratification persists (caste, kinship); modern equality codes (constitutional + electoral) reshape it.
  • Rural-urban continuum: village republics (Metcalfe's romanticism contested) → census 2027 shift will update urban share (~35% currently estimated).

2. Caste — structure and dynamics

2.1 Classical frame

  • Varna (4-fold) vs jati (thousands of endogamous units); regional jati mapping differs sharply.
  • Practices: endogamy, commensality, occupational linkage, ritual gradation; untouchability abolished (Article 17) — practice of it criminalised (PCR Act 1955 → SC/ST PoA Act 1989).

2.2 Change vectors

  • Reservation architecture: SC 15%·ST 7.5%·OBC 27% central posts; EWS 10% (103rd Amendment, 2019) — economic-only category; 106th Amendment Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023): 33% women in Lok Sabha & assemblies, post-delimitation.
  • Sanskritisation (Srinivas) → dominant-caste sect formation; Politics of "backwardisation": JP Ji/stirring: Maratha quota litigation — SC ruling (5-judge) "no breach of 50% canvas" (Indra Sawhney ceiling upheld) — later allow-state-only exception.
  • Census 2011 SECC; Bihar caste survey 2023 (OBC share 63%); national caste census debates into Census 2027 policy question — a live essay/PYQ topic.

3. Women & gender

  • Positions across domains: sex ratio 943 (2011; recent surveys ~1020 per NFHS-5, methodological caveats), LFPR female ~37–41% (up from 23% in 2017-18 — driven by self-employment coding), literacy still gender-gapped (state range wide).
  • Violence & protection: NCRB trend lines; POCSO (2012), POSH (2013), Dowry Prohibition (1961), DV Act (2005); criminal-law reforms of 2023–24 codes (BNS/BNSS/BSA) tighten sexual-offence language.
  • Policy spread: Beti Bachao Beti Padhao (2015), PM Ujjwala (2016), across maternity expansion (MB 26 weeks; NFSA nutrition support; Nari Shakti Vandan 106th Amdt → women 33% in legislatures post-delimitation).
  • SHG stack: ~9 crore women networked via SHGs (NRLM dashboards), "Lakhpati Didi" targets — evaluate empowerment vs self-exploitation ambiguity.

4. Population & demographics

  • NFHS-5 (2019–21): TFR 2.0 (below replacement), regional split (Bihar 3.4 vs Kerala <2); population momentum: India's share rises even as growth slows; median age ~28 (young vs China/US/Japan).
  • Composition: working-age bulge to ~2040 dividend window; elderly dependency rising by 2050 (old-age support policy gap).
  • Census 2027: two-phase (house-listing 2026 → enumeration 2027); digital-first; caste question as live debate (caseload for Mains essays).
  • Internal migration: ~450 mn migrants (2011 Census base); 2020 lockdown migration reversal crisis; inter-state remittance economies (UP/Bihar → metros/Gulf).

5. Urbanisation

  • Trajectory: ~17% (1951) → ~31% (2011) → ~>35% (mid-2020s); March of millions: 6 cities >10 mn; slum share declining but absolute numbers large.
  • Programmes: Smart Cities Mission (2016–), AMRUT/AMRUT 2.0, PM-AVAS Y;'Housing for All', PMAY-U 2.0 (2024), SAF/ODF ratings under Swachh Bharat 2.0.
  • Metropolitan governance gap: 74th Amendment (Nagarapalikas) weakly empowered ("directly elected mayors" vary; Mumbai vs Kolkata vs Chennai models).
  • Urban issues for essays: heat stress (wet-bulb thresholds; HAPs in 23+ states), water stress (Day Zero Chennai 2019), flooding & drainage, peri-urban ambiguity, liveable-density question.

6. Communalism, regionalism & secularism

Communalism

  • Phases (Sarkar framing): pre-1909 religion-coded reform; 1909 (separate electorates); 1940s Partition culmination; post-independence episodic riots + organised majoritarian politics.
  • Institutional guards: Art. 25–28 (religious freedom floor/ceiling), UCC direction (Art. 44; Uttarakhand UCC 2024 as test case), special NHRC/SHA mandates; syncretic buffers (shrines, shared festivals).
  • Framing point: riots have declined post-2002 in magnitude but hate-speech economy has moved online (algorithmic amplification).

Regionalism

  • Linguistic basis (SRC 1953–56, Fazl Ali Commission) — strongest single binding mechanism (AP split 1953; later state creations 2000: Uttarakhand/Jharkhand/Chhattisgarh; 2014 Telangana).
  • Dravidian movement (Periyar, DMK lineage) → two-language formula; Sons-of-soil strains (Mumbai 1960s–90s; later Assam movement).
  • North-East: insurgency declines, peace accords (Naga framework, Bodo 2020, Bru-Reang 2020), Look/Act East posture; 2023 Manipur violence as ethnic federal flashpoint.
  • Inter-state water disputes (Cauvery tribunal/Authority 2018, Mahanadi, Krishna-Godavari awards) — cooperative federalism testbed.

Secularism

  • No wall of separation; principled distance (Rajeev Bhargava) — positive-secular state can engage/disengage by principle (temple entry rights vs Sabarimala balance; hijab ban litigation Karnataka 2022).
  • Minority regimes: Art. 29–30 (educational rights); NCM Act 1992; PM 15-point programme; Haj subsidy ended 2018 → subsidy rationalisation.

7. Globalisation & Indian society

  • Material: IT/service diaspora remittances (~$107–120 bn/yr, world-leading); urban consumption-globalisation + rural monetisation.
  • Cultural: OTT cosmopolitanism vs family-system persistence; online dating vs arranged-marriage market digitalisation (Jeevansathi/BharatMatrimony economy).
  • Stratification: new inequality axes — English-medium premium, digital divide (rural female smartphone gap), platform-gig work ( aggregator vs employee status — Rajasthan Gig Workers Act 2023, Code on Social Security 2020 categories).
  • Syncretic adaptations: "McAloo Tikki" glocalisation examples; return of the swadeshi voucher — Atmanirbhar + GI exports.

8. PYQ anchors

  • "Caste is a changing institution" (MNC question 2019-ish); reservation as economic instrument debate.
  • Women's workforce participation puzzle (U-curve literature, Leela Dube legacy).
  • "Is urbanisation a sign of progress?" essay variegation.
  • Regionalism vs nationalism balance problem.
  • Impact of globalisation on elderly, marriage, and food habits.

10. Caste as a Changing Institution: Sanskritisation to the Census Question

Section 2 frames caste (varna–jati, untouchability-abolished, reservation architecture, Bihar-2023 survey); this section theorises the change — because \"caste is a changing institution\" (Section 8's first PYQ anchor) is answered in mechanisms, not assertions. Sanskritisation (M.N. Srinivas — Religion and Society among the Coorgs, 1952; Social Change in Modern India, 1966): lower jatis emulating upper-caste ritual practice (vegetarianism, dowry, Vedic rites) to claim higher status — mobility within the hierarchy, legitimising the hierarchy even as it climbs. Its limits (status without power — the Sanskritised jati still land-poor; the dominant-caste concept — Srinivas's repair: power via land, numbers, education, office — the Jats–Marathas–Reddys–Vokkaligas–Ezhavas who dominate villages whatever their varna rank) lead to the political inversion: universal franchise weaponised numbers, and the hierarchy's middle (OBCs — the Mandal moment 1990: V.P. Singh's implementation of the Mandal Commission's 27 per cent — the Indra Sawhney, 1992, settlement: 50 per cent ceiling, creamy layer, Article 16(4)'s \"adequate representation\" as enabling, not mandatory) converted demographic weight into reservation claims and reservation claims into political identity — caste associationalism (Rudolphs' \"modernity of tradition\": caste as interest-group, federation, vote-bank — the ethnicisation of jati).

The contemporary arc runs Mandal → EWS → sub-categorisation → census. The 103rd Amendment (2019) (EWS 10 per cent — economic-only category, breaching the caste-exclusive reservation logic — upheld 3:2 in Janhit Abhijan, 2022 — the majority: poverty as constitutional marker; the dissent: exclusion of SC–ST–OBC poor as discriminatory) reopened the basis-question (caste versus class as the distributive axis). Sub-categorisation (Davinder Singh, 2024 — 6:1 — States may sub-classify SCs for equitable distribution — the creamy-layer-inside-SC debate ignited — the \"quota-within-quota\" demand from Madiga–Mahar–Arunthathiyar movements) reopened the homogeneity-question (are SCs/OBCs single classes or unequal coalitions?). The Maratha quota litigation (§2 — the 5-judge Jaishri Laxmanrao Patil, 2021 — no breach of the 50 per cent ceiling, the 2018 SEBC Act struck down — States' power to identify SEBCs restored by the 105th Amendment 2021 — Maharashtra's 2024 legislation as the next round) tests the ceiling-question. And the caste-census question (Bihar 2023 — OBC-plus-EBC 63 per cent; SECC-2011's unreleased caste data; Census 2027's caste-enumeration decision — the announced inclusion — the live essay/PYQ topic, §2) will re-price every claim with numbers: the answer-formula is mechanism (Sanskritisation → dominant-caste → Mandal-ethnicisation) → architecture (SC–ST–OBC–EWS percentages, Articles 15–16, the 50 per cent ceiling) → live frontiers (EWS, sub-categorisation, Maratha, census) — argued, never listed.

11. Gender, Demography, Urbanisation: Three Puzzles Argued

Sections 3–5 map women, population and cities; this section converts each into its signature puzzle — because Mains society questions are puzzles (\"why X despite Y?\"), and puzzles need argued answers. The FLFPR puzzle (§3 — female labour-force participation ~37–41 per cent, up from 23 per cent in 2017–18 — the headline recovery): argue the decomposition (the rise driven substantially by self-employment coding — unpaid family labour counted as work — the quality caveat: regular-salaried women's share stagnant; the U-curve literature — Goldin: participation falls then rises with development — India on the curve's bottom with education-without-jobs (graduate women withdrawing — the \"discouraged graduate\" effect), care-burdens (the time-use surveys' unpaid-hours arithmetic), safety-and-mobility frictions (the transport-design audit), and the measurement debate (PLFS-coding versus payroll-data — EPFO–ESIC enrolments as the formal counter) — the answer lands: quantity recovering, quality lagging, the SHG stack (~9 crore women, Lakhpati Didi targets — empowerment-versus-self-exploitation ambiguity, §3) as the evaluated middle. The demographic puzzle (§4 — TFR 2.0 below replacement, yet population rising; median age ~28; dividend window to ~2040; elderly dependency looming by 2050): argue the momentum (population momentum — the large cohort of reproductive age keeps births high after rates fall — the Bihar-3.4-versus-Kerala-sub-2 split as the federal-demography challenge: delimitation, devolution, migration-politics all downstream of the split), the dividend's conditions (jobs, skills, health — the dividend as window, not gift — the NSQF-skills pipeline, the female-participation multiplier), and the ageing prologue (the old-age-support policy gap — pensions, geriatric care, the Maintenance-and-Welfare-of-Parents Act's thin enforcement — the 2050 crisis visible in 2026's data).

The urbanisation puzzle (§5 — ~31 per cent in 2011, ~35 per cent-plus mid-2020s, Census 2027 to confirm — \"is urbanisation progress?\"): argue both faces (agglomeration's productivity — the 6 cities above 10 million as growth engines; the governance deficit — the 74th Amendment's hollowed promise: functions–funds–functionaries denied, mayors ceremonial, parastatals (development authorities, SPVs, Smart-City vehicles) routing around elected municipalities — the city governed as a department, not a government), the programme ledger (Smart Cities 2016+, AMRUT/2.0, PMAY-U/2.0, Swachh-2.0 ODF ratings — evaluated by service-outcomes: water-hours, sewerage-coverage, the Day-Zero-Chennai-2019 memory), and the essay-issues (§5 — heat (wet-bulb thresholds, 23+ HAPs), water, floods, peri-urban informality, liveable density). The three-puzzle synthesis: gender puzzles resolve into quality-versus-quantity, demographic puzzles into window-versus-momentum, urban puzzles into engine-versus-government — each puzzle's second term being the examination's actual question.

12. Communalism, Regionalism, Secularism: The Pluralism Triad Under Examination

Section 6 maps the triad (Sarkar's communalism phases, SRC-regionalism, principled-distance secularism); this section argues it — because pluralism questions grade even-handedness under pressure, and even-handedness is a technique. Communalism: the phase-framing (§6 — pre-1909 reform-coding → separate electorates 1909 → Partition 1940s → post-independence episodic-plus-organised) extended into the present (riots declining in magnitude post-2002, the hate-speech economy migrating online — algorithmic amplification, the IT-Rules–BNS evidentiary frontier — the violence-form mutating from street to feed). The guards (§6 — Articles 25–28's floor-and-ceiling, Article 44's UCC direction with Uttarakhand-2024 as test case, NHRC–minority-commission mandates, syncretic buffers — shared shrines, festivals, the everyday-peace literature) evaluated honestly (guards holding institutionally, strained socially — the answer's balance). Regionalism: the linguistic settlement (§6 — SRC 1953–56 Fazl Ali → 1956 map → 2000 trio → Telangana 2014 — the binding mechanism that worked: language-states stabilised identity by housing it) against the live strains (sons-of-soil recurrences — domicile-job bills, the Haryana-75-per-cent saga and its judicial fate; the North-East's accord-arc — Bodo–Bru-Reang 2020, Karbi-Anglong 2021, ULFA-pro-talks 2023, the Naga framework pending — with Manipur-May-2023 (Meitei-ST-demand versus hill-autonomy-371C, President's Rule February 2025) as the ethnic-federalism flashpoint; inter-state waters — Cauvery Authority 2018, Mahanadi, Krishna-Godavari — cooperative federalism's testbed).

Secularism: principled distance (Bhargava — the state engages-or-disengages by constitutional principle, not by wall: temple-entry rights enforced, reform legislated, all religions equidistantly subject to Articles 25–28's public-order–morality–health ceiling) tested through the hard cases (Sabarimala 2018 — devotion-versus-equality, the review-reference pending — the balance unstruck; Karnataka hijab 2022 — classroom-uniform versus essential-practice — the split verdict, larger-bench questions; UCC direction — Uttarakhand 2024's live-test — uniformity-versus-plurality argued through gender-justice (the reform dividend: inheritance, marriage-age, divorce symmetry) and federal-minority anxieties (the trust deficit: reform-by-whom, imposed-how) — the examination-grade answer holding both (reform's substance welcomed, process's legitimacy questioned — sequencing: consultation, consensus-building, template-codes before compulsion). The triad's synthesis: communalism managed by guards-plus-buffers, regionalism by housing-plus-autonomy, secularism by principled distance under case-law stress — pluralism as perpetual maintenance, never solved settlement.

13. The Society Answer Method: Data Anchors, Sociological Vocabulary, Essay Bridges

Section 8's PYQ anchors (MNC-caste 2019, U-curve, urbanisation-progress, regionalism-nationalism, globalisation-effects) plus §7's globalisation map demand a method — and society answers have theirs. Data anchors first: every society answer opens its evidence with the freshest credible number (NFHS-5 TFR 2.0 + Bihar–Kerala split; FLFPR 37–41 per cent with the coding caveat; sex ratio 943-Census versus ~1020-NFHS with methodological caveats; urban ~35 per cent pending Census 2027; migrants ~450 million on the 2011 base; remittances ~$107–120 billion yearly world-leading; SHGs ~9 crore; HAPs 23+ States — §3–§7's pantry, quoted with source-and-round). Sociological vocabulary second: the concept-names that convert description into analysis — Sanskritisation, dominant caste, ethnicisation (caste); U-curve, discouraged-worker, care-economy (gender); momentum, dividend-window, dependency-ratio (demography); agglomeration, parastatal-bypass, peri-urban (cities); principled distance, syncretic buffers, sons-of-soil (pluralism); glocalisation, digital divide, platform-labour (globalisation — §7's McAloo-Tikki to Atmanirbhar-GI arc) — one to two concepts per answer, deployed (defined-then-applied), never paraded. Structure third: the society-answer frame (phenomenon → data → mechanisms (two Saddles: structural + policy) → evaluation (who gains, who pays — the distributional eye) → forward path (sequenced reform — first X, then Y)).

Essay bridges: society is the essay paper's feeder discipline (§8's \"variegation\" note) — every society unit converts (caste-census → the representation essay; FLFPR → the care-economy essay; urbanisation → the city-as-process essay; Manipur → the federalism essay; globalisation → the tradition-modernity essay — the topic-bank's families B, G, H fed directly). Maintain the bridge file: per society unit, one thesis-sentence, three anchors, one counterpoint (the index-card discipline — society preparation doubling as essay preparation, the overlap banked per the choosing-optional lesson's write-once-deploy-twice rule). Source discipline (§9): NCERT XII (Indian Society + Social Change) as the floor, Yogendra Singh (modernisation-of-tradition — the master-lens: tradition adapting, not dissolving) as the ceiling, EPW columns as the rhythm-trainers, NFHS fact-sheets as the data-bible. The method's close: society answers are data-anchored, concept-lit, bridge-ready — the candidate who quotes the number, names the mechanism, and banks the essay-bridge writes society as the GS-1 paper's most efficient subject: lived experience theorised, current data deployed, three papers fed at once.

Trap watch. Sanskritisation (Srinivas 1952/1966) climbs within hierarchy; dominant-caste (land–numbers–office) repairs it; Mandal-1990 ethnicises caste into interest-groups (Rudolphs). Indra Sawhney (1992): 50% ceiling, creamy layer, enabling provision. 103rd (2019, EWS 10%, Janhit Abhijan 3:2 2022) reopens basis; Davinder Singh (2024, 6:1) permits SC sub-classification; Maratha 5-judge (2021) holds the ceiling; Bihar-2023 (OBC+EBC 63%) and Census-2027's caste question re-price claims. FLFPR (~37–41%, self-employment-coded) decomposes into quantity-recovering-quality-lagging (U-curve, discouraged graduates, care arithmetic). TFR 2.0 (NFHS-5) with Bihar–Kerala split drives momentum, dividend-to-2040, ageing-by-2050. Urban ~35% (2027 to confirm): engine-versus-government — 74th hollowed (mayors ceremonial, parastatals bypassing). Communalism phases (Sarkar) to online hate-economy; regionalism housed by SRC-1956, strained by domicile bills, NE accords, Manipur-2023 (371C, PR Feb 2025), water disputes; secularism as principled distance (Bhargava) stress-tested (Sabarimala, hijab-2022, UCC-Uttarakhand-2024). Society answers: freshest number first (with caveats), one–two concepts deployed, phenomenon→data→mechanisms→distributional-evaluation→sequenced-path — and every unit banked as an essay bridge.

Previous Year Questions

What the examiner has actually asked.

Mains-heavy; society questions almost always appear in GS-1 (3–6 questions/yr approx). Outlines original; [PYQ-pattern] marks near-actual wording.

A. Prelims-style MCQs

Q1. Which constitutional article abolishes "untouchability" and forbids its practice? (a) Article 14 (b) Article 15 (c) Article 17 (d) Article 19

Model answer: (c). Article 17; enforcement via PCR Act 1955 → SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act 1989.

Q2. The Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam corresponds to which constitutional amendment? (a) 102nd (b) 103rd (c) 105th (d) 106th

Model answer: (d). The 106th Amendment (2023) institutionalises 33% women's reservation in Lok Sabha and state assemblies (post-delimitation). 103rd was EWS; 105th restored states' OBC list power.

Q3. Which statements about NFHS-5 (2019–21) are correct?

  1. India's TFR fell to 2.0.
  2. All states are below replacement fertility.

(a) 1 only (b) 2 only (c) Both (d) Neither

Model answer: (a). TFR = 2.0 nationally but Bihar (3.4), UP (~2.4), Jharkhand remain above replacement.

B. Mains questions with skeletons

Q4. [PYQ-pattern] "Caste system is assuming new identities and associational forms. Hence, the caste system cannot be eradicated in India." Comment. Skeleton: persistence: kinship networks, endogamy, political mobilisation → change: class overlay, urban anonymity, reservation mobility → associational forms (caste associations → pressure groups, "caste as identity-claim"); conclude: "transformative endurance — eradicated neither in social practice nor in political salience, yet structurally weakened."

Q5. Examine the paradox of rising female literacy coexisting with historically low female labour-force participation. How has recent data revised the picture? Skeleton: U-curve hypothesis (income effect vs structural transformation) → measurement critique (PLFS misses subsistence work) → NFHS/PLFS 2020s revisions (LFPR ~37–41%, quality caveat) → constraints: safety, care burden, social norms → policies: SHG economy (Lakhpati Didi), crèche push, gig code.

Q6. Regionalism in India is a "source of strength rather than weakness". Discuss in the context of linguistic states and the North-East.
