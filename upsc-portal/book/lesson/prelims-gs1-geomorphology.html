Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Interior, plate tectonics, earthquakes-volcanoes, and the four erosional agents. Statement-true/false territory.

1. Earth's interior

  • Layers: crust (continental ~35-70 km vs oceanic ~5-10 km; SIAL-SIMA terminology legacy), mantle (to ~2900 km; upper asthenosphere = partial-melt, plate lubricant), core (outer liquid, inner solid — Fe-Ni). Discontinuities: Conrad (crust-internal), Moho (crust-mantle), Repetti (mantle-internal), Gutenberg (mantle-core), Lehmann (inner core).
  • Evidence: direct (mining, drilling — deepest Kola ~12 km) vs indirect (seismic P-S wave shadow zones, meteorites density, isostasy — Airy vs Pratt).

2. Plate tectonics

  • Theory chain: continental drift (Wegener 1912: Pangaea-Panthalassa, jigsaw-fit, Glossopteris flora evidence) → sea-floor spreading (Hess 1961; palaeomagnetic stripes, youngest-at-ridge) → plate tectonics (1960s; 7 major plates: Pacific largest; Indian plate split from Gondwana, NE-drift ~5-6 cm/yr).
  • Boundaries: divergent (mid-Atlantic ridge, East African rift), convergent (ocean-continent: Andes; ocean-ocean: Japan arc; continent-continent: Himalaya), transform (San Andreas). Hotspots: Hawaii chain (intra-plate volcanism), Reunion-Deccan linkage (K-T flood basalt ~66 Ma).

3. Earthquakes & volcanoes

  • Earthquake terms: focus-hypocentre, epicentre, seismograph; Richter magnitude vs Mercalli intensity; waves P (fastest, all media) S (solids only) L/R (surface, most destructive). India seismic zones: II-V (zone V: Himalaya-NE-Rann-Kutch-Andaman).
  • Volcano types: shield (Mauna Loa), composite-strato (Fuji, Andes), cinder, caldera (Krakatoa), flood-basalt (Deccan); extrusive landforms (cone-crater-lava plateau) vs intrusive (batholith, laccolith, lopolith, phacolith, sill-dyke). Distribution: Pacific Ring of Fire (~2/3 of world volcanoes), mid-Atlantic, East African rift. Barren Island = India's only active volcano.

4. Erosional agents & landforms

  • Fluvial: youth (V-valleys, gorges, waterfalls)
  • maturity (meanders)
  • old (oxbow lakes, deltas — arcuate/bird-foot/estuarine)
  • peneplain concept. Arid: deflation-hollows, mushroom rocks, yardangs-zeugen, inselberg-playa
  • loess (wind-blown, China). Glacial: cirque-horn-arete (erosion), moraine-drumlins-eskers (deposition), U-valleys, fjords. Coastal: cliffs-wave-cut platforms, beaches-spits-bars, lagoons
  • Karst (groundwater): sinkholes, caves-stalactites-stalagmites.
  • India anchors: Himalaya (Siwalik-Lesser-Great-Karakoram-Ladakh ranges; syntaxial bends), Peninsular block stability, Deccan Traps, Western vs Eastern Ghats asymmetry, coastal plains (Konkan-Malabar vs Utkal-Coromandel), islands (Andaman-Nicobar extension of Arakan-Yoma; Lakshadweep coral atolls).

⚡ MCQ hooks

  • Moho = crust-mantle; Gutenberg = mantle-core; shadow zones = liquid outer core proof.
  • Sea-floor spreading youngest-at-ridge; Hess; palaeomagnetism evidence.
  • Zone-V list = Himalayan arc (J&K, HP, Uttarakhand) + NE states + Kutch + Andaman-Nicobar; Bihar is zone IV.
  • Barren Island active; Narcondam dormant.

5. Earth's Interior and the Evidence for It

Direct observation of the interior is impossible, so knowledge comes from indirect sources: density and pressure gradients, temperature increase with depth (the geothermal gradient), meteorites as analogues of planetary composition, gravitational and magnetic anomalies, and above all seismic waves.

Body waves are of two kinds. P-waves (primary) are longitudinal compressional waves, the fastest, and travel through solids, liquids and gases. S-waves (secondary) are transverse shear waves, slower, and travel only through solids — the single most important fact in this topic, because it proves the outer core is liquid. Surface waves (Love and Rayleigh) are the slowest but the most destructive.

The shadow zones follow from this. S-waves are absent beyond 103° from the epicentre, producing a shadow zone covering the entire region beyond that arc. P-waves are absent between 103° and 142°, a narrower band caused by refraction at the core-mantle boundary; beyond 142° they reappear. Note the asymmetry: the S-wave shadow zone is larger than the P-wave shadow zone.

Interior layers and their discontinuities: crust (oceanic sima, basaltic, thinner and denser; continental sial, granitic, thicker and lighter), separated from the mantle by the Mohorovičić discontinuity; the mantle, containing the plastic asthenosphere that permits plate motion, bounded below by the Gutenberg discontinuity; the liquid outer core, whose convection generates Earth's magnetic field by the dynamo effect; and the solid inner core, separated by the Lehmann discontinuity. The lithosphere — crust plus uppermost rigid mantle — is what fragments into plates. The Conrad discontinuity separates upper and lower crust; the Repetti separates upper and lower mantle.

6. Plate Tectonics and Boundary Types

The lineage runs from Wegener's continental drift (1912), which assembled evidence — the jigsaw fit of Atlantic coasts, matching Glossopteris flora and Mesosaurus fossils, the Gondwana coal and tillite deposits, and the Caledonian mountain alignment — but failed for want of a mechanism. Holmes proposed mantle convection currents; Harry Hess's sea-floor spreading (1960s) supplied the proof, confirmed by palaeomagnetic striping symmetrical about mid-ocean ridges and by the finding that oceanic crust is youngest at the ridge and oldest at the trenches, and nowhere older than about 200 million years. Plate tectonics synthesised all of this.

BoundaryMotionLandforms and phenomena
Divergent (constructive)Plates move apartMid-ocean ridges, rift valleys, shallow-focus earthquakes, fissure volcanism. Examples: Mid-Atlantic Ridge, East African Rift, Iceland
Convergent — oceanic/continentalDenser oceanic plate subductsDeep trench, fold mountains with explosive volcanoes, deep-focus quakes. Example: Andes, Nazca under South America
Convergent — oceanic/oceanicOlder denser plate subductsIsland arcs and trenches. Examples: Japan, Philippines, Aleutians, Mariana Trench
Convergent — continental/continentalNeither subducts; crust crumplesMassive fold mountains, no volcanism, shallow to intermediate quakes. Example: Himalaya, Alps
Transform (conservative)Plates slide pastFaults, shallow but severe earthquakes, no volcanism and no crust created or destroyed. Example: San Andreas Fault

The Pacific Ring of Fire owes its concentration of volcanoes and earthquakes to the near-continuous subduction ringing the Pacific plate. Note that the Himalaya, being a continent-continent collision, has fold mountains and earthquakes but no volcanoes — a discrimination examiners exploit. Hotspots such as Hawaii and the Deccan-forming Réunion plume are intra-plate, produced by stationary mantle plumes beneath moving plates, which is why they form age-progressive island chains.

7. Earthquakes, Volcanoes and Rocks

Earthquakes. The focus (hypocentre) is the point of origin within the Earth; the epicentre is the point vertically above it on the surface. Focal depth classes: shallow (under 70 km, most destructive), intermediate, and deep (up to about 700 km, confined to subduction zones along the Benioff zone). The Richter scale measures magnitude — energy released, logarithmic, so each whole number is about 32 times more energy; the Moment Magnitude Scale is now preferred for large events; the Mercalli scale measures intensity, that is, observed damage, and varies with distance and geology. Secondary effects: tsunami (generated by vertical displacement of the sea floor, not by lateral movement), liquefaction of saturated loose sediment, and landslides. India's seismic zoning runs from Zone II to Zone V, Zone V being the highest risk and covering the north-east, Kutch, Andaman and Nicobar and parts of the Himalaya — there is no Zone I.

Volcanoes classified by activity are active, dormant or extinct. By form and eruption: shield volcanoes from low-viscosity basaltic lava with gentle slopes (Hawaii, Mauna Loa); composite or strato volcanoes from viscous andesitic lava with alternating ash and lava layers, steep and explosive (Fuji, Vesuvius); caldera, formed by collapse after a violent eruption — the most explosive type; flood basalt provinces from fissure eruptions (the Deccan Traps); and mid-ocean ridge volcanoes. Silica content governs behaviour: high silica gives viscous lava and explosive eruptions, low silica gives fluid lava and effusive eruptions. Extrusive landforms include lava plateaus and cones; intrusive forms are the batholith, laccolith, lopolith, phacolith, sill and dyke — the dyke being discordant and vertical, the sill concordant and horizontal.

  • The rock cycle. Igneous rocks form from cooling magma — intrusive/plutonic with large crystals from slow cooling (granite), extrusive/volcanic with fine crystals from rapid cooling (basalt). Sedimentary rocks form by deposition and lithification, are stratified and uniquely contain fossils, and are the only source of coal and petroleum
  • they are mechanically, organically or chemically formed (sandstone, limestone, coal). Metamorphic rocks form from existing rock under heat and pressure, without melting. Fix the standard transformations: limestone

8. Weathering, Erosion and Agents of Landform Change

Weathering is in-situ disintegration with no transport; erosion involves removal and transport; mass wasting is downslope movement under gravity. Weathering types: physical (frost shattering, thermal expansion producing exfoliation and onion weathering, salt crystallisation, unloading), chemical (carbonation, hydrolysis, oxidation, hydration, solution), and biological. The distinction matters because chemical weathering dominates in the humid tropics and physical weathering in arid and periglacial climates.

Fluvial landforms by stage: youthful — V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, waterfalls, rapids, potholes, interlocking spurs; mature — meanders, floodplains, natural levees, river cliffs and point bars; old — oxbow lakes, deltas, braided channels. Davis's cycle of erosion (youth-maturity-old age, ending in a peneplain) is the classical model; Penck's and King's (pediplanation) are alternatives.

Glacial — erosional: cirque, arête, horn, U-shaped valley, hanging valley, roche moutonnée, fjord; depositional: moraine, drumlin, esker, kame, outwash plain. Aeolian — erosional: mushroom rock, yardang, zeugen, deflation hollow, ventifact; depositional: barchan (crescent, horns pointing downwind), seif (longitudinal), loess. Karst topography in limestone — lapies, sinkholes and dolines, uvala and polje, caverns, stalactites (from the ceiling) and stalagmites (from the floor), joining as pillars; underground drainage is its hallmark. Coastal — erosional: cliffs, wave-cut platforms, sea caves, arches, stacks and stumps; depositional: beaches, spits, bars, tombolos and lagoons.

Trap watch. S-waves cannot pass through liquids, which proves the outer core is liquid; the inner core is solid. The P-wave shadow zone lies between 103° and 142°, the S-wave shadow beyond 103°. Continent-continent collision produces fold mountains without volcanism, so the Himalaya has no volcanoes. Transform boundaries neither create nor destroy crust. Richter measures magnitude, Mercalli intensity. Only sedimentary rocks bear fossils. Barchan horns point downwind. Stalactites hang from the ceiling. India's seismic zones begin at II, not I.
