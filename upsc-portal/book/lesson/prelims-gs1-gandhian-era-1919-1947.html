Gandhian Era: Non-Cooperation to Quit India (1919–1947)

Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Mass politics: Non-Cooperation → Civil Disobedience → provincial ministries → Quit India → partition settlement.

1. Khilafat & Non-Cooperation (1920-22)

  • Khilafat movement (Ali brothers; Karachi khilafat conference 1921
  • Istanbul abolition 1924 ends cause) + Punjab grievances + Swaraj demand
  • Calcutta special session Sept 1920
  • Nagpur Dec 1920 programme: boycott councils-courts-schools-titles-foreign cloth
  • Chauri Chaura 5 Feb 1922
  • Bardoli resolution Feb 1922 withdrawal (Nehru-Bose dismay)
  • Swarajists (Motilal-C.R. Das — council entry 1923) vs No-changers (constructive: khadi, untouchability, Harijan 1933 paper).

2. Civil Disobedience (1930-34)

  • Salt March: Sabarmati 12 Mar 1930
  • Dandi 6 Apr (241 miles; 78 volunteers)

  • First RTC (Nov 1930, Congress absent)
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact 5 Mar 1931
  • Karachi session Mar 1931 (fundamental rights + economic programme resolution; Bhagat Singh execution mourning)
  • Second RTC (Gandhi sole rep) fails
  • resumed CDM
  • Third RTC (1932).
  • Communal Award 16 Aug 1932 (Ramsay MacDonald; separate dalit electorates) → Gandhi fast → Poona Pact 24 Sep 1932 (joint electorate + reserved seats — Ambedkar-Gandhi); CDM formally withdrawn 1934.
  • Left stream: HRA 1924
  • HSRA 1928 (Saunders killing — Bhagat Singh-Rajguru; Delhi Assembly bombs 1929; Lahore conspiracy; 23 Mar 1931 executions)
  • Meerut conspiracy 1929
  • Chittagong armoury 1930 (Surya Sen
  • Pritilata, Kalpana)
  • Bhagat Singh 'Why I am an Atheist'

  • Individual satyagraha (Vinoba first)
  • Cripps Mission Mar 1942 fails ('post-dated cheque' quip)

  • Azad Hind: INA reformed 1943 Singapore (Bose — Rani Jhansi regiment, Lakshmi Sahgal; 'Jai Hind'; Andamans renamed; Imphal-Kohima 1944; Red Fort trials 1945-46 — Shah Nawaz-Prem Sahgal-Sehgal).

  • Shimla Conference 1945 (Wavell) fails

5. Three movements, one escalating grammar — boycott, break law, quit

The trunk narrates Non-Cooperation (1920–22), Civil Disobedience (1930–34), and Quit India (1942) in sequence; the Mains move is to read them as one grammar with escalating verbs. Non-Cooperation's verb was boycott — titles, legislatures, courts, schools (Kashi Vidyapith, Jamia Millia, Gujarat Vidyapith founded), foreign cloth (bonfires, khadi, charkha), eventually taxes — launched at Calcutta (September 1920, Lajpat Rai presiding) and Nagpur (December 1920, the new linguistic-provinces constitution, "swaraj by peaceful and legitimate means," Rajagopalachari drafting, Kumarappa's khadi arsenal), fused with the Khilafat alliance (Ali Brothers) into the peak of Hindu–Muslim collaboration. Its sociology was withdrawal: deny the Raj Indian cooperation and watch administration seize. Civil Disobedience's verb was break — break the salt law (Dandi, 12 March–6 April 1930, 78 volunteers, 240 miles, 6:30 a.m.), break forest laws, break the legitimacy of RTCs staged without Congress; Karachi (March 1931, Patel presiding) converted disobedience into a programme (Fundamental Rights + Economic Programme — the Rooseveltian bridge). Quit India's verb was quit — no demands, no negotiations, just "Do or Die" (Gowalia Tank, 8 August 1942; arrests before dawn on the 9th) and the leaderless second phase: rail–telegraph–post sabotage, Ballia–Tamluk–Satara parallel governments, 90,000+ arrested, aircraft machine-gunning Midnapore.

The comparison table examiners want: NCM withdrew from strength preserved (Bardoli, 12 February 1922, after Chauri Chaura's 22 dead policemen on 5 February — Gandhi jailed March 1922, six years); CDM negotiated from strength demonstrated (Irwin Pact 5 March 1931 → Second RTC's staged failure → Communal Award August 1932 → Yeravada fast → Poona Pact 24 September 1932, reserved seats in joint electorates, Ambedkar consenting after supporting the Award); QIM fought to delegitimisation (crushed by 1944 — but, in the trunk's exact line, the British lost India the moment they had to rule it by machine-guns from aircraft). The escalation logic: each movement began where the previous one's settlement had failed — NCM where dyarchy insulted, CDM where Simon excluded, QIM where Cripps's "post-dated cheque on a failing bank" confirmed that war would not buy freedom. Verdict line: boycott taught India it could stand apart from the Raj; disobedience taught it could defy the Raj; Quit India taught the Raj it could no longer stand in India.

6. Gandhi's critics and the partition question — argued fairly

No Gandhian-era answer is complete without the critics, and no critic should be caricatured. Ambedkar's critique: Gandhi's Harijan campaign (magazine Harijan, February 1933; temple-entry framing via Vaikam 1924–25) treated untouchability as a Hindu conscience problem while opposing the political weapon the Depressed Classes wanted — separate electorates (Communal Award 1932); Mahad (1927) and Kalaram (1930) satyagrahas asserted rights, not charity; the Poona Pact, in this reading, traded political power for reserved seats. Bose's critique: Haripura (1938) and Tripuri (February 1939, defeating Pattabhi) mandates for militant confrontation were strangled by the old guard's non-cooperation with its own president; resignation → Forward Bloc (May 1939) → INA (1943) → Azad Hind Government (21 October 1943, Singapore) → Kohima–Imphal (1944) proved, he argued, that freedom would be seized, not gifted. The socialist critique (Congress Socialist Party 1934; CPI 1925; AITUC 1920; Kisan Sabha 1936 Faizpur — Sahajanand, Ranga; Tebhaga–Telangana–Warli 1940s): the trunk's movements mobilised peasants and workers but never against Indian landlords and capitalists — class struggle suspended inside national struggle. The revolutionary critique (Kakori 1925; Assembly bomb 1929; Bhagat Singh–Rajguru–Sukhdev hanged 23 March 1931; Chittagong 18 April 1930, Surya Sen hanged 1934): martyrdom moved the nation more than negotiation did.

The partition-responsibility debate must be handled with the trunk's chronology, not slogans. The structuralists cite: separate electorates since 1909 manufacturing communal electorates; the 1937–39 Congress ministries' resignations → League's Deliverance Day (22 December 1939); Cripps conceding the non-accession principle (1942); the Cabinet Mission's three-tier Union (Sections A/B/C, Union for foreign affairs/defence/communications, 10-year reconsideration) accepted-then-revoked after Nehru's 10 July 1946 press statement; Direct Action Day (16 August 1946, Great Calcutta Killings 4,000+) making partition the lesser evil; Mountbatten's 3 June Plan → Independence Act (assent 18 July) → 15 August. Gandhi's own ledger: the Khilafat gamble (unity 1920–22, collapse into Shuddhi–Sangathan and riots after), the sole-delegate humiliation at the Second RTC (September–December 1931, ranged against every minority claimant), and the final one-man peace missions (Noakhali autumn 1946, Calcutta August 1947, Delhi fast January 1948) ending at Birla House on 30 January 1948. The balanced verdict: Gandhi made the nation mass and the masses national — but the instruments that built Hindu–Muslim unity in 1920 could not survive the electorates that divided them after 1932; partition was overdetermined, and biography explains less than institutions.

7. The Gandhian method for answers — satyagraha anatomy

Close by converting the trunk's synthesis (§12) into a reusable answer machine. Satyagraha's anatomy has five organs: (i) a moral fact nobody can defend (Rowlatt's "no vakil, no daleel, no appeal"; the salt tax; Simon's all-white composition); (ii) a symbolic act the illiterate can perform (bonfire, handful of salt, Dharasana's unresisting ranks under Sarojini Naidu, 21 May 1930, Webb Miller watching); (iii) mass organisation built before the confrontation (Nagpur constitution 1920; Bardoli 1928 as the laboratory where Patel became Sardar; Khudai Khidmatgars in Peshawar 1930; women's first mass mobilisation — Kamaladevi, thousands rural and urban); (iv) negotiation from strength (Irwin Pact; Karachi's rights resolution as the price of peace); (v) strategic withdrawal before violence corrupts the weapon (Bardoli 1922; May 1934). Each organ answers a stock question: why salt (simplest commodity carrying a whole theory of illegitimate monopoly); why withdraw (non-violence is the weapon, and a rioting movement is a disarmed movement); why the constructive programme (khadi, Hindu–Muslim unity, Harijan work, prohibition — the movement's peacetime barracks).

  • Two final frames. The "impact on British" frame: Jallianwala (13 April 1919) destroyed moderate faith
  • NCM proved administration needed Indian cooperation
  • CDM proved law needed Indian consent (globalised via Miller's cables)
  • the 1937 ministries proved Indians could govern
  • QIM proved Britain could only rule by force
  • the INA trials (Red Fort 1945) and the naval ratings' revolt proved even the armed forces were no longer reliable — Attlee's 20 February 1947 statement (leave by June 1948) priced all of it. The "limits" frame (always concede before concluding): industrial workers peripheral until the 1940s
  • princely India largely outside
  • communal unity transactional
  • and every mass upsurge ended in elite negotiation — the masses marched, the Working Committee signed. Closing line: satyagraha was India's first technology of mass power — morally distinctive, tactically flexible, strategically finite, and sufficient exactly once, against exactly that empire, in exactly those decades.

1919–1922: Khilafat & Non-Cooperation

  • Khilafat Committee 1919 (Ali brothers, Maulana Azad); All-India Khilafat Conference Nov 1919 Delhi; Gandhi president.
  • Non-Cooperation adopted at Calcutta special session Sept 1920 (Lala Lajpat Rai), confirmed Nagpur Dec 1920 (C. Vijayaraghavachariar) — new Congress constitution, PCCs on linguistic basis, 4-anna membership.
  • Programme: boycott of titles, courts, schools, councils, foreign cloth; Tilak Swaraj Fund (₹1 crore); national institutions (Jamia Millia, Kashi Vidyapeeth, Gujarat Vidyapeeth).
  • Chauri Chaura 5 Feb 1922 → Bardoli resolution 12 Feb withdraws; Gandhi tried March 1922 (Judge Broomfield, 6 years).
  • Swaraj Party 1923 (C.R. Das president, Motilal Nehru secretary; Gaya session split "pro-changers" vs "no-changers").

1927–1931: Simon to Gandhi–Irwin

  • Simon Commission announced Nov 1927 (all-white); arrived 3 Feb 1928; Lahore lathi-charge — Lala Lajpat Rai dies (Nov 1928) → Saunders killed (Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Azad).
  • Nehru Report Aug 1928 (Motilal; dominion status; Jinnah's 14 points March 1929).
  • Bardoli Satyagraha 1928 (Patel earns "Sardar").
  • Assembly bomb 8 April 1929 (Bhagat Singh, B.K. Dutt); Lahore conspiracy → executed 23 March 1931.
  • Lahore Dec 1929 (Jawaharlal) — Purna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930 Independence Day.
  • Dandi March 12 March–6 April 1930 (Sabarmati → Dandi, 240 miles, 78 marchers)
  • Civil Disobedience
  • Dharasana raid (Sarojini Naidu)
  • Peshawar (Khudai Khidmatgars, Garhwali soldiers refuse fire)
  • Sholapur.
  • RTC-1 Nov 1930 (Congress absent)
  • Gandhi–Irwin Pact 5 March 1931
  • Karachi session March 1931 (Patel; Fundamental Rights & National Economic Programme)
  • RTC-2 Sept–Dec 1931 (Gandhi alone)
  • CDM resumed Jan 1932.

1932–1939: Communal Award to war

  • Communal Award 16 Aug 1932 (MacDonald) → Gandhi fast Yeravda → Poona Pact 24 Sept 1932 (Ambedkar; reserved seats 148 in provinces, joint electorate). Harijan Sevak Sangh.
  • GoI Act 1935; elections 1937 — Congress ministries in 8 provinces (July 1937), resigned Oct–Nov 1939 ("Deliverance Day" 22 Dec 1939, League).
  • Haripura 1938 & Tripuri 1939 (Bose defeats Sitaramayya; resigns; Forward Bloc May 1939).

1940–1947: Endgame

  • Lahore Resolution 23 March 1940 (League; "Pakistan" not named); August Offer 8 Aug 1940 (Linlithgow); Individual Satyagraha Oct 1940 (Vinoba first, Nehru second).
  • Cripps Mission March 1942 — "post-dated cheque"; rejected.
  • Quit India 8 Aug 1942 (Bombay, Gowalia Tank; "Do or Die"); underground: Jayaprakash, Aruna Asaf Ali, Usha Mehta (radio), parallel governments Ballia, Tamluk (Jatiya Sarkar), Satara (Prati Sarkar).
  • INA: Mohan Singh 1942
  • Bose takes over July 1943 (Singapore), Azad Hind government 21 Oct 1943
  • Imphal-Kohima 1944
  • Red Fort trials Nov 1945 (Shah Nawaz, Sahgal, Dhillon; Bhulabhai Desai defends).
  • C.R. formula 1944
  • Desai–Liaquat pact 1945
  • Wavell Plan / Simla Conference June 1945
  • RIN mutiny Feb 1946 (Bombay).
  • Cabinet Mission May 1946 (Pethick-Lawrence, Cripps, Alexander) — grouping A/B/C
  • Direct Action Day 16 Aug 1946
  • Interim Government 2 Sept 1946 (Nehru)
  • Constituent Assembly first meets 9 Dec 1946.
  • Attlee statement 20 Feb 1947 (June 1948 deadline)
  • Mountbatten Plan 3 June 1947
  • Indian Independence Act 18 July 1947
  • 15 Aug 1947; Radcliffe line.

⚡ Traps

  • Non-cooperation adopted at Calcutta (Sept 1920) but ratified at Nagpur.
  • Purna Swaraj Congress president was Jawaharlal Nehru (Lahore 1929); Karachi 1931 was Patel.
  • Lahore Resolution moved by Fazlul Haq; Bose was not at Tripuri's aftermath Forward Bloc as Congress president.

8 original prelims-pattern questions; key with one-line reasoning at the end.

Q1

The Non-Cooperation programme was ratified with a new Congress constitution at the:

  1. (a)Calcutta special session 1920
  2. (b)Nagpur session 1920
  3. (c)Ahmedabad session 1921
  4. (d)Gaya session 1922
Show answer
Correct: (b)Nagpur Dec 1920 (Vijayaraghavachariar); Calcutta adopted, Nagpur ratified.
Q2

Which session adopted the resolution on Fundamental Rights and National Economic Programme?

  1. (a)Lahore 1929
  2. (b)Karachi 1931
  3. (c)Faizpur 1936
  4. (d)Haripura 1938
Show answer
Correct: (b)Karachi 1931, presided by Patel; drafted largely by Nehru.
Q3

Consider the statements on the Poona Pact (1932): 1. It replaced separate electorates for depressed classes with reserved seats under joint electorates. 2. It was signed between Gandhi and Jinnah.

  1. (a)1 only
  2. (b)2 only
  3. (c)Both
  4. (d)Neither
Show answer
Correct: (a)Signed by Ambedkar and Madan Mohan Malaviya (for Gandhi) — not Jinnah.
Q4

Arrange chronologically: 1. Cripps Mission 2. August Offer 3. Quit India 4. Individual Satyagraha

  1. (a)2-4-1-3
  2. (b)4-2-1-3
  3. (c)2-1-4-3
  4. (d)1-2-4-3
Show answer
Correct: (a)Aug 1940 → Oct 1940 → Mar 1942 → Aug 1942.
Q5

The parallel "Jatiya Sarkar" during Quit India functioned at:

  1. (a)Ballia
  2. (b)Satara
  3. (c)Tamluk
  4. (d)Talcher
Show answer
Correct: (c)Tamluk (Midnapore) — Jatiya Sarkar; Satara had Prati Sarkar.
Q6

The Cabinet Mission (1946) did NOT include:

  1. (a)Pethick-Lawrence
  2. (b)Stafford Cripps
  3. (c)A.V. Alexander
  4. (d)Lord Wavell
Show answer
Correct: (d)Wavell was Viceroy, not a member.
Q7

Which of these is correctly matched?

  1. (a)Dharasana salt raid — Sarojini Naidu
  2. (b)Peshawar — Rajguru
  3. (c)Sholapur — Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan
  4. (d)Vedaranyam — Jawaharlal Nehru
Show answer
Correct: (a)Vedaranyam was Rajagopalachari; Peshawar was Ghaffar Khan.
Q8

The Attlee statement of 20 February 1947 fixed which date as the deadline for transfer of power?

  1. (a)15 August 1947
  2. (b)26 January 1948
  3. (c)June 1948
  4. (d)December 1947
Show answer
Correct: (c)June 1948; Mountbatten advanced it to 15 Aug 1947.
