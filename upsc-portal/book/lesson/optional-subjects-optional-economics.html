1. How Paper 1 Micro–Macro Is Won: Diagrams, Derivations, and the Model Ladder

Paper 1's micro–macro core is won by diagram-first culture with derivation discipline, because the marking is exacting in precisely these two currencies: the labelled graph and the one-step algebra. The micro ladder climbs consumer → producer → market → welfare, and each rung has its canonical diagram plus its canonical derivation. Consumer theory (utility → indifference → revealed preference — Hicks–Allen–Slutsky lineage; the Slutsky equation as the crown derivation — price effect = substitution + income, with the Giffen–inferior–normal taxonomy; Samuelson's revealed preference as the behaviouralist repair; Hicksian–Marshallian duality for the top band) must be drawable: the decomposition diagram, correctly labelled, answers the 10-marker before a word is written. Production–costs (Cobb–Douglas and CES properties, isoquant–isocost tangency, the envelope — short-run to long-run average cost; duality — cost functions implying input demands) feed market structures (perfect competition's efficiency benchmark → monopoly's deadweight triangle and price-discrimination degrees → monopolistic competition's excess capacity (Chamberlin) → oligopoly models — Cournot (quantity reaction), Bertrand (price undercutting), Stackelberg (leader–follower), kinked demand (Sweezy's rigidity), collusion–cartel stability — each with its reaction-function or payoff diagram; game theory basics — Nash equilibrium, prisoner's dilemma, dominant strategies). Welfare economics (Pareto criteria, the two fundamental theorems, social-welfare functions — Benthamite–Rawlsian–Bergson-Samuelson; market failures — externalities (Pigou versus Coase — tax against bargaining, the assignment-of-rights insight), public goods (Samuelson–Musgrave — non-rivalry–non-excludability, free-riding, Lindahl pricing), information asymmetry (Akerlof's lemons, Spence signalling, Stiglitz screening — the 2001 Nobel trinity)) closes the ladder: the failure taxonomy is the bridge to public finance and to every Paper-2 market-versus-state debate.

  • The macro ladder climbs accounting
  • money
  • synthesis
  • expectations
  • open economy. National-income debates (GDP–GNP–NNP, the base-revision and informal-sector measurement controversies — the Indian back-series debate as Paper-2 ammunition)
  • money and banking (money multiplier derivation, central-bank toolkit — CRR–SLR–repo–OMO–LAF corridor — the Indian operating framework as illustration)
  • inflation theories (demand-pull–cost-push–structural — the Latin American structuralists via Indian food-inflation; the Phillips curve — short-run trade-off, Friedman–Phelps expectations-augmented verticality, the NAIRU, New Keynesian Phillips Curve)
  • the IS-LM synthesis and beyond (Hicks–Hansen — fiscal–monetary effectiveness under slopes and traps — the liquidity-trap and classical-range extremes; the monetarist counter-revolution — Friedman's k-percent rule; new classical — Lucas (rational expectations, policy ineffectiveness), real business cycles (Kydland–Prescott — technology shocks, calibration)
  • new Keynesian — menu costs, efficiency wages, staggered contracts — microfoundations for stickiness
  • the DSGE synthesis and its post-2008 critics — the financial-friction repair)
  • open-economy macro (BOP accounting — current–capital–errors; Mundell–Fleming — the impossible trinity as the organising theorem — fixed rate + free capital + monetary autonomy, pick two
  • exchange-rate regimes — fear-of-floating, the RBI's managed float as illustration
  • crisis models — first-generation (Krugman–Flood-Garber — fiscal-deficit monetisation), second-generation (Obstfeld — self-fulfilling expectations), third-generation (balance-sheet–twin crises — the Asian-crisis lineage)). Six standard derivations, practised monthly (Slutsky, multiplier-with-imports, money multiplier, IS-LM slopes, Solow steady-state, Mundell–Fleming results), plus the diagram canon (ten graphs drawable blindfold) constitute the rung-by-rung mastery the paper prices.

2. How Paper 1 Growth–Public–International Is Won: Models, Theorems, and the Debate Bank

The second half of Paper 1 is won through named models with named critics — the examination prices the model–critique pair, never the model alone. Growth and development runs the model sequence as intellectual history: Harrod–Domar (the warranted–natural rate knife-edge — instability as the message), Solow–Swan (diminishing returns, steady state, the residue — technology as exogenous mystery; convergence — absolute, conditional, club — the Barro–Sala-i-Martin evidence), endogenous growth (Romer — knowledge spillovers, increasing returns; Lucas — human capital; Aghion–Howitt — creative destruction — policy back in the driver's seat), with development theory alongside (Lewis dualism — unlimited labour supply, the turning point; Rosenstein-Rodan's big push versus Hirschman's unbalanced growth — linkages, the deliberate-imbalance strategy; Rostow's stages as the foil; dependency — Prebisch–Singer, Frank — versus the East-Asian-statist reply; Sen's capabilities — development as freedom — functioning, agency, the HDI lineage; multidimensional poverty — Alkire–Foster). The environment–development interface (Kuznets curve debated, sustainable-development accounting — green GDP, genuine savings, the Dasgupta review's biodiversity economics) closes the block. Public finance runs Musgrave's three branches (allocation–distribution–stabilisation) through tax theory (incidence — statutory versus economic; efficiency — excess burden, the Ramsey rule — inverse-elasticity; optimal taxation — Mirrlees–Diamond-Saez lineage), fiscal federalism (Oates's decentralisation theorem, Tiebout sorting, flypaper effects, vertical–horizontal imbalances — the Finance Commission bridge to Paper 2), and debt dynamics (Domar sustainability — growth-versus-interest arithmetic; Ricardian equivalence debated — Barro versus the Keynesian–liquidity replies; fiscal rules — FRBM as the Indian case).

International economics runs trade theory as a ladder of repairs: Ricardo (comparative advantage — the single most robust theorem in the syllabus), Heckscher–Ohlin (factor endowments; the Stolper–Samuelson, Rybczynski, factor-price-equalisation corollaries; the Leontief paradox as the empirical sting), new trade theory (Krugman — scale economies, intra-industry trade, the home-market effect — the 2008 Nobel), new-new trade (Melitz — firm heterogeneity, exporter superstars), trade policy (tariff welfare triangles — the diagram-first 10-marker; effective protection; strategic trade — Brander–Spencer; the infant-industry–Mill–Bastable–Kemp criteria), WTO law basics (MFN, national treatment, the dispute-settlement crisis — Appellate Body paralysis since 2019), and BOP-crises literature (the three generations from §7, applied to 1991, 1997, 2008, 2013-taper). The debate bank (inflation-targeting band — 4±2's defenders versus growth-first critics; FRBM glide paths — rules versus discretion after COVID; fiscal–monetary dominance — the Sargent–Wallace unpleasant arithmetic; GST rate-architecture — slabs versus single rate, inclusion of petroleum; capital-account convertibility — Tarapore I–II versus the managed-float consensus; universal-basic-income versus targeted-DBT — the welfare-architecture debate) converts theory into Paper-2-ready positions: each debate with its theorem, its Indian datum, and its two named sides.

The cross-paper compounding is the optional's great dividend: every Paper-1 theorem is a Paper-2 lens (Solow for the demographic-dividend window, Mundell–Fleming for the rupee debate, Ramsey for GST design, Oates for the Finance Commission, Lewis for the farm-to-factory transition) — and answers that name the lens before applying it score in both papers' currencies simultaneously.

3. How Paper 2 Indian Economy Is Won: The Reform Lineage and Sectoral Debates

Paper 2's formula (data anchor → theoretical lens → policy evaluation → forward path) runs on the reform lineage as master narrative: planning era (Mahalanobis's heavy-industry strategy — the Second Plan's FEL model; the plan-holiday–Green-Revolution pivot — M.S. Swaminathan–Borlaug–Subramaniam; bank nationalisation 1969 — social control to public ownership; the license-permit raj's mature pathologies — Dagli, Hazari, the \"Hindu rate\" 3.5 per cent), the 1991 hinge (BOP crisis — reserves at weeks of imports; Rao–Manmohan Singh's two-track response — stabilisation (devaluation, IMF) plus structural reform (LPG); the Narasimham I–II financial-sector arc; the tariff march — 150 per cent peaks toward ASEAN parity; FERA-to-FEMA 1999; the small-scale reservation phase-out), and the post-2014 architecture (GST 2017 — the destination-based dual-VAT settlement and its rate/inclusion frictions; IBC 2016 — creditor-in-control, the NPA-cleanup lineage — AQR 2015, PCA, mergers, the bad-bank NARCL–IDRCL coda; inflation targeting 2016 — the MPC framework; DBT–JAM as welfare plumbing; the PLI–Gati-Shakti–NMP industrial push; the labour-code consolidation's notified-but-pending state; farm-law rise-and-repeal 2020–21 as the political-economy caution). Every sectoral answer hangs on this lineage: the question is never \"describe agriculture\" but \"where does this sector stand on the reform arc, and what remains.\"

Sectoral debates carry the formula's evaluation step. Agriculture (MSP–APMC — the Shanta Kumar–NITI efficiency critiques versus the farmer-income defence; the 2006 Bihar APMC repeal as natural experiment; contract farming's bargaining-power debate; water productivity — Punjab's rice–wheat–aquifer crisis, micro-irrigation economics; the Ashok Dalwai doubling-farmers'-income arithmetic); industry–MSME (PLI's disbursement-versus-announcement gap; labour codes' flexibility–security bargain; MSME's 45 per cent-export weight against its credit–delay pathologies — TReDS, the 45-day payment rule; the services-led growth puzzle — Bhalla–Banga on premature deindustrialisation versus the GCC–digital-services optimism); macroeconomic policy (FRBM's glide-path suspensions and the NK Singh committee's debt-anchor; monetary framework — the 4-per-cent target's food-fuel volatility problem, core-versus-headline, the MPC's voting-record transparency; fiscal–monetary coordination — the COVID-era burying of the dominance debate, then its post-inflation return); external (current-account dynamics — the 3-per-cent comfort thesis; FDI–FPI policy — the retrospective-tax repeal 2021 as credibility repair; rupee regimes — the REER-management debate; trade agreements — the RCEP-exit line 2019 — dairy–steel–China-deficit reasoning — versus the UAE–Australia–UK–EFTA FTA wave). Development policy (poverty-lineage — Tendulkar–Rangarajan–Multidimensional; PLFS employment — the LFPR–WPR–youth puzzles; inequality scholarship — Chancel–Piketty's top-share series; federal finance — 15th/16th Finance Commission devolution, the cess-divisible-pool distortion; welfare architecture — DBT savings claims debated, the rights–DBT synthesis; climate finance — the $100-billion promise, ISA–CDRI as Indian vehicles) closes Paper 2: the lens (Sen capabilities, Lewis turning, Oates decentralisation) named before the datum deployed.

4. Scoring Discipline: Numericals, Survey-Mapping, and the Precision Premium

Economics' exacting marking is exacting about exactness — the paper shelters precise writers and penalises the under-prepared through three mechanisms the preparation must mirror. Numerical drills weekly: the 10-mark numerical (multiplier arithmetic, national-income identities, tariff welfare triangles, Solow steady-state computation, index-number construction, BOP balancing) is fully deterministic — the complete method earns full marks and the half-method earns nearly nothing — so one timed numerical set weekly from week one, with the six standard derivations rotated monthly, converts Paper 1's scariest questions into its safest marks. Derivation discipline: one-step algebra shown explicitly (the Slutsky split, the money-multiplier series, the Domar debt equation) — examiners award the step, not the result; the memorised conclusion without the working is priced as memory, the working as understanding. Diagram-first culture: the answer opens with the labelled graph (axes, curves, equilibria, shifts numbered in sequence), then narrates the diagram — 10-markers in micro, macro and trade often live entirely on a correct graph plus three sentences; incomplete diagrams (unlabelled axes, unnumbered shifts) are the documented mark-killer.

Survey-mapping is Paper 2's compounding machine: each Economic Survey chapter is read as a ready-made answer skeleton (diagnosis → evidence → framework → recommendation), its boxes mined for data anchors, its chapter titles memorised as deployment headings. The mapping artefacts are the data sheet (two pages: growth, inflation, fiscal, external, sectoral, social — one table per macro family, Survey–RBI–Budget sourced, updated at each release), the debate bank (§8 — positions plus data, rehearsed as two-sided paragraphs), and the Budget–RBI bridge (Budget at a Glance for fiscal arithmetic — receipts, deficits, capex ratios; the RBI Annual Report's boxes for monetary-financial texture; the Finance Commission report for federal arithmetic). Paper 2's opening sentence is always the data anchor (\"Growth at 6.5 per cent with headline inflation at…\" — the Survey figure first), its middle is the lens application (theorem named, mechanism traced), its close is the two-sided evaluation plus the forward path (reform-next-step, explicitly sequenced — \"first X, then Y\" — because sequencing is policy judgment made visible).

Engineers and quant-comfortable candidates carry the numerical dividend — spend it on derivations and diagrams, but insure against the prose deficit: Paper 2's evaluation steps and the growth–development essays demand sustained economic prose (mechanism traced in sentences, positions weighed in paragraphs), and the all-maths script that cannot argue the policy case bleeds the 15-markers. Conversely, economics graduates must insure against the staleness deficit: degree-vintage theory (pre-1991 Indian illustrations, pre-DSGE macro) updated through the Survey–Budget–RBI cycle — the examiner prices currency, and last year's data outranks last decade's elegance. The twelve-week first pass (Section 6) compounds through the revision rotation: diagrams blindfold-redrawn, derivations timed, debate paragraphs rehearsed two-sided, data sheet refreshed — precision automated, judgment foregrounded, the exacting marker met on his own terms.

Trap watch. Slutsky (substitution + income) is the consumer crown — Giffen needs inferiority plus income-effect dominance. Cournot (quantity), Bertrand (price), Stackelberg (leader-follower) — never interchange the choice variable. Pigou taxes the wedge; Coase bargains over assigned rights — the policy pairing. New classical (Lucas rational expectations, RBC technology shocks) versus new Keynesian (menu costs, efficiency wages, stickiness microfoundations) — the macro master-pair. Mundell–Fleming's impossible trinity organises every open-economy answer — pick two of three. Solow's residue is exogenous technology; Romer endogenises it (knowledge spillovers) — the growth hinge. Lewis's turning point, Rosenstein-Rodan's big push versus Hirschman's linkages-imbalance, Sen's capabilities-development-as-freedom — the development triad. Ramsey (inverse-elasticity) prices tax efficiency; Oates decentralises fiscal power; Domar (g vs r) judges debt sustainability. Ricardo's comparative advantage is the most robust theorem in the syllabus; Krugman (2008) adds scale and intra-industry trade. 1991 is the hinge (stabilisation + LPG); IBC (2016), GST (2017), MPC targeting (2016) are the post-2014 architecture; the RCEP exit (2019) is the trade-stance line. Survey chapters are answer skeletons — data anchor first, lens named, sequencing explicit.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1

Decompose the price effect into income and substitution effects (Hicksian or Slutskian — state your choice). (10 marks)

  • Approach: compensated-variation construction
  • Slutsky equation sign logic (normal vs inferior vs Giffen)
  • labelled indifference-map with auxiliary budget line
  • one-line relevance: Giffen evidence debates.
Q2

Compare Cournot and Stackelberg duopoly outcomes. (10 marks)

  • Approach: simultaneous-quantity vs first-mover games
  • reaction-function diagram
  • outputs/prices ranking (Stackelberg leader gains, industry output higher than Cournot, lower than competition)
  • welfare note.
Q3

"An economy cannot simultaneously fix the exchange rate, allow free capital flows and run independent monetary policy." Elaborate. (15 marks)

  • Approach: Mundell-Fleming trilemma
  • corner vs middle-ground solutions
  • India's managed-float + partial-capital-controls position
  • reserve-accumulation logic
  • diagram: triangle vertices.
Q4

Contrast Solowian and endogenous growth models on the role of policy. (15 marks)

  • Approach: Solow — exogenous technology, convergence, policy affects levels not steady-state growth
  • Romer — ideas' non-rivalry, R&D externalities, policy shifts long-run growth
  • evidence sketch: persistent growth differentials
  • India hook: PLI/R&D-mission framing.
Q5

Explain the Ramsey rule of efficient taxation and its equity tension. (10 marks)

  • Approach: inverse-elasticity logic (tax inelastic bases more)
  • efficiency defence
  • equity critique (basics are inelastic)
  • resolution paths: differentiated GST architecture, Pigouvian layer.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6

"1991 was a balance-of-payments crisis that became a policy philosophy." Trace the reform sequence. (15 marks)

  • Approach: crisis anatomy (CAD, reserves cover, gold airlift episode)
  • stabilisation (fiscal correction, devaluation) vs structural reform (delicensing, trade, financial — Narasimham, FEMA path)
  • sequencing debates
  • unfinished-agenda ledger (factor markets, SOEs).
Q7

Evaluate inflation targeting as India's monetary framework. (10 marks)

  • Approach: MPC design (4% ±2 band, CPI headline)
  • record: anchoring gains, supply-shock tensions (food-weighted CPI)
  • debates: band adequacy, core-vs-headline, growth-trade-off
  • way-ahead: review levers (house-price inclusion? keep general: index refinements, communication).
Q8

Discuss MSP-APMC reforms through an economics lens — efficiency versus procurement-security trade-offs. (15 marks)

  • Approach: MSP's open-ended procurement geography concentration (paddy-rice belts)
  • water-table externality + crop-mix distortion
  • APMC frictions and market-fee wedge
  • reform options: price-deficiency payments, warehouse-based (PM-AASHA line), private-market competition with farmer-safeguards
  • federal politics acknowledged.
Q9

Analyse the IBC's incentive effects on credit culture. (10 marks)

  • Approach: design shift: debtor-in-possession
  • creditor-in-control, time-boxed resolution
  • record: recovery-architecture gains; haircuts & tribunal-capacity constraints
  • behavioural effect: pre-IBC discipline (market pricing of default risk)
  • completion items: cross-border insolvency, DFI? — keep: resolution-professional ecosystem.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: P1 hot nodes — Slutsky, market structures, trilemma, Solow-Romer, Ramsey; P2 — 1991 lineage, MPC, GST/IBC, agri-reforms, federal finance.
  • Weekly: 2 graph-complete answers + 1 derivation; refresh Survey/RBI figures each cycle.
