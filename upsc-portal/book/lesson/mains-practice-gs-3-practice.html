Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Economy, agriculture, science-tech, environment, security & disaster management — turning the pyqs/ bank into marks.

1. What GS-3 Demands

GS-3 rewards solution craft: every answer is a mini policy note — diagnose (data), analyse (structure), prescribe (mechanism). It is also the diagram paper: cyclical flows (inflation, disaster cycle), simple graphs (Laffer-style trade-offs, yields), and schematic layouts (security architecture) convert thin knowledge into visible competence.

2. The Data Magazine (load before practice)

  • Economy: GDP growth band, CPI/WPI ball-parks, FRBM targets, GST collections trend, current-account deficit range, forex reserves order of magnitude, unemployment series (PLFS), capital-expenditure ratios.
  • Agriculture: share in employment/GDP, MSP coverage facts, PM-KISAN quantum, irrigation coverage %, foodgrain output band, fertilizer subsidy scale.
  • Environment: NDC targets (45% intensity cut by 2030, 500 GW non-fossil), forest-cover band, renewable installed capacity order, tiger/elephant numbers' decade trend.
  • Sci-tech: space launch cadence, semiconductor mission outlay, AI-mission shape, 5G rollout facts, defence export figure.
  • Security: left-wing-extremism district-count trend, cyber-crime growth band, defence budget % GDP.

Rule: approximate honestly ("about", "roughly") — a rounded correct order beats a precise wrong number.

3. Answer Anatomy for GS-3

  • Intro: define + one datum that frames magnitude.
  • Body: causes/drivers → impacts (distributional where relevant) → constraints → mechanism-level solutions (who does what, funded how, sequenced when).
  • Diagram: at least half the answers; label everything.
  • Conclusion: link to a national mission or SDG hook without sloganising.

4. The Solution-quality Test (three questions)

  1. Who is the implementing agent (ministry/state/agency)?
  2. What is the incentive change (why will behaviour shift)?
  3. What is the constraint acknowledged (fiscal, federal, capacity)?

If any answer is "none", the solution is a slogan. Rewrite.

5. Security Answers — Sub-structure

  • Internal: root causes (governance vacuum, alienation) → terrain/technology factors → strategy triad (development + intelligence + calibrated force) → human-rights guardrails.
  • Cyber: threat map (critical infrastructure, finance, disinformation) → institutional stack (NCIIPC, CERT-In, data laws) → public-private + user-hygiene layer.
  • Disaster (NDMA cycle): prevention-mitigation → preparedness → response → recovery, with a recent case as evidence.

6. Weekly Cycle

  • Mon economy (2) · Tue agriculture+environment (2) · Wed sci-tech (2) · Thu security+disaster (2) · Fri sectional 10-Q · Sat evaluation + data-magazine update · Sun rewrite two + one diagram re-draw.

8. The Directive Verb in a Prescriptive Paper

GS-3 directives look identical to those in GS-1 and GS-2, but they behave differently because the paper tests a different faculty: not organised memory (GS-1) and not institutional judgment (GS-2), but solution craft — the ability to diagnose a problem with data, analyse its structure, and prescribe a mechanism. The directive in GS-3 therefore tells you where the solution lives in the answer. Verbs of diagnosis (examine, analyse, discuss, account for) want the mechanism of the problem exposed; verbs of assessment (evaluate, assess, critically examine) want a policy or scheme weighed against its outcomes with data; and verbs of prescription (suggest, recommend, \"what measures\", \"way forward\") want named agents doing sequenced things with acknowledged constraints. The characteristic GS-3 failure is answering a prescriptive question descriptively: a correct, data-rich account of why farm incomes stagnate that never names a mechanism for raising them has answered half the question, and the missing half is where the upper-band marks live.

The verbs cluster into three families in this paper. The diagnostic family — examine, analyse, discuss, account for, bring out — wants causal structure: drivers, transmission channels, distributional incidence. \"Examine the causes of persistent food inflation in India\" wants the supply-chain anatomy (monsoon dependence, mandi intermediation, storage and cold-chain deficits, export bans as second-best instruments, MSP's floor-price effect) separated from demand-side drivers (protein transition, rural wage pass-through) — not a chronicle of onion prices. The evaluative family — evaluate, assess, critically examine, comment — wants a verdict on a scheme, mission or doctrine against evidence. \"Critically examine the PM-KISAN scheme\" wants coverage and transfer quantum (₹6,000 per year in three instalments to landholding farmer families) weighed against exclusion errors (tenant farmers, landless labourers outside the frame), fiscal cost, and the comparative alternative (per-acre versus per-household designs, the Rythu Bandhu precedent) — then a judgment. The prescriptive family — suggest, recommend, outline a strategy, \"way forward\" — is where Section 4's solution-quality test (agent, incentive, constraint) applies directly: \"Suggest measures to reduce India's edible-oil import dependence\" wants the National Mission on Edible Oils–Oil Palm (2021) assessed, the oilseed-yield gap quantified, and then mechanism-level moves sequenced — seed systems, price signals, processing clusters, trade-policy coherence — each with a named implementer.

Two refinements separate trained candidates from the rest. The first is that GS-3 questions are disproportionately two-clause — diagnosis-plus-prescription (\"Examine the dimensions of cyber threats in India and suggest a framework to counter them\") or description-plus-evaluation (\"Describe the salient features of the semiconductor mission and assess its prospects\"). The word limit is a budget for both clauses, and the marks split roughly in proportion to clause length; candidates who spend 220 words diagnosing and 30 prescribing have forfeited the clause that carries the paper's distinctive skill. Pre-allocate before writing: a 15-marker with \"…and suggest\" is a 9-mark analysis and a 6-mark reform section, full stop. The second refinement is the data imperative: in GS-3 a quantitative claim without an order of magnitude reads as invention, while an honestly rounded figure (\"about\", \"roughly\", with the source named — PLFS, NFHS, Economic Survey, NCRB, ISFR) converts a normative complaint into an assessed argument. Load the data magazine from Section 2 before practice, update it quarterly, and never deploy a number whose source you cannot name.

9. Five Question Families and the Skeletons That Score

Nearly every GS-3 question since 2013 falls into one of five families, and each family has a skeleton stable enough to be learned cold. The skeleton is what lets you begin writing within ninety seconds: you are filling slots, not inventing structure under time pressure.

The first family is economy and infrastructure: growth, inflation, fiscal policy, GST, banking, external sector, transport, energy, logistics. The skeleton is magnitude (one datum) → structural drivers → distributional incidence → constraint → mechanism-level reform. The distributional incidence — who gains, who bears the cost, which region or sector or decile — is the step most often skipped and most heavily rewarded, because it converts macro description into political-economy analysis. The second family is agriculture and food management: MSP and procurement, PDS and the National Food Security Act 2013, irrigation, subsidies, land, allied sectors. The skeleton is outcome datum → input-output anatomy → incentive analysis → comparative alternative → sequenced reform. The incentive analysis is the scoring slot: MSP's floor-price effect on cropping patterns, fertiliser subsidy (urea's fixed pricing versus decontrolled P&K under the NBS) and its nutrient-imbalance consequence, power subsidy's groundwater consequence. Name the incentive and you have diagnosed the policy; prescribe against the incentive and you have a mechanism.

The third family is science, technology and innovation: space, nuclear, defence indigenisation, semiconductors, AI, biotech, health. The skeleton is capability datum → institutional stack → bottleneck → global comparison → mission-mode fix. The institutional stack is the GS-3 equivalent of GS-2's authority bank — ISRO and the 2023 space policy with IN-SPACe and NSIL, BARC and the three-stage nuclear programme, DRDO and the iDEX/startup route, the Semiconductor Mission (2021, ₹76,000 crore outlay) and Tata-PSMC's Dholera fab, the IndiaAI Mission (2024, ₹10,372 crore) — and the global comparison (one named country, one named programme) is what lifts the answer from inventory to assessment. The fourth family is environment, ecology and disaster management: NDCs, renewables, forests, pollution, EIA, the NDMA cycle. The skeleton is commitment or stock datum → driver anatomy → federal-capacity constraint → instrument choice (regulatory versus market versus mission) → monitored fix. The instrument choice is the analytical core: perform-achieve-trade versus a carbon market (the CCTS notified 2023–24), command-and-control versus compensatory afforestation and the Green Credit Programme, bans versus extended producer responsibility for plastics and e-waste. Name the instrument class and defend it, or the answer is a slogan.

The fifth family is internal and external security: left-wing extremism, Jammu and Kashmir, the Northeast, border management, terrorism, organised crime, cyber security, defence modernisation. The skeleton is threat map → root-cause triad (governance vacuum, alienation, terrain-technology) → strategy triad (development, intelligence, calibrated force) → institutional stack → rights guardrail. The institutional stack must be current to be credible: the Chief of Defence Staff and Department of Military Affairs (2019), theatreisation (in progress), Agnipath (2022), CERT-In and NCIIPC for cyber, the NIA (2008, amended 2019 for extraterritorial jurisdiction), NATGRID, the NCTC proposal's federal fate. The rights guardrail — AFSPA's staged withdrawals, UAPA's procedure debate, surveillance under the Telegraph Act and now the Telecommunications Act 2023 — is the step that separates a security answer from a parade-ground essay: the examiner wants to see that force is calibrated by law, not merely deployed.

10. The Data Magazine as a Machine, and the Diagram Discipline

The data magazine that Section 2 prescribes only becomes usable when it is built as a retrieval machine rather than a collection. Give every entry five fields and no more: the figure (honestly rounded); the source (Economic Survey 2025–26, PLFS, NFHS-5, NCRB, ISFR 2023, RBI Bulletin); the year or reference period; the question family it serves; and a one-line deployment — the sentence in which you will actually use it. The one-line deployment is the discipline that separates learning from hoarding: a number you cannot place inside an argument is not yet usable. Refresh the magazine quarterly against the Saturday protocol, and prune ruthlessly: thirty figures you can deploy beat three hundred you can only recognise. Rank sources the way the examiner does — official statistics and survey reports first, ministry and RBI releases second, reputable secondary trackers (PRS, CAG reports, NITI Aayog indices) third, newspapers last — because the cited source tells the examiner where your preparation lives.

Three standing pages deserve permanent places at the front of the magazine. The macro page carries the growth band, CPI ball-park, fiscal-deficit glide path (4.4 per cent of GDP targeted for 2025–26, with the post-FRBM debt-anchor debate as context), GST collections trend, CAD range, forex-reserves order of magnitude and the PLFS unemployment series. The agri-environment page carries employment-versus-GDP shares, foodgrain output band, MSP coverage facts, the NDC pair (45 per cent emission-intensity cut by 2030 from 2005 levels; 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030, with the Panchamrit's net-zero-by-2070 capstone), forest-and-tree-cover band from the ISFR, and renewable installed-capacity order. The security-tech page carries the LWE district-count trend, the defence-budget share of GDP, the space-launch cadence, and the two mission outlays (semiconductor, AI) with their years. Each page should also carry one comparison anchor — China or Vietnam for manufacturing, Israel for drip irrigation and innovation density, Bangladesh or Vietnam for textiles — because GS-3 evaluation questions reward the candidate who can say \"against X, India's record is…\" with a number attached.

The diagram discipline is GS-3's equivalent of GS-1's map rule, and it should be pre-committed rather than improvised. Five diagram types cover ninety per cent of the paper: the flow schematic (inflation transmission, MSP-to-mandi chain, disaster-management cycle), the trade-off sketch (growth-versus-inflation, subsidy-versus-investment, security-versus-liberty), the stack diagram (institutional architecture — NCIIPC/CERT-In/NIA for cyber, NDMA–SDMA–DDMA for disaster, the three-stage nuclear programme), the distribution map (industrial corridors, mineral belts, LWE geography, cyclone coasts), and the before-after table (pre- versus post-GST, pre- versus post-IBC). Rules: label everything, cite the datum on the diagram, draw in under a minute, and never decorate — an examiner rewards a labelled flow that carries analysis and ignores a pretty irrelevance. The error log from the GS-1 system transfers directly with GS-3's three recurring defects: slogan solutions (no agent, no incentive, no constraint — the fix is the Section 4 test), datum-free diagnosis (claims without magnitudes — the fix is the magazine), and clause starvation in two-part questions (the fix is pre-allocation). Read the log after twenty answers; most candidates discover they are not short of knowledge but of mechanism.

11. Marking Psychology and the Failure Modes That Cost Two Marks Each

An evaluator reads a few hundred scripts in a few weeks and spends on the order of six to eight minutes on each answer. That constraint has design consequences that are not cosmetic. Structure is a scoring variable: the examiner is checking whether the problem was diagnosed with a magnitude, whether the analysis exposed a mechanism rather than narrated instances, and whether the prescription named an agent, an incentive change and a constraint — all three visible in the first four lines if the answer is built on a skeleton. Short paragraphs with one idea each, underlined data points with sources, a labelled visual where the family rewards one, adherence to the word limit within roughly ten per cent, and a two-line conclusion linking to a national mission or SDG hook without sloganising are the conditions under which those judgments can be made quickly. An answer that requires re-reading is scored from memory of its opening, and a prescription addressed to nobody is scored as decoration.

The failure modes, in descending order of damage, are stable across cohorts. Slogan prescription is the first and the GS-3-specific killer: "raise awareness", "ensure political will", "strengthen coordination" — each unbacked by an agent or an incentive change, each forfeiting the marks that a mechanism would have earned, because in a prescriptive paper the mechanism is the competence being tested. Datum-free diagnosis is the second: a correct causal account with no magnitude anywhere, which reads as opinion rather than analysis. Scheme description without evaluation is the third — reciting PM-KISAN's instalment structure or the semiconductor mission's outlay when the question asked for assessment against outcomes — because features are not performance. Federal blindness is fourth: prescribing Union action for subjects where implementation lives with the States (agriculture, water, police, land) without naming the Centre–State instrument (Centrally Sponsored Scheme design, Finance Commission grants, NITI Aayog coordination, Article 256–257 directions). Constraint denial is fifth — recommending expenditure without acknowledging the fiscal frame, or technology without acknowledging capacity — and unallocated two-part answers, where the prescriptive clause starves, is sixth.

Calibration follows from the same logic. The gap between a 4 and a 6 out of 10 is not additional content; it is one honest datum framing the magnitude and one mechanism naming an agent. The gap between a 6 and an 8 is incentive analysis (why behaviour will shift) plus a named comparison or precedent, and constraint acknowledgment — fiscal, federal or capacity. Above 8 the answer additionally holds its own nuance: it states the limiting condition under which its prescription would fail, or sequences reforms across time horizons (immediate administrative fixes, medium-term institutional builds, long-term structural shifts). Practise against that ladder, not against a word count, and let the log tell you which rung you are actually standing on.

Trap watch. PM-KISAN pays ₹6,000 per year in three instalments to landholding farmer families — tenants and the landless are outside the frame, which is the standard exclusion critique. The National Food Security Act is 2013 (75 per cent rural, 50 per cent urban coverage; 5 kg per person per month); PMGKAY's free-foodgrain extension from January 2023 subsumed the NFSA subsidy architecture — do not describe them as parallel schemes. Urea pricing is fixed and controlled while P and K were decontrolled under the Nutrient-Based Subsidy (2010) — the resulting NPK imbalance is the incentive story. The NDC pair is a 45 per cent emission-intensity cut by 2030 (2005 base) and 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030, under the Panchamrit with net zero by 2070 — intensity, not absolute emissions. The Semiconductor Mission (2021) carries a ₹76,000 crore outlay; the IndiaAI Mission (2024) ₹10,372 crore — the years and outlays are routinely swapped. IN-SPACe regulates private space activity and NSIL commercialises ISRO capability under the 2023 space policy — regulator versus commercial arm. The CDS and Department of Military Affairs date to 2019; Agnipath to 2022; the NIA to 2008 (2019 amendment for extraterritorial jurisdiction). CERT-In handles incident response and NCIIPC protects critical information infrastructure — response versus protection. The fiscal deficit target for 2025–26 is 4.4 per cent of GDP; the FRBM debate has moved to a debt anchor. MSP is a floor price for 22 mandated crops plus sugarcane's FRP, but effective procurement concentrates on wheat, paddy and now coarse grains in a handful of states — announced coverage is not realised coverage. LWE geography has contracted to a fraction of its 2010 footprint; cite the trend, not a frozen district count.

30 original questions (economy, agriculture, science & technology, environment, disaster management, internal security) with compact answer frameworks. Data points reflect the position as of early 2026 — update numbers before the exam.

A. Economy

Q115 marks

'India's growth is investment-led but consumption-constrained.' Examine with reference to recent trends and Budget 2025–26.

  • Body: GFCF ~30% of GDP, public capex ₹11.2 lakh crore; private capex revival uneven; consumption — rural recovery post-2024 monsoon, urban slowdown, K-shape; Budget levers — income-tax relief (₹12 lakh exemption), GST 2.0 (Sept 2025); wage stagnation, household debt (RBI FSR); way forward — labour-intensive manufacturing, urban jobs, credit to MSMEs.
Q210 marks

Discuss the rationale and risks of the RBI's shift to a neutral stance and rate cuts in 2025.

  • Body: CPI below 4% for much of 2025 (food disinflation), growth concerns, global easing; cuts to 5.25% by Dec 2025; CRR cut to 3%; risks — transmission lags, currency (rupee ~90/USD), tariff shock, core inflation stickiness, fiscal–monetary coordination; MPC composition and accountability.
Q315 marks

Evaluate the GST reforms of September 2025 ('GST 2.0') against the objectives of simplicity, revenue neutrality and cooperative federalism.

  • Body: two-rate structure 5/18 plus 40% demerit; inverted duty corrections; compensation cess wind-down; revenue loss estimate ~₹48,000 crore, States' concerns; pending — petroleum inclusion, GST Appellate Tribunal (operational 2025), rate on insurance exempted; assessment — simplification achieved, federal trust and revenue buoyancy still to be proved.
Q410 marks

'Fiscal consolidation should shift from deficit targets to debt anchors.' Discuss in the light of the Union Budget 2025–26.

  • Body: FD 4.4% (2025–26), debt 56% of GDP → 50±1% by 2031; advantages — flexibility, focus on sustainability, counter-cyclicality; risks — off-budget borrowings, State debt (~30%), interest burden (about 20% of receipts); N.K. Singh FRBM review; need an independent fiscal council.
Q510 marks

What are the causes of India's persistently low manufacturing share in GDP? Assess the PLI schemes as a remedy.

  • Body: factor-market rigidities, logistics cost, tariff inversion, MSME missing middle; PLI 14 sectors ₹1.97 lakh crore — electronics success (mobile exports USD 24 bn), pharma; weak in textiles, solar modules; sunset concerns; complement — labour codes (Nov 2025), National Manufacturing Mission, land banks, FTAs, ease of doing business at State level.
Q615 marks

'The 2025 US tariffs are a shock that India should convert into a reform opportunity.' Examine.

  • Body: 50% tariff (Aug 2025) hits textiles, gems, shrimp, leather — about USD 48 bn exposure; responses — export diversification (UK CETA, EFTA in force, EU FTA talks, ASEAN review), domestic demand (GST cuts), credit support for exporters, Bharat Trade Net; longer-term — tariff rationalisation of our own, quality infrastructure, GVC integration, rupee trade; avoid retaliation spiral.
Q710 marks

Discuss the role of the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code in improving credit culture and its remaining weaknesses.

  • Body: recoveries ~32% of admitted claims, 1,000+ resolutions; behavioural change (pre-admission settlements ₹10 lakh crore); weaknesses — delays (average 650+ days), NCLT vacancies, haircuts (Videocon, Jet), personal guarantor cases, cross-border framework absent; 2025 amendment bill — creditor-led resolution, group insolvency.
Q810 marks

Examine the significance of the four Labour Codes brought into force in November 2025 for workers and enterprises.

  • Body: wages floor, gig-worker social security fund, fixed-term employment with gratuity after one year, women in night shifts with consent, threshold for retrenchment permission raised to 300; concerns — union rights, State rule-making delays, inspection dilution; expected effects on formalisation and FDI.

B. Agriculture & Food

Q915 marks

'Legal guarantee of MSP is neither fiscally viable nor agronomically desirable.' Critically examine alternatives.

  • Body: MSP covers ~6% of farmers directly; legal guarantee costs — estimates vary (₹1–10 lakh crore) depending on design; distortions — paddy in Punjab, groundwater; alternatives — price deficiency payments (Bhavantar), income support (PM-KISAN), crop-neutral incentives, decentralised procurement, pulses/oilseeds procurement (Mission for Aatmanirbharta in Pulses 2025), diversification package, FPO market access; Shanta Kumar and Ramesh Chand recommendations.
Q1010 marks

Evaluate the PM Dhan-Dhaanya Krishi Yojana as a strategy for lagging agricultural districts.

  • Body: 100 low-productivity districts, convergence of 36 schemes, ₹24,000 crore/year for six years, district plans modelled on Aspirational Districts; strengths — targeted, outcome dashboards; risks — State capacity, one-size-fits-all indicators; complements — irrigation, storage, credit, extension.
Q1110 marks

How can India reconcile the push for natural farming with food security?

  • Body: National Mission on Natural Farming (2024, ₹2,481 crore, 1 crore farmers); Sri Lanka's 2021 lesson; evidence from Andhra Pradesh's APCNF; approach — phased conversion in rain-fed areas, soil-health monitoring, certification (PGS), market premiums, avoid mandatory rollouts in Punjab-type systems.
Q1215 marks

Discuss the implications of genome-edited crops for Indian agriculture and the regulatory framework that governs them.

  • Body: SDN-1/SDN-2 exempted from GM rules (2022) vs SDN-3; DRR Dhan 100 (Kamala) and Pusa DST Rice 1 released May 2025 — yield, drought/salinity tolerance; benefits — speed, no foreign DNA, IPR autonomy; concerns — activists' 'GM by another name', seed-cost, monitoring; contrast with Bt cotton and GM mustard stalemate; way forward — transparent biosafety data, farmer participation, public-sector pipeline.

C. Science & Technology

Q1310 marks

'India's space reforms have moved the sector from a state monopoly to a state-enabled ecosystem.' Examine.

  • Body: IN-SPACe, NSIL, Indian Space Policy 2023, FDI 2024, ₹1,000 crore venture fund; private launches (Agnikul, Skyroot), 300+ startups; ISRO's shift to R&D, Gaganyaan, BAS, NISAR; remaining — Space Activities Bill, insurance, spectrum for satcom, procurement predictability.
Q1415 marks

Evaluate the IndiaAI Mission and India's AI governance approach against the EU and US models.

  • Body: ₹10,372 crore, compute (38,000 GPUs), sovereign models (Sarvam, BharatGen), datasets (AIKosh), safety institute; governance — guidelines (Nov 2025), techno-legal approach, no standalone law; EU risk-based statute vs US deregulatory turn; India's opportunities — language models, DPI+AI, AI Impact Summit 2026; risks — deepfakes, jobs (IMF 40% exposed), energy, data-centre water; recommendations — sectoral regulators, algorithmic audits, skilling.
Q1510 marks

Discuss the strategic and economic logic of the India Semiconductor Mission and the obstacles it faces.

  • Body: supply-chain security (Taiwan risk), electronics import bill, design talent; 10 units, first packaged chips 2025, Tata fab 2026–27; obstacles — no advanced-node fab, water/power, ecosystem of gases and chemicals, talent at fab level, subsidy dependence; ISM 2.0 focus on equipment, materials, design-linked incentives.
Q1610 marks

How does the SHANTI Act, 2025 change India's nuclear energy landscape?

  • Body: replaces Atomic Energy Act 1962 and CLNDA 2010; private participation in generation, capped supplier liability, independent regulator; targets — 100 GW by 2047, Nuclear Energy Mission ₹20,000 crore, five SMRs by 2033, Bharat Small Reactors for captive use; concerns — safety culture, waste, fuel security (uranium imports), public acceptance (Kudankulam, Jaitapur history).
Q1715 marks

'Biotechnology is India's next sunrise sector.' Examine the potential and the policy ecosystem, with reference to BioE3.

  • Body: bioeconomy USD 165 bn (2024) → USD 300 bn by 2030; BioE3 (Aug 2024) — biomanufacturing hubs, bio-AI, bio-foundries; successes — vaccines, CAR-T (NexCAR19), gene therapy trials (BIRSA-101), GenomeIndia; gaps — clinical-trial ecosystem, biosafety regulator overlaps (GEAC/RCGM), venture funding, regulatory delays in ethanol/enzymes; recommendations — single-window regulator, bio-clusters, IP support.

D. Environment & Disaster Management

Q1815 marks

Critically evaluate the outcomes of COP30 (Belém) from the perspective of developing countries.

  • Body: NCQG follow-up 'Baku-to-Belém roadmap to USD 1.3 trillion', adaptation indicators, Tropical Forests Forever Facility, just transition mechanism, fossil-fuel roadmap outside formal text; gaps — no new mitigation ambition, NDC 3.0 shortfall (2.3–2.5 °C trajectory), US absence; India's positions — equity, CBDR, finance definitions; next — COP31 Antalya 2026.
Q1910 marks

Examine the challenges of implementing the Carbon Credit Trading Scheme in India.

  • Body: compliance market (9 sectors, targets 2025–27) and offset market; issues — baseline setting, MRV capacity, price discovery, double counting with RECs/PAT, integrity lessons from voluntary market, CBAM interaction; institutions — BEE administrator, CERC regulator, Grid-India registry.
Q2010 marks

'India's forest cover statistics mask ecological degradation.' Discuss with reference to ISFR 2023 and the 2023 amendment to the Forest Conservation Act.

  • Body: 25.17% forest+tree cover but dense forest losses in the North-East and Western Ghats, plantations counted as forest, Kerala/Wayanad landslides 2024; Van Adhiniyam 2023 exemptions (border 100 km, zoos, linear projects), 'deemed forest' dilution; SC interim protection Feb 2024; recommendations — canopy-quality indicators, community forest rights (FRA), compensatory afforestation quality audits.
Q2115 marks

Examine the growing frequency of Himalayan disasters (Joshimath, Sikkim GLOF 2023, Wayanad 2024, Uttarkashi 2025) and suggest a risk-informed development framework.
