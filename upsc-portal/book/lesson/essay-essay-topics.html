Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Grouped practice bank for the Essay paper. For each topic: an angle line you can bend into a thesis. Rotate the 6-move framework (see essay-frameworks/detailed-notes/essay-writing.html).

A. Technology & Society

  1. "Artificial intelligence will not replace humans, but humans who fail to govern AI will be replaced by those who do." — agency vs governance; compute equity (IndiaAI Mission), skill-transition gaps, algorithmic accountability.
  2. "The smartphone made the world flat and the mind hollow." — attention economy, education shifts, political polarisation, digital public infrastructure as counterweight (DPI example).
  3. "Data is the new commons." — DPDP 2023 tensions, platform power, open-standards diplomacy (MOSIP, UPI links).
  4. "Space is no longer the final frontier; it is the next ledger." — commercial space (Space FDI 2024, IN-SPACe, Skyroot/Agnikul), satellite data commons (NISAR), station ambitions 2035.
  5. "Quantum promises are cheap; responsible computation is expensive." — NQM ₹6,003 cr, cryptography transitions (post-quantum migration), talent bottleneck.

B. Gender & Society

  1. "Women's voices will be heard when women are present to speak." — 106th Amendment rollback calendar (post-delimitation), SHG economies (Lakhpati Didi), care-work accounting.
  2. "The glass ceiling is actually a labyrinth." — FLFPR ~37–41% with quality caveat; boardroom pipelines; returnship economics.
  3. "Marriage, migration, and the market: the three forces reshaping Indian families." — NFHS-5 data anchors, joint→nuclear gradient, digital matchmaking marketplaces.
  4. "Safety is the infrastructure of equality." — POSH compliance math, transport design audits, BNS 2023 offence modernisation.

C. Environment & Climate

  1. "Growth without ecology is a self-defeating arithmetic." — GBF 30×30, CCTS carbon markets, adaptation-as-development (HAPs 23+ states).
  2. "The monsoon is India's oldest economy." — 2025 monsoon metrics (~105–107% LPA band), Mission Mausam, crop-calendar fragility.
  3. "Water disputes are federalism's stress test." — Cauvery Authority dynamics, Ken-Betwa clearances, aquifer governance deficit.
  4. "Great Nicobar: strategy against the sea, or against the self?" — ethics of mega-projects, tribal consent (FRA), cumulative impact assessment case.
  5. "The right against climate change" — M K Ranjitsinh (2024) constitutional recognition, enforceability questions, insurance-fiscal links.

D. Polity & Constitution

  1. "A constitution keeps its meaning by asking to be read again." — Kesavananda lineage; 131st Amendment defeat (Apr 2026) as federal bargaining lesson.
  2. "Elections are won at booths, democracies are won in committees." — committee decline narrative, Electoral Bonds judgment (Feb 2024) funding-transparency open question.
  3. "Federalism lives in the plumbing, not the plumbing bill." — GST council patterns, FC transitions (16th FC), cesses vs divisible pool tension.
  4. "Reserved seats, represented voices?" — constituency delimitation timing, women's-reservation rollout design, rotation mechanics.

E. Economy & Development

  1. "Inclusive growth is not a slogan; it is a spreadsheet." — DBT savings claims (~₹2.7 lakh cr), NFSA coverage, PLI asymmetries (14 sectors).
  2. "The future of work is a negotiation between code and care." — Rajasthan Gig Workers Act 2023, Code on Social Security gig categories, platform-labour judiciary trends.
  3. "India's demographic dividend has an expiry date." — working-age peak ~2040; skills pipeline (NSQF), female participation as multiplier.
  4. "Trade agreements are the new trade routes." — UAE CEPA, Australia ECTA, EFTA TEPA ($100 bn pledge), UK CETA (Jul 2025), RCEP abstention logic.

F. Ethics, Culture & Philosophy

  1. "Reality does not conform to the ideal, but confirms it." — ideals as measuring norms; reform episodes (socio-religious movements, abolition paths).
  2. "The empires of the future will be the empires of the mind." — soft power ledger (diaspora, DPI exports, G20 presidency narrative, AU induction in G20).
  3. "Tradition is the votes of the dead." — Chesterton frame; heritage politics (Moidams UNESCO 2024, temple reconstructions), custodial continuity.
  4. "Peace is the only dignified technology of the 21st century." — Ukraine/Gaza arcs, India as messenger state, mediation credibility.

G. Rurban & Urban Futures

  1. "A city is not a place; it is a process." — 74th Amendment fiscal starvation, sponge-city norms, heat-action equity.
  2. "The village is not vanishing; it is migrating." — Census 2027 stakes, remittance economies, peri-urban informality.

H. India & The World

  1. "Strategic autonomy is a method, not an ideology." — multi-alignment evidence (QUAD + BRICS + SCO), connectivity diplomacy (IMEC, Chabahar).
  2. "Multipolarity without multilateralism is organised chaos." — UNSC reform, UNSC deadlock episodes, WTO dispute-crisis, India's reform pitch.

How to use this bank

  • Pick 1 topic per family weekly; write in 70 minutes; self-score with the rubric in essay-frameworks/pyqs/essay-frameworks-pyqs.html.
  • Keep a topic-index card: thesis + 3 dimensions + 3 evidence anchors + 1 counterpoint for each finished essay.
  • Before mocks, refresh the anchor pantry in essay-frameworks/short-notes/essay-writing-revision.html.

Topic selection in 90 seconds

  • Read all options; shortlist two; ask of each: Can I supply 4+ dimensions? 6+ varied examples? A balanced thesis?
  • Choose breadth over depth: essays reward dimension-spanning, not expertise.
  • Reject topics that tempt a single-story script (pure biography, pure statistics).

Angle generators (when mind goes blank)

  • Historical → present → future arc.
  • Individual → family → society → state → globe zoom-out.
  • Thesis → antithesis → synthesis.
  • Economic · political · social · ethical · environmental (the five-lens sweep).

Ten-second drill

  • For any prompt, speak for 60 seconds: definition → two dimensions → one example each → stance. Practise 5 prompts daily in the last month.

Key Points & Mindmap

Skeleton facts and recall triggers.

Selection and handling rules — the decision layer before writing begins.

ASCII mindmap

ESSAY TOPIC DECISION (first 15 min)
├── READ ALL ...... under every option, note 2 sparks
├── SHORTLIST 2 ... dimensions? examples? balance?
├── SCORE 1-5 ..... familiarity · dimension-spread · example-supply
│                  (pick highest TOTAL, not highest familiarity)
├── THESIS ........ one sentence, balanced, yours
└── BLUEPRINT ..... 5-7 sections + example slots before para 1

Selection scoring card

  • Familiarity — can I define every term in the prompt?
  • Dimension-spread — ≥4 lenses (social, political, economic, ethical, tech/environmental)?
  • Example-supply — ≥6 examples across history/law/schemes/data/personal observation?
  • Balance-potential — can I argue both sides without straw-manning?
  • Trap-detector — absolute-word prompts ("never", "only") need a nuanced thesis, not acceptance.

Handling absolute and paradox prompts

  • "X is the only way to Y" → agree with core, complicate the edges ("necessary but not sufficient").
  • Paired prompts ("A without B is…") → define both, then the relation: harmony, tension, sequence.
  • Quote-based prompts → unpack the quote into two claims; the essay = their conversation.

Blueprint discipline

  • 5 minutes of outlining beats 30 minutes of mid-essay drift.
  • Map example slots first (intro anecdote, 2 data anchors, 2 scheme examples, 1 thinker, 1 counter-case).
  • End-of-outline test: would my section titles alone tell a coherent story?

Pitfall flags

  • Don't pick the rarefied philosophical prompt to show off — execution risk is high.
  • Don't switch topics after 20 minutes — the sunk-cost rewrite never finishes.
  • Never argue with the prompt's premise rudely; engage it generously.

Golden phrase bank

  • "The essay is decided in the reading, written in the outline, and merely typed in the answer sheet."
  • "Pick the topic you can spread, not the one you can recite."

Previous Year Questions

What the examiner has actually asked.

How UPSC's essay paper has behaved, and drills to internalise it. Prompts below are pattern-descriptions for practice, not verbatim reproductions.

Observed patterns in the essay paper

  • Structure: two sections, four topics each; one to be written; 125 marks ×2; 3 hours.
  • Section 1 skews philosophical/abstract ("…state of mind", "…journey", paired concepts); Section 2 skews concrete-governance (tech, economy, environment, gender).
  • Recurrence: technology-and-human (multiple years), women's empowerment as process, local-vs-global tension, "hand-holding vs hand-out" development debates, self-mastery themes.
  • What changed recently: prompts got shorter and more paradoxical — a premium on definitional clarity in the introduction.

Practice prompts (pattern-style)

  1. "Contentment is the seed of ambition, not its enemy." — philosophical pair.
  2. "A census is the autobiography of a nation." — data-society.
  3. "The city plans; the street disposes." — urbanism.
  4. "Handmade minds and human minds: education in the age of machines." — tech-education.
  5. "Rivers are the oldest parliaments of civilisation." — nature-culture.
  6. "Empowerment measured in minutes saved is still freedom." — gender-development (time-poverty angle).
  7. "The court of public opinion needs a constitution too." — speech-accountability.
  8. "Subsidies feed a season; skills feed a lifetime." — welfare design.

Drills

  • Full-length rhythm: one full essay weekly under exam clock (15 min select/outline, 150 min write, 15 min review).
  • Skeleton sprints: outline (thesis + 5 sections + examples) for three different prompts in 30 minutes — builds selection speed.
  • Family coverage: ensure each of the seven families gets at least two full essays before the exam.

Self-review grid

  • Did the introduction define the terms and state a thesis? (2 checks)
  • Does every section open with a signpost and close with a link?
  • Are examples varied (law, scheme, data, thinker, lived case)?
  • Is there a genuine counter-view, engaged fairly?
  • Does the conclusion resolve — not merely repeat?
