Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Paper 1: administrative theory — thinkers, structures, processes. Paper 2: Indian administration — union to panchayat, welfare machinery and reforms. Heavy GS-2/GS-4 resonance, a thinker-canon to memorise, and a governance-currents coupling that rewards daily newspaper reading with an administrator's eye.

Paper 2 — Unit Map (Indian Administration)

  • Foundations: Mauryan-Mughal legacies → colonial administration → constitutional framework (Art. 53, 73rd/74th, All-India Services' Art. 312).
  • Union & state machinery: Cabinet secretariat, NITI Aayog vs Planning Commission, ministries-DOPT ecosystem; CM secretariat, chief secretary's role; field administration — DM's evolving role debate.
  • District & local: district planning committees, PESA, municipal acts, devolution index debates, e-governance stack (DBT, e-Office, CMIS?), actually: e-Office, GeM, PFMS.
  • Personnel & reforms: civil-services values (GS-4 handshake), training architecture (LBSNAA, mid-career), performance appraisal reform, police-reform line (Prakash Singh), administrative-reforms lineage (1st & 2nd ARC volumes).
  • Welfare & issue administration: poverty/food (NFSA-PDS), health/education missions, women-child (Mission Shakti/Poshan), tribal & area development, disaster management architecture (DM Act 2005), law-and-order interface, corruption-controls (Lokpal/CVC), citizen-centricity (CPGRAMS, charters, social audit).

Booklist (lean and sufficient)

  • Thinkers: Prasad & Prasad (thinker chapters) + one theory text (Nicholas Henry's paradigms overview).
  • Structures: standard coaching compilations + Fadia or Sharma-Sadana selective.
  • India: Rajni Goyal & Arora (Indian Public Administration) + 2nd ARC summaries + annual Economic Survey governance chapters.
  • Current: PIB-governance watch, ARC reports, punching bag debates (simultaneous elections, police reform) as case stock.

Answer Strategy

  • Theory formula: thinker → context → core claim → Indian evidence/critique → contemporary echo ("Simon's critique returns in NPM dashboards").
  • Paper 2 formula: constitutional hook → institutional map → performance record → reform proposal (ARC-referenced) → case datum.
  • Currents coupling: every Paper-2 answer gains one recent programme/court line; maintain a "reform ledger" (police, district, appraisal, delivery).
  • Draw the machinery: secretariat-flow diagrams, budget cycle ring, accountability-web sketch — one per answer.

Scoring Profile and Fit

  • Best for: GS-2 lovers, working professionals (experiential dividend), interview-compound seekers.
  • Watch-outs: large competitive pool with polarised scoring — answers must be thinker-dense (P1) and case-dense (P2); the subject's "common sense" trap lures shallow writing.

Twelve-Week First Pass

  • Wk 1-2 intro + paradigms. Wk 3-5 thinker canon (3-4 per week with application cards). Wk 6 structures + accountability/finance/law. Wk 7 comparative-development + OTs. Wk 8-10 Indian machinery + reforms ledger. Wk 11 welfare-administration cases. Wk 12 PYQ mapping + sectional.

Revision Digest

Condensed for the last mile before the exam.

Why candidates pick it

  • GS-2 governance heartland sync + GS-4 ethics resonance; small-to-medium ecosystem (coaching footprint lighter than sociology/thinner than 2010s peak but sufficient).

Syllabus map

  • Paper-I: administrative theory lineage (Classical/Wilson-Taylor-Fayol-Gulick, Human Relations/ Mayo-McGregor, Behavioural/Simon-Barnard, Systems/Open-system; Public-Choice (Buchanan), NPM (self-institution logic), Governance-buzz (network governance), post-NPM (Neo-Weberian state)); theories/meanings on organisation, bureaucracy (Weber-type); accountability & control (executive-legislative-judicial triads + citizen-centricry right-to-information); administrative behaviour (decision making: Herbert Simon's bounded rationality; communication, morale, motivation (Maslow/Herzberg/McGregor), leadership (LIKERT-style systems)); public organisations & public enterprises dynamics; development administration (Weidner) → development dynamics; personal administration systems (recruitment, training, promotion, performance-appraisal, employer-employee relations (record Gentleman-agreements), Civil-service neutrality/activism/anonymity debates + 2nd-ARC ethos calibrations); public policy (models of policy-making: Lindblom incrementalism, Dror optimal-model etc; implementation sciences); financial administration: budget concepts (PPBS lineage, new performance-budget; fiscal federalism machinery in India — Finance Commission-centre-state-divisible-pool debates), parliamentary control (PAC/Estimates/COPU trilogy + CAG).
  • Paper-II (Indian administration): historical framework (Kautilya Arthashastra admin; Mughal admin lineages, British civil-service creation story, constitutional-civil-service anchor Art 308–323 protections/ART-311 Dismisal/due-process + officer accountability structures); Union executive (President-PM-CoM-CabSec machinery), parliament-judiciary gates, central secretariat vs field administration blur; Ministries-departments-Cabinet-committees-trinity, PMO-lore growth patterns, NITI Aayog bypass discussion (post-PC era reforms), State admin/Wild-West zones (Governor-CM dynamics, Secretariat-Directorate vitiations; district-administration emblematic DM narrative), municipal/local-side 73rd/74th implementation realities incl. FFC state-finance-commission; public-sector-undertakings (CPSE classification Maharatna-Navratna-Miniratna schools + recent strategic-disinvestment movement: Air India sale etc); civil services reforms arc & ARC reports (1st ARC + 2nd ARC digest) — specific 2nd ARC report names (Ethics in Governance; Citizen-Centric Administration; Personnel-administration report) — memorium rundowns; disaster management + welfare administration segments; social-sector governance armoury.

Framework skeleton

  • Every answer: theorist attribution (1) → Indian administrative example (1) → reform instrument name (1) → critique/deviation line (1).
  • High-yield anchor kits: Simon (bounded rationality), Weber (authority types), Taylor (scientific management), Barnard (informal orgs), Follett (constructive conflict), Dror/Lindblom on policy models, Weidner on development admin, Riggs (sala model ecology-admin) — classic recycled favourites for 10/15 markers.

Standard spine books

  • Mohit Bhattacharya (theory), Fadia & Fadia (comprehensive mug), Nicholas Henry (public-administration-public-affairs), IGNOU MPA blocks, Prasad & Prasad (Thinkers), ARC digest compilations (2nd ARC summaries — core for Paper-II ammunition), Laxmikanth for governance flesh depth patches.

PYQ pattern notes

  • Rotation spread: NPM-vs-Weber fault lines, civil-service neutrality pendulum, accountability structures comparison questions, district-administration centrality incantations, public-sector reform recent-narrative touch-ups.

Caution

  • Thin coaching ecosystem = fewer current-models series; solution = PYQ answer-banks and EPW article-mixing.

Previous Year Questions

What the examiner has actually asked.

Paper-wise patterns framed on recurring themes (official papers carry verbatim texts). Model approaches included.

Paper 1 — pattern questions

Q1. "Simon buried the proverbs; NPM dug them up." Comment. (10 marks)

Approach: Simon's critique — principles as contradictory proverbs (span vs flatness) → NPM's reinvention of techniques (targets, decentralisation) → post-NPM re-centralisation (whole-of-government) → verdict: pendulum with learning, not progress-by-refutation alone.

Q2. Compare Weber's bureaucracy with Barnard's cooperative system on authority. (15 marks)

Approach: Weber — legal-rational hierarchy, authority from office, obedience as duty vs Barnard — acceptance zone, authority from below-in-effect, communication-efficiency conditions → Indian bridge: transfer-policy cynicism vs team-based mission modes → assessment: hybrid reality.

Q3. Explain Riggs's prismatic-sala model; assess its fit for India. (10 marks)

Approach: fused → prismatic (heterogeneity, formalism, overlapping) → diffracted; sala as prismatic bureaucracy → fits: implementation gap, formal-informal duality → critiques: static, non-falsifiable, modernisation teleology → contemporary twist: digital-era prisms (e-formalism).

Q4. "Zero-based budgeting answers yesterday's question." Evaluate ZBB and PPBS lineage. (10 marks)

Approach: PPBS — programme-objective link, systems analysis; ZBB — decision packages, justification from zero → Indian pilots (ZBB 1980s, outcome budgets, UT-style zero-based reviews) → limits: paperwork, politics of baselines → current echo: output-outcome framework, DFPRs.

Q5. Delegated legislation: necessity, dangers and safeguards. (15 marks)

Approach: necessity (technical detail, time) → dangers (bypass, scrutiny deficit) → safeguards — parliamentary (committees on subordinate legislation), judicial (ultra vires, proportionality), procedural (consultation, publication) → Indian case: rule-making under tax/environment statutes.

Paper 2 — pattern questions

Q6. "NITI Aayog is a think-tank asked to do what a commission once commanded." Compare. (10 marks)

Approach: Planning Commission — allocative command, plan-cell federalism vs NITI — cooperative-competitive federalism tools (indices, DPI? no — keep: indices, three-year action agendas) → evaluation: traction without purse (aspirational districts as soft-steer success; misses: PSB? — keep general: capacity-conditionality) → verdict: influence-reform agenda incomplete without fiscal levers.

Q7. The DM's role has been redefined by development and disaster duties. Discuss. (15 marks)

Approach: classical revenue-magisterial core → development-convergence chair (District Planning Committees, aspirational-model) → DM Act 2005 incident-command duties (DDMA chairmanship) → frictions: police-line authority, capacity → reforms: mission-DM staffing, ICS training institutionalisation.

Q8. Assess the Prakash Singh directives' implementation as a police-reform ledger. (10 marks)

Approach: directives recap (fixed tenure, separation, SPCs) → compliance patchwork (states vs SPC constitutions) → deeper causes: political crime-governance nexus → way-ahead: Model Police Act adoption incentives, DGP-empanelment transparency, tech-audit (CCTNS analytics).

Q9. Evaluate CPGRAMS as citizen-centric administration infrastructure. (10 marks)

Approach: design (single portal, escalation ladder, timelines) → performance record (disposal vs grievance-quality critique) → upgrades: adaptive time-limits, root-cause analytics (DAC?), feedback calls → frontier: integration with charters + social audit, AI-assisted routing with human oversight.

How to practise

  • Map 8 years of papers: P1 hot nodes — thinkers, accountability, budget, admin law; P2 — reforms, local govt, welfare delivery, DM.
  • Maintain the reform ledger (4 cards) and 12 thinker-application cards; weekly 3 answers with one diagram each.
