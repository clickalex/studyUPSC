Lesson 1.1.16

Painting: Miniature, Mughal & Modern Schools

In this lessonDetailed StudyRevision DigestKey Points & MindmapDiagrams, Maps & FlowchartsPrevious Year Questions

Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Chain: wall murals → palm-leaf minis → Mughal atelier → Rajasthani-Pahari offshoots → folk living traditions. Pair school-patron-signature.

1. Wall murals

  • Ajanta (2nd BCE–6th CE): tempera (fresco-secco) on mud-plaster; icons: Padmapani (cave 1), Dying Princess (16), Bodhisattva & Jataka panels (17); Chalukya Badami, Pallava Panamalai panels; Kailasa (Ellora) Shaiva panels; Lepakshi (Vijayanagara, Veerabhadra — hanging pillar lore); Kerala: Mattancherry (Ramayana panels), Guruvayur; Odisha: Raghurajpur pata-chitra (palm + cloth).
  • Technique note: true fresco (wet lime) vs Indian tempera (dry plaster + binder) — one-line differentiator.

2. Miniature arc

  • Pala (Bengal-Bihar, 8th-12th): palm-leaf Buddhist manuscripts, monastic patronage, Vajrayana icons.
  • Jain (western Indian): Kalpasutra/Kalakacharya katha; Gujarat-Rajasthan; profuse gold, 'pata' style; profile eyes convention; paper from 13th-14th c.
  • Mughal: Humayun returns from Persia with Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad → the imperial atelier is born; Akbar: Tutinama, Hamzanama (1400 folios), Razmnama (Mahabharata Persian), Akbarnama — Indian-Persian synthesis, Daswant and Basawan masters; Jahangir: connoisseur peak — portraits, flora-fauna (Ustad Mansur — dodo/painted stork), European prints arrive (Jesuit missions); Shah Jahan: courtly polish, jewel tones, formal borders, harem albums, Padshahnama; decline post-aurangzeb → provincial offshoots (Awadh, Murshidabad).
  • Rajasthani (Rajput courts): Mewar (Chawand Ragamala 1605, Mewar Ramayana), Kishangarh (Bani-Thani — Savant Singh-Nihal Chand), Bundi-Kota (hunting, love scenes), Bikaner (Mughal-trained), Jodhpur (Marwar bold), Dhundhar-Amber-Jaipur; themes: Ragamala, Baramasa, Bhagavata, Gita-Govinda.
  • Pahari (Himalayan foothills): Basohli (c.1660-90 — bold primaries, Rasamanjari (Devidasa atelier); 'Chaurapanchasika' lineage), Kangra (post-1765 Guler-trained: Manaku-Nainsukh family; Gita-Govinda, Bihari Satsai; lyric wash style under Raja Sansar Chand), Guler (first naturalism), Garhwal, Jammu.
  • Deccan: Bijapur (Ibrahim Adil Shah II), Golconda (lush gold, dancing girls), Ahmadnagar ('Nujum al-Ulum' manuscript).

3. Folk-tribal living traditions

  • Madhubani/Mithila (Bihar): kohbar (wedding-chamber) symbols; fish motif; women-led; Bharni-Kachni styles; post-1934 earthquake revival credit (William Archer documentation).
  • Warli (Maharashtra): white rice-paste on mud walls, tarpa dance circles, chauk (marriage square), Jivya Soma Mashe revival.
  • Phad (Shahpura, Rajasthan): Pabuji-Devnarayan epic scrolls, bhopa-bhopi performance.
  • Patachitra: Odisha (Jagannath icons) + Bengal (Patua scroll-singers), Sanjhi (Mathura paper-stencil).
  • Kalamkari (Andhra-Telangana): Srikalahasti (pen) vs Machilipatnam (block) — draw-with-pen vs block-print; myrobalan + mordant chemistry.
  • Gond (MP), Pithora (Gujarat-Rajasthan, Bhilala), Cheriyal (Telangana scrolls), Thangka (Ladakh-Sikkim-Tawang — Vajrayana iconometry), Manjusha (Bihar), Kalighat (Bengal pat).

MCQ hooks

  • Bani-Thani = Kishangarh; 'India's Mona Lisa' tag.
  • Mansur = Jahangir's animal painter; Hamzanama = Akbar launch project.
  • Basohli vs Kangra: bold vs lyrical; Nainsukh = Guler-trained realist.
  • Kalamkari twin towns: Srikalahasti pen, Machilipatnam block.
  • Warli tarpa circle ≠ Madhubani kohbar.
  • Ajanta tempera ≠ European true fresco.

Revision Digest

Condensed for the last mile before the exam.

Prelims GS-I › History & Culture › Art & Culture. School-identification by patron/theme/palette; a favourite for "consider the following" questions.

Pre-historic & cave

  • Bhimbetka (MP; Wakankar 1957; Mesolithic hunting scenes; UNESCO 2003).
  • Ajanta (2nd BCE–6th CE; tempera/fresco-secco on dry plaster; Padmapani & Vajrapani (cave 1), Dying Princess (16), Jataka narratives; Vakataka phase), Bagh (MP; Gupta; secular), Sittanavasal (TN; Pandya; Jain; lotus pond), Ellora (Kailasa ceiling), Badami (Chalukya Mangalesha, cave 3 — earliest Brahmanical), Lepakshi (Vijayanagara; Veerabhadra; no primary colours, black outlines), Armamalai, Jogimara (Chhattisgarh; earliest c. 1st BCE), Kerala murals (Mattancherry, Padmanabhapuram).
  • Vishnudharmottara Purana (Chitrasutra; shadanga — six limbs of painting, cited via Vatsyayana).

Manuscript/miniature (east & west)

  • Pala (9th–12th; palm-leaf; Buddhist — Ashtasahasrika Prajnaparamita; Dhiman & Vitpala; sinuous lines) → Western Indian/Apabhramsa (Gujarat-Rajasthan; Jain Kalpasutra, Kalakacharya Katha; protruding farther eye, angular faces; 11th–15th c.).
  • Sultanate/pre-Mughal: Nimatnama (Mandu, c. 1500), Chaurapanchasika group, Laur-Chanda.

Mughal school

  • Humayun brought Mir Sayyid Ali & Abdus Samad (Persian Safavid). Akbar: Tutinama, Hamzanama (1,400 large cloth folios), Razmnama, Akbarnama; artists Daswant, Basawan, Miskin, Kesu Das; tasvir khana; European influence (Jesuits, 1580). Jahangir: peak — naturalism, portraiture, Ustad Mansur (birds/animals — Nadir-ul-Asr; Siberian crane, dodo, zebra), Abul Hasan (Nadir-uz-Zaman), Bishandas, Manohar; allegorical Jahangir preferring a Sufi sheikh; muraqqa albums; halo. Shah Jahan: formal, court scenes, Padshahnama, margins; Aurangzeb: decline; artists migrate → provincial/Rajput/Pahari.
  • Deccani: Ahmednagar (Tarif-i-Husain Shahi), Bijapur (Ibrahim Adil Shah II — Nujum-ul-Ulum; Ragamala), Golconda; Persian + Turkish + Vijayanagara elements; lavish gold, tall figures; Hyderabad later.

Rajasthani (Rajput) schools

  • Mewar (Sahibdin — Ragamala 1605 Chawand; Rasikapriya; Nathdwara pichhwai — Shrinathji), Marwar/Jodhpur (Pabuji ki phad; Man Singh), Bikaner (Mughal-influenced; Usta family), Bundi (lush landscapes, Ragamala, Chitrashala), Kota (hunting scenes), Kishangarh (**Nihal Chand — Bani Thani, Savant Singh; elongated eyes), Jaipur/Amber** (Sahib Ram; large portraits), Malwa (bold flat colours), Alwar.

Pahari schools

  • Basohli (earliest, 17th c.; bold primary colours, beetle-wing cases for jewels, Rasamanjari of Devidasa 1694), Guler (Nainsukh, Manaku — Pandit Seu family; soft, lyrical), Kangra (Sansar Chand; Gita Govinda, Bhagavata, Nala-Damayanti; delicate feminine ideal), Chamba (rumal embroidery too), Garhwal (Mola Ram), Kulu, Mandi, Jammu.

Company & Modern

  • Company school (Patna/Kalighat/Delhi/Tanjore-Trichy; Sewak Ram, Ghulam Ali Khan; watercolour for British patrons), Kalighat pats (19th c.; bold brush; satire), Tanjore/Mysore (gesso, gold leaf, glass; Maratha & Wodeyar patronage).
  • Raja Ravi Varma (Kerala; oil, academic realism; oleograph press Lonavala 1894; Shakuntala, Damayanti), Bengal school (Abanindranath — Bharat Mata 1905, wash technique; E.B. Havell; Nandalal Bose — Haripura posters, Constitution illustrations; Asit Haldar, Kshitindranath Majumdar), Amrita Sher-Gil (Three Girls, Bride's Toilet), Jamini Roy (Kalighat folk revival), Rabindranath (doodles), Gaganendranath (cubism), Progressive Artists' Group 1947 (Souza, Raza, Husain, Ara, Bakre, Gade), K.G. Subramanyan, Tyeb Mehta, Bhupen Khakhar, Ram Kinker Baij (sculptor, Santhal Family).

Folk & tribal (GI-tagged mostly)

  • Madhubani/Mithila (Bihar; Kohbar; natural dyes; Sita Devi, Ganga Devi), Warli (Maharashtra; white on mud; Jivya Soma Mashe), Pattachitra (Odisha; Raghurajpur; Jagannath), Kalamkari (Srikalahasti pen; Machilipatnam block), Gond (MP; Jangarh Singh Shyam; dots/dashes), Pithora (Rathwa, Gujarat), Phad (Rajasthan scroll), Cheriyal scrolls (Telangana), Thangka (Buddhist), Saura (Odisha), Rogan (Kutch; castor oil), Kalighat, Mata ni Pachedi (Gujarat), Sohrai & Khovar (Jharkhand; GI 2020), Manjusha (Bihar), Kavad (Rajasthan), Chitrakathi (Maharashtra), Tholu Bommalata (AP leather puppets), Mandana, Aipan (Uttarakhand), Chittara (Karnataka), Pichhwai.

Traps

  • Ajanta is tempera, not true fresco; Padmapani is cave 1.
  • Bani Thani = Kishangarh (Nihal Chand); Bharat Mata = Abanindranath; Haripura posters = Nandalal.
  • Basohli is the earliest Pahari school; Kangra the most refined.

Key Points & Mindmap

Skeleton facts and recall triggers.

School → patron → signature drills.

Pairs flash

  • Padmapani/Dying Princess → Ajanta (tempera) · Lepakshi → Vijayanagara murals · Mattancherry → Kerala.
  • Pala → palm-leaf Buddhist · Jain school → Kalpasutra, Gujarat gold.
  • Hamzanama/Tutinama/Razmnama → Akbar · Mansur flora-fauna → Jahangir · Padshahnama polish → Shah Jahan.
  • Mir Sayyid Ali + Abdus Samad → arrive under Humayun · Bichitr/Bishandas → Jahangir portraits embassy scene.
  • Chawand Ragamala → Mewar 1605 · Bani-Thani → Kishangarh (Nihal Chand) · Rasamanjari → Basohli · Gita-Govinda lyrical → Kangra.
  • Manaku-Nainsukh → Guler-Kangra family · Sansar Chand → Kangra patron raja.
  • Srikalahasti → pen kalamkari · Machilipatnam → block kalamkari.
  • Kohbar → Madhubani · tarpa + chauk → Warli · Pabuji scroll → Phad · Jagannath → Odisha pata.
  • Thangka → Ladakh/Sikkim · Cheriyal → Telangana · Sanjhi stencil → Mathura.
  • Golconda-Bijapur → Deccan lush miniatures · Nujum al-Ulum → Ahmadnagar.

Trap flash

  • Kangra ≠ Basohli (lyrical vs bold); both = Pahari.
  • Mughal art declines under Aurangzeb (patron cut) — not a new school.
  • Bani-Thani painter = Nihal Chand, patron-poet = Savant Singh.
  • Madhubani revival credit → 1934 earthquake + Archer surveys, not post-Independence only.
  • Razmnama = Persian Mahabharata (Akbar), NOT Ramayana (that = Ramayana translations under Akbar too).
