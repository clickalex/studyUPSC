Lesson 1.1.7

Rajput Kingdoms, Palas & Cholas

Detailed Study

The full treatment of this lesson.

Post-Harsha political mosaic: tripartite Kannauj war (Palas-Pratihara-Rashtrakuta), Deccan switches, southern imperial Cholas, and eastern/northern satellites.

1. North & Deccan powers

PowerCore anchors
Palas (Bengal-Bihar)Gopala (elected — Tibetan tradition); Dharmapala (founded Vikramashila + Somapura; Kannauj nominee Chakrayudha), Devapala (Balaputradeva letters, Nalanda grants); Buddhist patrons; Mahipala I late revival
Gurjara-PratiharasNagabhata I (shield against Arab raids), Vatsaraja-Mihira Bhoja (Adivaraha coins; Gwalior prashasti traces the 'Pratihara' line), Mahendrapala I (Kashmir poet reach — Rajasekhara); Kannauj seat; decline → Ghazni window
RashtrakutasDantidurga overthrows Chalukyas; Krishna I — Kailasa Ellora; Dhruva & Govinda III (both Kannauj campaigns); Amoghavarsha (Kavirajamarga — earliest Kannada poetics; capital Manyakheta; Jain lean — Jinasena); Arab travellers praise (Al-Masudi)
ChandelasJejakabhukti (Bundelkhand); Yashovarman (Lakshmana temple), Kandariya Mahadeva era (Vidyadhara — meets Ghazni raids); Khajuraho cluster
Paramaras & SolankisRaja Bhoja (Dhara — Samaranganasutradhara architecture treatise, Bhojshala); Solankis/Chaulukyas of Anhilwara: Modhera Sun temple, Dilwara Jain marble (Vimala Vasahi, Tejpala), Somnath (Ghazni 1025-26 raid hook)
Gahadavalas & SenasGovindachandra (Varanasi grants; Kumaradevi vihara at Sarnath), Jaichandra (defeated by Ghori at Chandawar 1194); Senas: Vijayasena, Lakshmanasena (Jayadeva's Gitagovinda at court; Nadia falls to Bakhtiyar Khalji c.1204)

2. Imperial Cholas (850–1279)

  • Vijayalaya (Thanjavur capture) → Rajaraja I (Brihadeshwara 1010; Sri Lanka, Maldives; navy begins) → Rajendra I (Gangaikondacholapuram new capital; Srivijaya naval expedition — Kadaram; Gangetic march titles) → Rajadhiraja (Koppam), Kulottunga I (Chola-Kalinga wars; 'sungam tavirtta' tax abolition fame).
  • Administration: Uttaramerur inscriptions — kudavolai (pot-ticket) village committees (sabha/ur), variyam committees (garden, tank, gold); land survey; vellanvagai vs brahmadeya vs devadana land types; nadu-valanadu-mandalam tiers.
  • Economy: temple as bank-employer; trade guilds Ainnurruvar (Five Hundred), manigramam; embassies to Song China (1077); bronze panchaloha Nataraja peak; Chola 'dravida' vimana style apex.

3. Pan-Indian features

  • Tripartite war for Kannauj (Pala-Pratihara-Rashtrakuta) — prestige symbol post-Harsha.
  • Feudal markers: land-grant proliferation (agrahara, devagrahara), samanta hierarchy, tax-transfer titles (brahmadeya/devadana), temple urbanisation.
  • Arab raids begin (Sindh 712 — Muhammad bin Qasim) → maritime pressure → eventual Ghaznavid (Mahmud: 17 raids 1000-1027; Somnath 1025) → Ghorid conquest (Tarain I 1191 loss, Tarain II 1192 win; Chandawar 1194; Bakhtiyar Khalji east c.1204).

MCQ hooks

  • Kavirajamarga = Amoghavarsha (Kannada) — authorship sometimes credited to court poet Srivijaya.
  • Uttaramerur = Chola village self-government (kudavolai system).
  • Kailasa Ellora = Krishna I; Somapura = Dharmapala.
  • Bhoja's treatise = Samaranganasutradhara; Modhera = Solanki.
  • Srivijaya raid = Rajendra I; Gangaikondacholapuram = same king.
  • Ghazni 17 raids, Somnath 1025; Ghori Tarain pair 1191/1192.

Revision Digest

Condensed for the last mile before the exam.

Prelims GS-I › History & Culture › Medieval History. Dynasty–capital–founder–monument matching is the pattern.

Tripartite struggle for Kannauj (8th–9th c.)

  • Palas (Bengal-Bihar; Gopala elected c. 750; Dharmapala — Vikramashila, Somapura/Paharpur; Devapala — Nalanda patron; Buddhist), Pratiharas (Nagabhata I repelled Arabs; Mihira Bhoja; Kannauj capital; Al-Masudi calls them Al-Juzr), Rashtrakutas (Dantidurga; Krishna I — Kailasa temple Ellora; Amoghavarsha — Kavirajamarga, Manyakheta; Govinda III; Indra III sacked Kannauj 916).

Rajput clans

  • Chauhans/Chahamanas (Ajmer-Delhi; Prithviraj III — Tarain 1191 win, 1192 loss; Prithviraj Raso Chand Bardai), Chandelas (Khajuraho; Kandariya Mahadeva c. 1030; Vidyadhara vs Mahmud), Paramaras (Malwa; Bhoja — Bhojpur, Bhojshala, Samarangana Sutradhara), Solankis/Chaulukyas (Gujarat; Anhilwara; Bhima I — Modhera Sun temple; Rani ki Vav Udayamati; Dilwara Vimala Vasahi 1031), Gahadavalas (Kannauj; Jaichandra), Tomars (Delhi/Dhillika c. 736; Anangpal), Kalachuris (Tripuri), Senas (Bengal after Palas; Ballala Sena kulinism; Lakshmana Sena — Jayadeva Gita Govinda; fled Bakhtiyar Khalji 1204).
  • Origin theories: Agnikula (Chand Bardai; four clans from Mt Abu fire), foreign origin (Tod), Kshatriya-descent (Gaurishankar Ojha).

Cholas (imperial phase 850–1279)

  • Vijayalaya took Thanjavur c. 850; Rajaraja I (985–1014) — Brihadishvara/Rajarajeshvara Thanjavur 1010, Sri Lanka north, Maldives; Rajendra I (1014–44) — Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Ganges expedition (Gangaikonda), Srivijaya naval raid 1025, Kadaram; Kulottunga I (Chola-Chalukya merger, abolished tolls "Sungam tavirtta").
  • Administration: mandalam → valanadu → nadu → kurram/ur; Uttaramerur inscriptions (Parantaka I, 919 & 921) — kudavolai (pot-ticket) election to sabha committees (variyams); ur (general village), sabha/mahasabha (Brahmadeya), nagaram (merchants); guilds Manigramam, Ainnurruvar (Five Hundred of Ayyavole), Nanadesi; land survey Rajaraja; kani rights.
  • Bronze Nataraja (lost-wax, cire perdue); Dravida vimana peak.

Other south

  • Cheras of Mahodayapuram (Kulasekhara Alvar), Pandyas second empire (Jatavarman Sundara — Marco Polo 1293), Hoysalas (Belur 1117 Vishnuvardhana, Halebidu, Somnathpur 1268), Kakatiyas (Warangal; Rudramadevi; Ramappa temple 1213 — UNESCO 2021), Yadavas (Devagiri; Hemadri), Eastern Gangas (Anantavarman Chodaganga — Jagannath Puri; Narasimhadeva I — Konark 1250).

Arab & Turkish contact

  • Muhammad bin Qasim conquers Sindh 712 (Dahir); Mahmud of Ghazni 17 raids 1000–1027 (Somnath 1025–26; Alberuni Kitab-ul-Hind; Firdausi); Muhammad Ghori — Multan 1175, Gujarat repulse 1178 (Bhima II/Naiki Devi), Tarain 1191/1192, Chandawar 1194 (Jaichandra), died 1206.

Traps

  • Kailasa at Ellora = Rashtrakuta Krishna I, not Chola.
  • Uttaramerur belongs to Parantaka I, not Rajaraja.
  • Rajendra I's naval expedition was against Srivijaya (Sumatra), not Sri Lanka alone.

Key Points & Mindmap

Skeleton facts and recall triggers.

Power → anchor drills.

Pairs flash

  • Elected Gopala → Palas · Vikramashila + Somapura → Dharmapala · Gitagovinda court → Lakshmanasena.
  • Gwalior prashasti + Adivaraha coins → Mihira Bhoja (Pratihara) · Arab shield → Nagabhata I.
  • Kailasa Ellora → Krishna I · Kavirajamarga → Amoghavarsha · Manyakheta → Rashtrakuta capital.
  • Khajuraho Kandariya → Chandelas · Modhera + Dilwara + Somnath → Solankis · Bhojshala + Samarangana → Bhoja (Paramara).
  • Chandawar 1194 → Jaichandra (Gahadavala) · Nadia sack → Bakhtiyar Khalji vs Senas.
  • Brihadeshwara → Rajaraja I · Srivijaya raid + Gangaikondacholapuram → Rajendra I · Uttaramerur kudavolai → Chola sabha.
  • Ainnurruvar Five Hundred → Chola trade guild · manigramam → south guild too · Song China embassy 1077.
  • Sindh 712 → M.B. Qasim · Somnath 1025 → Mahmud Ghazni · Tarain II 1192 → Ghori.

Trap flash

  • Tripartite war contestants: Palas, Pratiharas, Rashtrakutas (NOT Cholas).
  • Uttaramerur = kudavolai pot-ticket (not ballot paper anachronism).
  • Two Krishnas: Krishna I (Rashtrakuta, Kailasa) vs Krishna III (Takkolam vs Cholas).
  • Kannauj = tripartite prize; Thanjavur + Gangaikondacholapuram = Chola twin capitals.
  • Mahmud = raider-poet court (Firdausi, Alberuni came), Ghori = conqueror-settler.
